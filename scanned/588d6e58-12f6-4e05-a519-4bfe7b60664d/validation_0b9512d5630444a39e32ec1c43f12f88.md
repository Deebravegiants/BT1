### Title
Withdrawal fee rounds to zero for small `lp_token_burn`, allowing fee evasion via transaction splitting - ([File: substrate/frame/asset-conversion/src/lib.rs])

### Summary
`do_remove_liquidity` computes `withdrawal_fee_amount = T::LiquidityWithdrawalFee::get() * lp_token_burn` and then derives the redeemable share from `lp_token_burn - withdrawal_fee_amount`. Because `LiquidityWithdrawalFee` is a `Permill`/`Perbill`-style ratio applied to an integer, this multiplication rounds down to zero whenever `lp_token_burn` is small enough, letting a liquidity provider redeem LP tokens for their exact proportional (fee-free) share by splitting a withdrawal into many minimal-unit calls.

### Finding Description
The fee is computed on the *input* (`lp_token_burn`), not on the resulting output amounts:
`withdrawal_fee_amount = Permill::get() * lp_token_burn` then `lp_redeem_amount = lp_token_burn - withdrawal_fee_amount`, and `amount1/amount2 = Self::mul_div(reserve, lp_redeem_amount, total_supply)`. For sufficiently small `lp_token_burn` (e.g. `lp_token_burn = 1`), any fixed fee ratio strictly less than 100% causes `Permill::get() * lp_token_burn` to round down to `0`, so `lp_redeem_amount == lp_token_burn` and the caller pays no fee at all for that call. This is reachable by any signed LP holder through the public `remove_liquidity` extrinsic / `MutateLiquidity::remove_liquidity` trait impl with no privileged origin required [1](#0-0) .

However, in each such call `mul_div(reserve, lp_redeem_amount, total_supply)` also rounds down proportionally to `lp_token_burn`, so the attacker only ever receives the fair, integer-rounded proportional share for the LP tokens actually burned — never more than they are entitled to. The "fee" that is skipped is not diverted to a treasury but represents the portion of LP tokens that would normally be burned without redemption (i.e., value that would otherwise be left behind to benefit remaining LPs). Skipping it via rounding therefore evades a value-transfer-to-remaining-LPs mechanism, not a theft of extra principal from the pool.

For the exploit to yield anything non-zero per call, `reserve / total_supply` must be large enough that `mul_div(reserve, 1, total_supply) >= 1` (and also `>= amount_min`), which only occurs in pools with a skewed reserve-to-LP-supply ratio (e.g., due to very different asset decimals or heavy prior fee accrual). In a typical pool where reserves and LP supply are comparable in magnitude, `lp_token_burn = 1` yields `amount1 = amount2 = 0`, which fails the `amount_min` check and the call reverts.

### Impact Explanation
The scoped impact — "asymptotic drain the pool for free" — is not fully substantiated. Each successful zero-fee micro-withdrawal only returns the caller's exact proportional share of the pool for the LP tokens burned; it does not extract more value than the caller is entitled to, so no direct theft of other LPs' principal occurs. The concrete, real impact is narrower: the intended withdrawal-fee mechanism (which normally leaves a small residual value in the pool for remaining LPs, or otherwise discourages rapid add/remove cycles) can be bypassed at negligible-but-nonzero scale by splitting withdrawals into minimal-unit calls in pools with skewed reserve/supply ratios, at the cost of one transaction fee per unit withdrawn. This is fee-revenue/anti-churn evasion, not confirmed fund theft or full pool drainage.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitability is limited: the attacker must already hold a large LP balance, must operate in a pool where `reserve/total_supply` is skewed enough that `mul_div` returns non-zero for `lp_token_burn = 1`, and must pay per-transaction extrinsic fees for every unit withdrawn — making large-scale exploitation economically self-defeating relative to the tiny per-call fee evaded. It is feasible only as a low-value griefing/precision issue in specific pool configurations, not a generally reachable, high-impact drain across arbitrary pools.

### Recommendation
Apply the withdrawal fee with rounding that favors the protocol/remaining LPs (round up the fee rather than round down), or enforce a minimum effective fee (e.g., `withdrawal_fee_amount = max(1, Permill::get() * lp_token_burn)` when the ratio is non-zero), and/or impose a minimum `lp_token_burn` per call to prevent fee evasion via dust-sized withdrawal splitting.

### Proof of Concept
Integration test in `substrate/frame/asset-conversion/src/tests.rs`:
1. Create a pool with reserves scaled such that `reserve1 / total_supply >= 2` (e.g., asset1 with high decimals, asset2 with low decimals) and add substantial liquidity to obtain a large LP balance.
2. Loop calling `remove_liquidity` with `lp_token_burn = 1` for `k` iterations, tracking cumulative `amount1`/`amount2` received and cumulative LP tokens burned.
3. Separately, call `remove_liquidity` once with `lp_token_burn = k` from a fresh identical pool state.
4. Assert: `sum(fees deducted across k calls of size 1) == 0` while `fee deducted in single call of size k > 0`, demonstrating fee bypass; but also assert `sum(amount1_i) approx== amount1_single_call` (within `k` rounding-unit tolerance), confirming no extra value beyond fair share is extracted — validating the fee-evasion characterization rather than a fund-theft/drain characterization.
