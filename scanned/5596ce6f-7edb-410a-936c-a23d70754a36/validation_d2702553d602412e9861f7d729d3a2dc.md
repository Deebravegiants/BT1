### Title
Proxy filter is not applied recursively to the derivative-account effect of `Utility::as_derivative` - (File: substrate/frame/proxy/src/lib.rs)

### Summary
`pallet_proxy`'s permission check (`InstanceFilter::filter`) is evaluated only against the outer `RuntimeCall` variant supplied to `Proxy::proxy`, not against the effective call/origin that ultimately executes. When the outer call is `Utility::as_derivative{ index, call }`, `pallet_utility` internally re-dispatches `call` with a brand-new signed origin derived deterministically from `(real, index)` via `TrailingZeroInput` entropy, so any `ProxyType` variant whose filter matches on `RuntimeCall::Utility(..)` generically effectively grants the delegate authority to act as *any* derivative sub-account of `real`, not just the scope the delegation was meant to cover.

### Finding Description
`Proxy::proxy` (and `proxy_announced`) resolve the caller's permitted `ProxyDefinition` and then call `def.proxy_type.filter(&call)` before dispatching `call` with `RuntimeOrigin::signed(real)` [1](#0-0) . This check inspects only the shape of the immediate `RuntimeCall` value that was submitted to `proxy`; it has no visibility into what that call does internally once dispatched.

`Utility::as_derivative` computes a pseudonymous `AccountId` from `(real, index)` and re-dispatches the inner `call` with that derived account as the new signed origin, bypassing the original `real` identity entirely from the perspective of the inner dispatch [2](#0-1) . Because the proxy's `filter` sees only `RuntimeCall::Utility(pallet_utility::Call::as_derivative{..})` and many `InstanceFilter` implementations across runtimes match `RuntimeCall::Utility(..)` broadly (to permit convenience batching for a given `ProxyType`), the filter approves the outer call without ever inspecting or re-filtering the wrapped inner `call` or the fact that it will execute under a completely different (derived) account than `real`.

This means a delegate authorized only for a narrow purpose (e.g. `CancelProxy`, or any `ProxyType` whose filter allows `Utility{..}` generically) can pick an arbitrary `index`, deriving any of `real`'s pseudonymous sub-accounts, and dispatch an arbitrary inner call (e.g. `Balances::transfer_allow_death`) as that derived account. If that derived account happens to hold independent privileges, deposits, or balances (which is a common real-world pattern — many pallets create derivative accounts for specific roles), the delegate obtains unauthorized control over funds/actions never explicitly scoped by the proxy relationship.

### Impact Explanation
A delegate with a narrowly-scoped proxy relationship (only intended to permit, say, `Utility::batch` of specific harmless calls, or where `Utility{..}` was allowed as a convenience) can escalate to acting as an arbitrary derivative account of `real`, performing unauthorized transfers or privileged actions tied to that derivative account, exceeding the explicitly authorized scope of the proxy delegation.

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires: (1) a proxy relationship exists with a `ProxyType` whose `InstanceFilter::filter` matches `RuntimeCall::Utility(..)` (or `as_derivative` specifically) without recursively filtering the wrapped call/effective origin, and (2) the resulting derivative account of `real` holds assets/privileges of value. Given that several `InstanceFilter` implementations across the workspace's runtimes permit `Utility` calls broadly for usability, and any signed account can call `Proxy::proxy` once granted such a delegation, the attack is straightforward and fully repeatable — the attacker only needs to pick the `index` and craft the inner call.

### Recommendation
`InstanceFilter` implementations must not treat `RuntimeCall::Utility(..)` as safe by default; they should either exclude `as_derivative` (and `batch`/`batch_all`/`dispatch_as`) from generic Utility approval, or recursively apply `self.filter(inner_call)` to the wrapped call(s) before allowing the outer `Utility` call, ensuring the filter is evaluated against the effective dispatched call and its true origin rather than only the immediate wrapper.

### Proof of Concept
Integration test combining `pallet_proxy` + `pallet_utility` in a mock runtime:
1. Configure `ProxyType::CancelProxy` (or any type whose filter matches `RuntimeCall::Utility(..)`) as the only permission granted from `real` to `delegate`.
2. From `delegate`, call `Proxy::proxy(real, None, RuntimeCall::Utility(Call::as_derivative{ index: N, call: Box::new(Balances::transfer_allow_death{ dest: attacker, value: X }) }))`.
3. Assert the dispatch succeeds and that funds are moved out of the derivative account of `(real, N)` despite `real` never granting a proxy scope covering `Balances::transfer_allow_death` or that specific derivative account — proving the filter was bypassed via the wrapper.
4. Repeat for each `ProxyType` variant in-repo whose `filter` matches `RuntimeCall::Utility(..)` generically, asserting the same bypass occurs.
