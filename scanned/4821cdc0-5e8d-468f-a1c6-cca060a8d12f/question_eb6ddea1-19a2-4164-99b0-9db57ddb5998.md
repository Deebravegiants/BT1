In `EmporiumUpgradeable.payRelayFees`, when `signerAddress == address(0)`, `relayFee` is computed via `$._hinkalHelper.calculateRelayFee(sumAbs, flatFee, feeStructure.variableRate)` and paid directly via `sendToRelay`. But when `signerAddress != address(0)`, `relayFee = flatFee` (skipping the variableRate calculation entirely) and is paid via `sendToRelayFromWallet` which calls `IHinkalWallet(signerAddress).doSendToRelay(relay, relayFee, feeToken)` — meaning the wallet itself, not Emporium, sources the funds. Can an attacker set `stack.signerAddress` to point at ANY contract implementing `IHinkalWallet.doSendToRelay` (including a contract the attacker deploys that simply returns without reverting and never actually pays), causing `payRelay` to believe the fee was paid (no return-value or balance check after `doSendToRelay`) while the real Emporium-side accounting in `runAction`'s `handleOut` still credits `msg.sender` the full `balanceChange` computed BEFORE relay-fee deduction, i.e. is the relay fee ever actually verified to have left `stack.signerAddress`'s balance, or is `doSendToRelay`'s success purely trusted? Equality broken: relay fee `circomData.feeStructure.flatFee` intended to be paid to `circomData.relay` != actual balance decrease of the fake wallet / actual balance increase of `circomData.relay`. Impact: High, permanent freezing/theft of relay fees since a fake `IHinkalWallet` can silently skip paying while the transaction still completes and mints the attacker's UTXO in full via `handleOut`. Proof: Foundry test deploying a stub `IHinkalWallet` whose `doSendToRelay` is a no-op, running an Emporium action with `stack.signerAddress` pointing to it, and asserting the relay's token balance is unchanged while the attacker's UTXO is minted with `handleOut` at full value.
