### Title
Stale stamped liquidation threshold lets a permissionless liquidator seize collateral after governance raises a rescue LT — (File: contracts/controller/src/risk/params.rs)

### Summary
Every supply position stamps `loan_to_value`, `liquidation_threshold`, `liquidation_bonus` and `liquidation_fees` from the spoke listing at creation, and the controller thereafter trusts the stored stamp rather than the live listing (`calculate_account_risk_totals` uses `position.liquidation_threshold` for HF). The stamp is only restamped by `merge_supply_leg`, `merge_withdraw_leg`, or `update_account_threshold`. A liquidation (`liquidate`) never restamps — so after governance raises an asset's liquidation threshold (e.g., to rescue borrowers during a downturn), an account that is healthy under the live LT remains liquidatable under its stale, lower LT until some unrelated restamp path happens to touch it.

This is the same bug class as Hats M-14: a stale locally-cached value silently overrides the current source of truth, and a third party can exploit the window before anyone syncs it — the sandwich attack described in the report maps to racing `liquidate` against the permissionless `update_account_threshold` restamp.

### Finding Description
- `apply_gated_liquidation_params` (params.rs:68-93) gates the whole tuple on `favors_liquidator`; a *borrower-favorable* change (higher LT) is never gated, yet is only ever applied by the three refresh paths.
- `merge_withdraw_leg` explicitly skips restamping for `WithdrawKind::Liquidation` via `leg_may_restamp_risk_params` (supply.rs:337-339, 356).
- `liquidate` is permissionless (any address may liquidate an account whose HF < 1 under stored params).
- `update_account_threshold` is also permissionless, but it is a separate transaction; nothing forces a sync before `liquidate` evaluates HF.

Sequence:
1. Governance proposes and executes `AdminOperation::EditAssetInSpoke` raising `liquidation_threshold` for an asset (e.g., from 6000 → 8000 to protect accounts hit by a price drop). Executing a ready governance operation is permissionless.
2. Victim accounts stamped with LT 6000 remain at 6000 in storage. Under stored LT their HF < 1; under live LT 8000 their HF > 1.
3. An attacker calls `controller::liquidate` in the first ledger(s) after execution, seizing collateral with bonus, before any owner top-up, withdraw, or keeper `update_account_threshold` restamps the leg.
4. Even if the victim or a keeper submits `update_account_threshold(caller, true, [id])` immediately, the attacker's `liquidate` can be sequenced first — the classic sandwich window from M-14.

### Impact Explanation
Accounts that governance explicitly re-parameterized to be safe are liquidated at their stale, harsher terms. The borrower loses the liquidation bonus on seized collateral (base bonus plus the HF-scaled curve bonus, floored to whole units); this is theft of user funds through premature liquidation — economically identical to the Hats issue where a hat "intended to be off" is used by an attacker during the transition window. The protocol has already decided these accounts should not be liquidatable (that is what raising LT means); the stale stamp defeats that decision.

### Likelihood Explanation
The trigger requires an LT raise on a listed asset while accounts are underwater at the old LT — exactly the scenario where governance raises LT (market stress). The exploit path needs no privilege: `liquidate` is open to anyone, and no sync is forced beforehand. The rescue call (`update_account_threshold`) and the attack are both permissionless, so ordering is decided by ledger inclusion — an attacker monitoring governance execution can land the liquidation first. The only mitigation is that a keeper or the victim wins the race, which is timing luck, not a control.

### Recommendation
Before evaluating health factor in `liquidate`, restamp borrower-favorable risk parameters: run `refresh_supply_risk_params` (or at least the non-`favors_liquidator` half of `apply_gated_liquidation_params` — apply the new tuple when the change raises LT, lowers bonus, or raises fees) over the target account's listed supply legs inside `liquidate`. The adverse direction is already safely gated by `THRESHOLD_UPDATE_MIN_HF`; the favorable direction is ungated everywhere and can be applied unconditionally at liquidation time, mirroring the recommended `checkHatToggle()` sync-before-switch fix.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice supplies USDC and borrows ETH; her USDC leg is stamped LT 6000 (`get_or_create_supply_position` copies the listing).
2. USDC price drops so `calculate_account_risk_totals` with stored LT 6000 gives HF = 0.97 (`is_liquidatable == true`).
3. Governance executes `EditAssetInSpoke` raising USDC `liquidation_threshold` to 8000; under LT 8000 Alice's HF = 1.29 — the listing change is meant to save the account.
4. Storage still holds LT 6000. No restamp path has run: Alice hasn't supplied/withdrawn, and nobody called `update_account_threshold`.
5. Attacker calls `liquidate(attacker, alice_id, [(ETH, debt)], SeizeMode::Transfer)`. `merge_withdraw_leg` skips the restamp for `WithdrawKind::Liquidation`, HF is computed from stale LT 6000, HF < 1 passes, and the attacker seizes collateral plus bonus — funds the live listing says were not seizable.
6. A competing `update_account_threshold(keeper, true, [alice_id])` submitted in the same ledger sequence does not help if the liquidate lands first; after it lands, the stored tuple becomes 8000 and the account is no longer liquidatable — confirming the loss was purely a sync-ordering artifact.
