### Title
Supply-index floor clamp resurrects unbacked "phantom" supply claims that drain recapitalized cash - ([File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs](contracts/pool/src/interest.rs))

### Summary
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` writes the total-supplied-value loss into `supply_index`, but clamps the result upward at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`) instead of letting it fall to its true, near-zero value. When socialized bad debt meets or exceeds total supplied value, the clamp leaves every supplier's scaled shares redeemable at a floor index the market's cash cannot back. Withdrawal is gated by cash and utilization, not by the supply-entry backing check, so the first cash that re-enters the market — notably via the permissionless `recapitalize` — is immediately claimable by holders of the phantom shares. The protocol's own unit test proves the drain: a residual position withdraws exactly a fresh depositor's funds and leaves the pool empty.

### Finding Description
The bug-class analog is a value interpreted in the wrong domain: scaled supply shares continue to be valued at a *floored* index rather than the economically true one, giving dead claims a new life — the same "object outlives its backing" shape as the reported use-after-free.

Root cause in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89`:

```rust
let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
let new_supply_index = cache.supply_index().mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);
cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
```

When `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value` (`remaining == 0`), `new_supply_index` should be 0 but is forced up to `RAY/1000`. Every `scaled_amount` in the market then still unscale to positive token claims via `unscale_supply_floor` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:77-81`), while `cash` is unchanged at whatever remains.

The repo's own regression test demonstrates the theft mechanics verbatim: `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:317-369` (`test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard`) drives the index to the floor, shows `stranded > 0` phantom claim, then has a new deposit `c` enter and the stranded holder withdraw `gross == c`, ending with `cash == 0` and the honest claim `b_claim` unbacked.

Reachability for an unprivileged caller:

1. `controller::supply` — attacker becomes the dominant supplier of market X.
2. `controller::borrow` on a second account — attacker borrows market X to the utilization limit against cheap collateral.
3. Collateral price collapses (own Aquarius/Soroswap trade is in scope) until `is_socializable_bad_debt` holds: `total_debt > total_collateral` and `collateral <= 5 WAD`.
4. `controller::clean_bad_debt` (permissionless, `contracts/controller/src/lib.rs` `clean_bad_debt`) socializes the residual debt. Once accrued interest pushes `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value` — reachable because `borrow_index` grows without bound while utilization caps entry at supply — the index is clamped to the floor and the attacker's own supply shares become phantom claims dominating the market.
5. Withdrawal path (`pool ops::withdraw` → `resolve_withdrawal` + `require_reserves`, no backing gate — the backing shortfall check only gates supply *entry*) lets phantom holders pull the first cash to arrive. The permissionless `controller::recapitalize` pays measured cash into exactly this shortfall, and the phantom claim converts it into attacker withdrawals.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds: any cash that re-enters the wiped market — a victim calling `recapitalize`, or fee-on-transfer/donation flows that raise measured cash — is paid out against supply claims that were economically zeroed by the wipeout. The attacker, holding the dominant share of phantom supply from step 1, captures nearly all of it. The test asserts precisely this outcome (`userA drains exactly userB's fresh deposit out of the pool`, `pool cash () can no longer cover userB's claim`).

### Likelihood Explanation
Requirements: an insolvent position whose debt exceeds the market's total supplied value, and a later cash inflow. Debt exceeding supply is achievable through sustained accrual at high utilization plus a collateral crash — all reachable by the same unprivileged address using in-scope calls (`borrow`, `clean_bad_debt`, own DEX trades, `withdraw`). The attacker controls every step except the recapitalizer/victim inflow, which `recapitalize` makes permissionless and attractive exactly when a market shows a backing shortfall. No privileged action, timing race, or oracle dishonesty beyond market prices is needed. Medium severity: conditional setup cost, but deterministic payout once triggered.

### Recommendation
Do not clamp `supply_index` upward past the true written-down value. Options:
- Let the index reach its floored computed value (or zero with an explicit zero-index branch that rejects unscale/withdraw), so wiped positions carry no residual claim; or
- On a wipeout that hits the floor, burn/zero the market's outstanding supply bookkeeping (`supplied`, `revenue`) alongside the index write-down, keeping `supplied` consistent with `cash` so no later inflow can be claimed by dead shares; or
- Gate `withdraw`/`resolve_withdrawal` on the same backing invariant used at supply entry (`guards::backing_shortfall`), so phantom claims can never consume fresh cash.

Also reconsider whether `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`'s stated purpose ("avoid a zero index") should instead be handled by treating index-at-floor as market-dead rather than as a live claim value.

### Proof of Concept
```
1. ALICE supplies 1_000_000 X via controller::supply (owns ~all of pool supply).
2. MALLORY supplies dust collateral Y, borrows X to the utilization cap via
   controller::borrow.
3. MALLORY trades on Aquarius to collapse Y's price; interest accrues until
   debt_usd(X) > total supplied value of X.
4. Any address calls controller::clean_bad_debt(MALLORY_account).
   -> pool::ops calls apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache, bad_debt)
   -> reduction_factor = 0, new_supply_index clamped to SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW
   -> ALICE's scaled shares now unscale to a positive "phantom" claim with
      cash = 0 behind them (interest.rs:88).
5. VICTIM calls controller::recapitalize(X, amount) — permissionless —
   crediting cash up to the backing shortfall.
6. ALICE calls controller::withdraw for X.
   -> resolve_withdrawal values her shares at the floored index; withdrawal
      checks reserves/cash, not the supply-entry backing gate
   -> ALICE receives VICTIM's recapitalized tokens; the pool returns to
      cash = 0 while ALICE's account was already wiped of debt.

Reference assertion already in-repo:
  contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:317-369 shows gross == fresh deposit c
  and post-withdraw cash == 0 for a floor-residual position.
```

Note: whether step 5's recapitalization admits a net-profit extraction depends on the share of phantom supply the attacker retains versus the shortfall `recapitalize` will fill; the mechanics (phantom claims converting the first post-wipeout cash into withdrawals) are proven by the existing test, and the backing-gate asymmetry between supply entry and withdrawal is the key defect to fix regardless.
