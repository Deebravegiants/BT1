### Title
Whale market at sustained high utilization overflows the RAY value domain before the borrow-index cap, permanently freezing all market verbs - (File: common/src/rates/scaling.rs)

### Summary
CVE-2018-18440 is a size-mishandled buffer overflow: an attacker-supplied length drives a fixed-size buffer past its capacity. The structural analog in XOXNO Lending is an attacker-supplied magnitude driving fixed-width `i128` RAY-scaled arithmetic past its domain. Every pool verb accrues interest first; accrual calls `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)`, which panics with `MathOverflow` once `borrowed * borrow_index` leaves `i128`. An unprivileged user can put a market in a state where this panic becomes inevitable and irreversible, freezing all supplier and borrower funds in that market forever.

### Finding Description
The pool represents debt as RAY-scaled shares multiplied by a RAY index (`common/src/rates/index.rs:34`, `common/src/rates/scaling.rs`). `mul` panics with `MathOverflow` when `shares * index / RAY` exceeds `i128` (`common/src/math/fp_core.rs:108-118`). The documented cap `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (10^36) bounds the index, but the value `borrowed * index` overflows `i128` well before the index reaches its cap whenever `borrowed` is large (`docs/reference/formulas.md:432-437`).

The harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360`) proves end-to-end reachability on production code: an 18-decimal market with ~1e9-whole-token scale, 98% borrowed, accruing on the XLM rate curve hits `MathOverflow` inside `scaled_to_original` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` — the index cap never engages. After the cliff:

- `update_indexes` reverts (`try_update_indexes_for` fails),
- `withdraw` reverts,
- `repay` reverts — so liquidation and `clean_bad_debt`, which accrue first, also revert.

Every protected verb on that (hub, token) book accrues before acting, so the panic is permanent: there is no path that lowers `borrowed` or `borrow_index` without first evaluating the overflowing product. The attacker's own position is also frozen, but all supplier balances in the market are frozen with it.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds: the entire supplier base of the affected market loses the ability to withdraw, and borrowers cannot repay or be liquidated. `recapitalize` also accrues first, so governance cannot unstick the market through the normal bad-debt path. This satisfies the "permanent freezing of funds" acceptance criterion for a hub/spoke book reachable entirely by unprivileged `supply`/`borrow` calls.

### Likelihood Explanation
A single unprivileged address needs to supply a very large notional into a high-decimals market and borrow to high utilization (or supply the liquidity another borrower uses). Two constraints temper likelihood: the required capital is large (the fixture uses ~10^27 base units at 18 decimals), and the overflow needs sustained high borrow-rate accrual over an extended period rather than a single transaction. Smaller markets still hit the cliff proportionally (`borrowed * index` is what matters), and the state is irreversible once reached — no transaction can roll accrual back. High severity on impact, moderate on likelihood; overall High.

### Recommendation
Bound the RAY-scaled value domain, not just the index: before admitting new supply/borrow, check that `borrowed * MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY / RAY` (or a stricter projected bound) fits `i128`, and make accrual clamp `borrowed`/`index` growth instead of panicking — e.g., cap `borrow_index` at `min(MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY, i128::MAX * RAY / borrowed)` so verbs keep operating (accepting zero further interest) rather than freezing. At minimum, give `recapitalize`/bad-debt paths a non-accruing emergency mode so the market can be unwound after value overflow.

### Proof of Concept
`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360` is already an executable PoC: it builds an 18-decimal market with caps lifted, supplies `BILLION * 10^18`, borrows ~98%, advances ledger time year by year until `update_indexes` returns `MathOverflow`, then asserts `withdraw` and `repay` revert with the same error while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. All steps use unprivileged entrypoints (`supply`, `borrow`, `update_indexes`).

Relevant code: `common/src/rates/index.rs:29-45` (`update_supply_index`), `common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-66` (accrue calls `scaled_to_original` before anything else), `common/src/math/fp_core.rs:108-118` (`mul_div_half_up` panics on `i128` overflow), `docs/reference/formulas.md:432-437` (documents that value overflow can precede the index ceiling and block repayment/withdrawal).
