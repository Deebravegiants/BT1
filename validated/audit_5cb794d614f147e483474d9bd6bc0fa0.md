### Title
Bad-debt supply-index floor clamp resurrects wiped supplier claims that drain future deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
The heap-overflow class maps here as an accounting write that corrupts a global boundary shared by all users: `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` writes the post-socialization supply index down by `remaining / total_supplied_value`, but then clamps the result **up** to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`). When bad debt consumes (nearly) the entire supplied value of a market, the clamp leaves every supplier's existing scaled shares with a non-zero floor-valued claim while the pool's cash is ~zero. That phantom claim is later paid out of fresh deposits, which is theft of user funds and insolvency of the new suppliers' claims.

### Finding Description
In `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89`, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes `remaining = total_supplied_value - min(bad_debt, total_supplied_value)`, derives `reduction_factor = floor(remaining / total_supplied_value)`, and sets `supply_index = max(supply_index * reduction_factor, SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)`. When `bad_debt ≈ total_supplied_value` (i.e. `remaining / total_supplied < 1/1000`), the index is clamped **up** rather than taking positions to zero, so `unscale_supply_floor(old_scaled)` still returns a positive claim (`unscale_supply_floor` in `contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:60` uses `scaled.mul_floor(supply_index)`).

The pool's own test `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-427`) demonstrates the exact consequence: after `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache, 5_000*RAY)` on a book with `supplied = 1_000*RAY` and `cash = 0`, the clamped index leaves `stranded > 0`; a fresh deposit of `stranded` tokens is then fully drained by the old position's `resolve_withdrawal`/`require_reserves`/`debit_cash` sequence, leaving `cash < fresh_claim` — the fresh supplier's claim is unbacked.

Reachability: `clean_bad_debt` is permissionless for accounts whose collateral is at or below the $5 dust threshold (docs/reference/invariants.md INV-LIQ-04; docs/reference/formulas.md "Bad debt"). The bad debt removed is the account's remaining ceiled debt and is written down against that market's supply index via `seize_settle`/`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs` calls into it; `interest.rs:73`). A whale borrower whose debt dominates a near-100%-utilized market (utilization can drift past `max_utilization` through accrual since the guard is only checked on mutation entry points) and whose collateral crashes below $5 triggers a write-down of essentially the entire supplied value, hitting the floor clamp.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. After the clamped write-down, the market has real share claims (floor-valued) with ~zero cash. The next supplier's deposit becomes withdrawable by the pre-wipeout shareholders via the normal `withdraw` path (`resolve_withdrawal` → `require_reserves` → `debit_cash`), so old claims are paid out of new money — an effective Ponzi leg inside the market — and the new supplier's claim is left unbacked. No shares are burned for the stranded value and no `recapitalize` is required to mint them; the loss is silently socialized onto whoever supplies next.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires an extreme but reachable state: utilization approaching 100% on a hub market, a single borrower accounting for nearly all debt, and a price move making that borrower deeply insolvent (collateral < debt, residual collateral ≤ $5) so the permissionless `clean_bad_debt` socializes almost the whole book. Interest accrual and the `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` clamp are deterministic once reached; the trigger is market conditions plus one unprivileged `clean_bad_debt` call rather than any privileged action. Rated accordingly (conditional, high-severity tail event).

### Recommendation
When `remaining` floors the reduction below `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, do not clamp the index upward to keep phantom claims alive. Either (a) burn/reset `supplied` shares alongside the write-down so `supplied × supply_index` stays consistent with `remaining` (zero shares when the book is wiped, matching the debt that was zeroed), or (b) record the unpayable residual as explicit bad debt that `recapitalize` must settle before new supply mints shares against the pool. At minimum, gate `supply` on `cash >= floor-valued supplied claims` or treat a clamped index as a halted market until recapitalized.

### Proof of Concept
The repository already encodes it as a demonstration test: `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-427` (`test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard`). End-to-end unprivileged path:

1. Whale supplies/borrows until market utilization ≈ 100% (accrual pushes `borrowed` past the `max_utilization` gate post-entry, since `require_utilization_below_max` is only enforced on borrow/withdraw/revenue-claim).
2. Collateral price crashes so the whale's collateral ≤ $5 while debt ≈ entire supplied value.
3. Anyone calls `clean_bad_debt` on the whale account; `seize_settle` calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` with `bad_debt ≈ total_supplied_value`, and the index clamps to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead of zeroing shares (`interest.rs:88`). Cash is now ~0 but `supplied` shares are untouched.
4. A victim calls `supply(fresh_cash)`; shares mint at the clamped index and `cash` increases.
5. Any pre-wipeout holder calls `withdraw`; `resolve_withdrawal` returns `gross = stranded > 0`, `require_reserves` passes against the fresh deposit, and `debit_cash` pays the old claim out of the victim's money — leaving the victim's claim unbacked, exactly as asserted at `interest.rs:419-425`.

Uncertainty note: I verified the clamp arithmetic and the unit-test demonstration directly, but did not get to read `contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs` in full to confirm whether the production `seize_settle` path performs an additional share-reset or cash guard before/after calling `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`; the test's own comment ("invariant only masked by require_reserves") indicates no such guard exists in the cache layer, and a wipeout-level write-down is the only state where this matters.
