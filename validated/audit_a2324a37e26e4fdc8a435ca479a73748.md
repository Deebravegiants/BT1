### Title
Bad-debt write-down floor preserves wiped-out supplier claims and lets them drain future deposits - ([File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs])

### Summary
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` caps a bad-debt write-down at total supplied value, but then clamps the resulting supply index to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead of clearing the claims that were economically written down to zero. After a full wipeout, existing supply shares retain a positive claim at the floored index and can withdraw cash deposited later by new suppliers.

### Finding Description
The controller’s bad-debt cleanup path submits every remaining supply and debt position to `pool_seize_positions` through `execute_bad_debt_cleanup`. [1](#0-0) 

On the pool’s borrow side, `seize::apply` converts the debt shares into a RAY-denominated `bad_debt` amount, calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, and burns the debtor’s shares. [2](#0-1) 

`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes `total_supplied_value = supplied * supply_index`, caps `bad_debt` at that value, and derives a write-down factor from the remainder. [3](#0-2) 

When `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, `remaining` is zero and the mathematically correct post-write-down index is zero. The function nevertheless stores `max(new_supply_index, SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)`, resurrecting every old scaled supply share to a positive claim. [4](#0-3) 

The existing unit proof demonstrates the resulting loss directly: after a bad-debt amount larger than the old supply value, the floor leaves `old_scaled` with a positive claim; a new supplier deposits cash; withdrawal of `old_scaled` pays out that cash and leaves less than the new supplier’s claim. [5](#0-4) 

This is analogous to the reported bug because stale lower/upper accounting state is intersected with a new bounded result: instead of resetting the economically exhausted claim domain to zero, the code clamps the new index against a stale floor and preserves a false positive balance.

### Impact Explanation
An unprivileged account can create an underwater position and, once the cleanup predicate is met, invoke the controller’s bad-debt cleanup path. If the written-down debt meets or exceeds the total value of supply shares in that market, all existing suppliers should lose their claims, but the floor preserves them as positive claims. Those stale claims can subsequently withdraw newly deposited cash, causing theft of supplier funds and immediate market insolvency.

The production withdrawal path resolves an amount against the stale `pos_scaled` using the compromised `supply_index`, while reserve checks only verify currently recorded cash rather than proving that pre-wipeout shares still have backing. [6](#0-5) 

The included pool test proves that the stale claim pays out the full fresh deposit and leaves the pool unable to cover the fresh supplier. [7](#0-6) 

### Likelihood Explanation
The bug requires bad debt at least equal to total supplied value in one market. This can arise after severe collateral depreciation, debt growth, or an underwater account reaching the protocol’s dust-level cleanup condition. Cleanup is designed as a permissionless account operation, so no admin or leaked key is needed once the qualifying account exists.

The arithmetic condition is deterministic: `bad_debt` is capped at `total_supplied_value`, making `remaining == 0`, after which the floor unconditionally restores a nonzero supply index. [8](#0-7) 

### Recommendation
Do not clamp a complete bad-debt write-down to a positive supply index while retaining the old share supply. On `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, explicitly reset or retire the pre-wipeout supply share base so those shares cannot later unscale to assets. A safer state transition is to record an epoch/generation reset or mark the market’s existing supply claims as written off before accepting new deposits. At minimum, make `resolve_withdrawal` or the share-accounting layer distinguish shares created before a complete write-down from shares minted afterward.

### Proof of Concept
The repository already contains a direct executable proof in `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard`:

1. Seed `supplied = 1_000 * RAY`, `supply_index = RAY`, and zero cash.
2. Call `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache, 5_000 * RAY)`.
3. Observe `supply_index == SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` while the old `1_000 * RAY` shares still unscale to a positive amount.
4. Deposit fresh cash equal to that stranded claim and mint equivalent fresh shares.
5. Resolve withdrawal of the old shares; the pool pays the fresh deposit to the stale claim and can no longer cover the fresh supplier’s claim.

The decisive assertions are implemented in `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs`. [9](#0-8)

```rust
pub(crate) fn apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache: &mut Cache, bad_debt: Ray) {
    let total_supplied_value = cache.supplied().mul(cache.env(), cache.supply_index());

    if total_supplied_value == Ray::ZERO {
        return;
    }

    let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
    let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);

    let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
    let new_supply_index = cache
        .supply_index()
        .mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);

    cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
```
