### Title
`liquidate` applies debt repayment without any minimum-collateral guard — a leg that rounds to zero at execution is dropped while the repayment is still pulled — ([File: contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/math.rs](contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/math.rs))

### Summary
The controller's `liquidate` entrypoint (`liquidator, account_id, debt_payments, seize_mode`) takes no minimum-seizure or slippage parameter. The liquidation plan is fully recomputed at execution time, and collateral legs that round to zero shares or zero token units are silently dropped *without* releasing the corresponding repayment. An unprivileged liquidator whose quote is invalidated between simulation/signing and execution — by price drift, interest/index accrual, or a competing liquidation — can end up paying debt while receiving less collateral than the plan implied, including legs that seize nothing at all, producing a net loss.

### Finding Description
`process_liquidation` builds the plan on-chain via `build_liquidation_plan` and applies repayments before seizures. In `calculate_seized_collateral` (math.rs:383-502), each supply leg computes a pro-rata seizure and then drops the leg via `continue` at three points:

- `seizure_ray <= Ray::ZERO` (line 419-421)
- `seized_scaled <= Ray::ZERO` after `div_floor` by the supply index (line 456-458)
- `capped_amount <= 0` after `to_asset_floor` (line 465-467)

Only the sub-3-decimal whole-unit branch (lines 401-416) accumulates `unseized_usd` that is later released via `release_unbacked_repayment` (plan.rs:73-79). The `continue` drops at lines 456 and 465 do **not** add to `unseized_usd`, so the repayment backing those legs is kept. `scale_seizures_to_received` then executes the (reduced) seizure set against the full measured repayment.

The repayment side is protected — payments are capped at ceiled debt and trimmed to the collateral-backed quote — but the collateral side has no floor. The liquidator specifies only `debt_payments`; there is no `min_collateral` argument anywhere in `interfaces/controller` or the `XoxnoLending::liquidate` signature. On Soroban, envelope timebounds provide only coarse expiration, not output protection: any state change after signing (oracle price move, supply-index accrual, another liquidator taking part of the collateral) can cause seizure legs to floor to zero shares/tokens while the full trimmed repayment is still pulled to the pool.

### Impact Explanation
A liquidator can pay real debt tokens and receive materially less collateral value than the repayment — in the extreme, legs seize nothing and the repayment is still applied. That is a direct, unrecoverable loss of the liquidator's own funds inflicted by state drift they could not guard against on-chain. The planner's own documentation acknowledges the contract accepts a liquidation that repays debt and seizes nothing, shifting the burden entirely onto off-chain simulation timing.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires collateral drift between quote and inclusion: a competing liquidation, an index accrual boundary (millisecond-chunked accrual and ceil/floor unscaling make small shifts routine), or a price tick that pushes a small or low-decimals collateral leg below one token unit / one share of seizure. Not exploitable by an attacker for profit in general, but reachable by any unprivileged liquidator through the normal `liquidate` path, and partial liquidations of multi-position accounts make dropped legs common. Rated Medium: real loss path, but conditional on execution-time drift and typically bounded to the affected legs.

### Recommendation
Add a `min_seized` (per-asset minimum amounts, or a minimum total seized USD) parameter to `liquidate`, checked after `scale_seizures_to_received`, reverting with a dedicated error if the delivered seizure is below the liquidator's floor. Alternatively, add each dropped leg's USD value (`seizure_for_asset_usd`) to `unseized_usd` before the `continue` at math.rs:419, 456 and 465 so repayment that no collateral backs is released rather than pulled.

### Proof of Concept
1. Victim account has HF < 1 with debt D in asset `DEBT` and several supply positions including a small `COL` leg whose pro-rata seizure at quote time is barely above one token unit (or barely above one RAY share after `div_floor` by the supply index).
2. Liquidator simulates `get_liquidation_estimate`, submits `liquidate` authorizing the planned `DEBT` payment.
3. Before inclusion, the supply index accrues (or `COL` price ticks down, or a competing liquidation runs first), so `capped_ray.div_floor(supply_index)` or `capped_ray.to_asset_floor(decimals)` for that leg evaluates to 0.
4. Execution: the leg is dropped via `continue` at math.rs:456/465; `unseized_usd` is unchanged, so no repayment is released. `apply_liquidation_repayments` pulls the full planned `DEBT` amount from the liquidator; `apply_liquidation_seizures` delivers only the surviving legs.
5. Result: liquidator paid the full quoted debt repayment but received strictly less collateral than the quote — a realized loss with no on-chain way to have prevented it.
