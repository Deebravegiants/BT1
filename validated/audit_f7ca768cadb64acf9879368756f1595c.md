### Title
RAY-value overflow in `accrue_step` permanently freezes an entire market once accrued debt value exceeds `i128::MAX` - (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary
The analog of CVE-2017-7597's "value outside the range of representable values" crash is XOXNO Lending's fixed-point valuation: every interest-accrual step converts scaled share totals to RAY asset values with `scaled_to_original`, which panics with `MathOverflow` when `scaled * index / RAY` does not fit `i128`. Because `global_sync` runs accrual before every pool mutation and there is no non-accruing exit path, once `borrowed * borrow_index` (or `supplied * supply_index`) crosses the `i128` boundary the market is permanently bricked — `update_indexes`, `supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and all flash/strategy paths through that market all revert forever.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` (`common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-61`) unconditionally evaluates

```rust
let borrowed_original = scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index);
let supplied_original = scaled_to_original(env, supplied, supply_index);
```

`scaled_to_original` is `scaled.mul(env, index)` → `fp_core::mul_div_half_up` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`, `common/src/math/fp.rs:50-52`). The `I256` widening in `fp_core` only makes the *intermediate product* exact; when the final quotient exceeds `i128::MAX` the function panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` rather than saturating or clamping — exactly the "unrepresentable value → crash" shape of the reference bug.

Crucially, this overflow is reachable *before* either index ceiling engages, because the caps bound the **indexes** (`MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, `MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` = 10^36, enforced inside `update_borrow_index`/`update_supply_index` at `common/src/rates/index.rs:13-45`) but nothing bounds `borrowed * borrow_index`, which is a *value*, not an index. `docs/reference/formulas.md:434-437` admits this: "Accrued position values and market totals must independently fit the RAY domain; valid caps and bounded indexes do not guarantee that future accrual fits." The dedicated regression test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-360`) proves it on-chain: after the cliff, `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay` all revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and the index cap never engages.

Unlike the LibTIFF crash (mere DoS), here the panic is **latched into shared state**: the overflow depends on `borrowed`, `borrow_index`, `supplied`, `supply_index`, all of which are persistent market state, and `accrue_step` is executed first by `global_sync` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33`) on every verb. No path skips it, so the panic is not transient — it permanently gates every subsequent call.

### Impact Explanation
**Permanent freezing of funds** (an accepted impact class). Once `borrowed * borrow_index > i128::MAX * RAY`-equivalents, every operation on that physical pool market — withdrawals of all suppliers, repayments, liquidations, bad-debt cleanup, revenue claims — reverts in accrual before reaching any settlement logic. Supplier principal and unclaimed yield are unrecoverable; under-collateralized positions cannot be liquidated, so the bad debt is also unclosable, pushing the market toward effective insolvency. Because hub/spoke books share one physical pool balance, freezing a large market freezes real pooled tokens, not just bookkeeping.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by unprivileged addresses only:

1. **Setup**: any number of ordinary users supply and borrow up to the listed cap (`cap <= max_cap_for_decimals(d)`, `common/src/validation.rs:48-56` — up to ~1.7e11 whole tokens for an 18-decimal asset). At 98% utilization on a steep curve segment, `borrowed * borrow_index` grows with time.
2. **Trigger**: anyone calls the permissionless `update_indexes` (or simply submits any supply/borrow/withdraw tx) after enough time has elapsed. The test shows the cliff is hit in a finite number of yearly steps at the configured XLM-style curve; at the 200% APR cap (`MAX_BORROW_RATE_RAY`) the index growth is faster.

The likelihood is gated by economics, not authorization: it requires a genuinely large market (cap-scale positions, i.e. tens of billions of base units) and sustained high utilization, so it is a whale-scale setup rather than a cheap grief. However, once the state exists the freeze needs no further attacker action — the next honest user's transaction triggers it — and there is no governance recovery path that avoids the panicking accrual. Documenting a numeric limit does not mitigate that valid cap configurations admit the state. Severity: Medium (high impact, high economic preconditions).

### Recommendation
- Bound the invariant the protocol actually needs: enforce a **value ceiling** on `borrowed * borrow_index` and `supplied * supply_index` (e.g., refuse new borrows/supplies or clamp accrual) rather than only bounding the indexes. The current caps on index (`MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`) and on cap-size (`max_cap_for_decimals`) leave the product unconstrained.
- Make `scaled_to_original` for *market totals* saturating or `I256`-valued in `accrue_step` (`common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-61`), so utilization/rate computation can proceed and at least exits (withdraw, repay, liquidate, `clean_bad_debt`) keep working. Per-position conversions can keep panicking.
- Alternatively, emit/act on the approach: keep `simulate_update_indexes`-style checks and let `update_indexes` stop accrual gracefully at the value ceiling (freeze *interest*, not *exits*), since at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` accrual already produces no interest — extend that fail-open behavior to the value-overflow case.

### Proof of Concept
The repository already contains an executable PoC: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-360`. It:

1. Lists an 18-decimal market with the XLM curve, disables utilization ceilings, lifts caps.
2. `supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1e9 * 10^18)` and `borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", 98% of principal)` — ordinary unprivileged `supply`/`borrow` calls.
3. Advances time one year per iteration calling the permissionless `update_indexes` until it fails — the failure is `errors::MATH_OVERFLOW` thrown inside `accrue_step` → `scaled_to_original`, while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (cap never engages).
4. Asserts the market is then frozen: `withdraw` and `repay` both revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW` — permanently, since every verb accrues first and no state transition can reduce `borrowed * borrow_index` below the cliff without first passing through accrual.

The analogous reachability argument for the audit scope: attacker-controlled `supply`/`borrow` amounts create the pre-cliff state; the permissionless `update_indexes` (or any later user's transaction) triggers the unrecoverable panic.
