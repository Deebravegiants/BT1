### Title
Unprivileged dust top-up of an insolvent account permanently blocks permissionless `clean_bad_debt` socialization - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs)

### Summary
`supply` is callable by any authenticated address on a foreign account as long as every hub asset already exists in the account's supply positions (`require_third_party_existing_supply`, `contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs:78-97`). `clean_bad_debt` is permissionless but only admits an insolvent account whose half-up unweighted collateral is at or below the fixed $5 `BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD` (`is_socializable_bad_debt`, `contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/curve.rs:25-27`; gate in `socialize_bad_debt` `BadDebtGate::DustCapped`, `contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs:229-235`). A griefing caller can therefore keep any qualifying account permanently above the dust cap by repeatedly supplying a few cents of an asset the account already supplies, locking the account out of socialization — the analog of "any authenticated user may lock resources belonging to other users."

### Finding Description
The permissionless cleanup path requires `total_debt > total_collateral && total_collateral <= 5 USD` (WAD). Because `total_collateral` is computed from the account's supply shares (`calculate_account_risk_totals`), any increase in the victim's supply shares raises `total_collateral` and closes the gate. A third-party `supply(caller, account_id, spoke_id, assets)` on an existing supply leg does exactly that and is unrestricted: the only check is that each supplied `hub_asset` is already a key in `account.supply_positions` (`supply.rs:86-96`), and a deeply insolvent account has no gate that refuses the deposit. The attacker watches for `clean_bad_debt` calls (or simply tops up whenever collateral drifts below $5), supplying e.g. $0.01–$5 of an already-held asset to push `total_collateral` to `BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD + ε`, making every `clean_bad_debt` call revert with `CannotCleanBadDebt`. The same trick defeats the automatic post-liquidation cleanup in `check_bad_debt_after_liquidation` (`apply.rs:313-315`), which uses the identical predicate.

### Impact Explanation
Bad debt that should be socialized permissionlessly instead sits on the book and keeps accruing borrow interest against suppliers of the affected markets. The only remaining removal path is the owner-gated, timelocked `force_socialize_bad_debt` (`BadDebtGate::InsolventOnly`), so each blocked account requires a governance proposal and delay while its debt grows — converting an instant keeper operation into a recurring governance burden and letting the eventual supply-index write-down be strictly larger. The attacker can repeat indefinitely for a cost of a few dollars per cycle (the donated dust is reclassified to protocol revenue at cleanup anyway, so the attacker does not recover it, but the cost per block is bounded by the $5 threshold). This is a sustained denial of the protocol's bad-debt resolution mechanism, worsening protocol insolvency — qualifying under the accepted impact classes (protocol insolvency / temporary freezing of supplier funds backed by the stuck market).

### Likelihood Explanation
- Entrypoint is in-scope and permissionless: `supply(caller, account_id, spoke_id, assets)`; the third-party restriction only limits *which assets*, not *which accounts*.
- Preconditions are met naturally: any account that reaches insolvency with ≤$5 residual collateral necessarily holds at least one supply leg to top up (otherwise `total_collateral` would be 0 and it would also be gated — actually with `total_collateral = 0`, `is_socializable_bad_debt` already passes; the attack needs an existing leg, which holds whenever collateral is between 0 and $5, or after the attacker's first top-up creates... — no, a first top-up on an account with zero supply legs is rejected by `NotAuthorized`; but accounts reaching the dust gate via partial liquidation almost always retain residual supply shares, so a leg exists).
- Cost per blocking cycle is at most ~$5 of a token the attacker may already hold, versus unbounded accrual delay.
- No invariant or flag blocks it: supply entry gates (`validate_position_entry_gates`) check listing `paused`/`frozen` and caps, none of which distinguish insolvent targets; `supply` is only stopped by the global pause.

### Recommendation
Reject third-party top-ups that would move an insolvent account out of the dust gate — e.g., in `process_supply`, when the account has open debt and `total_debt > total_collateral`, refuse top-ups that push `total_collateral` above `BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD` for non-owner callers; or make the cleanup gate re-check eligibility against collateral measured at the time the account *became* insolvent (snapshotted), rather than its live value. Alternatively, exempt dust-sized collateral legs from `total_collateral` in `is_socializable_bad_debt` so marginal top-ups cannot straddle the threshold.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice supplies 100 USDC and borrows to the limit; the collateral price crashes so that `D > C` and `C ≈ $4` (`is_socializable_bad_debt` true — mirroring `test_deep_crash_socializes_bad_debt` in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/liquidation_extreme.rs:267-282`).
2. Attacker calls `supply(attacker, alice_id, spoke_id, [(usdc_key, 2_000_000)])` — a $2 top-up of the existing USDC leg. `require_third_party_existing_supply` passes because USDC is already a supply key.
3. `total_collateral` is now ~$6 > `BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD` (5 WAD, `contracts/controller/tests/positions/liquidation_curve.rs:38`).
4. Any `clean_bad_debt(keeper, alice_id)` reverts `CannotCleanBadDebt` at `mod.rs:235`; post-liquidation auto-cleanup silently skips at `apply.rs:313`.
5. Whenever accrual or price moves push `C` below $5 again, the attacker repeats with a minimal top-up, indefinitely forcing reliance on the timelocked `force_socialize_bad_debt` governance path.
