### Title
Self-liquidation lets an under-collateralized borrower reclaim their own collateral at a bonus-discounted debt payment, pushing the unpaid remainder to bad-debt socialization - (File: mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar)

### Summary
The `liquidate` function in `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar` never checks that the caller (`contract-caller`, bound to `liquidator`) is different from the `borrower` being liquidated. Combined with the liquidation-bonus math, a borrower whose own position has crossed the liquidation threshold can call `liquidate` on themselves, receive their own collateral back at the liquidation-bonus-discounted price, and leave any debt shortfall to be written off through bad-debt socialization instead of paying it back in full.

### Finding Description
`liquidate` computes `liquidator` directly from `contract-caller` with no restriction against it being equal to `borrower`: [1](#0-0) 

Nothing in the assertion block excludes self-liquidation - only pause, same-block, amount and slippage checks are enforced: [2](#0-1) 

When a position's LTV has crossed `ltv-liq-partial`/`ltv-liq-full`, the function grants a bonus (`liq-penalty`) on the collateral seized relative to debt repaid: [3](#0-2) 

When collateral is insufficient to cover `debt-actual-usd * (1 + liq-penalty)` (i.e., the position is at or beyond ~100% LTV), `process-collateral-asset` caps the collateral seized at the user's full balance, and `calc-final-liquidation-amounts` recalculates the debt actually repaid *downward*, using the bonus-inclusive formula, so the repayer pays strictly less than the collateral's USD value: [4](#0-3) 

If the resulting `coll-final`/`scaled-to-remove` leaves the position with no collateral left, the remaining unpaid debt is socialized (written off across the lending pool) rather than collected from the borrower: [5](#0-4) 

The value identity broken is:
`collateral_returned_to_self (USD, at full balance) > debt_actually_repaid (USD, discounted by liq-penalty)`, with the difference (plus any additional undercollateralized shortfall) absorbed by the protocol/lenders via bad-debt socialization instead of by the defaulting borrower.

Because `liquidate` allows `borrower == liquidator` and `collateral-receiver` defaults to `liquidator` (i.e., back to the same account), the self-liquidating borrower:
1. Repays only `debt-final` (< full outstanding debt, discounted by the bonus factor when collateral is capped).
2. Reclaims their entire remaining collateral balance back to their own wallet.
3. Leaves any unrepaid debt to be socialized among the pool's lenders/vault.

This is economically equivalent to the referenced StakeWise analog: the liquidation premium meant to incentivize third parties to fix bad debt instead incentivizes the defaulting party to self-liquidate and extract value that should have stayed as collateral backing the debt (or should have required full repayment via a normal `repay`), pushing losses onto other pool participants.

### Impact Explanation
This results in protocol insolvency / socialized losses: the self-liquidating borrower extracts more collateral value than the debt they repay, and the shortfall becomes bad debt absorbed by the vault (funded by other depositors' principal), matching the "theft of principal / protocol insolvency" impact bar. It also constitutes a permanent-freezing/loss vector for other depositors, since the socialized bad debt directly reduces the vault's backing for outstanding zTokens/shares.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate-to-high for any borrower whose position naturally crosses the liquidation threshold (e.g., due to price movement or accrued interest) — such users have direct economic incentive to call `liquidate` against their own `borrower` principal (any account can invoke it, no allowlist or `is-eq borrower liquidator` exclusion exists) rather than performing a normal `repay`, since it strictly reduces their required repayment while recovering their full collateral balance up front, before the position would otherwise be fully liquidated by a third party at the same or worse terms.

### Recommendation
Add an explicit check in `liquidate` (mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar) rejecting `(is-eq borrower liquidator)` (and by extension, any `collateral-receiver` resolving back to the borrower when caller equals borrower), forcing under-collateralized borrowers to use `repay` (which requires full-value repayment with no bonus discount) rather than the discounted `liquidate` path.

### Proof of Concept
1. Borrower deposits collateral and borrows debt such that, after a price move or interest accrual, `current-ltv >= ltv-liq-partial` (and, for the maximal exploit, `current-ltv >= ~10000` i.e. ~100%+ LTV so that `process-collateral-asset`'s cap kicks in).
2. Borrower (as `contract-caller`) calls `liquidate(borrower, collateral-ft, debt-ft, debt-amount, min-collateral-expected, none, none)` on themselves — no check in [2](#0-1)  prevents this.
3. `process-collateral-asset`/`calc-final-liquidation-amounts` compute `coll-actual = user-coll-balance` (capped) and `debt-final-usd = coll-actual-usd * BPS/(BPS+liq-penalty)` — strictly less than `coll-actual-usd` — per [6](#0-5) .
4. `collateral-receiver` defaults to `liquidator` (the borrower themselves), so all remaining collateral is transferred back to the borrower's own wallet: [7](#0-6) .
5. Any residual unpaid debt is subject to socialization when `no-collateral-left` is true, per the logic starting at [8](#0-7) , imposing the loss on the lending pool rather than the borrower.

;; Calculate collateral to seize (includes liquidator bonus)
;; collateral-repay = debt-repay * (BPS + liq-penalty) / BPS
(define-private (calc-liq-collateral-repay (debt-repay uint) (liq-penalty uint))

;; Calculate actual debt repayment when collateral is capped
