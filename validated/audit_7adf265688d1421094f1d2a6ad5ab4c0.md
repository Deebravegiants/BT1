### Title
Liquidation functions lack slippage control - (Project: twyne [File: twyne/SCOPE.md])

### Summary
The Twyne project explicitly states that its liquidation functions, `liquidate()` or `handleExternalLiquidation()`, do not include slippage checks. This aligns with the vulnerability described in the external report, where the absence of slippage control can expose liquidators to front-running, potentially leading them to pay more than anticipated.

### Finding Description
The `twyne/SCOPE.md` document, under the "Public Disclosure of Known Issues" section, lists "No slippage check on liquidate() or handleExternalLiquidation(). Liquidators need to check for slippage on their side."  This indicates that the Twyne protocol's liquidation mechanisms do not inherently protect against price fluctuations or front-running during the liquidation process. The responsibility for managing slippage is placed on the liquidator.

### Impact Explanation
Without slippage control, a liquidator initiating a `liquidate()` or `handleExternalLiquidation()` transaction could be front-run. This front-running could manipulate the price of the assets involved, causing the liquidator to pay a higher amount of collateral (or receive less of the liquidated asset) than intended or expected at the time of transaction submission. This leads to financial loss for the liquidator.

### Likelihood Explanation
The likelihood of this issue being exploited is moderate. In a competitive liquidation environment, malicious actors can observe pending liquidation transactions and execute their own transactions to manipulate prices before the liquidator's transaction is confirmed. This is a common front-running vector in DeFi protocols lacking slippage protection.

### Recommendation
It is recommended to implement slippage control mechanisms within the `liquidate()` and `handleExternalLiquidation()` functions. This would involve adding a parameter, such as `maxAmountToPay` or `minAmountToReceive`, allowing liquidators to specify their acceptable price tolerance. If the actual execution price falls outside this tolerance, the transaction should revert, protecting the liquidator from unfavorable price movements.

### Proof of Concept
The proof of concept is derived from the explicit statement in the project's scope document.
1. A liquidator calls `liquidate()` or `handleExternalLiquidation()` without specifying a maximum amount they are willing to pay or a minimum amount they expect to receive. 
2. A malicious actor observes this transaction in the mempool.
3. The malicious actor executes a transaction that manipulates the price of the relevant assets, making the liquidation less favorable for the original liquidator.
4. The liquidator's transaction executes at the manipulated, less favorable price, resulting in them paying more than they would have if slippage control was in place.
