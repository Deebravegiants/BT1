### Title
Frontrunning `remove_delegate` lets a delegate drain the account before revocation lands - (File: contracts/controller/src/lib.rs)

### Summary
An active position-manager delegate of a lending account can watch for the owner's `remove_delegate` transaction and front-run it with `borrow` and/or `withdraw`, extracting the account's equity to an arbitrary recipient before its authority is actually cut. The delegate keeps full owner-equivalent spending power on `borrow`/`withdraw` right up to the ledger in which the revocation executes, and the revocation transaction itself carries no protection against a racing drain.

### Finding Description
This mirrors the `revokeAll()` frontrun class: a revocation action is only effective once it executes, and the target's extractive rights remain fully live until then.

In XOXNO Lending, the "recipient" is an active delegate (a governance-approved position manager granted on an account via `add_delegate`) and the "claim()" is `controller::borrow` / `controller::withdraw`:

- `remove_delegate` is owner-gated (`require_account_owner`) and takes effect only when the owner's transaction executes — the docs state "Owner revocation is immediate," i.e. there is no grace, queue, or epoch boundary on either side.
- `borrow` pays "to `to` or the caller" and `withdraw` likewise sends collateral to `to`, after `require_owner_or_delegate` passes for an active grant.
- The threat model explicitly confirms the power being raced: "Delegates can borrow/withdraw to their chosen recipient within account gates. Those gates do not constrain them to acting in the owner's economic interest." (docs/explanation/threat-model.md, Account authority)
- The test `a_removed_delegate_can_still_repay_but_cannot_borrow_or_withdraw` (tests/test-harness/tests/composition/delegate_revocation_between_legs.rs:12-48) proves the boundary is purely sequential: the same delegate can `borrow` while the grant is live and is only blocked after `remove_delegate` executes.

So a delegate that sees a pending `remove_delegate` in the transaction queue submits `borrow(account_id, max_borrows, to: delegate)` and `withdraw(account_id, all_collateral_down_to_solvency_floor, to: delegate)` with a higher fee. If its transaction orders first, it walks away with tokens worth the account's entire borrowable headroom plus withdrawable collateral, and the revocation then lands on an already-drained account.

The residual debt stays on the owner's account — but the delegate extracted transferable value that the revocation was intended to prevent it from taking, identical to the vesting case where the recipient "claims tokens they should not be able to receive."

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds. A delegate can convert the owner's collateral into its own balance up to the solvency floor (`INV-RISK-01` only requires post-op solvency, not owner benefit): borrow to the max LTV paying `to: delegate`, and withdraw all collateral still permitted given the new debt. The owner is left holding a maxed-out debt position whose proceeds went to the (now-revoked) delegate. The extracted amount is bounded only by the account's collateral value and market liquidity, and it can be repeated across every asset the delegate was granted on.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a pre-existing delegate grant, so it is conditional rather than open to arbitrary strangers — matching the original report's dependency on being a vesting recipient. Delegates are reachable by an unprivileged address in the rule's sense: any governance-approved position manager can be granted by any owner, and position managers are exactly the kind of automated/custodial actor that watches mempool state. On Soroban, transaction ordering can be influenced through fee bidding, so a motivated delegate can reliably race a revocation once it observes it. No oracle movement, price manipulation, or privileged role is needed — only the delegate's own signature on `borrow`/`withdraw`.

### Recommendation
Make revocation take effect at submission time, not execution order. Options:

- Two-step revocation: `remove_delegate` first marks the grant `pending_removal` (effective immediately for `require_owner_or_delegate` checks, but still allowing `repay`-style inflows), matching the withdrawal-queue recommendation in the source report.
- Epoch-stamp grants: store a per-account `delegate_epoch` bumped by `remove_delegate`; grants record the epoch at grant time and are only valid if `grant.epoch == current_epoch`, so any transaction built against the old epoch ordering is rejected deterministically.
- Alternatively, allow the owner to revoke and freeze in one call (a `remove_delegate_and_pause_account` path that blocks new `borrow`/`withdraw` on the account in the same transaction), so a frontrunner at worst preserves the status quo rather than extracting.

At minimum, document the race: owners should remove delegates only on accounts with no borrowable headroom, or accept that the final valid delegate action may be a drain.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual sequence (all entrypoints in `contracts/controller/src/lib.rs`):

1. Alice owns `account_id` (NFT holder). Governance has registered `manager` as an active position manager (`set_position_manager`). Alice calls `add_delegate(caller=alice, account_id, delegate=manager)` — grant is live.
2. Alice submits `remove_delegate(caller=alice, account_id, delegate=manager)`. Until it executes, `is_owner_or_delegate(env, account_id, manager, owner)` still returns true — the grant is only deactivated by the executing transaction (see the pre/post assertions in `transfer_revokes_prior_owner_and_delegates` and `a_removed_delegate_can_still_repay_but_cannot_borrow_or_withdraw`).
3. `manager` observes the pending revocation and submits, with a higher resource fee:
   - `borrow(caller=manager, account_id, borrows=[(hub_asset, max_amount)], to=Some(manager))` — proceeds paid to the delegate.
   - `withdraw(caller=manager, account_id, withdrawals=[(hub_asset, 0 /* full */)], to=Some(manager))` — collateral paid to the delegate, bounded only by post-withdraw solvency.
4. Ordering: delegate transactions execute first and succeed (`require_owner_or_delegate` passes on the still-active grant). `remove_delegate` executes second and also succeeds — but the account is already drained of transferable value and loaded with debt whose proceeds went to the delegate.

Note: docs describe the non-atomicity risk only from the delegate-power side ("Those gates do not constrain them to acting in the owner's economic interest"); no mechanism bounds what a still-active delegate can take in the window between revocation submission and execution. If the intended design is that delegation is fully trusted until revoked, the residual race — where the revocation's *purpose* is defeated by the racing drain — remains unmitigated.
