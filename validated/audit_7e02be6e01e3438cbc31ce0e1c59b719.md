### Title
Stale secondary oracle legs are blended into collateral prices, allowing undercollateralized borrows - ([File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs])

### Summary

The dual-source oracle accepts a nearly day-old secondary/anchor reading when its configured staleness budget has not expired, then averages that stale price with a fresh primary price. Because the cross-leg timestamp-spread check applies only when both sources have `FeedNature::Market`, a stale non-market anchor can bypass the one-hour spread bound even while both legs remain within the configured price-tolerance band. An unprivileged borrower can wait for the real market price to move while the anchor remains stuck inside its configured window, then call `Controller::borrow` against collateral valued at the inflated midpoint and extract more debt than the fresh price supports.

### Finding Description

`read_feed` marks a provider observation stale only when it exceeds that feed's `max_stale_seconds`; `read_source` separately compares it against the oracle-level `max_price_stale_seconds`. A RedStone-style anchor can therefore remain valid after a long configured interval such as 86,400 seconds.

After both legs produce readings, `blend` marks the result stale if either leg is stale or if two `Market` legs differ by more than `MAX_LEG_AGE_SPREAD_SECONDS`. The age-spread protection is disabled when the anchor is declared as a non-market/fundamental feed. Provided the two prices pass `within_tolerance_band`, the aggregator returns their arithmetic midpoint.

This creates a predictable valuation skew. For example:

- stale anchor: `$1.00`, timestamp approximately 23 hours old;
- fresh primary: `$0.91`;
- configured tolerance: 10%;
- ratio: `$1.00 / $0.91 ≈ 109.89%`, so the tolerance check passes;
- accepted price: `($1.00 + $0.91) / 2 = $0.955`, approximately 4.95% above the fresh market price.

The repository's focused test pins this behavior: a stale RedStone anchor inflates the reported collateral value by more than 4%, makes a 70,000-USDC borrow succeed, and the fresh-anchor control rejects the identical borrow with `InsufficientCollateral`.

### Impact Explanation

This is a direct protocol-insolvency path. An ordinary account supplies the volatile collateral, waits until the stale-but-accepted anchor and fresh primary diverge while remaining inside the configured tolerance, and borrows at the midpoint-inflated valuation. If the price remains near the fresh primary value, the withdrawn debt can exceed the account's economically safe capacity. The account can then abandon the collateral, leaving the pool with debt that ordinary liquidation cannot recover.

The impact scales with deposited collateral and pool liquidity. Repeated or large positions can turn the oracle skew into realized supplier bad debt, matching the critical externally reported pattern of a trusted verification input accepting forged or economically false state while downstream contracts behave correctly.

### Likelihood Explanation

No private key, governance authority, oracle compromise, or contract exploit is required. The attacker only needs:

1. a market configured with a primary and a slow secondary source;
2. a secondary timestamp still within its permitted staleness window;
3. sufficient price movement for the midpoint to create excess borrowing capacity while remaining inside tolerance;
4. permissionless access to `Controller::borrow`.

Oracle update gaps and rapid market moves are routine operating conditions. The 24-hour maximum permitted oracle staleness was introduced to support slow feeds, and the midpoint calculation automatically gives half weight to an old anchor rather than rejecting it for timestamp inconsistency. A borrower can continuously monitor provider timestamps and act only when the divergence becomes profitable, making this a reliable opportunistic exploit rather than a narrow race.

### Recommendation

Treat timestamp inconsistency as a validity failure independent of feed nature:

- Apply `MAX_LEG_AGE_SPREAD_SECONDS` to every two-source oracle, not only when both legs are `FeedNature::Market`.
- If a source deliberately has a long heartbeat, require the composite result to use contemporaneous observations or configure an explicit pair-level maximum age spread compatible with its volatility.
- Do not blend a source near the end of its staleness window with a materially fresher leg; prefer a bounded freshness-weighted policy only after bounding worst-case valuation error.
- Reduce `max_stale_seconds` for volatile collateral or require tighter sanity/tolerance bands when a long heartbeat is unavoidable.
- Add regression coverage based on the stale-anchor test and extend it to withdrawals, flash-position collateral checks, and liquidation paths.

### Proof of Concept

The repository already contains a reproducible focused test in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_borrow_withdraw_liquidate_stale_anchor_blend.rs`.

Relevant setup:

- the frozen anchor reports `$1.00`;
- the true fresh price is `$0.91`;
- tolerance is `1000` BPS;
- the anchor accepts a maximum age of `86_400` seconds;
- the test positions the anchor `82_800` seconds old;
- Alice supplies `100_000` XLM and attempts to borrow `70_000` USDC.

The exploit branch writes the stale RedStone timestamp:

```rust
let stale_ms = now.saturating_sub(ANCHOR_LAG_SECONDS) * 1000;
redstone_client.set_price_data(&feed_id, &ANCHOR_FROZEN_PRICE, &stale_ms, &stale_ms);
```

The fresh control branch writes the current timestamp and `$0.91` price. The assertions then prove the same borrow succeeds only under the stale-anchor condition and that the fresh-anchor control fails with `InsufficientCollateral`:

```rust
assert!(inflation > 1.04);
assert!(exploit.borrow.is_ok());
assert_contract_error(control.borrow, errors::INSUFFICIENT_COLLATERAL);
```

The vulnerable behavior is implemented in `contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs`: `read_feed` accepts the observation while it is inside the configured per-feed staleness budget, while `blend` suppresses the cross-leg timestamp-spread check unless both feeds are market feeds and returns the midpoint of the two accepted prices.
