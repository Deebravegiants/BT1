**Reasoning:** `CacheValueSizes::charge_read_ref_cached` and the `MemoryTrackedGasMeterImpl` in [1](#0-0)  are part of the Move VM's execution-gas/memory-accounting subsystem, invoked from `charge_read_ref` in [2](#0-1)  and defined abstractly in [3](#0-2) . Even if the abstract stack/heap size estimates used for gas charging were imprecise for a specific value shape (e.g., `Object<FungibleStore>`), this only affects how much *gas fee* is charged for VM instruction execution — it does not touch any custody state: it does not read, write, or validate ownership refs, `FungibleStore` balances, `ExtendRef`/`TransferRef`/`BurnRef` capabilities, or any authorization check for transfer/mint/burn/freeze operations in the Fungible Asset or Object frameworks.

A gas-undercounting bug (if it existed) could at most let an attacker execute more instructions per unit of fee paid — a resource-accounting/DoS concern — but it cannot itself move, mint, burn, freeze, or reassign ownership of any asset, since those state transitions are still gated by the normal capability/ref checks in the `fungible_asset.move` and `object.move` modules, which this gas-metering code does not touch or bypass. This fails the custody impact gate: it is a possible cosmetic/economic gas-pricing mismatch at best, not a change to who can own, move, mint, burn, freeze, upgrade, or recover value, and it does not meet the decision standard requiring a real custody boundary crossing that changes asset control.
