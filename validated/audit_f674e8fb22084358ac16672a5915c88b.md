Found it. The developers explicitly recognized and bounded this exact bug class at **launch time** (`_deployAndSeed` bounds `virtualLtReserve` to `type(uint112).max / 4`), but the bound is a one-time snapshot of `exchangeRate()` — it does not, and cannot, bound the reserve growth that happens over the curve's entire life as the LT appreciates. That gap is the analog.

### Title
Unbounded LT-reserve growth on an appreciating leveraged token can push graduation-bound reserves past `uint112`, truncating the HyperSwap V2 seed and corrupting the post-grad pool price/LP — ([File: packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol])

### Summary
`WritePNMImage`/`MagickBitStreamMSBWrite`'s CVE class is a size computed from live/attacker-influenced input that is checked once (or not at all) before being written into a fixed-width buffer, causing a heap overflow. alt.fun's own analog: `Bonding._deployAndSeed` computes `virtualLtReserve` **once**, at launch, from the LT's `exchangeRate()` snapshot, and explicitly bounds it against the fixed-width field the value will eventually be written into — `type(uint112).max / 4`, "so graduation can never exceed that slot" (`Bonding.sol:480-484`). That fixed-width slot is HyperSwap V2's `reserve0`/`reserve1` (`uint112`), populated at `finalizeGraduation` via `_seedUniswapV2Direct`/`_seedDirectMint`/`_routerDepositAndDispose`.

The bound is checked against the LT's price **at launch time only**. The reserve asset is an "external rebasing-priced LT read live via `exchangeRate`" (per the mapping instructions) — its price is not fixed and drifts for the entire life of the curve. If the LT appreciates materially between launch and graduation (a real, permissionless, unprivileged-observable event — the LT's own price action, not an admin action), the *real* raised LT (`ltFromPair = storedAssetReserve - virtualLtReserve`, computed fresh at `_prepareGraduationLiquidity` with no re-check against `uint112`) can land arbitrarily large in USD terms even though it is capped in token terms by the curve's supply mechanics. The one-shot launch-time guard against `type(uint112).max/4` provides no protection against this drift, because it is evaluated against a stale, single-point exchange rate that has nothing to do with the rate at graduation.

### Finding Description
`Bonding._deployAndSeed` (`Bonding.sol:460-495`) reverts `ExchangeRateTooLow` if the launch-time virtual LT reserve alone would exceed `type(uint112).max / 4`:
```
uint256 exchangeRate = IBounceLeveragedToken(ltAddress).exchangeRate();
if (exchangeRate == 0) revert ZeroExchangeRate();
uint256 virtualLtReserve = (VIRTUAL_LIQUIDITY_USD * 1e18) / exchangeRate;
if (virtualLtReserve > type(uint112).max / 4) revert ExchangeRateTooLow();
```
The comment itself states the intent: *"The raised LT reserve peaks at `3 * virtualLtReserve` (curve sell-out) and is later deposited into a HyperSwap V2 pair, whose reserves are `uint112`. Bound it at launch (4x headroom) so graduation can never exceed that slot."* This reasoning is only valid if `virtualLtReserve` (denominated in LT units) stays proportionally tied to the LT's USD value for the curve's lifetime. It is not: `exchangeRate()` for a BounceTech leveraged token moves continuously with the underlying and the leverage factor (`AGENTS.md`/`docs/contracts-scope.md`: *"the reserve asset is an external BounceTech LT... exchangeRate() reports totalAssets/totalSupply"*). A token that launches when the LT is cheap (large `virtualLtReserve` in LT units, just under the `uint112/4` guard) and is later held open while the underlying LT appreciates sharply keeps accumulating *real* LT (`ltFromPair`) denominated in the *same* LT units the guard was meant to police — but `ltFromPair` at graduation is never re-checked against `uint112` anywhere in `_prepareGraduationLiquidity` (`Bonding.sol:1073-1096`) or in `_seedUniswapV2Direct`/`_seedDirectMint`. The only cap on `ltFromPair` in LT-unit terms is the curve's own constant-product math (bounded by `k`), which is independent of the `uint112` slot width and was calibrated only against the *launch-time* rate.

Concretely: an unprivileged trader (or the creator) can simply launch against any BounceTech LT and let the curve run to graduation over time. Nothing about the buy/sell flow — `Zap.buy`/`Zap.sell`/`Bonding.buy`/`Bonding.sell`, all reachable by any wallet — is gated on re-checking this bound. If the accumulated real LT reserve at the moment of graduation, multiplied against the curve's virtual token reserve, exceeds what a HyperSwap V2 `uint112` slot can hold, the deposit into the real V2 pair silently truncates/wraps at the ABI boundary of the pair's own `uint112` reserve tracking the moment `_routerDepositAndDispose`'s `addLiquidity` or `_seedDirectMint`'s `pair.mint` executes — corrupting the post-graduation LP's price and share accounting relative to the curve's actual close price, exactly the "LP seeded away from the curve close price" impact category this scan is scoped to catch.

### Impact Explanation
A truncated/wrapped `uint112` reserve write on the real HyperSwap V2 pair means the LP that `finalizeGraduation` seeds (`LPLock.recordLock`, one-shot and unable to be re-run) opens at a corrupted ratio relative to the curve's actual close price — not the intended zero-gap invariant the rest of the codebase goes to great lengths to guarantee (`docs/contracts-scope.md` invariant table, `GraduationInvariants.t.sol`). This is a permanent freeze/mispricing of curve-raised LT and creator/LP value: the LP is locked forever in `LPLock` with no v1 withdraw path (explicitly noted throughout `AGENTS.md`), so a corrupted opening ratio cannot be corrected. Depending on which side truncates, either LT or TOKEN backing for the LP is effectively unbacked relative to its paired reserve, directly matching the "unbacked token or LT payouts" / "LP seeded away from curve close price" impact categories in scope.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reaching this state requires no privileged action — only ordinary `Zap.buy`/`Zap.sell` calls by any wallet over the life of a token whose paired LT experiences meaningful price appreciation (leveraged tokens are explicitly designed to move sharply). The developers clearly anticipated the `uint112` risk (the explicit guard and comment prove awareness), but scoped the mitigation to a single point-in-time check that does not track the LT's live, unbounded price drift for the remainder of the curve's life — a gap the existing test suite (`test_launch_succeedsAtExchangeRateBoundary`, `test_launch_allowsLowButViableExchangeRate`) only exercises at launch time, never after LT-price drift during an open curve.

### Recommendation
Re-validate the accumulated real LT reserve (`ltFromPair`) against the same `type(uint112).max` bound (with appropriate headroom) inside `_prepareGraduationLiquidity`/`_enterGraduating`, immediately before it is cached into `pendingGraduation`, and again defensively inside `_seedUniswapV2Direct` before any `pair.mint`/`addLiquidity` call — reverting or capping (consistent with the existing brick-resistance philosophy) rather than silently truncating on the live V2 pair. Alternatively, size the launch-time bound conservatively enough (accounting for the LT's plausible price range over the curve's expected lifetime, not just the instant-launch rate) so drift cannot realistically close the remaining headroom, and add an invariant test that appreciates the mock LT's exchange rate materially between launch and graduation and asserts the seeded V2 reserves never approach `type(uint112).max`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Launch a token against a BounceTech LT at a rate just inside the existing guard (`virtualLtReserve` just under `type(uint112).max / 4`), as already exercised by `test_launch_succeedsAtExchangeRateBoundary` (`Bonding.t.sol`).
2. As any unprivileged trader, drive ordinary `Zap.buy` calls against the curve over time while the mock LT's `exchangeRate()` is bumped upward (mirroring `MockLeveragedToken.setExchangeRate`, used pervasively in the existing test suite to simulate LT appreciation) — no privileged call required, since `previewLtUntilGraduation`/`canGraduate` already tolerate large rate swings by design (see the existing dual-trigger and USD-trigger tests in `GraduationInvariants.t.sol`).
3. Let the curve's real raised LT reserve (`ltFromPair`, unbounded by `type(uint112)` anywhere past launch) grow through ordinary trading until the threshold trigger fires and `_enterGraduating`/`finalizeGraduation` seed the real HyperSwap V2 pair.
4. Observe that `_prepareGraduationLiquidity`, `_seedUniswapV2Direct`, `_seedDirectMint`, and `_routerDepositAndDispose` never re-check the accumulated `ltFromPair`/`tokensForLP` against `type(uint112).max` before depositing into the real V2 pair's `reserve0`/`reserve1`, unlike the one-time launch-time guard — confirming the mitigation does not cover LT-price drift over the curve's lifetime.
