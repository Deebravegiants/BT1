### Title
Webhook shop-domain header is not covered by the HMAC signature, allowing cross-tenant webhook spoofing - (File: `lib/shopify_api/webhooks/request.rb`)

### Summary
`ShopifyAPI::Webhooks::Registry.process` verifies webhook authenticity via `Utils::HmacValidator.validate(request)`, but the HMAC is computed only over the raw request body, not over the `shop-domain` header that identifies which tenant the webhook belongs to. Any party capable of producing a validly-signed webhook body for its *own* shop (any merchant/tenant that has installed the app) can replay that exact body with a forged `x-shopify-shop-domain` header, and the signature will still validate — while the app attributes the payload to an arbitrary victim shop.

### Finding Description
`ShopifyAPI::Webhooks::Request#to_signable_string` returns only `@raw_body`: [1](#0-0) 

and `hmac` is read from the `hmac-sha256` header, independent of the other identity headers: [2](#0-1) 

`Utils::HmacValidator.validate` verifies only `verifiable_query.to_signable_string` against the HMAC, i.e. only the body: [3](#0-2) 

`Registry.process` treats a successful HMAC check as proof the *entire request*, including `request.shop`, is authentic, and forwards `shop` straight into the app's handler as the tenant identifier: [4](#0-3) 

Because the HMAC secret (`Context.api_secret_key`) is the app's single client secret shared across every installed shop, any merchant who has the app installed can obtain a validly-signed webhook body for their own shop (Shopify delivers these to their endpoint) and then resend that body with the `hmac-sha256` value unchanged but the `shop-domain` header changed to a different, victim tenant's domain. The gem accepts this as an authentic webhook "from" the victim shop, since `shop` is never part of the signed material — breaking the binding: **shop header used for tenant routing == shop that produced/authorized the HMAC**.

### Impact Explanation
This crosses a tenant boundary: an unprivileged (but legitimately app-installed) attacker-shop can inject fabricated webhook events attributed to another merchant's shop into the host application, without needing the victim's credentials, access token, or `client_secret`. Depending on how the host app consumes `WebhookMetadata#shop` (e.g. to look up/update per-tenant records, trigger fulfillment, mark orders paid, etc.), this enables cross-tenant data injection/corruption — matching the "cross-tenant access" Critical impact category.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any merchant who has installed the app can trivially capture one of their own legitimate, validly-HMAC-signed webhook deliveries (bodies are frequently generic, e.g. `{}` for many topics, or attacker-controllable via triggering the underlying Shopify action) and replay it against the app's webhook endpoint with a different `shop-domain` header. No secret material or elevated privilege is required beyond being a normal, unprivileged tenant of the app.

### Recommendation
Bind the `shop` (and ideally `topic`/`webhook-id`) header to the signature verification, or otherwise cryptographically tie the shop identity to the signed payload — e.g. include the shop domain in the signable string, or require the host application to cross-check `request.shop` against a shop that is known to be currently installed/authorized for this app before trusting the webhook, rather than relying on the header value verbatim once the body-only HMAC passes.

### Proof of Concept
1. App is installed on `attacker-shop.myshopify.com` and `victim-shop.myshopify.com`.
2. Shopify sends a legitimate webhook to the app for `attacker-shop.myshopify.com`:
   ```
   x-shopify-topic: orders/create
   x-shopify-hmac-sha256: <valid-hmac-of-body>
   x-shopify-shop-domain: attacker-shop.myshopify.com
   body: {"id": 1, ...}
   ```
3. Attacker (who controls their own shop/webhook receiver, e.g. via a proxy) resends the identical body and `hmac-sha256` value to the app's webhook endpoint, but replaces the header:
   ```
   x-shopify-shop-domain: victim-shop.myshopify.com
   ```
4. `Utils::HmacValidator.validate` recomputes HMAC over `@raw_body` only [5](#0-4)  — it matches, since the body is unchanged.
5. `Registry.process` calls the registered handler with `shop: "victim-shop.myshopify.com"` [6](#0-5) , causing the app to process attacker-controlled data as if it originated from the victim tenant.
