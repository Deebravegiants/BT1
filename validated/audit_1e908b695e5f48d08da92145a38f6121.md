### Title
Webhook `shop-domain`, `topic`, and `webhook-id` headers are not covered by the HMAC signature, allowing cross-tenant webhook spoofing - (File: `lib/shopify_api/webhooks/registry.rb`, `lib/shopify_api/webhooks/request.rb`)

### Summary
`ShopifyAPI::Webhooks::Registry.process` authenticates an inbound webhook solely by validating the HMAC over the raw request body, then trusts the `shop-domain`, `topic`, and `webhook-id` values taken from unauthenticated HTTP headers when dispatching to the handler. The identity binding the app relies on — "the shop the HMAC proves the webhook came from" == "the shop the handler is told the webhook is for" — does not hold, because the HMAC never covers the headers.

### Finding Description
`Utils::HmacValidator.validate` computes the signature over `verifiable_query.to_signable_string` and compares it with the `hmac` field [1](#0-0) . For a webhook `Request`, `to_signable_string` returns only the raw request body, and `hmac` is parsed from the `hmac-sha256` header, while `shop`, `topic`, `webhook_id`, and `api_version` are all read straight from separate, unauthenticated headers with no cryptographic linkage to the body or to each other [2](#0-1) .

`Registry.process` validates only the HMAC over the body, then immediately uses the header-derived `request.topic` to look up the handler and constructs `WebhookMetadata` from the header-derived `request.shop`, `request.webhook_id`, and `request.api_version`, handing it directly to the app's handler: [3](#0-2) . The `WebhookMetadata` struct that the handler receives simply stores whatever `shop` string it was given [4](#0-3) .

Because the HMAC secret (`api_secret_key`) is shared across every shop that has the app installed, any merchant who has the app installed receives their own legitimately-signed webhook deliveries (body + valid HMAC) from Shopify. That attacker-controlled merchant can capture one such (body, hmac) pair and replay it to the app's webhook endpoint with the `shopify-shop-domain` header swapped to a victim shop's domain (and/or a different `shopify-topic`/`shopify-webhook-id`). `HmacValidator.validate` still succeeds, because it only checks the body against the shared secret and never checks the headers [5](#0-4) . The handler then processes the payload believing it is data for the victim shop, i.e., `data.shop` is trusted as the tenant identity for that webhook without being bound to the signature.

### Impact Explanation
This breaks the equality "shop cryptographically proven by the HMAC" == "shop the handler acts on," enabling cross-tenant webhook injection: an attacker with any shop connected to the app can make the app believe arbitrary (attacker-chosen, replayed) webhook content/topic belongs to a shop they don't control. Any host application that keys its logic (e.g., session/token lookup, order/customer record updates, mandatory-topic redaction handling) off `WebhookMetadata#shop` as returned by this gem is exposed to cross-tenant data confusion, meeting the "cross-tenant access" Critical impact bar.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation requires the attacker to control at least one shop with the app installed (to legitimately receive one signed webhook), which is a low, self-service bar for a public/free app, and no possession of `api_secret_key` or any leaked credential is needed — only replay of a header value that the gem never authenticates.

### Recommendation
Bind the routing/identity fields into the signed material, or otherwise cryptographically verify that `shop`, `topic`, and `webhook_id` correspond to the specific delivery that produced the HMAC (e.g., include them in `to_signable_string`, or require the caller to independently verify `shop` against a known, previously-registered shop/session before trusting `WebhookMetadata#shop`) in `lib/shopify_api/webhooks/request.rb` and `lib/shopify_api/webhooks/registry.rb`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker installs the target app on their own shop `attacker.myshopify.com` and lets Shopify deliver a legitimate webhook: body `{"id":1}"`, headers `x-shopify-shop-domain: attacker.myshopify.com`, `x-shopify-hmac-sha256: <valid HMAC of body>`.
2. Attacker replays the exact same HTTP request to the app's webhook endpoint, only changing `x-shopify-shop-domain` to `victim.myshopify.com` (and optionally `x-shopify-topic`/`x-shopify-webhook-id`).
3. `Registry.process` calls `Utils::HmacValidator.validate(request)`, which recomputes the HMAC over the unchanged body and succeeds [5](#0-4) .
4. The handler is invoked with `WebhookMetadata.new(shop: "victim.myshopify.com", ...)` [6](#0-5) , causing the host application to process attacker-controlled data under the victim's tenant context.
