### Title
Slow fundamental oracle leg can inflate blended collateral and enable undercollateralized borrowing - ([File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs](contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs))

### Summary
A dual-source oracle blends a fresh market leg with a `FeedNature::Fundamental` leg without imposing the one-hour cross-leg timestamp bound used for two market legs. [1](#0-0)  If the fundamental leg remains within its own configured staleness window but contains an old price, the aggregator accepts its midpoint with the fresh leg and the controller uses that inflated value for collateral checks. [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

### Finding Description
`blend` applies `MAX_LEG_AGE_SPREAD_SECONDS` only when both legs have `FeedNature::Market`; a market/fundamental pair is exempt regardless of how far apart their observations are. [1](#0-0)  The outcome is stale only when a leg exceeds its own budget or the market-market spread rule fires, while the final price is still the two-leg midpoint. [4](#0-3)  An unprivileged borrower can call `supply` to establish collateral and then `borrow`; those controller entrypoints consume the aggregator's strict price for solvency checks. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5)  The repository's regression test models a Reflector market price of $0.91, a 82,800-second-old RedStone anchor at $1.00, and an 86,400-second anchor budget, then shows the stale-anchor account borrows $70,000 while the fresh-anchor control is rejected. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7) 

### Impact Explanation
The stale anchor raises collateral valuation by more than 4% in the tested configuration, which is sufficient to cross the borrow boundary and withdraw assets beyond the account's economically supported capacity. [9](#0-8)  At the tested scale, the borrower receives $70,000 of USDC despite only $91,000 of current-valued XLM collateral, while the blended valuation reports approximately $95,500 before LTV weighting. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10)  This can leave supplier funds underbacked and ultimately socialize a shortfall through bad-debt handling rather than merely causing an incorrect quote. [12](#0-11) 

### Likelihood Explanation
The borrower requires no privileged role: they own the collateral, the account, and the withdrawal recipient, while the oracle divergence can arise from an ordinary missed heartbeat on a deliberately slow fundamental source. [5](#0-4) [13](#0-12)  The attack window exists only while the slow leg remains inside its configured budget and the two returned prices remain inside the configured tolerance, but the production test demonstrates that a 23-hour-old fundamental leg can satisfy those conditions. [14](#0-13) [15](#0-14) 

### Recommendation
Apply an explicit timestamp-spread bound to mixed market/fundamental pairs, or bound the midpoint's deviation from the fresh market leg more tightly than the configured generic tolerance. [16](#0-15)  Alternatively, configure fundamental legs with a materially smaller `max_stale_seconds`, reject a blend when the older leg's timestamp trails the market leg beyond a safe window, or use the lower of the two accepted legs for collateral valuation. [4](#0-3) [17](#0-16) 

### Proof of Concept
1. Configure a collateral asset with a fresh `Market` leg and a slow `Fundamental` leg; give the fundamental leg a long staleness budget such as 86,400 seconds. [18](#0-17) 
2. Let the fundamental leg remain at the old $1.00 price for 82,800 seconds while the market leg reports the current $0.91 price. [19](#0-18) 
3. Supply 100,000 units of the collateral through `supply`, then call `borrow` for $70,000 of USDC. [11](#0-10) 
4. The stale-anchor transaction succeeds because the accepted midpoint overstates collateral by more than 4%, while the equivalent fresh-anchor transaction fails with `InsufficientCollateral`. [8](#0-7)

```markdown
### INV-LIQ-04 — Bad-debt socialization is explicit and total

Permissionless cleanup requires ceil risk debt greater than half-up unweighted
collateral and collateral at or below the fixed $5 dust threshold. Owner-only
forced cleanup omits the dust cap. Both require debt, readable account and NFT
state, valid required prices and no active flash guard. Listing flags and
global pause do not block standalone cleanup.

Cleanup reclassifies all remaining collateral shares as revenue and writes off
all remaining debt against each debt's market. It releases spoke usage and
atomically removes account entries and the NFT. It does not net same-market
supply against debt. Standalone cleanup emits `CleanBadDebtEvent` with
pre-cleanup USD totals, without a controller position-update batch.

Ordinary liquidation and cleanup apply no final account-health or full-backing
assertion. The index floor can leave a shortfall. Recapitalization fills that
shortfall without restoring the lost index or deleted account.
```
