### Title
Bad-debt wipeout clamps `supply_index` to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead of invalidating wiped supply shares, leaving phantom claims that drain fresh deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
The kernel bug (CVE-2024-27389) is a reference-counting/invalidation error: code used `d_drop()`/`dput()` where only `d_invalidate()` was correct, leaving stale references alive past the point they should have been dropped. The XOXNO Lending analog is in bad-debt socialization: when a wipeout would push `supply_index` below the floor, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps the index **up** to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`) rather than invalidating (zeroing/burning) the wiped supply shares. The unburned scaled supply positions keep a positive claim — a stale reference that was never invalidated — and can withdraw real cash deposited later by fresh suppliers.

### Finding Description
`supply_index` is explicitly non-monotone: `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` scales it down to socialize losses, floored at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`), per `contracts/pool/README.md` [1](#0-0) . When the socialized loss exceeds what the floor can express, the clamp leaves every surviving scaled supply position with a residual positive value: `unscale_supply_floor(old_scaled) > 0`.

The dedicated unit tests prove the mechanism end to end on the real `Cache` functions:

- `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-428`): after `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` wipes the market, the old position still unscale-withdraws exactly the fresh depositor's cash, leaving `cash < fresh_claim` — insolvency.
- `test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:430-495`): same shape via the seizure path — `burn_debt` plus index clamp leaves Alice's wiped supply claim stranded, and `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, alice_scaled)` pays out Bob's entire fresh deposit.

Reachability for an unprivileged address: the controller's liquidation and `clean_bad_debt` paths call pool `seize_positions`, whose borrow-side legs "socialize bad debt onto the supply index and burn the debt" [2](#0-1) . Any user can create the under-collateralized position (borrow, then price move or self-liquidation) and invoke `clean_bad_debt`/liquidate to trigger `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`. The wipeout regime requires bad debt whose index-adjusted write-down exceeds `supply_index − floor`, i.e. debt value larger than ~99.9% of total supply value — reachable in a small/young market or one already deep in bad debt.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent loss / theft of user funds and protocol insolvency: wiped suppliers retain shares that unscale to a positive claim. Once any new cash enters the market (a fresh `supply`), those claims withdraw first and drain real deposits, while `require_reserves` and `require_supply_for_debt` do not block it because the book still shows the phantom `supplied` shares. The test asserts the exact outcome: `gross == fresh_cash`, `cash < bob_claim` [3](#0-2) .

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a near-total bad-debt wipeout in a market (bad debt ≳ supply-side value, or a market already mostly socialized down toward the floor). Unprivileged actors can manufacture this via `borrow` → price deterioration or self-liquidation → `clean_bad_debt`, and thin or newly created markets reach the clamp far sooner. Not a one-click exploit, but no privileged action is needed and the post-condition is deterministic.

### Recommendation
On wipeout, invalidate rather than clamp: when `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` hits `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, proportionally burn (or flag) the residual supply shares so `unscale_supply_floor` of wiped positions is zero — the equivalent of `d_invalidate()`. Alternatively track an explicit "wiped" marker per market and reject withdrawals on pre-wipe positions, or restamp positions so survivors' scaled amounts are rewritten to zero in the same transaction that clamps the index.

### Proof of Concept
Demonstrated by the in-repo tests `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-495`: seed a market, call `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` with bad debt exceeding supply coverage, observe `supply_index == SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` while `unscale_supply_floor(old_scaled) > 0`; a fresh deposit equal to that stranded claim is then fully withdrawn by the wiped position via `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, …)` + `debit_cash`, leaving the fresh depositor undercollateralized.

Caveat: the tests are named "…without supply guard" and note the invariant is "only masked by `require_reserves`" — I could not fully trace within this session whether the production `seize_positions`/`withdraw` composition adds a guard that blocks the stranded withdrawal in every case. The `withdraw` path's guards (`require_reserves`, `require_supply_for_debt`) check solvency but do not distinguish wiped shares, so once fresh cash exists the drain appears executable; a Devin session should confirm no additional wipeout guard exists on the withdraw path.
