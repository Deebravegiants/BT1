### Title
Self-inflicted Aquarius LP price outage via `min_pool_value_wad` shields an underwater account from liquidation and bad-debt cleanup - ([File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs](contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs))

### Summary
A borrower can plant a dust-sized Aquarius LP collateral leg and then push that pool's total value below the configured `min_pool_value_wad` floor with their own liquidity withdrawal. The LP price read then returns `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity`, the controller's strict price cache fails closed, and every risk-valued path over the account — `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and even the borrower's own `withdraw` — reverts. This converts an ambient fail-closed oracle assumption into an attacker-triggered, single-transaction denial of liquidation that can run until pool liquidity returns or leg prices change.

### Finding Description
The Aquarius provider rejects a pool whose WAD-valued share supply is below `lp.min_pool_value_wad`:

```rust
// contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs
if pool_value_wad < lp.min_pool_value_wad {
    return Err(OracleError::InsufficientAquariusLiquidity);
}
``` [1](#0-0) 

`pool_value_wad` is derived from live pool reserves and total shares, so any LP — including the attacker — can shrink it by withdrawing liquidity from the Aquarius pool in an ordinary, permissionless trade.

On the controller side, every collateral leg of an account must price successfully for risk totals, liquidation planning, and bad-debt admission:

- `liquidate` builds its plan and post-totals through `risk::calculate_account_risk_totals` over all supply positions [2](#0-1) 
- `clean_bad_debt` requires "valid required prices" before socializing [3](#0-2) 
- The threat model explicitly acknowledges this shape: "an indebted borrower can add a dust leg of any listed collateral and choose which feed outage shields the account. For an Aquarius LP leg, liquidity providers can cause that outage by withdrawing pool value below `min_pool_value_wad`. The same leg blocks bad-debt cleanup and force-socialization." [4](#0-3) 

Crucially, `supply` does not need a valid price for the new leg, so the poisoned position can be opened *while* the feed is already failing — the attacker never needs a fresh price for the LP asset at any point. The harness test demonstrates the identical mechanism with a stale Reflector feed: a 0.001 dust WBTC leg planted during staleness bricks `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and `withdraw` with `PRICE_FEED_STALE`, and all three recover the moment the feed refreshes [5](#0-4) .

The Almanak analog class — operational failure causing temporary inability to claim/exit — maps here as an attacker-induced temporary freezing of exit/liquidation functionality rather than an external outage.

### Impact Explanation
While the shield holds, the account cannot be liquidated no matter how far below HF = 1 it falls, `clean_bad_debt`/`force_socialize_bad_debt` cannot socialize the growing shortfall, and interest continues accruing. The result is protocol insolvency risk borne by suppliers (bad debt accumulates with no reachable remedy) plus temporary freezing of the collateral and of any funds that depend on risk-valued exits. The attacker can lift the shield at will by re-adding liquidity, making the freeze duration fully attacker-controlled — matching "temporary freezing of funds" and "protocol insolvency" impact classes.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by a single unprivileged address using only whitelisted actions: `controller::supply` of a dust amount of a listed Aquarius LP collateral into their own account, plus their own liquidity withdrawal on Aquarius itself. Cost is bounded by the dust leg plus the capital needed to move pool value under `min_pool_value_wad`; for thin pools this is cheap, and the attacker also retains an LP withdrawal they can reverse. No privileged role, leaked key, or third-party oracle dishonesty is required — the outage is produced by the market mechanics of the venue itself. The main constraint is that a listed spoke must accept an Aquarius LP share as collateral and the pool must be drainable below its floor; the attacker can also simply wait for natural LP exits rather than funding the whole drain.

### Recommendation
- For Aquarius LP collateral listings, set `min_pool_value_wad` conservatively low and/or prefer pools whose depth makes a sub-floor drain uneconomical; document the drain cost as a listing criterion.
- Consider decoupling per-leg price failure from whole-account valuation: e.g., treat an unpriceable collateral leg as zero value for HF purposes (still conservative for the borrower, but it removes the liquidation shield) and exclude it from seizure legs.
- At minimum, allow `clean_bad_debt` and liquidation planning to skip collateral legs whose price read fails with `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity`, since a leg contributing nothing to collateral cannot be seized anyway.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance lists an Aquarius LP share token as collateral in a spoke, backed by a `PriceKey::Token` configured with an `AquariusLpSource` with floor `min_pool_value_wad` (see `AssetOracle`/`AquariusLpSource` handling in `attest`/`read` [6](#0-5) ).
2. Attacker opens account via `supply(account_id = 0)`, supplies collateral, and `borrow`s up to the LTV limit.
3. Collateral price drops; account HF < 1. Attacker calls `supply` adding a dust amount of the LP share — accepted because supply requires no price.
4. Attacker (or any LP) withdraws liquidity from the underlying Aquarius pool until `price_wad * total_shares / share_unit < min_pool_value_wad`.
5. `liquidate(liquidator, attacker_id, …)` → reverts propagating `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` through the strict price cache; `clean_bad_debt` reverts identically; attacker's `withdraw` also reverts, confirming the freeze is symmetric.
6. Debt accrues interest unliquidatably until the pool is refilled above the floor; the attacker can re-add liquidity at any time to unfreeze.

The stale-feed variant of steps 3–5 is already exercised in `audit_supply_setup_blocks_liquidation_via_stale_dust_leg` [7](#0-6) , which confirms the controller-side revert behavior; the `min_pool_value_wad` branch in `read` supplies the attacker-controllable trigger.
