### Title
Interest accrual can push utilization over `max_utilization`, after which the utilization guard permanently reverts every withdrawal and revenue claim on the market - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs)

### Summary
The pool enforces a post-hoc solvency guard, `require_utilization_below_max`, on every non-liquidation withdrawal (`contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:114-116`) and on `claim_revenue` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/revenue.rs:44`). The guard was added to cap utilization on *new* exposure (borrows), but it also gates *exits*. Because the borrowed-side RAY index grows strictly faster than the supply-side index whenever `reserve_factor > 0` or utilization < 100%, a market sitting at the utilization boundary drifts over `max_utilization` through pure interest accrual — with no further user action. From that point on, every `withdraw` reverts with `UtilizationAboveMax`, freezing all supplier principal in that market until someone voluntarily repays debt or adds new supply.

### Finding Description
`mint_debt` enforces `require_utilization_below_max` after minting debt (`contracts/pool/src/ops/borrow.rs:78`), so a borrow can bring utilization exactly to `max_utilization` but never above it. The check in `guards.rs:19-34` computes `borrowed.div_ceil(supplied) <= max_utilization` on index-scaled values. Since `borrow_index` accrues at the borrow rate while `supply_index` accrues at `borrow_rate × utilization × (1 - reserve_factor)`, the ceiled-debt/floored-supply ratio is strictly increasing over time in any market with positive debt and a nonzero reserve factor. One millisecond accrual chunk after the boundary borrow, utilization exceeds the cap.

Withdraw accounting calls `gate_and_debit`, which runs `guards::require_utilization_below_max` for every withdrawal except liquidation withdrawals and footprint-only closes (`withdraw.rs:78-79`, `withdraw.rs:111-119`). `revenue::accounting` runs the same guard (`revenue.rs:44`). Neither path offers a bypass for ordinary suppliers. The state is self-reinforcing from an attacker's perspective: repaying is voluntary, `clean_bad_debt` does not apply (the market is solvent — utilization > max does not mean insolvency), and liquidation withdrawals skip the check but only fire on unhealthy accounts and only move collateral, not the debt-side utilization of this market.

### Impact Explanation
Temporary freezing of user funds, attacker-influenced: once utilization crosses `max_utilization`, no supplier can withdraw any amount from that market (the guard is on the post-withdraw market state, so even a dust withdrawal reverts), and the owner cannot `claim_revenue`. The freeze persists until debt is repaid or new supply dilutes utilization — both actions the protocol cannot compel. An attacker who opened the boundary borrow can simply refuse to repay, holding the market frozen for as long as they maintain their position's health, while suppliers' principal is locked. This mirrors the CVE class precisely: a safety check intended to protect the system is itself triggerable into a livelock/DoS reachable by an unprivileged address via `borrow`.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. Reaching the boundary requires only that an attacker borrow up to `max_utilization` of the market — a normal `controller.borrow` call, unprivileged, subject to having sufficient collateral (which can be collateral in other markets or even a flash-position `multiply`). The attacker needs no special timing: any market whose utilization sits at or near the cap tips over automatically at the next accrual. The attacker's cost is the interest on their own borrow and the collateral locked. The freeze is broken if a third party supplies enough to restore utilization, which limits permanence but does not restore access on demand — suppliers have no self-help path since their own exit is exactly what is blocked.

### Recommendation
Do not apply `require_utilization_below_max` to exits. Utilization caps exist to bound *new* exposure; applying them to withdrawals punishes suppliers for a state they did not create and cannot fix by withdrawing. Either remove the guard from `gate_and_debit` (keeping `require_supply_for_debt` and reserve checks, which are the actual solvency constraints), or exempt withdrawals that do not increase utilization — which is all of them, since burning supply while cash leaves cannot raise the borrowed/supplied ratio beyond what a proportional cash debit implies; at minimum gate only on net cash-out beyond the borrowed share. Similarly, exempt `claim_revenue`, which only moves already-owed protocol funds.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance lists a market with `max_utilization = 0.8e27` and a positive `reserve_factor`.
2. Suppliers deposit `S` units; attacker supplies collateral in a second market.
3. Attacker calls `controller.borrow` drawing the market to utilization `0.8e27 − ε` — accepted by `mint_debt`'s post-mint check.
4. `env.ledger().set_timestamp` advances; `update_indexes` accrues. `borrow_index` grows faster than `supply_index`, so `unscale_borrow_ceil(borrowed) / unscale_supply_floor(supplied)` crosses `0.8e27`.
5. Any supplier calls `controller.withdraw`; pool `withdraw::accounting` reaches `guards::require_utilization_below_max` (`withdraw.rs:115`) which evaluates `borrowed.div_ceil(supplied) <= max_utilization` as false and panics with `UtilizationAboveMax` (`guards.rs:29-33`). Every subsequent withdrawal and `claim_revenue` reverts identically until the attacker repays.
