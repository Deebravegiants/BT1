### Title
Bad-debt supply-index floor leaves a residual phantom claim that drains fresh deposits - (File: contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs)

### Summary
When `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` writes down a market's supply index, it clamps the index at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`10^24`) instead of letting it fall to the mathematically correct value. After a wipeout-scale write-down, suppliers keep their RAY-scaled shares and the floored index still converts those shares into a positive underlying claim. That residual claim is not backed by anything, but it is honored against any later `cash` credited to the market — including a fresh supplier's deposit. The in-repo test `test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` proves the mechanics end to end at the cache layer: a stranded holder withdraws exactly the fresh deposit, leaving the honest supplier's claim unbacked. This is the direct analog of CVE-2015-8553: an incomplete fix (the floor clamp) leaves residual, uninitialized-backed value that leaks into later state.

### Finding Description
- `Cache::unscale_supply_floor` converts scaled shares to underlying using `supply_index`; after `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps the index up to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, a pre-wipeout share balance still resolves to a non-zero payout (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:339-341`).
- `Cache::resolve_withdrawal` + `require_reserves` + `burn_supply` + `debit_cash` pay that residual claim out of `cash` with no check that the claim corresponds to surviving backing (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:351-360`; `contracts/pool/src/cache/cash.rs:15-41`).
- `credit_cash` / `mint_supply` let any later depositor's funds become the exit liquidity for the residual claim (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:343-368`).

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency: every unit a residual holder withdraws after the clamp is paid out of cash belonging to post-write-down suppliers, whose claims become unbacked (test asserts `cash() < b_claim` and `cash() == 0`). An unprivileged user can reach this via the listed entrypoints: supply, accrue bad debt (e.g., via a self-liquidated/dust position), have it written down through `clean_bad_debt`, retain scaled shares, then `withdraw` once new deposits land.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a write-down large enough to saturate the floor — i.e., bad debt exceeding what the true index can absorb — plus at least one subsequent deposit. The attacker controls the timing: hold shares passively and withdraw against any later inflow. Because the residual is proportional to retained scaled shares, even small fresh deposits are capturable.

### Recommendation
On bad-debt write-down, either burn the pro-rata scaled supply shares so no residual claim survives the floor, or cap `unscale_supply_floor` payouts at actual `cash` attributable to pre-write-down backing. The clamp should reset the index base rather than preserve phantom value: track and zero claims when the index would have fallen below `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`.

### Proof of Concept
`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:316-369` already encodes it: `supplied = 1_000_000 * RAY` at `supply_index = RAY`; `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(2_000_000 * RAY)` clamps the index to the floor; `stranded = unscale_supply_floor(scaled_a) > 0`; a fresh deposit `c` is credited; `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, scaled_a)` pays out `gross == c`, draining the pool to zero while the honest supplier's claim `b_claim == c` remains unbacked. Caveat: if this residual is an explicitly accepted design trade-off (dust flooring), it should be documented as such; otherwise it is a live insolvency vector reachable by any address holding scaled shares across a wipeout.
