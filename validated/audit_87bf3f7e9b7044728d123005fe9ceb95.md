### Title
Relayer delivery-credit attribution (`_msgSender()` on `dispatchIncoming`/`recordEpoch`) can be frontrun to steal Hyperbridge relayer rewards - (File: `evm/src/core/HandlerV2.sol`, `evm/src/core/EvmHost.sol`)

### Summary
Hyperbridge's relayer reward system (fee accumulation, `OutboundConsensusDeliveryReward`, `OutboundRequestDeliveryReward`) pays whichever address is recorded on the destination chain as having delivered a message or consensus proof. That attribution is `msg.sender` of the delivery transaction on the destination EVM chain, not a signature bound to the relayer who assembled the proof off-chain. Because the delivery calldata (Merkle multiproof, requests, consensus proof) is built entirely from publicly observable source-chain/consensus state, any third party who sees a pending delivery transaction in an L2 mempool can copy its calldata into their own transaction with higher gas and land first, becoming the address credited for delivery and thus receiving the reward, exactly the "keeper does the work, frontrunner claims the payment" pattern described in the reported `PythOracle.commitRequested` issue.

### Finding Description
Delivery credit on EVM destinations is assigned to `_msgSender()` at the point of proof verification, with no requirement that the caller be the one who produced or is entitled to the proof:

- `HandlerV2.handlePostRequests` verifies the MMR multiproof and then calls `host.dispatchIncoming(leaf.request, _msgSender())` for every request in the batch [1](#0-0) . `dispatchIncoming` stores the caller's address into `_requestReceipts[commitment]` as the "relayer responsible for delivery," which is what later gets read back by Hyperbridge's fee/reward pipelines.
- `HandlerV2.handleConsensus` similarly credits the *epoch rotation* to whoever called the handler: `host.recordEpoch(epoch, _msgSender())` [2](#0-1) , and `EvmHost._epochs[set_id]` records that address.
- Hyperbridge's `pallet-relayer` then pays out based solely on what address is recorded at those slots:
  - `accumulate` reads the destination `RequestReceipts`/receipt slot and credits `Fees[state_machine][delivery_address]` to whatever address is found there [3](#0-2) .
  - `claim_outbound_consensus_delivery_reward` proves `EvmHost._epochs[set_id]` and pays the treasury reward to the signer that matches the recorded address [4](#0-3) .
  - `claim_outbound_request_delivery_reward` similarly proves `RequestReceipts[commitment]` on the destination and pays the treasury reward to whoever's signature matches that recorded address [5](#0-4) .

None of these attributions are cryptographically bound to the relayer who produced the proof — the multiproof, request batch, and consensus proof are all reconstructible from public chain data (source-chain events, BEEFY/consensus messages already broadcast to Hyperbridge, and the destination's already-known state commitments). A relayer's delivery transaction sitting in an L2 mempool exposes this calldata to anyone. A frontrunner copies the exact calldata into their own transaction with a higher gas price, gets included first, and `_msgSender()` at that point is the frontrunner's address, not the original relayer's. All downstream reward mechanisms (fee accumulation, consensus-delivery reward, request-delivery reward) then pay the frontrunner instead of the relayer who actually did the off-chain proof-construction and consensus/state tracking work.

### Impact Explanation
This directly mirrors the reported bug class: a party that performs no independent verification work (fetching/tracking consensus state, computing merkle proofs, monitoring source-chain events) can copy a pending transaction's calldata and capture the payout meant for the relayer that did the work. Over time this:
- Removes the economic incentive for honest relayers to do delivery work, since rewards can be systematically captured by MEV searchers/frontrunners on any Ethereum L2 with a public mempool (explicitly in scope per the prompt's rule that "any Ethereum L2 is in the scope").
- Threatens message delivery liveness: if relayers learn their rewards are routinely stolen, they stop delivering hyperbridge-originated system messages (host-executive parameter updates, intents-coprocessor responses, token-governor messages, relayer withdrawal requests) and regular user messages, since delivery is economically unrewarded despite gas cost. This is a "route unable to deliver messages" outcome and a permanent freezing-of-funds/rewards outcome for the legitimate relayer.
- Consensus-delivery rewards (`OutboundConsensusDeliveryReward`) are especially sensitive: `recordEpoch` is a race for a fixed limited reward per epoch, so systematic frontrunning could deter relayers from tracking and submitting authority-set rotations promptly, which risks stale consensus state on the destination.

### Likelihood Explanation
High on any Ethereum L2 with a visible mempool (or even with private-order-flow but public-broadcast fallback paths): delivery calldata carries no relayer-specific secret, so copying it costs nothing beyond gas and requires no computation. This is a purely mechanical MEV/frontrunning attack, requiring only mempool visibility and a slightly higher gas bid, and is economically profitable whenever the reward exceeds the gas premium needed to win the race.

### Recommendation
Bind delivery credit to a signature/commitment supplied by the relayer that cannot be trivially replayed by a third party, rather than to raw `msg.sender` at time of proof verification. Options: require the delivery transaction to include a signed commitment (e.g., relayer's address signed over the batch/consensus proof) that is checked against a value pre-registered off-chain, use a commit-reveal scheme for delivery submission, or use private relay/flashbots-style bundle submission to avoid public mempool exposure. At minimum, document/mitigate the MEV risk and consider a small reward-sharing or priority mechanism (e.g., first-valid-submitter bond) that raises the cost of frontrunning relative to the reward.

### Proof of Concept
1. Relayer R observes a source-chain event and a Hyperbridge dispatch root update, builds `PostRequestMessage` (with MMR multiproof) for delivery to an EVM destination, and broadcasts `HandlerV2.handlePostRequests(host, request)` as a transaction to the destination chain's public mempool.
2. Attacker A monitors the mempool, copies the entire calldata of R's pending transaction (all fields — `proof`, `requests` — are public/derivable), and submits an identical transaction with a higher gas price.
3. A's transaction lands first. `handlePostRequests` calls `host.dispatchIncoming(leaf.request, _msgSender())` with `_msgSender() == A` [1](#0-0) , storing A as the delivery address in `_requestReceipts[commitment]`.
4. R's original transaction now reverts with `DuplicateMessage` (the receipt is already set) [6](#0-5) .
5. When the relayer-fee/reward pipeline later runs `accumulate_fees` or `claim_outbound_request_delivery_reward` against this delivery, the destination's `RequestReceipts[commitment]` slot decodes to A's address, so the reward (protocol fee or `OutboundRequestDeliveryReward`) is paid to A instead of R [3](#0-2) [5](#0-4) .
6. The same pattern applies to `HandlerV2.handleConsensus` → `EvmHost.recordEpoch(epoch, _msgSender())`, letting an attacker steal the `OutboundConsensusDeliveryReward` by frontrunning the consensus-rotation delivery transaction [2](#0-1) .
