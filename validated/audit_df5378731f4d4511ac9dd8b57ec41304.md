### Title
Accrual-ordering RAY-value overflow panics `accrue_step`, permanently freezing every verb on a whale market - (File: contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs)

### Summary
The BIND bug is a crash triggered by the *ordering and timing* of externally supplied responses: a delayed negative answer arriving after a positive one kills the process. The lending analog is ordering/timing-dependent accrual in the pool: interest is applied in `MAX_COMPOUND_DELTA_MS` chunks whose boundaries are determined by *when* `update_indexes` (or any verb) happens to be called, and on a sufficiently large book the chunked accrual eventually pushes a `scaled * index` product past the `i128` RAY-value ceiling. `scaled_to_original` panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` inside `global_sync`, which runs first inside every state-changing entrypoint, so once the cliff is reached the market can never accrue again — no repay, withdraw, supply, liquidation, or cleanup ever succeeds.

### Finding Description
`Cache::calculate_utilization` and the unscale helpers convert `Ray` share counts to asset value via `scaled_to_original(scaled, index)` (contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:23-24, common/src/rates). Every op loads the market through `Cache`, calls `global_sync` (contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33), which loops `accrue_chunk` → `accrue_step` (interest.rs:39-53). `accrue_step` computes borrower interest as `half_up(borrowed * new_index)` minus `half_up(borrowed * old_index)`; the multiplication `scaled * index` is bounded by `i128::MAX` (~1.7e38), while each value is RAY-scaled (1e27). A market with ~1e9 units of an 18-decimal token holds `borrowed ≈ 1e27` scaled shares, so the product overflows once the borrow index reaches ≈ 170× — a level the XLM-style steep utilization curve reaches in a few years at sustained ~98% utilization, *before* `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` engages.

Because the panic is inside the accrual step, `last_timestamp` is never advanced (`mark_accrued` at interest.rs:32 is unreachable on the failing chunk), so every subsequent call to `update_indexes`, `supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `flash_loan`, or any router strategy touching that market reverts identically. The harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-361) reproduces exactly this: `update_indexes` panics `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and `try_withdraw`/`try_repay` then panic the same way.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds for every supplier and borrower in that market: supplier principal and accrued yield are unwithdrawable, borrower debt is unrepayable (including by liquidators, so collateral is trapped too), and `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` cannot run because they accrue first. Governance cannot repair it — `recapitalize` also accrues, and no entrypoint can touch the market without passing through `global_sync`.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by unprivileged addresses only: `supply` (create the whale book), `borrow` at near-100% utilization to sit on the steep curve segment, and permissionless `update_indexes` calls. Requirements are real but heavy: a ~10^9-unit position on a high-decimals asset plus sustained high utilization over a multi-year horizon, and caps (`supply_cap`, `borrow_cap`, `max_utilization`) set high enough to allow it — governance must lift or mis-size caps for the cliff to be in range, and ledger time cannot be fast-forwarded by the attacker. The chunking order means the exact cliff point also depends on call cadence, so the failure is genuinely ordering-dependent, matching the bug class. Given the capital and time requirements, likelihood is low-to-moderate; impact is critical, so overall High.

### Recommendation
Saturate rather than panic on RAY-value overflow in the accrual path: clamp `borrowed * index` (or the accrued interest delta) at `i128::MAX`/`MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` inside `accrue_step` so `global_sync` always completes and `last_timestamp` advances; enforce the existing index ceiling *before* the value product is computed. Alternatively, commit each chunk's `last_timestamp` incrementally so a later overflow cannot replay the same failing chunk, and add a governance escape hatch (e.g., a non-accruing pause-forced recapitalize) for markets already past the cliff. Validate per-market caps against worst-case index growth at listing time, as the threat model already warns (docs/explanation/threat-model.md:319-324).

### Proof of Concept
1. Configure a market for a high-decimals asset (e.g., 18 decimals) on a steep borrow curve, with supply/borrow caps raised above ~10^9 whole units and `max_utilization` disabled.
2. Attacker calls `supply(BOB, asset, 1_000_000_000 * 10^18)` then, from a separately collateralized account, `borrow(ALICE, asset, ~98%)`, holding utilization near the curve's steep segment.
3. Over subsequent ledger time, keep utilization high (attacker tops up borrows); any caller invoking `update_indexes` advances the chunked accrual.
4. Once `borrowed * borrow_index` approaches `1.7e38`, the next `accrue_step` panics `MathOverflow` in `scaled_to_original` before `mark_accrued` runs — the index cap `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` is never reached.
5. `withdraw(BOB, asset, 1)`, `repay(ALICE, asset, ...)`, `liquidate(...)`, `clean_bad_debt(...)`, and `recapitalize(...)` all revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW` for the rest of the market's life.
