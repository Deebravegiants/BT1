### Title
Bad-debt write-down clamped at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` resurrects wiped supplier claims that drain later deposits — ([File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs])

### Summary
The original advisory is a miscompilation where a shared/constant value is corrupted and a later consumer reads the wrong value. The analog in XOXNO Lending is `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89`): when socialized bad debt equals or exceeds the total supplied value, the supply index is clamped **up** to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW = 10^24` instead of zeroing supplier shares. Wiped-out suppliers therefore retain a phantom claim (`scaled_amount × 10^-3`), which `withdraw` later pays out of **other suppliers' deposits**, causing real token loss.

### Finding Description
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes `remaining = total_supplied_value - min(bad_debt, total_supplied_value)` and multiplies the supply index by `remaining / total_supplied_value`. On a full wipeout `remaining = 0`, so the index should collapse to zero — instead it is floored at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`: [1](#0-0) 

Scaled supply shares are **not** burned or marked, so every pre-existing supplier still holds `scaled_amount` shares now worth 0.1% of face value at the floored index. On the seize path this runs through `apply` in `contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:23-28`, which computes `bad_debt = unscale_borrow_ceil_ray(position)`, applies the write-down, and commits.

Because the floored index is nonzero, `resolve_withdrawal` and `unscale_supply_floor` still return a positive payout for the wiped positions (`contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:97-121`). The only barrier is `require_reserves` — i.e., the phantom claim is paid as long as the pool holds any cash, including cash deposited by *new* suppliers who joined after the wipeout (a fresh deposit at `supply_index = 10^24` mints shares at the same corrupted index). The protocol's own unit test demonstrates exactly this drain: after the clamp, an old supplier's withdrawal `drains exactly the fresh deposit` and leaves `pool cash < fresh supplier claim`: [2](#0-1) 

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. After a full-supply-index wipeout (bad debt ≥ total supplied value), wiped suppliers can withdraw a phantom balance worth ~0.1% of their original scaled position. That payout comes from the pool's shared cash, directly reducing what post-wipeout suppliers can reclaim. The impact scales with the wiped book size: e.g., a market with $10M supplied that is fully written down leaves ~$10k of phantom claims payable out of any fresh liquidity.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by any unprivileged address via `clean_bad_debt` (permissionless per scope) on an account whose collateral is fully exhausted — a realistic state after a sharp price drop or oracle lag, which is precisely when `clean_bad_debt` exists. Whether the write-down reaches the floor depends on bad debt ≥ total supply value in that market, i.e., a total-wipeout event; partial write-downs lower the index normally. Markets with a configured dust threshold may skip cleanup for small debts, but a large insolvency bypasses dust gating. Once clamped, extraction only requires the wiped supplier (or anyone holding a wiped position NFT) to call `withdraw`, which is always available.

### Recommendation
When `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, do not rely on the `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` clamp: either explicitly zero/rebase supplier shares (e.g., set `supplied = 0` and sweep residual share claims to revenue/bad-debt accounting) or block withdrawals on wiped markets. The floor exists to avoid a zero index for future deposits; the correct fix is to decouple "index value for new deposits" from "claim value for old shares" — old `scaled_amount` claims must not retain value after a full write-down. The test `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` should be inverted into an assertion that wiped positions pay nothing.

### Proof of Concept
1. Bob supplies X tokens to hub market M; Alice borrows against other collateral and becomes insolvent with bad debt ≥ total supplied value of M.
2. Anyone calls `controller.clean_bad_debt(alice_account_id)` → `seize` with `AccountPositionType::Borrow` → `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes `remaining = 0`, `new_supply_index = 0`, then `set_supply_index(max(0, 10^24))` — Bob's `scaled_amount` is untouched (`contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:25-28`).
3. Carol supplies fresh tokens to M; her shares mint at the clamped index and her deposit lands in `cash`.
4. Bob calls `controller.withdraw(0)` → `resolve_withdrawal` returns `floor(scaled_bob × 10^24 / 10^27)` > 0, `require_reserves` passes against Carol's cash, and Bob is paid from Carol's deposit — as proven by the repo's own test asserting `gross == fresh_cash` and `cash < fresh_claim`.
