### Title
Permissionless, front-runnable pool initialization allows griefers to seize a market's deterministic AMM PDA and set an attacker-controlled initial price - (File: `program/src/processor.rs`)

### Summary
`process_initialize2` derives the `amm`, `target_orders`, `lp_mint`, `coin_vault`, and `pc_vault` PDAs solely from `program_id` and the caller-supplied `market_info.key` seed account, with no privileged-signer restriction and no check that the caller is the intended pool creator. Because the accounts it creates via `generate_amm_associated_account`/`generate_amm_associated_spl_token`/`generate_amm_associated_spl_mint` are deterministic functions of the market key alone [2](#0-1) , and `Initialize2` sets the initial pool price entirely from attacker-supplied `init.init_pc_amount`/`init.init_coin_amount` [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) , any unprivileged actor who learns of (or predicts) the market seed account a project intends to use can front-run the legitimate `Initialize2` call, permanently occupying the PDA at a manipulated price ratio.

### Finding Description
`process_initialize2` requires only that `user_wallet_info` is a signer [5](#0-4)  — there is no restriction on *who* that signer may be, and the `market_info` account is explicitly documented as "just a seed for AMM account. Can be any account" [6](#0-5) . All the pool's core accounts (`amm`, `target_orders`, `lp_mint`, `coin_vault`, `pc_vault`) are derived via `get_associated_address_and_bump_seed`, keyed only on `program_id` and `market_account.key` [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7) .

Once any account successfully calls `Initialize2` for a given market seed, `amm.status` is set away from `Uninitialized` [9](#0-8) , and any subsequent legitimate `Initialize2` for that same market seed fails the `AlreadyInUse` guard [1](#0-0) . The initial liquidity ratio (and thus the pool price) is set entirely by the caller's chosen `init_pc_amount`/`init_coin_amount`, transferred from the caller's own token accounts [3](#0-2) , with the LP amount computed as `sqrt(pc_amount * coin_amount) - 10^decimals` and minted to the caller [10](#0-9) .

This mirrors the reported bug class exactly: pool/pair creation is permissionless and keyed on a discoverable/predictable identifier (there, the `WAVAX/token` pair; here, the `market` seed account), so a griefer can win the race to create the pool first, denying the intended creator the ability to ever initialize that specific PDA, and can additionally choose an unfavorable initial price since there is no reference-price or slippage check tying the initial deposit ratio to any canonical value.

### Impact Explanation
A griefer (or MEV searcher watching the mempool for a pending `Initialize2` transaction, or one who simply predicts/observes the target's chosen market seed account off-chain) can:
1. Submit their own `Initialize2` transaction first with minimal `init_pc_amount`/`init_coin_amount` at an arbitrary, self-serving ratio, occupying the deterministic `amm`/`target_orders`/vault/`lp_mint` PDAs for that market seed.
2. Permanently deny the legitimate project team from ever initializing their intended pool at that market address (`AlreadyInUse`), forcing them to use a different (unintended) market seed and abandon liquidity/marketing plans tied to the original address, or
3. Seize the initial LP-minting privilege and set a bad price ratio, forcing the legitimate side to either accept the bad price or arbitrage/rebalance it themselves, and capturing the "free" initial LP tokens (LP minted = `sqrt(x*y) - 10^decimals` to the attacker).

This is a Medium/High severity availability and value-leakage issue: it does not always directly move third-party escrowed funds (the attacker must fund the initial deposit), but it denies use of a specific pool address, can lock in an off-market price that must be arbitraged, and lets a griefer harvest LP fees on a pool meant for someone else — analogous to the confirmed Medium-severity Trader Joe finding.

### Likelihood Explanation
`Initialize2` is fully permissionless — reachable from a single transaction with attacker-chosen accounts and data (only the amounts and mints/market are inputs, and market can be "any account" per program docs). No special privilege, leaked key, or off-chain assumption is required; watching for or predicting a target market/pool address before it is initialized is the only precondition, which is realistic for any project announcing a pool launch or reusing a well-known market identifier.

### Recommendation
- Do not treat the deterministic PDA collision as acceptable; require an explicit binding between the caller and the intended pool (e.g., a whitelist/authority check, or a per-creator seed component) so that arbitrary third parties cannot preempt a specific market's pool PDA.
- Alternatively/additionally, enforce a reference-price or minimum-liquidity/slippage check against an oracle or expected ratio in `process_initialize2` so that an attacker cannot set an arbitrary initial price, and consider allowing `Initialize2` to succeed at "attaching" to an already-initialized-but-owned account only by the same recognized issuer/authority.
- Emit and check an authority field (e.g., `amm_owner`/`config_feature::amm_owner`) at initialization time rather than only after creation, so pool creation itself can be gated to prevent unauthorized front-running.

### Proof of Concept
1. Off-chain, observe (or predict) the `market` account pubkey a legitimate project intends to use for `Initialize2` (e.g., an announced or newly created market/seed account).
2. Compute the deterministic `amm`, `target_orders`, `lp_mint`, `coin_vault`, `pc_vault` PDAs via `get_associated_address_and_bump_seed(program_id, market, seed, program_id)` — identical derivation the legitimate transaction would use [7](#0-6) .
3. Submit an `Initialize2` instruction first (front-running/racing the legitimate transaction) as an unprivileged wallet, supplying attacker-owned `user_token_coin`/`user_token_pc` accounts and arbitrary `init_coin_amount`/`init_pc_amount` to set a self-serving price ratio [11](#0-10) .
4. The attacker's transaction succeeds, creating the pool at the deterministic PDA, minting LP tokens to the attacker's wallet [12](#0-11) , and setting `amm.status` to `WaitingTrade`/`SwapOnly` [13](#0-12) .
5. The legitimate project's subsequent `Initialize2` call for the same `market` seed now fails with `AmmError::AlreadyInUse` [1](#0-0) , permanently denying them the intended pool address and leaving the pool initialized at the attacker's chosen (potentially unfavorable) price.
