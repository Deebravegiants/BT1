### Title
Depressed supply index after bad-debt cleanup lets an attacker mint cheap shares and capture `recapitalize` funds - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs)

### Summary
The DODO `adjustPrice` report describes sandwiching an event that re-prices/re-concentrates liquidity: buy into the pool before the liquidity-improving change, extract value after it. XOXNO Lending has a structurally identical pair of permissionless-reachable events: bad-debt socialization writes down a market's supply index (contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs:212-237, pool write-down per docs/reference/formulas.md:386-395), and `recapitalize` then restores token backing **without minting shares and without restoring the index** (docs/reference/invariants.md:477-479). An unprivileged attacker can insert `supply` between the two calls, minting RAY supply shares at the depressed index, and then capture a proportional share of the recapitalizer's donation.

### Finding Description
- `socialize_bad_debt` burns the insolvent account's debt shares and lowers the market `supply_index` by `reduction_ray = floor((total_supply_value − bad_debt) / total_supply_value)` with a floor of `RAY/1000` (docs/reference/formulas.md:390-394). Existing suppliers' share counts are unchanged; each share is simply worth less.
- `recapitalize` pushes tokens into the pool to fill the backing shortfall but deliberately does not mint shares or restore the index (docs/reference/invariants.md:477-479; tests/integration/flows/liquidation.sh:384-390 shows it is callable by any payer, e.g. `CAROL`).
- `supply` mints shares at the *current* index, so supplying between the write-down and the recap mints roughly `index_old/index_new` times more shares per token — up to 1,000× more at the index floor (docs/reference/formulas.md:427).
- After the recap raises cash-per-share back toward parity, the attacker's outsized share position redeems for more than deposited, the excess coming from the recapitalizer's tokens.

Attack sequence (all unprivileged):
1. Governance `ForceSocializeBadDebt` reaches its execution delay (or a `clean_bad_debt` dust-gate opening becomes observable) — execution of a ready operation is in scope.
2. Attacker calls `supply(caller, account_id, spoke_id, assets=[(hub,debt_asset)])` immediately after cleanup executes, while `supply_index` is still depressed.
3. Attacker (or the runbook operator) calls `recapitalize(payer, hub_asset, amount)`; the donated tokens raise the cash backing per share.
4. Attacker calls `withdraw` to redeem the inflated shares for more underlying than deposited.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of funds: the recapitalizer's donation (and, more subtly, part of the write-down protection) is diverted to the attacker's freshly minted shares instead of restoring all pre-cleanup suppliers. This is the same profit mechanism as the DODO finding — a predictable liquidity/value-changing state transition sandwiched by a cheap entry and an exit at restored value.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. The dust-gated `clean_bad_debt` path is permissionless, so a searcher can bundle the cleanup itself with the sandwich, making execution timing controllable rather than speculative. The forced path is a publicly scheduled, delayed governance operation, giving a reliable window. Preconditions (a market with socializable bad debt and an expected recapitalization) are exactly the conditions the protocol's own runbook (docs/reference/runbooks/force-socialize-bad-debt.md) documents as occurring.

Caveats I could not fully verify within the tool-call budget: whether `supply` contains an explicit post-write-down guard (e.g. the "supply fails the backing gate until recapitalization" note at formulas.md:399-401 may mean supply is blocked while under-backed — if that gate rejects new supply before the index write-down settles, the attack window narrows to suppliers who only dodge the loss by withdrawing beforehand, which still socializes a larger share of loss onto non-acting suppliers but reduces profitability), and the exact recapitalize share/index handling in `contracts/pool/src/ops/market.rs`.

### Recommendation
Treat the cleanup→recapitalize transition as a single atomic repricing event:
- Execute recapitalization atomically with (or in the same transaction as) `force_socialize_bad_debt`, or have cleanup itself accept a paired recap deposit.
- Alternatively, block `supply`/`withdraw` in a market whose supply index sits at the write-down floor until recapitalization completes (a same-block guard on cleanup analogous to the report's option 1), so no new shares can be minted at the depressed index.

### Proof of Concept
1. BOB supplies 100k USDC; ALICE's account goes insolvent with ETH-denominated debt (fixture in tests/test-harness/tests/controller/same_market_bad_debt_cleanup_arithmetic.rs:12-30).
2. `force_socialize_bad_debt(account)` executes; ETH market `supply_index` drops per the two-floor formula (asserted at bad_debt_index.rs:174-178).
3. Attacker calls `supply` of X ETH units; shares minted = `X * RAY / supply_index_new`, i.e. up to 1,000× the shares BOB holds per unit.
4. Operator calls `recapitalize(payer, ETH_hub_asset, R)` restoring cash.
5. Attacker calls `withdraw`; redemption value ≈ `attacker_shares / total_shares * (residual + R)` > X, extracting most of R.
