### Title
Interest-index overflow permanently freezes an overlarge high-utilization market - ([File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs](common/src/rates/simulate.rs))

### Summary
An unprivileged borrower can leave a very large debt position at sustained high utilization until `borrowed * borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX`. The next accrual panics before the borrow-index ceiling is reached, and because every affected pool operation accrues first, repayment, withdrawal, liquidation, and index maintenance for that market all remain blocked. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` computes `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` before applying or clamping the next borrow index. [3](#0-2)   
`scaled_to_original` performs fixed-point multiplication of the two RAY values, while `update_borrow_index` only caps the index after another multiplication has already succeeded. [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4)   
Consequently, when total scaled debt remains representable but its value at the grown index no longer fits in `i128`, accrual raises `MathOverflow` instead of clamping the index or bounding the valuation. [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6)   
The same overflow is reachable through `update_indexes` and is inherited by debt and collateral operations because each pool leg loads and syncs the market before executing its state transition. [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8) [10](#0-9) 

### Impact Explanation
This is a permanent freeze of all funds tied to the affected market, not merely a failed user input. [11](#0-10)   
Once the state crosses the RAY-value capacity boundary, suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot rescue the account, and keepers cannot advance the market index. [7](#0-6)   
The existing integration proof demonstrates the state with a 1-billion-token 18-decimal market borrowed at 98% utilization: accrual later fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, the stored borrow index is still below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and both withdrawal and repayment fail with the same error. [12](#0-11) 

### Likelihood Explanation
Reaching the condition requires extreme market scale, high utilization, caps configured near the admitted asset-domain maximum, and sustained interest accumulation. [13](#0-12)   
Those requirements are not impossible protocol inputs: caps are admitted up to `i128::MAX / 10^(27-decimals)`, while user-controlled `supply`, `borrow`, and permissionless `update_indexes` provide the path that creates and triggers the state. [14](#0-13) [15](#0-14)   
The practical likelihood is limited by available token supply and the need for long accrual, but the resulting failure is deterministic once the product crosses the `i128` domain. [16](#0-15) [17](#0-16) 

### Recommendation
Do not calculate `borrowed * new_borrow_index` or `supplied * supply_index` in a representation whose overflow becomes an unrecoverable state boundary. At minimum, clamp the borrow index before debt valuation so `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` engages before `scaled_to_original` can panic, and split accrual so valuation uses the previous committed index until the clamped index is stored. The stronger fix is to bound total scaled supply and debt such that `scaled * MAX_*_INDEX` always fits the RAY domain, or to use an explicitly saturating/index-clamping valuation for accrual while preserving panicking arithmetic for user-provided amounts. Add a regression assertion that repayment, withdrawal, liquidation, and `update_indexes` remain executable at the configured index ceiling and at the largest admitted cap.

### Proof of Concept
1. Configure an 18-decimal market and collateral market with caps lifted to the admitted domain ceiling and maximum-utilization disabled, matching the repository's regression fixture. [18](#0-17) 
2. Supplier Bob calls controller `supply` for `1_000_000_000 * 10^18` units of the market token. [19](#0-18) 
3. Borrower Alice supplies sufficient collateral and calls `borrow` for 98% of that market principal. [20](#0-19) 
4. Advance ledger time and call permissionless `update_indexes` until the debt valuation overflows inside the pre-clamp accrual calculation. [8](#0-7) [21](#0-20) 
5. The harness observes `MATH_OVERFLOW`, proves `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and then confirms both `withdraw` and `repay` fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW`. [17](#0-16)
