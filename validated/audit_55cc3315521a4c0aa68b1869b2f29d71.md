### Title
`swap_debt` and `swap_collateral` enforce `require_hub_active` only on the source leg, letting users open positions in a halted destination hub - (File: contracts/controller/src/strategies/swap_debt.rs, contracts/controller/src/strategies/swap_collateral.rs)

### Summary
The OpenClaw report describes a policy (SSRF allow/deny rules) enforced on some tab-action branches but skipped in the select/close siblings. The same shape exists in the controller's strategy routes: `process_repay_debt_with_collateral` calls `config::require_hub_active` on **both** legs' hubs, but `process_swap_debt` and `process_swap_collateral` call it only on the *source* leg (`existing_debt.hub_id` / `current.hub_id`), never on the destination hub (`new_debt.hub_id` / `new.hub_id`). The downstream spoke-asset flag checks (`require_can_borrow`, `require_can_supply`) validate the per-`(spoke, asset)` listing's `paused`/`frozen`/`is_borrowable`/`is_collateralizable` flags, not the hub-level active flag — otherwise the explicit `require_hub_active` calls in all three strategies would be redundant.

### Finding Description
- `process_swap_debt` checks `config::require_hub_active(env, existing_debt.hub_id)` (the repaid leg) but omits `new_debt.hub_id` (the leg being borrowed). `borrow_into_controller` → `validate_position_entry_gates` → `require_can_borrow` only inspects the spoke-asset listing config. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 
- `process_swap_collateral` checks `config::require_hub_active(env, current.hub_id)` but omits `new.hub_id`; `require_can_supply` again only checks the listing's `is_collateralizable` and halted flags, and `process_deposit` re-runs the same spoke-asset-level gates. [3](#0-2) 
- The sibling strategy shows the intended policy: `process_repay_debt_with_collateral` calls `require_hub_active` on both `collateral.hub_id` and `debt.hub_id`, even though its downstream leg functions already enforce spoke-asset flags. [4](#0-3) 

So the hub-halt policy is enforced on the borrow/exit leg of these routes but bypassed on the destination leg — directly parallel to the SSRF policy being enforced on some tab routes and bypassed on select/close.

### Impact Explanation
When governance deactivates a hub (e.g., in response to a market incident), a single unprivileged account owner/delegate can still:
- `swap_debt(account_id, existing_debt = <active hub market>, new_debt_amount = X, new_debt = <halted hub market>, swap)` — mint fresh debt in the halted hub, drawing pool liquidity the halt was meant to freeze.
- `swap_collateral(account_id, current = <active hub market>, from_amount = X, new = <halted hub market>, swap)` — acquire a new collateral position booked against the halted hub.

Since the borrowed funds are physically paid out of the shared pool, re-entering a halted hub weakens the emergency brake and can expose the protocol to the very risk the halt was meant to stop (e.g., borrowing an asset whose market was frozen because of pricing or liquidity concerns). This qualifies as a policy-bypass weakening of protocol protection reachable entirely by an unprivileged caller through listed entrypoints.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a hub to be deactivated while its spoke-asset listings still allow borrow/supply flags — exactly the configuration where the bypass matters. No privileged role, timing, or oracle condition is needed; one transaction through `swap_debt` or `swap_collateral` suffices.

### Recommendation
Add `config::require_hub_active(env, new_debt.hub_id)` in `process_swap_debt` and `config::require_hub_active(env, new.hub_id)` in `process_swap_collateral`, mirroring the dual-hub check in `process_repay_debt_with_collateral`. Audit `multiply`, `flash_position`, and `migrate_from_blend` for the same one-sided hub check.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance deactivates hub `H2`; spoke listing for asset `D` in the caller's spoke remains `is_borrowable`, unpaused, unfrozen.
2. Attacker (account owner) calls `swap_debt` with `existing_debt` = any market in active hub `H1` where they hold debt, `new_debt` = `(H2, D)`.
3. `require_hub_active` passes on `H1`; `require_can_borrow` passes on the `(spoke, D)` listing since it checks spoke-asset flags, not hub state.
4. `borrow_into_controller` mints new `D` debt in halted hub `H2` and the pool pays out `D`, completing the refinance the hub halt was intended to block.
