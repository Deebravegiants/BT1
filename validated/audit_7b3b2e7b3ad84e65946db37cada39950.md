### Title
Unaccounted ETH drain from `EmporiumUpgradeable` when `address(0)` is omitted from `circomData.erc20TokenAddresses` - (File: contracts/external-actions/emporium/upgradeable/EmporiumUpgradeable.sol)

### Summary
`EmporiumUpgradeable.runAction`'s balance-reconciliation loop, `BalanceChangeShouldBePositive` guard, `handleOut`, and `payRelayFees` all iterate exclusively over `circomData.erc20TokenAddresses`, an array whose contents (including whether `address(0)`/ETH is present at all) are fully attacker-controlled. Any ETH that is resident in the Emporium contract at the time of a call, but whose address is simply left out of that array, is never measured, never reconciled against `deltaAmountChanges`, and can be moved out via an `EmporiumOperation.value` with zero enforcement.

### Finding Description
Equality that should hold: for every asset touched by `stack.ops[].value` calls, `balanceAfter - balanceBefore` must be reconciled against the corresponding `deltaAmountChanges[i]` entry, enforced by [1](#0-0) 

This loop, and the outer reconciliation loop in `Hinkal.transact`, are both keyed strictly by `circomData.erc20TokenAddresses`: [2](#0-1) 

`erc20TokenAddresses` is attacker-supplied calldata; the only Solidity-side constraint on it is that its length equals `dimensions.tokenNumber`, with no requirement that ETH (`address(0)`) be included: [3](#0-2) 

The zk circuit (`MainEVMCircuit`) is likewise agnostic to whether `address(0)` is one of the `erc20TokenAddresses` entries — it just processes whatever `tokenCount` addresses/amounts are supplied as public/private inputs, with no invariant tying it to ETH accounting: [4](#0-3) 

In `runAction`, the ops loop executes stateless calls with `op.value` taken directly from the Emporium contract's own ETH balance, controlled entirely by `stack` (decoded from attacker-supplied `externalActionMetadata`), with `signerAddress == address(0)` requiring no signature at all: [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

Attack: craft `circomData.erc20TokenAddresses` (using an already-registered, normal verifier for some `tokenNumber ≥ 1`, e.g. one real ERC-20) that simply omits `address(0)`. Craft `stack.ops` with `signerAddress = address(0)` and one operation whose `endpoint`/`callData` sends ETH (`op.value = X`) to an attacker-controlled address, funded from Emporium's on-chain ETH balance (`receive()` accepts arbitrary ETH, and any prior dust/residue sits there). Because `address(0)` is absent from `erc20TokenAddresses`, neither `balancesBefore`/`balancesAfter` arrays in `runAction` nor the `Hinkal.transact` reconciliation loop ever look at ETH, so:
- `BalanceChangeShouldBePositive` never fires for ETH,
- `handleOut`/UTXO creation never accounts for the ETH movement,
- `payRelayFees` never touches ETH either,
- the outer `slippageValues` check in `Hinkal.transact` is likewise never evaluated for ETH.

The ETH leaves the contract with the equality `sum(ETH sent by ops) == 0` (no `deltaAmountChanges` entry exists to authorize or record it) being silently violated.

### Impact Explanation
Any ETH balance resident in the `EmporiumUpgradeable` contract — from direct `receive()` transfers, rounding/residue from relay-fee math, or any other source not already withdrawn — can be permanently drained by any unprivileged caller who is on the `onlyAllowedRecipient` allow-list path (i.e., any user going through `Hinkal.transact`), with zero on-chain accounting or slippage/balance check preventing it. This is repeatable for as long as ETH accumulates in the contract, and requires no owner/relay/admin privilege. Because the stolen value is not necessarily the attacker's own declared UTXO/deposit but arbitrary residual contract balance (which may represent other users' or the protocol's leftover value), this constitutes theft of protocol/relay-adjacent funds resident in a Hinkal-controlled contract, matching the High severity bar ("theft ... of protocol/relay fees", "executing calls ... a wallet owner or prover never authorised").

### Likelihood Explanation
Preconditions: (1) a registered verifier already exists for some `tokenNumber ≥ 1` excluding ETH (a common, normal deployment case — e.g., pure ERC-20 flows), (2) `EmporiumUpgradeable` holds some ETH balance at call time. Attacker cost is a single valid transaction and proof for a token set that never had to include ETH; no special/degenerate zero-token verifier is required. This makes the bypass practical wherever the protocol operates with non-ETH token verifiers and any ETH balance sits idle in the Emporium contract (e.g., accumulated dust from imprecise relay-fee division, or ETH sent directly via `receive()`), which is realistic over the contract's operating lifetime.

### Recommendation
Do not key balance reconciliation solely off `circomData.erc20TokenAddresses`. Either (a) forbid any `op.value > 0` unless `address(0)` is present in `erc20TokenAddresses` for that transaction, or (b) independently track and reconcile the contract's native ETH balance change against total `op.value` spent regardless of whether ETH is a "declared" token, reverting via `BalanceChangeShouldBePositive` semantics if unaccounted ETH leaves the contract.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry test plan:
1. Deploy `EmporiumUpgradeable`, a mock `Hinkal`/verifier registered for `tokenNumber = 1` (one ERC-20, no ETH), and fund the Emporium contract with ETH via its `receive()` (simulating residue/dust).
2. Craft `CircomData` with `erc20TokenAddresses = [erc20Token]` (no `address(0)`), `amountChanges = [0]`, matching `deltaAmountChanges` computed by `Hinkal._externalTransact`.
3. Encode `EmporiumStack` with `signerAddress = address(0)` and one `EmporiumOperation{ endpoint: attackerEOA, invokeWallet: false, value: X, callData: "" }` where `X` ≤ Emporium's ETH balance.
4. Call `Hinkal.transact` with a valid proof for the registered `tokenNumber=1` circuit.
5. Assert: `attackerEOA.balance` increased by `X`; Emporium's ETH balance decreased by `X`; no revert occurred (`BalanceChangeShouldBePositive` never triggered); left side `sum(ETH sent by ops) = X` vs right side `authorized by deltaAmountChanges for ETH = 0` (no entry exists) — confirming the broken equality.
