### Title
Utilization and backing guards run at claim time and can freeze already-accrued pool revenue - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/revenue.rs)

### Summary
Analogous to M-03 (a solver-activity check enforced at settlement time rather than invoice-creation time freezes legitimately earned funds), the pool's `claim_revenue` enforces `require_utilization_below_max` and `require_supply_for_debt` **after** burning claimable revenue shares and **unconditionally** — even though the revenue being claimed was accrued while the market was healthy. When the market's utilization sits above `max_utilization`, or total floored supply is zero with outstanding debt, the claim reverts and protocol revenue (unclaimed yield) cannot be paid out.

### Finding Description
In `contracts/pool/src/ops/revenue.rs`, `accounting()` loads the synced market, calls `cache.burn_claimable_revenue()`, and then runs both guards before debiting cash:

```rust
let net_transfer = cache.burn_claimable_revenue();

guards::require_utilization_below_max(env, &cache);
guards::require_supply_for_debt(env, &cache);
cache.debit_cash(net_transfer);
``` [1](#0-0) 

The revenue shares were minted during past accruals (`interest.rs`, revenue included in total supply per `docs/reference/formulas.md` lines 78-80). Their validity was never conditioned on utilization at mint time — the same class flaw as checking `_isSolverActive` at `settleInvoice` rather than at creation. The utilization gate divides ceiled debt value by floored supply value (`docs/reference/formulas.md` lines 96-100), so it can fail purely because of market state that changed *after* the revenue was earned:

- A liquidator's collateral withdrawal skips the utilization check ("liquidation withdrawal skips it", `formulas.md` line 98), so an unprivileged liquidator can push utilization above the cap.
- Withdrawal of supply (also guarded, but liquidity can drain via `seize_positions` reclassification or bad-debt write-down) can shrink floored supply.
- `require_supply_for_debt` fails when floored supply value is zero with outstanding debt — reachable after `clean_bad_debt` writes down the supply index.

The controller-side entrypoint `claim_revenue` is permissionless (`require_authorized_caller` only — any address can invoke it, `contracts/controller/src/markets.rs` lines 129-138), so there is no alternate privileged path that bypasses the guard either; the pool claim itself reverts.

### Impact Explanation
Accrued protocol revenue becomes unclaimable while the market is over-utilized or under-backed. This is temporary freezing of unclaimed yield: revenue shares remain minted but cannot be converted to cash until borrowers repay or suppliers add liquidity — an action no single actor may be willing or able to take at scale. Unlike M-03 the freeze is not strictly permanent (repayment eventually restores the claim), matching the "temporary freezing of funds / freezing of unclaimed yield" impact class.

### Likelihood Explanation
- Over-utilization is reachable by unprivileged actors: liquidation withdrawals bypass the utilization gate, and `clean_bad_debt` / supply-index write-downs shrink floored supply without moving debt.
- The condition self-perpetuates in distressed markets — precisely when revenue claims matter most — because the same insolvency that blocks `require_supply_for_debt` also discourages new supply.
- Mitigating factor: the state is recoverable through ordinary repayments/recapitalization, and a zero-payout claim is documented to still run the guards (`pool/README.md` lines 342-346), suggesting partial awareness — but the guard also blocks *positive* payouts, which is the harmful case.

### Recommendation
Skip the utilization/backing guards when `net_transfer == 0`, and more fundamentally gate claims on the claim's own effect rather than absolute market state: a revenue claim *reduces* supply-side liabilities, so permitting it when `burn_claimable_revenue` yields a positive payout cannot worsen utilization (debt is unchanged, cash decreases but utilization is debt/supply). At minimum, only enforce `require_supply_for_debt`/`require_utilization_below_max` when the claim would worsen the metric, mirroring the report's fix of letting pending obligations settle before enforcing the stricter state.

### Proof of Concept
1. Bootstrap a market; borrowers draw debt so interest accrues and `get_revenue` is positive.
2. Drive utilization above `max_utilization` via unprivileged means — e.g., a liquidation whose collateral `withdraw` skips the utilization gate, or a `clean_bad_debt` supply-index write-down that shrinks floored supply while debt persists.
3. Any caller invokes controller `claim_revenue(assets)` → `pool_claim_revenue_call` → `ops::revenue::accounting` → `require_utilization_below_max` (or `require_supply_for_debt`) panics with `UtilizationAboveMax` (127) / `PoolInsolvent` (123), reverting the claim despite positive accrued revenue.
4. Revenue remains frozen until a third party repays debt or supplies — freezing of unclaimed yield caused by a guard evaluated at claim time rather than at accrual time.

pub(crate) fn accounting(env: &Env, hub_asset: HubAssetKey) -> RevenueOutcome {
    let mut cache = ops::renewed_market(env, &hub_asset);

    let net_transfer = cache.burn_claimable_revenue();

    guards::require_utilization_below_max(env, &cache);
    guards::require_supply_for_debt(env, &cache);
    cache.debit_cash(net_transfer);

    cache.commit();
