### Verdict

No vulnerability found for this question.

### Reasoning

The question's core premise — that "nothing in `CircomDataBuilder` or `MainEVMCircuit` constrains `utxoIndex` or the resulting per-UTXO offset" — does not hold up under tracing.

**1. The offset is a deterministic function of already-bound fields.**
`utxo.timeStamp = circomData.timeStamp + utxoIndex` in `DepositOnChainUtxosExternalAction.runAction` is computed purely from `circomData.timeStamp` and the loop position derived from `utxoAmounts` (decoded from `circomData.externalActionData.externalActionMetadata`). [1](#0-0) 

Both inputs to that deterministic computation are cryptographically bound before the action ever runs:
- `circomData.timeStamp` is directly hashed into `getSignedMessageHash`'s `hash1` and placed as a distinct public-input slot in `formBasicInput`. [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 
- `circomData.externalActionData` (which contains `externalActionMetadata`, i.e., the exact `utxoAmounts` array whose shape determines every `utxoIndex`) is hashed into `getHashedCalldata1`, which feeds `getHashedCalldata`. [4](#0-3) 

`performHinkalChecks` requires `CircomDataBuilder.getHashedCalldata(circomData) == circomData.calldataHash` before the external action even executes, and `calldataHash` is itself part of `getSignedMessageHash` and a public-input slot. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) 

So any attempt to alter the resulting per-UTXO offsets (by changing the metadata/array structure) changes `getHashedCalldata`, which breaks the `calldataHash` equality check and the signature over `signedMessageHash`. The offset is not "unconstrained" — it is fully determined by data that is checked and signed before `runAction` executes.

**2. On-chain commitments were never circuit-verified in the first place.** `createOnchainCommitment`'s `hash4(amount, erc20Address, stealthAddress, timeStamp)` is computed in Solidity and inserted directly; the circuit's `MainEVMCircuit`/`OriginalCommitmentCalculator` only constrains *input* (spent) and *off-chain output* commitments via `outTimeStamp` (which maps to the single `circomData.timeStamp` public-input slot), not on-chain-created UTXOs. [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8) 
Their integrity instead comes from the balance-diff `require` in `Hinkal._externalTransact`/main transact loop, which is untouched by this issue. [10](#0-9) 

**3. Even a hypothetical commitment collision would not enable theft/double-spend.** Nullifiers are computed as `f(commitment, signature)` where `signature = f(nullifyingPrivateKey, commitment)`, and the circuit's `SignatureVerifier`/`calcInPublicKeys` recompute the stealth-address-derived `publicKey` from the prover's own private key material. [11](#0-10) 
An attacker without the victim's `nullifyingPrivateKey` cannot produce a valid nullifier for a colliding leaf regardless of duplicate commitment values, and `nullifiers` is checked/marked independent of tree position, so no double-spend or leaf "overwrite" results even in the described scenario.

The scenario also requires the attacker to predict a victim's exact future `stealthAddress` (an ECDH-derived value tied to victim's private key) plus exact amount/erc20/timestamp — a precondition the question itself frames as "victim's future stealth address reuse," i.e., a user-side address-reuse assumption rather than a flaw exploitable purely through this contract's logic.

Given that (a) the claimed "unconstrained" offset is in fact bound via `calldataHash`/`signedMessageHash`, and (b) even a leaf collision would not translate into fund theft or a spendable double-nullifier due to the private-key-bound signature/nullifier scheme, the equality the question claims is broken is not actually broken.
