### Title
Manipulable Aquarius LP liquidity gates liquidation and risk settlement - ([File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs](contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs))

### Summary
An Aquarius LP collateral price becomes unavailable whenever the referenced AMM pool's total value falls below `min_pool_value_wad` [1](#0-0) . Because every collateral and debt token held by an account is priced when calculating its account-wide health factor, an LP holder can supply a small amount of LP collateral, retain or acquire enough external LP shares, and withdraw liquidity from the Aquarius pool to deliberately break the price [2](#0-1) . Liquidation cannot bypass that collateral leg because `liquidate` first builds an account-wide plan and requires a strictly computed HF below 1 [3](#0-2) .

### Finding Description
The Aquarius provider derives an LP share price from current pool reserves and total shares, then rejects the observation when total pool value is less than `lp.min_pool_value_wad` [4](#0-3) . The controller requests strict prices for every distinct asset across the account's supply and debt positions [5](#0-4) . `prices` panics if any requested key cannot resolve to a usable price [6](#0-5) , and the controller similarly panics if the returned map lacks any requested token [7](#0-6) .

`liquidate` calls `build_liquidation_plan`, which values the complete `supply_positions` and `borrow_positions` maps before admitting the account [8](#0-7) . A failing price therefore aborts the entire transaction, not merely the affected collateral leg [3](#0-2) . An attacker can intentionally create that failing price through ordinary Aquarius liquidity withdrawal, making the outage economically self-inflicted rather than dependent on oracle-operator failure.

### Impact Explanation
While the LP pool remains below the configured minimum value, every liquidation attempt against the indebted account fails during account-wide valuation [3](#0-2) . The borrower can keep an unsafe position unliquidated while interest accrues, preventing lenders and liquidators from recovering collateral through the normal liquidation path. The attacker can later restore the Aquarius pool value and resume normal operation, so this is at minimum a temporary freezing of liquidation access and can materially increase the protocol's exposure to bad debt.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires an account already holding the listed LP token as a supply position and enough control over the underlying Aquarius pool's liquidity to bring total pool value below `min_pool_value_wad` [1](#0-0) . Supplying LP collateral uses the permissionless owner path: `supply(caller, account_id, spoke_id, assets)` creates or updates the caller's account and records measured LP-token receipts [9](#0-8) . No privileged role or leaked key is needed. Likelihood is bounded by the liquidity requirement and the cost of borrowing or holding LP shares, but the attacker can choose a thin eligible pool and a very small collateral position.

### Recommendation
Do not make the entire account valuation depend on the availability of every collateral price. For liquidation admission and sizing, isolate an unusable collateral price to that collateral leg: treat it as unavailable or zero for collateral valuation while continuing to price debt, or permit liquidation of independently priceable collateral under an explicitly bounded policy. Separately, consider adding a conservative LP valuation fallback only if it cannot overvalue collateral; otherwise retaining strict failure is safer than inventing a price.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance has listed an Aquarius LP token as collateral, and its oracle configuration references an Aquarius pool with `min_pool_value_wad > 0`.
2. Attacker obtains the LP token and calls:
   - `supply(attacker, 0, spoke_id, [(HubAssetKey { hub_id, asset: lp_token }, dust_amount)])`
   - This creates an attacker-owned account containing the LP supply position [10](#0-9) .
3. Attacker supplies other collateral and calls `borrow(attacker, account_id, [(debt_key, amount)], None)`, creating debt close to the liquidation boundary.
4. Outside the controller, the attacker withdraws their liquidity from the referenced Aquarius pool until the LP share's calculated `pool_value_wad < min_pool_value_wad`.
5. A liquidator calls:
   - `liquidate(liquidator, account_id, [(debt_key, payment)], SeizeMode::Transfer)`
   - The plan calls `calculate_account_risk_totals` over every supply and borrow position [3](#0-2) .
   - `load_markets` requests prices for all distinct account assets [11](#0-10) .
   - The Aquarius provider returns `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` [1](#0-0) , and strict `prices` resolution panics [6](#0-5) .
6. The liquidation reverts even if all other collateral and debt markets have usable prices. Repeating the call remains unsuccessful until someone adds enough liquidity/value back to the Aquarius pool.

pub(crate) fn fetch_prices(env: &Env, assets: &Vec<Address>) -> Map<Address, PriceFeedRaw> {

    let keyed = PriceAggregatorClient::new(env, &aggregator).prices(&token_keys(env, assets));
    let mut out = Map::new(env);
    for asset in assets.iter() {
        match keyed.get(PriceKey::Token(asset.clone())) {
            Some(feed) => out.set(asset, feed),
            None => panic_with_error!(env, OracleError::OracleNotConfigured),
        }
    }
