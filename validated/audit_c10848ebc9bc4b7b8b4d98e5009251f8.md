### Title
Relayer fee rewards can be permanently lost if `Fees` is zeroed before destination-chain delivery and the beneficiary is denylisted by the fee token - (File: `modules/pallets/relayer/src/withdrawal.rs`)

### Summary
The relayer fee-withdrawal flow zeroes a relayer's accrued `Fees` balance on Hyperbridge as soon as the withdrawal extrinsic dispatches the ISMP request, before the destination chain ever confirms the token transfer. If the destination `feeToken` is a denylist-capable token (e.g. USDC) and the chosen beneficiary is or becomes blocklisted, the destination-side `safeTransfer` in `EvmHost.withdraw` will always revert, the request can never be successfully delivered, and the relayer's `Fees` entry — already zeroed — can never be reclaimed or reassigned to a different beneficiary. This mirrors the reported ArrakisV2 pattern where a blacklistable transfer target permanently locks accrued rewards, but here the loss is guaranteed by the ledger being cleared optimistically before settlement.

### Finding Description
`Pallet::withdraw` in `modules/pallets/relayer/src/withdrawal.rs` lets any relayer with a signed payload request payout of their accumulated fee to a `beneficiary` address of their choosing (or their own key by default) [1](#0-0) . It builds a `WithdrawalParams { beneficiary_address, amount, token }` ABI-encoded body and dispatches an ISMP POST request addressed to the destination's `host_manager` [2](#0-1) . Immediately after dispatch — with no confirmation that the request was ever delivered or executed on the destination — the pallet zeroes the relayer's fee balance: [3](#0-2) 

On the destination EVM chain, `HostManager.onAccept` decodes the `Withdraw` action and calls `IHostManager(_params.host).withdraw(withdrawParams)` [4](#0-3) . `EvmHost.withdraw` then performs `IERC20(params.token).safeTransfer(params.beneficiary, params.amount)` for ERC-20 fee tokens [5](#0-4) . If `params.beneficiary` is blocklisted by the fee token contract (analogous to USDC's blacklist), this transfer reverts unconditionally and permanently for that address.

Per the documented delivery semantics, a failing `onAccept` call causes the host to delete the receipt and leave the message "deliverable" for retry rather than reverting the whole batch [6](#0-5) . Because the request body (and its blacklisted beneficiary) is fixed once dispatched and the `DispatchPost.timeout` is `0` (no timeout) [7](#0-6) , every retry of the same request will fail identically forever — the transfer can never succeed.

Crucially, the relayer cannot recover by submitting a new withdrawal with a different, non-blacklisted beneficiary: `Fees::<T>::get` for that `(dest_chain, address)` pair is already zero (line 177 ran before any destination confirmation), so a follow-up call fails the minimum-balance check in `Pallet::withdraw` [8](#0-7)  with `Error::<T>::NotEnoughBalance`.

### Impact Explanation
Accrued relayer rewards become permanently and unrecoverably frozen the moment a withdrawal is initiated to a beneficiary the fee token subsequently denies (or already denies) — with no admin or protocol path to reissue the payout to a different address, since the source-chain accounting is cleared optimistically at dispatch time rather than at confirmed settlement. This is a stronger variant of the referenced bug class: not only is the transfer permanently blocked, but the underlying balance record that would let a manager/relayer retry with a corrected beneficiary is itself already destroyed.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any relayer can trigger this by supplying an arbitrary `beneficiary` in `WithdrawalInputData` [9](#0-8) ; the condition requires only that the destination `feeToken` be a denylist-capable token (explicitly anticipated, since `feeToken` is documented as "typically DAI" but configurable, and stable-coin-style fee tokens like USDC are plausible fee tokens on EVM destinations) [10](#0-9) . No malicious governance or admin action is required — accidental blacklisting (sanctions screening, compliance action against the relayer's own address) is sufficient to trigger permanent loss.

### Recommendation
Do not zero `Fees::<T>` optimistically before the destination transfer is confirmed. Either (a) settle accounting only after a delivery-success acknowledgment is received back on Hyperbridge, or (b) keep a retry path that lets the relayer redirect an undelivered withdrawal to a new beneficiary (e.g., track withdrawal state keyed by nonce rather than clearing the balance immediately), so a denylisted beneficiary does not permanently destroy the underlying reward record.

### Proof of Concept
1. Relayer `R` accrues fees for EVM destination chain `D` whose `feeToken` is a denylist-capable stablecoin.
2. `R` calls the withdrawal extrinsic with `beneficiary = R`'s own address (or any address), signed per `message()` [11](#0-10) .
3. `Pallet::withdraw` dispatches the ISMP POST to `D`'s `host_manager` and immediately zeroes `Fees::<T>[D][R]` [3](#0-2) .
4. Before or concurrently, the fee token issuer blocklists `R`'s address.
5. On delivery, `EvmHost.withdraw` calls `safeTransfer(R, amount)`, which reverts every time it is retried [5](#0-4) .
6. `R` calls the withdrawal extrinsic again with a fresh, non-blocklisted beneficiary, but `Fees::<T>[D][R]` is already `0`, so the call fails `NotEnoughBalance` [8](#0-7) . The reward is permanently lost.
