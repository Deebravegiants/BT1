### Title
Cross-chain calldata replay via account-nonce reset in `pallet-hyper-fungible-token` - (File: modules/pallets/hyper-fungible-token/src/module.rs)

### Summary
`Pallet::<T>::on_accept` in the `hyper-fungible-token` ISMP module lets a bridged token transfer carry an optional signed `runtime_call` that is dispatched on the beneficiary's behalf. Replay protection for this embedded call is implemented by comparing the signature against the beneficiary's **`frame_system` account nonce**, not against any per-message or per-request identifier. Because `frame_system` account nonces reset to `0` whenever an account is reaped (balance falls below the Existential Deposit and it is killed), a signed `(nonce, runtime_call)` payload that was valid once can become valid again after the beneficiary account is reaped and later recreated, allowing an unprivileged relayer/bridger to replay a previously observed authorization.

### Finding Description
In `on_accept` [1](#0-0) , when the bridged message carries `data`, the pallet decodes a `SubstrateCalldata { runtime_call, signature }` and, if a signature is present, fetches the beneficiary's **current on-chain account nonce** via `frame_system::Pallet::<T>::account_nonce(beneficiary.clone())` and uses `(nonce, runtime_call)` as the signed payload to verify against Ed25519/Sr25519/ECDSA signatures [2](#0-1) . After a successful dispatch, the pallet increments this nonce with `frame_system::Pallet::<T>::inc_account_nonce(origin)` [3](#0-2) .

This design conflates two unrelated concepts:
- `frame_system`'s account nonce is meant to order *locally signed extrinsics* from that account and is reset to `0` whenever the account is reaped (its `AccountInfo` is deleted once free balance drops below the `ExistentialDeposit` and no providers/consumers remain), which is standard, expected Substrate behavior.
- Here it is repurposed as a message-authorization nonce for an entirely separate channel (cross-chain calldata execution), with no binding to the specific ISMP request/commitment, chain ID, or a monotonically-increasing pallet-local counter that would survive reaping.

Since the beneficiary is a plain user account (its address is taken directly from the bridged message's `to`/`from` field [4](#0-3) ), and the pallet performs a normal `transfer`/`mint_into` to that account with `ExistenceRequirement::AllowDeath`/`Preservation::Expendable` [5](#0-4) , the account is fully capable of falling below the Existential Deposit and being reaped by ordinary user activity (e.g. spending/transferring out the bridged funds) after the pallet has already incremented its nonce once. Once reaped, `account_nonce` reverts to `0` in storage.

An attacker (any party able to submit an ISMP `PostRequest` targeted at this module — i.e. an unprivileged bridger/relayer initiating a transfer to the same beneficiary) can capture a previously observed, publicly visible `(nonce, runtime_call, signature)` triple from an old bridged message body (message bodies and their commitments are public), and embed the exact same `data` bytes in a brand-new bridge transfer to the same beneficiary. Because `handle_incoming_message`'s duplicate-request protection operates on the ISMP `Request` commitment (source, dest, **nonce**, from, to, timeout, body) [6](#0-5) , a new request with a different source-chain nonce produces a different commitment/receipt and is not treated as a duplicate at the ISMP layer, even though the embedded `SubstrateCalldata` payload and its signature are byte-for-byte identical. If the beneficiary's `frame_system` nonce has reset to the value the old signature was signed over, the stale signature verifies again and the captured `runtime_call` is re-dispatched with `RawOrigin::Signed(beneficiary)` [7](#0-6) .

### Impact Explanation
Successful replay causes an arbitrary previously-authorized `runtime_call` (filtered only by `BaseCallFilter`, not the original bridging transaction's intent) to be re-executed under the beneficiary's own signed origin without fresh consent. Depending on what the original call was (e.g. a `transfer`, an approval, a governance vote, staking action, or any other extrinsic the base call filter allows), this can lead to unauthorized asset movement or unauthorized state-changing actions being taken on the victim's behalf — a concrete unauthorized-app-action / fund-theft vector reachable purely through unprivileged bridge message submission.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires: (1) a user who has, at least once, used the signed-calldata feature to authorize a `runtime_call` via a bridge transfer, (2) the beneficiary account subsequently being reaped (falling below Existential Deposit — plausible for many light users who bridge and then spend/transfer the received funds elsewhere), and (3) an attacker able to observe the publicly emitted `data` bytes and resubmit a new bridge transfer targeting the same beneficiary with the same `data`. All of these are realistic, unprivileged, and require no special access — any relayer or bridger can trigger step 3, and account reaping is a routine occurrence on Substrate chains for accounts near the Existential Deposit. This mirrors exactly the reported bug class (nonce reset after balance drop below ED enabling signature replay).

### Recommendation
Do not reuse `frame_system`'s account nonce as a cross-chain authorization nonce. Instead:
- Maintain a dedicated, monotonically increasing nonce/counter per beneficiary inside `pallet-hyper-fungible-token` storage that is never reset by account reaping, and use it as the signed payload's replay-protection value.
- Bind the signed payload to the specific ISMP request (e.g. include the request commitment, source/dest chain identifiers, and a domain separator) so a captured signature cannot be replayed via a different, freshly constructed bridge message.
- Alternatively, track and reject already-consumed `(beneficiary, nonce)` or full-payload-hash pairs explicitly in pallet storage, independent of `frame_system::Account`.

### Proof of Concept
1. User `B` bridges tokens from an EVM chain to the Substrate chain via `HyperFungibleToken`, including `message.data` = `SubstrateCalldata { runtime_call: C, signature: sig }`, where `sig` is B's Sr25519 signature over `(nonce=N, C)`, with `N` equal to B's current `frame_system` account nonce (initially `0` for a fresh account).
2. `on_accept` mints/transfers tokens to `B`, verifies `sig` against `(N, C)`, dispatches `C` as `B`, then calls `inc_account_nonce(B)`, advancing B's nonce to `1`.
3. `B` later transfers out enough of the received balance that B's account balance drops below `ExistentialDeposit`; `pallet_balances`/`frame_system` reaps the account, resetting B's stored nonce back to `0`.
4. An attacker observes the historical bridge message body (public on-chain data) containing `(N=0, C, sig)`, and submits a brand-new bridge transfer (any amount) to `B` on the EVM source chain, copying the exact same `data` bytes into the new message.
5. The resulting new `PostRequest` has a fresh source-chain `nonce`/commitment and passes ISMP's duplicate-request check. `on_accept` runs again, reads B's current account nonce (`0`, matching the captured signature's `N`), signature verification succeeds, and `runtime_call C` is dispatched again as `B` — replaying an action B only authorized once.
