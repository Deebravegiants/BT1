## Title
Front-runnable pool creation permanently freezes a market's AMM pool address via deterministic PDA derivation - (File: `program/src/processor.rs`)

### Summary
The `Registry`/`SafeGuardFactory` bug class is: a deterministic, precomputable address can be "claimed" by an attacker before the legitimate caller finishes initialization, causing all future legitimate initialization attempts to permanently fail. Raydium's `process_initialize2` has the same structural weakness: the AMM pool account, its coin/pc vaults, and its LP mint are all derived deterministically from `(program_id, market pubkey, fixed seed)`, and the `market` account passed in is **not validated to be a genuine Serum/OpenBook market** — it is explicitly documented as "Just a seed for AMM account. Can be any account." An attacker who observes (or simply guesses) the market pubkey a legitimate pool creator intends to use can front-run `Initialize2` with attacker-controlled dummy mints and dust liquidity, permanently occupying the deterministic addresses tied to that market and freezing legitimate pool creation for it.

### Finding Description
In `process_initialize2`, the AMM info account, coin vault, and pc vault addresses are computed with `get_associated_address_and_bump_seed`, which derives a PDA solely from `program_id`, the `market_account` key, and a fixed seed constant (`AMM_ASSOCIATED_SEED`, `COIN_VAULT_ASSOCIATED_SEED`, `PC_VAULT_ASSOCIATED_SEED`): [1](#0-0) 

These derivations are invoked when creating the coin vault, pc vault, and AMM account: [2](#0-1) 

Crucially, the `market_info` account supplied to `Initialize2` is not verified to be an actual Serum/OpenBook market — the code comments explicitly state it can be "any account": [3](#0-2) 

Once any account is used to compute the deterministic addresses and `generate_amm_associated_account`/`generate_amm_associated_spl_token` succeed once (i.e., the associated addresses' owner is the system program), the accounts become owned by the AMM program / token program. Any subsequent attempt to reuse the same `market_info` to initialize the "real" pool fails, because the helper explicitly rejects re-creation: [4](#0-3) 

Additionally, even if account creation were somehow skipped, the `AmmInfo` status check independently blocks re-initialization once the account is non-`Uninitialized`: [5](#0-4) 

The only requirements to win this race are: a valid, owned `amm_coin_mint`/`amm_pc_mint` pair (attacker can create their own SPL mints), a small non-zero deposit amount (`init_coin_amount`/`init_pc_amount` just need to be nonzero, e.g., 1 unit), the global `amm_config` PDA (public, not attacker-restricted), and payment of the `create_pool_fee` if nonzero. All of this is attacker-affordable and requires no privileged signer.

### Impact Explanation
Because the AMM pool address, LP mint, and vault addresses for a given market are entirely deterministic and computable in advance by anyone who knows (or can predict) the market pubkey, an attacker can front-run the legitimate `Initialize2` transaction for a target market with a garbage/dust pool. This permanently occupies the deterministic addresses for that specific market — the legitimate project can never create the "real" pool at that market through this program, since the AMM/vault/mint addresses are fixed by the market key and are already claimed. This is a permanent freezing of the ability to launch a pool for that market (loss of functionality/availability for the intended pool creator and any liquidity providers who expect the market's canonical pool), directly analogous to the `SafeGuardFactory`/`Registry` front-running freeze in the referenced report.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is high: pool-creation transactions for high-value token launches are frequently visible in the mempool/RPC prior to confirmation (or the target market address can be known in advance from off-chain coordination such as a serum/openbook market listing), and computing the deterministic PDA addresses requires no special access — just `find_program_address` with public constants. The attacker's cost is minimal (one SPL mint creation plus a dust deposit and the pool-creation fee).

### Recommendation
- Validate that `market_info` is an actual, program-owned Serum/OpenBook market account (verify its owner is the expected market program and that it decodes as a valid market), rather than accepting an arbitrary account as documented.
- Consider deriving the AMM address from additional attacker-non-controllable entropy (e.g., requiring the coin/pc mints to also be part of the seed, or tying pool creation to a permissioned/verified market registration step) so that merely knowing a market pubkey is insufficient to preempt legitimate pool creation.
- Optionally, add an authorization mechanism (e.g., only the market's original creator, or a governance-approved allowlist) to gate which market accounts may be used to instantiate a pool for a given market key.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker observes (or independently learns) the Serum/OpenBook `market` pubkey `M` that a legitimate project intends to use for their canonical pool.
2. Attacker computes `amm_pool = find_program_address([program_id, M, AMM_ASSOCIATED_SEED], program_id)` and similarly for `coin_vault`/`pc_vault`/`lp_mint`, exactly as `get_associated_address_and_bump_seed` does. [1](#0-0) 
3. Attacker creates two throwaway SPL mints they control, mints a trivial amount to themselves, and submits `Initialize2` using `market_info = M`, `amm_coin_mint`/`amm_pc_mint` = their throwaway mints, `init_coin_amount = init_pc_amount = 1`, and pays the `create_pool_fee`.
4. The program creates the coin vault, pc vault, and AMM account at the addresses derived from `M` and sets `amm.status` to `WaitingTrade`/`SwapOnly`. [6](#0-5) 
5. When the legitimate project later submits their own `Initialize2` for market `M` with their real mints, the call fails: `generate_amm_associated_spl_token`/`generate_amm_associated_account` return `AmmError::RepeatCreateAmm` (or the `AmmInfo.status` check returns `AmmError::AlreadyInUse`), permanently blocking the creation of the intended pool for market `M`. [7](#0-6) [5](#0-4)
