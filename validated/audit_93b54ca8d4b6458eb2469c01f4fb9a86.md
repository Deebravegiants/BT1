### Title
Tokens transferred directly to the pool are permanently locked — cash accounting ignores the real token balance and there is no rescue path - (File: contracts/pool/src/lib.rs)

### Summary
The pool contract keeps an internal `cash` ledger per market that is deliberately decoupled from the contract's actual SAC token balance. Because every credit/debit goes through `cache.credit_cash`/`debit_cash` and no entrypoint reconciles or sweeps surplus balance, any tokens transferred directly to the pool contract address (outside of a controller-mediated flow) are permanently frozen: they are never booked as cash, never counted as backing, and cannot be withdrawn by anyone, including the owner.

### Finding Description
Anyone can call `token.transfer(pool_address, amount)` on any listed asset's Stellar Asset Contract, increasing the pool's real balance without touching its accounting. The pool's solvency math is entirely book-based:

- `backing_shortfall` computes backing as `cache.cash() + ceiled debt`, not the token balance, so donated tokens do not even reduce a bad-debt shortfall (`contracts/pool/src/guards.rs:61-66`).
- `recapitalize` credits only `min(amount, backing_shortfall)` to the cash book and refunds the rest; it never reads the token balance, so pre-existing surplus balance is invisible to it (`contracts/pool/src/ops/recapitalize.rs:52-57`).
- Withdrawals pay out only up to the internal cash book, so the surplus is never reachable by suppliers either.
- The full public surface (`LiquidityPoolInterface`, `contracts/pool/src/lib.rs:100-326`) contains no `skim`, `sweep`, or `rescue` entrypoint, and the controller likewise exposes none (`contracts/controller/src/lib.rs:89-543`).

This mirrors the Carapace `payable`-functions issue: value can reach the contract through a path the protocol never intended to use, and there is no way to send it back.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds. Any user, integration, or router that pushes tokens directly to the pool contract — a realistic mistake since the pool address is public via `get_pool_address` and direct `token.transfer` is the natural Stellar payment primitive — loses those tokens forever. The funds benefit no one: they are not credited to suppliers, not usable as liquidity, and not recoverable by governance, since no upgrade-free mechanism exists to extract them. (Whether a surplus could additionally break the flash-loan's balance-equality check in `ops/flash.rs` is plausible but was not fully verified.)

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium-low. It requires an accidental or mis-integrated direct transfer rather than deliberate attack, but unlike EVM `payable` annotations there is nothing on the pool address signalling "do not send tokens here," and third-party routers/aggregators interacting with flash loans already hold the pool address. Occasional dust or larger misdirected amounts over the contract's lifetime is realistic, matching the original report's Medium severity.

### Recommendation
Add an owner- or controller-gated `skim`/`rescue` entrypoint that transfers out `token.balance(pool) - total_book_cash` (i.e., only the surplus above all markets' booked cash), or reconcile the surplus into protocol revenue shares during `claim_revenue`/`update_indexes`. At minimum, document that direct transfers are irrecoverable and consider a defensive check in the flash-loan path so a surplus cannot interfere with balance assertions.

### Proof of Concept
1. Owner lists a market for token `T`; users supply/borrow normally, so `cache.cash() = X` and `token.balance(pool) = X`.
2. Any unprivileged address calls `T.transfer(pool, D)` with `D > 0`. Now `token.balance(pool) = X + D` while `cache.cash() = X`.
3. No reachable entrypoint changes this:
   - `withdraw`/`borrow`/`repay`/`claim_revenue`/`recapitalize` all operate on the cash book and indexes only;
   - `backing_shortfall` ignores the real balance, so `D` cannot be applied even to a shortfall;
   - there is no rescue/sweep function on the pool or controller.
4. `D` remains locked in the pool contract permanently unless the contract is upgraded to add an extraction path.

Note on scope: the original `payable` framing maps onto Soroban as direct SAC `token.transfer` to the pool, which is on the allowed reach list. The finding does not depend on admin misconfiguration, upgrades, or oracle honesty.
