### Title
Stale cross-nature oracle anchor inflates collateral and permits undercollateralized borrowing - ([File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs])

### Summary
A dual-source price can blend a fresh market leg with an anchor observation nearly 23 hours old while still returning a valid price. Because the inter-leg age-spread check runs only when both legs have `FeedNature::Market`, a `Fundamental` RedStone anchor can remain valid under its own 86,400-second staleness limit while its timestamp diverges arbitrarily from the market leg. [1](#0-0) 

### Finding Description
The controller’s `borrow` path is reachable by the owner of the borrowing account and evaluates collateral using the aggregated price. [2](#0-1) 

In `blend`, the outcome is stale only when either leg is independently stale, or when both legs are market feeds whose timestamps differ by more than one hour. [3](#0-2) 

The age-spread guard is therefore skipped for a market-nature Reflector TWAP paired with a fundamental RedStone feed. [4](#0-3) 

A source-specific anchor staleness limit as large as 86,400 seconds permits a report that is 82,800 seconds old. [5](#0-4) 

The two accepted legs are then averaged rather than rejected or decomposed into separate risk treatment. [6](#0-5) 

For example, a fresh 0.91 USD market price blended with a 1.00 USD stale anchor produces approximately 0.955 USD, inflating collateral valuation by about 4.95%. [5](#0-4) [7](#0-6) 

### Impact Explanation
An attacker can open or reuse their own account, supply the affected collateral, and call `borrow` for an amount that the true current collateral value would reject. [8](#0-7) 

The resulting excess debt is real token liquidity withdrawn from the pool, while subsequent price normalization can leave the position undercollateralized and socialize the deficit through liquidation or bad-debt handling. [9](#0-8) 

The in-tree reproduction demonstrates a 100,000-unit collateral position, a fresh price of 0.91 USD versus a stale 1.00 USD anchor, and a successful 70,000 USD borrow; the fresh-anchor control rejects the same borrow as insufficient collateral. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10) 

### Likelihood Explanation
The attacker needs only an owned account and collateral in an affected market; no privileged controller or pool call is required. [2](#0-1) 

The trigger is an ordinary market movement that remains inside the configured two-source tolerance while one anchor is merely within its long configured staleness window. [12](#0-11) 

Severity is Medium because profitable impact depends on a dual-source configuration that combines a long anchor staleness window with tolerance wide enough to admit the resulting skew, but the reachable loss is bounded by that tolerance and the market’s liquidation threshold. [13](#0-12) 

### Recommendation
Apply the one-hour inter-leg age-spread check to every dual-source blend, regardless of `FeedNature`, or mark a blend stale whenever the older leg exceeds a tight protocol-level freshness bound. [14](#0-13) 

If fundamental anchors intentionally require longer update windows, validate them against the market leg using a substantially tighter age bound or reject them for collateral valuation while allowing them only in non-risk views. [15](#0-14) 

Also consider using a conservative aggregation such as `min(primary, anchor)` for collateral, rather than midpoint, when the two legs have materially different observation times. [6](#0-5) 

### Proof of Concept
1. Configure collateral `XLM` with a Reflector TWAP primary and a RedStone `Fundamental` anchor having `max_stale_seconds = 86_400`. [16](#0-15) 
2. Initialize both sources near 1.00 USD, then move the fresh market price to 0.91 USD while leaving the anchor at 1.00 USD and timestamping it 82,800 seconds old. [17](#0-16) 
3. Supply 500,000 USD of USDC liquidity from a separate lender account. [18](#0-17) 
4. Supply 100,000 XLM to an attacker-owned account and call `borrow(account_id, borrows=[(hub_id, USDC), amount=70_000 USD], to=None)`. [10](#0-9) [19](#0-18) 
5. The blend reports approximately 0.955 USD and the borrow succeeds; replacing the anchor with a fresh 0.91 USD observation causes the same borrow to reject with insufficient collateral. [11](#0-10)

/// Converts composed `Legs` into an `Outcome`. For two readings, marks the
/// outcome stale when either leg is individually stale, or when both legs
/// are market-nature feeds whose timestamps differ by more than
/// `MAX_LEG_AGE_SPREAD_SECONDS`. Takes the earlier of the two timestamps,
/// flags a deviation when the legs fall outside the oracle's tolerance band, and
/// sets the blended price to the midpoint of the two legs (zero if the
/// midpoint computation fails).
fn blend(env: &Env, oracle: &AssetOracle, legs: Legs) -> Outcome {
    match legs {
        Legs::Empty => Outcome::unreadable(),
        Legs::One(r) => Outcome::one(r),
        Legs::Partial { reading, slot } => Outcome::partial(reading, slot),
        Legs::Two { primary, anchor } => {
            let spread_bounded =
                primary.nature == FeedNature::Market && anchor.nature == FeedNature::Market;
            let age_spread = primary.timestamp.abs_diff(anchor.timestamp);
            let stale = primary.stale
                || anchor.stale
                || (spread_bounded && age_spread > MAX_LEG_AGE_SPREAD_SECONDS);
            let ts = primary.timestamp.min(anchor.timestamp);
            let deviation =
                !within_tolerance_band(env, anchor.price_wad, primary.price_wad, &oracle.tolerance);

            let price_wad = midpoint_price_or_zero(anchor.price_wad, primary.price_wad);

### INV-LIQ-04 — Bad-debt socialization is explicit and total

Permissionless cleanup requires ceil risk debt greater than half-up unweighted
collateral and collateral at or below the fixed $5 dust threshold. Owner-only
forced cleanup omits the dust cap. Both require debt, readable account and NFT
state, valid required prices and no active flash guard. Listing flags and
global pause do not block standalone cleanup.

Cleanup reclassifies all remaining collateral shares as revenue and writes off
all remaining debt against each debt's market. It releases spoke usage and
atomically removes account entries and the NFT. It does not net same-market
supply against debt. Standalone cleanup emits `CleanBadDebtEvent` with
pre-cleanup USD totals, without a controller position-update batch.

Ordinary liquidation and cleanup apply no final account-health or full-backing
assertion. The index floor can leave a shortfall. Recapitalization fills that
shortfall without restoring the lost index or deleted account.

/// Panics with `OracleError::InvalidStalenessConfig` if
/// `asset_max_stale_seconds` falls outside
/// `MIN_PRICE_STALE_SECONDS..=MAX_PRICE_STALE_SECONDS`, or if any source's
/// staleness bound (`properties.loosest_max_stale_seconds`) is looser than
/// the asset's configured bound.
pub(crate) fn staleness_envelope(
    env: &Env,
    asset_max_stale_seconds: u64,
    properties: &SourceProperties,
) {
    if !(MIN_PRICE_STALE_SECONDS..=MAX_PRICE_STALE_SECONDS).contains(&asset_max_stale_seconds)
        || properties.loosest_max_stale_seconds > asset_max_stale_seconds
    {
        panic_with_error!(env, OracleError::InvalidStalenessConfig);
    }
