### Title
Donation-inflated Aquarius LP fair-value price enables over-borrowing against LP collateral - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs)

### Summary
The price aggregator derives the USD value of Aquarius LP share tokens — which can be listed as lending collateral — directly from the pool's *live* `get_reserves` balances via `fair_lp_price_wad` (`2 * sqrt(value_a * value_b) / total_shares`) or `fair_stable_lp_price_wad` (`D * min(price_a, price_b) / total_shares`). Both formulas grow monotonically with either reserve balance, so an unprivileged attacker can inflate the collateral price of an LP token mid-transaction simply by transferring underlying tokens directly into the Aquarius pool contract (a donation, no shares minted). This mirrors the UwuLend second exploit's mechanism: an attacker-held collateral position is overvalued through an injected-balance read, the account borrows far beyond real value, and the borrowed assets are kept while the manipulated valuation persists or is unwound, leaving bad debt.

### Finding Description
`read()` in `contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs` fetches `aquarius_pool_reserves_call` and `aquarius_total_shares_call` on every price resolution and feeds them straight into the fair-value formulas (`common/src/oracle/lp.rs::fair_lp_price_wad` lines 57–87; `common/src/oracle/lp_stable.rs::fair_stable_lp_price_wad` lines 80–110). Neither formula uses a TWAP, EMA, virtual reserves, or reserve-ratio sanity bound. For a constant-product pool, inflating `reserve_a` by factor `k²` scales the reported LP price by `k`. For a stable pool, donating either coin raises the solved invariant `D` and hence the LP price.

The controller values supply collateral at `Context`-resolved strict prices (`common/src/rates/value.rs::position_value_floor`) and enforces the borrow gate on LTV-weighted collateral and the withdraw gate on liquidation-threshold-weighted collateral (`contracts/controller/src/risk/totals.rs::calculate_account_risk_totals`). An attacker path:

1. Take `flash_loan` (or `flash_position`) of a token that is a leg of a listed Aquarius LP collateral market — or use own capital.
2. Transfer the flash-borrowed tokens directly to the Aquarius pool contract, inflating its reserves and therefore `fair_lp_price_wad`/`fair_stable_lp_price_wad` on the very next price read.
3. `supply` a modest amount of the LP token (already held or freshly minted before manipulation) as collateral, or rely on a pre-existing LP supply position.
4. `borrow` up to the inflated LTV limit across all liquid hub assets, and `withdraw` any excess collateral permitted by the still-inflated HF.
5. Recover a large portion of the donation by `remove_liquidity` (the attacker, as a dominant LP holder, reclaims the donated reserves pro-rata), repay the flash loan, keep the borrowed assets.

Because the price read happens inside the borrow's `Context`, a single Soroban transaction ordering donation → supply/borrow → unwind is fully achievable, and all entrypoints used (`flash_loan`, `supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, plus direct SAC transfers to the Aquarius pool and the attacker's own Aquarius liquidity ops) are in-scope and unprivileged.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency: the account ends with debt whose real collateral backing is a fraction of the borrowed value. The position is left as bad debt, ultimately socialized through `seize_positions` supply-index write-downs onto honest suppliers (per the bad-debt documentation), or requires `recapitalize` to restore backing. The only mitigation is `min_pool_value_wad` (`aquarius.rs` line 118–122), which gates total pool *value* — not reserve composition — and does nothing against donation-based inflation of an already-liquid pool.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires an Aquarius LP token to be listed as collateral in a spoke with meaningful LTV, and enough liquidity (flash or own) to move the reserve product materially. `flash_loan` provides the capital-free path and is explicitly flashloanable per market. The donation is largely self-recovering through LP redemption, so cost is bounded. The gaps in my verification: I could not confirm a deployed spoke actually lists an LP-token collateral (config-dependent), and I did not enumerate per-market flashloanability — likelihood is conditional on such a listing existing.

### Recommendation
Make LP valuation donation-resistant:
- Derive reserves from a manipulation-resistant source: Aquarius TWAP/cumulative price observations, or reserve snapshots with a short EMA, rather than instantaneous `get_reserves`.
- Alternatively, cap the fair value at a valuation computed from time-smoothed reserve ratios, or bound the per-read price deviation against a stored/last-good LP price (a sanity band), so a single-transaction reserve spike fails the tolerance check.
- Short-term: keep `min_pool_value_wad` conservative and set low LTV/liquidation thresholds on LP collateral markets until a smoothed reserve source exists.

### Proof of Concept
1. Assume spoke lists `LP_X` (Aquarius constant-product pool `P` over tokens `A`/`B`) with `loan_to_value = 7500`, and `P` holds reserves `(R_a, R_b)`.
2. Attacker holds (or acquires) `LP_X` shares; flash-loan `A` via `Controller::flash_loan` for amount `D` comparable to `R_a`.
3. Inside the callback, SAC-transfer `D` of `A` to `P` (donation; no shares minted).
4. Call `supply(account_id, [(hub, LP_X, shares)])` then `borrow(account_id, [(hub, WETH), ..., (hub, USDC), ...])` up to the new inflated LTV limit — `get_reserves` now returns `R_a + D`, so `price_wad` and the resulting `ltv_collateral` rise by `~sqrt(1 + D/R_a)`.
5. `withdraw` remaining free collateral legs, then call `P.remove_liquidity` to reclaim the donated reserves pro-rata, and return the flash principal + fee.
6. Account retains debts exceeding the true collateral value; liquidation cannot recover the shortfall, forcing `seize_positions` write-down or `recapitalize`.
