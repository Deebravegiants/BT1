### Title
Uncapped-ratio LP seeding in `_seedRebalancing`/`_pairRebalance` lets an attacker-preseeded HyperSwap pair dictate the graduation swap price, extracting curve-raised LT into a self-owned LP - (File: `packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol`)

### Summary
`Bonding.finalizeGraduation` is a permissionless, high-trust operation that moves the entirety of a token's curve-raised LT and 250M LP-reserved tokens into a HyperSwap V2 pair. When that pair has been pre-seeded with a hostile `mint()` (Regime 3 in `_seedUniswapV2Direct`), the protocol's own correction logic (`_seedRebalancing` → `_pairRebalance`) trusts the pool's live, attacker-set reserves to price a "rebalancing" swap and caps that swap at a fixed 99%-of-inventory budget rather than at whatever is actually required to reach the curve-close ratio. For a sufficiently extreme (but not "dust") pre-seed, the cap binds, the correction is incomplete, and the mandatory `_routerDepositAndDispose` deposit that follows is locked in at the still-skewed post-swap ratio - not the true curve-close price. This is exactly the "LP seeded away from the curve close price" outcome the review criteria calls out as high-impact, and it is reachable purely by a single unprivileged wallet that (a) creates the HyperSwap V2 TOKEN/LT pair and (b) seeds it with a deliberately imbalanced `mint()` before the permissionless `finalizeGraduation` keeper call lands.

### Finding Description
`finalizeGraduation` (`packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol:1000-1034`) computes graduation inventory from `PendingGraduation` (frozen at curve-close in `_prepareGraduationLiquidity`, `Bonding.sol:1073-1096`) and calls `_seedUniswapV2Direct` (`Bonding.sol:1201-1234`) to deposit it into the HyperSwap pair. Anyone can front-run this by calling `IUniswapV2Factory.createPair(token, lt)` and then `pair.mint()` with self-funded TOKEN/LT at an arbitrary ratio — the pair address is deterministic and permissionlessly creatable, and nothing in `Bonding` or `Router` restricts who may seed it first.

`_seedRebalancing` (`Bonding.sol:1279-1354`) detects this (`totalSupply() != 0`) and tries to correct the ratio via a single direct-to-pair swap in `_pairRebalance` (`Bonding.sol:1414-1429`), sized by `_noFeeSwapInput` (`Bonding.sol:1507-1522`) and capped at `_swapBudget(...)` = 99% of Bonding's available inventory on the input side (`Bonding.sol:1374-1378`): [1](#0-0) 

The natspec on `_swapBudget` itself concedes the failure mode: *"It only kicks in for catastrophic pre-seeds beyond our budget capacity, where the alternative is bricking."* i.e. the designers explicitly chose "swap at an incomplete/attacker-favorable rate" over "brick," but the resulting price impact was not treated as a first-class fund-safety concern.

After the (possibly incomplete) swap, `_routerDepositAndDispose` (`Bonding.sol:1449-1486`) deposits the remaining balanced subset via `router.addLiquidity(..., 1, 1, lpLock_, ...)`: [2](#0-1) 

`addLiquidity`'s `quote()`-based optimal split locks in whatever ratio the pair is *actually* holding at that moment — which, for a catastrophic pre-seed, is still skewed toward the attacker's original mint ratio, not the `tokensForLP : ltFromPair` curve-close ratio the whole mechanism was designed to preserve (see the `_seedUniswapV2Direct` natspec at `Bonding.sol:1132-1200`, which states the goal is exactly this ratio equality).

Because the attacker minted the pre-existing LP supply themselves, they hold 100% of it going into the rebalance swap. The `pair.swap()` that Bonding is forced to execute in `_pairRebalance` trades directly against the attacker's own pool at the attacker-chosen price, and — being capped short of full correction for a big-enough pre-seed — settles the pool at a materially mispriced point. The attacker can then withdraw their pre-existing LP position (a standard V2 `burn`) to redeem the favorable side of that swap, extracting real curve-raised LT (backed by trader funds) that was only ever intended to end up locked in `LPLock` at the curve-close price.

### Impact Explanation
This directly produces the two protected outcomes named in the validation criteria: an LP permanently seeded away from the curve close price, and theft of LT/trader funds — the curve-raised LT is user capital collected across every buy on the bonding curve, and the mispriced correction swap siphons a slice of it to the attacker's self-funded, self-owned LP position rather than to the token holders' locked pool. The loss is realized the moment `finalizeGraduation` executes and is irreversible: `LPLock.recordLock` (`Bonding.sol:1031`) is a one-shot call with no rescue path, so the mis-seeded pool state cannot be corrected after the fact.

### Likelihood Explanation
- Pair creation and pre-seeding are fully permissionless (`_ensureUniswapV2Pair`, `Bonding.sol:1121-1130`, and standard V2 `mint`), require no special role, and can be executed by any wallet holding LT and the launched token (buyable on the still-open curve before graduation, or acquired post `TokenGraduating`).
- `finalizeGraduation` is permissionless and keeper-driven with only a short expected delay (the natspec targets "~60s"), giving an attacker a public, observable window between the `TokenGraduating` event and the keeper's call to front-run with a hostile `mint()`.
- The exploit only needs the pre-seed to be large enough to escape the `DIRECT_MINT_PRESEED_BPS` (1 bp) dust threshold on at least one side while being large enough on the other axis to exceed the 99%-of-inventory swap budget — a moderate, attacker-controllable size, not an implausible extreme.

### Recommendation
Do not lock in a deposit at a ratio that has not been fully corrected to the cached curve-close target. Options: (1) revert `finalizeGraduation` (and let a retry/rescue path handle it) rather than depositing at a partially-corrected ratio when `_pairRebalance`'s required input exceeds the safety budget, instead of silently proceeding; (2) size the swap budget relative to how far the pre-seed ratio deviates from target rather than a flat 99% of Bonding's inventory, so the correction swap is guaranteed sufficient up to the full inventory before any deposit is attempted; (3) add a minimum-acceptable post-correction ratio check before calling `router.addLiquidity`, reverting (or falling back to the owner-rescuable dust path) if the deposit would land materially off the cached `tokensForLP : ltFromPair` ratio.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker observes a token approach graduation and, immediately after `TokenGraduating` fires (or even earlier once `canGraduate` becomes true), calls `IUniswapV2Factory.createPair(token, lt)` via the known factory (`Bonding.uniswapV2Factory()`), obtaining the deterministic pair address.
2. Attacker acquires TOKEN (via curve buys) and LT (via minting from the LT contract) and calls `pair.mint(attacker)` with an extreme ratio designed so that: (a) neither reserve is small enough to trip the `DIRECT_MINT_PRESEED_BPS` dust fallback, and (b) the no-fee correction input `_noFeeSwapInput(...)` computed from these reserves and the (publicly known, from the `TokenGraduating` event) `(tokensForLP, ltFromPair)` target exceeds `_swapBudget` of Bonding's available inventory on the relevant side.
3. Keeper (or attacker, since it's permissionless) calls `Bonding.finalizeGraduation(token)`. `_seedRebalancing` executes `_pairRebalance`, which swaps only the capped 99%-of-budget amount against the attacker's skewed pool at the attacker-set price, then `_routerDepositAndDispose` deposits the remainder at the still-skewed ratio into `LPLock`.
4. Attacker calls `pair.burn()` (or the router's `removeLiquidity`) on their pre-existing LP tokens, redeeming the favorable side of the mispriced correction swap plus their proportional share of the resulting pool — net-extracting curve-raised LT/token value that should have gone entirely into the honest, curve-priced locked LP.

Note: I was not able to execute this scenario in a live/fork test (no test runner available in this environment), so the magnitude of extractable value depends on exact `tokensForLP`/`ltFromPair` sizing and reserve choices at attack time; the mechanism itself, however, is directly supported by the cited code and its own natspec acknowledging the cap can bind for "catastrophic pre-seeds."

    ///      so the subsequent `addLiquidity` always has a non-zero amount
    ///      of BOTH sides to deposit. Without this, an extreme hostile
    ///      pre-seed (massively imbalanced reserves) drives the
    ///      unconstrained `_noFeeSwapInput` past our per-side budget,
    ///      `_pairRebalance` clamps to the full budget, and the swap
    ///      consumes 100% of one side. `_routerDepositAndDispose` then
    ///      skips `addLiquidity` (`remToken == 0` or `remLT == 0`),
    ///      `finalizeGraduation` returns `liquidity = 0`, and
    ///      `LPLock.recordLock(...)` records a zero-sized lock — the
    ///      attacker's pre-existing LP becomes 100% of the pool. Reserving
    ///      1% guarantees the deposit leg always lands AND mints non-zero
    ///      LP at the post-swap ratio. The 1% comes off the swap, not the
    ///      deposit — for any realistic pre-seed `s_unconstrained` is
    ///      orders of magnitude below `maxSwap`, so the cap doesn't bind
    ///      and behaviour is unchanged. It only kicks in for catastrophic
    ///      pre-seeds beyond our budget capacity, where the alternative
    ///      is bricking.
    function _swapBudget(
        uint256 budget
    ) internal pure returns (uint256) {
        return (budget * 99) / 100;
    }
