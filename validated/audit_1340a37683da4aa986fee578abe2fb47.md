### Title
i128 overflow in interest accrual (`scaled_to_original`) permanently freezes a whale-sized market — all repay/withdraw/liquidate calls revert — (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
The nasm CVE is a buffer overflow that crashes the scanner on a crafted input — i.e., uncontrolled memory/value growth hitting a hard limit and aborting. The analog in XOXNO Lending is an `i128` value overflow inside interest accrual: once `borrowed_scaled × borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX`, `scaled_to_original` panics with `MathOverflow` inside `accrue_chunk`, and because every mutating entrypoint calls `global_sync` before acting, the panic is unrecoverable and the market freezes forever. The `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap never engages because the value overflow occurs before index capping.

### Finding Description
`global_sync` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs` runs `accrue_chunk` in chunks of `MAX_COMPOUND_DELTA_MS`, calling `accrue_step` from `common::rates` which recomputes market totals via `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` (`contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:23-26`, `common/src/rates/scaling.rs`, `common/src/rates/compound.rs`). The scaled principal and the index are both RAY (1e27) `i128` values; their product is effectively capped by `i128::MAX` (~1.7e38). For a high-decimal asset (18 decimals) with a large scaled supply (~1e36 raw ray for 1e9 whole tokens), a borrow index growth of ~170x — reachable at the steep segment of the XLM-style interest curve at ~98% utilization — pushes `scaled × index` past `i128::MAX` and the next accrual panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow`.

Because `global_sync` is invoked at the head of every market mutation (`interest.rs:20-33`), and the panic is inside the accrual itself, no verb can execute: `withdraw`, `repay`, `borrow`, `seize_positions` (liquidation), `net_settle`, `claim_revenue` all revert. There is no path to shrink `borrowed` or the index without first accruing, so the state is terminal. The harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-361` demonstrates exactly this: `try_update_indexes_for`, `try_withdraw_raw`, and `try_repay` all fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, confirming the index cap does not protect against the value ceiling. The test also notes the documented bound in `docs/reference/formulas.md` is wrong.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds. All supplier principal and interest in the affected market become unwithdrawable, borrower repayments cannot settle, and liquidations cannot execute — so collateral in other markets tied to positions borrowing this asset is also effectively bricked. An attacker does not need privileged access: the state is reached purely through `supply` and `borrow` (plus time at high utilization, which the attacker can maintain by controlling both sides of the market and keeping utilization near the curve's steep segment).

### Likelihood Explanation
An unprivileged attacker seeds the market with a very large supply of a high-decimals asset and borrows to ~98% utilization via their own position (collateralized elsewhere or in the same account via other supplied assets). Sustained high utilization drives the borrow rate up the steep segment; after enough compounding (the test shows the cliff within ~40 years of simulated time at 98% utilization on the XLM curve — and sooner at higher debt/decimal combinations), the next accrual traps the market. It is capital-intensive but requires no privileges, no oracle manipulation, and no third-party cooperation. Once hit, there is no recovery function: `recapitalize` and `clean_bad_debt` also accrue first and hit the same panic.

Caveat: I confirmed the overflow path via the harness test and the accrual/scale plumbing, but did not fully read the body of `accrue_step` in `common/src/rates/compound.rs`; the test's assertions nonetheless prove the panic originates in `scaled_to_original` during accrual.

### Recommendation
- Cap the borrow index growth inside `accrue_step` so `borrowed_scaled × borrow_index` stays below `i128::MAX` (e.g., clamp `borrow_index` at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` *before* recomputing the unscaled borrowed value, and socialise/cap the residual debt).
- Alternatively, widen the unscale math to `I256` in `scaled_to_original`/`unscale_borrow_ceil` so accrual can proceed and the index cap can engage.
- Add a governance-free fallback that lets `recapitalize`/`clean_bad_debt` run without accrual when accrual itself is the failure point.

### Proof of Concept
```rust
// tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321
let mut t = LendingTest::new()
    .with_market(big("BIG18", 18, xlm_curve()))
    .with_market(col())
    .with_max_utilization_disabled_all_markets()
    .build();
lift_caps(&t, "BIG18", 18);
lift_caps(&t, "COL", 7);
let principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18);
t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);              // supply()
let debt = principal / 100 * 98;
t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);                 // borrow() to 98% util

// advance ledger time; eventually:
assert_contract_error(t.try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"]), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
// market permanently frozen:
assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
assert_contract_error(t.try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
// borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY => the cap never engaged
```
