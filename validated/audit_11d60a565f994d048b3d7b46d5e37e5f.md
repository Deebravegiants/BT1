### Title
Self-planted dust collateral leg with a stale/unusable price feed bricks liquidation, `clean_bad_debt`, and withdrawal — a borrower can hold un-liquidatable bad debt - (File: contracts/controller/src/risk/totals.rs)

### Summary
The analog of the Mesos crash is a "poison-pill" input that makes every downstream operation on the same state abort. `calculate_account_risk_totals_body` calls `cache.load_markets` over the *union of every supply and borrow key* of an account, and every cached price read is strict (fail-closed under ADR-0005 / INV-ORACLE-01). `supply` does not require a usable price for the newly opened leg, so a borrower can open a dust-sized collateral leg in an asset whose oracle feed is stale, dead, or permanently misconfigured. From that moment, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and even the account's own `withdraw` revert with `PriceFeedStale`/`PriceFeedInvalid` for as long as that feed is unusable — while the borrower's debt keeps accruing.

### Finding Description
- `contracts/controller/src/risk/totals.rs:163` — `cache.load_markets(&portfolio_hub_keys(supply_positions.keys(), &borrow_positions.keys()))` fetches market data for **all** positions.
- `contracts/controller/src/risk/totals.rs:172` and `:49` — each leg calls `cache.cached_price(&hub_asset.asset)`, which panics on a missing/stale/invalid feed (INV-ORACLE-01 strict reads).
- `process_liquidation` (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs:58,105`) and `clean_bad_debt` both evaluate these risk totals, so one unpriceable leg aborts the entire operation.
- Reproduced by the harness: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_liquidate_and_clean_stale_leg.rs` and `audit_supply_stale_shield.rs` show `try_supply` succeeding with a stale WBTC feed, then `try_liquidate`, `try_clean_bad_debt_by_id`, and `try_withdraw` all reverting with `PRICE_FEED_STALE` until the feed recovers.

### Impact Explanation
Temporary freezing of funds / protocol insolvency. A borrower anticipating liquidation plants a dust collateral leg in an asset with a fragile feed (single-spot Reflector key, LP-derived price, or a feed that can be retired). When their position goes unhealthy, no liquidator can repay it and `clean_bad_debt` cannot socialize it — every attempt reverts on valuation of the poisoned leg. The debt accrues interest unpaid; if the feed never recovers (delisted asset, deprecated source), the bad debt becomes permanent and the supply-index write-down falls on suppliers when governance finally force-socializes — except `force_socialize_bad_debt` *also* requires valid prices for every position (`docs/reference/runbooks/force-socialize-bad-debt.md` step 3), so a permanently dead feed leaves no recovery path at all.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires only the borrower's own `supply` call (unprivileged, permissionless) plus a feed that goes stale — an ordinary oracle outage, not adversarial oracle manipulation. The attacker pays only a dust deposit. The window persists for the whole outage; permanence depends on whether the feed recovers, which is outside the protocol's control.

### Recommendation
At `supply`/`flash_position` admission, require a fresh valid price for any newly opened supply leg when the account carries (or will carry) debt — or, safer and simpler, exclude zero/near-zero-value legs from the strict valuation set during liquidation and cleanup: value a supply leg below the dust threshold as zero instead of demanding its price, and skip its seizure. Alternatively, let `clean_bad_debt` treat an unpriceable collateral leg as zero-value (conservative for the `D > C` gate) so orphaned feeds cannot shield bad debt.

### Proof of Concept
Reproduced exactly by `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_liquidate_and_clean_stale_leg.rs`:
1. Borrower supplies 10,000 USDC, borrows 3 ETH.
2. Attacker (same account) makes WBTC's feed stale (`set_price_at(wbtc, …, now − 3600)`), then calls `supply(WBTC, 0.001)` — accepted despite the stale feed.
3. Crash USDC to $0.50 → borrower HF < 1.
4. `liquidate(...)` → `PRICE_FEED_STALE`; `clean_bad_debt(account_id)` → `PRICE_FEED_STALE`; borrower's own `withdraw(WBTC)` → `PRICE_FEED_STALE`. Debt remains un-liquidatable until WBTC's feed recovers — or forever if it never does.
