### Title
Position NFT transfers to contract addresses are unchecked, permanently locking the account's collateral and control - (File: contracts/position-nft/src/contract.rs)

### Summary
The external finding's bug class — an NFT ending up at an address incapable of ever moving it — maps directly onto XOXNO Lending's `PositionNft`. The contract exports the stock OpenZeppelin `transfer` / `transfer_from` entrypoints (`contracts/position-nft/README.md:62-63`), which perform `from.require_auth()` but impose no check whatsoever on `to`. On Soroban, a contract address can only authorize `require_auth` from inside its own execution; a contract that does not itself expose an entrypoint that calls `PositionNft::transfer` or the controller's position entrypoints can never act as the token's owner. Because `owner_of(account_id)` is the sole identity record (`contracts/position-nft/README.md:4-9`) and every owner-gated controller call goes through `require_owner_or_delegate` (`contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs:150`), an account whose NFT sits at such a contract is permanently unusable: no `withdraw`, `borrow`, `repay`, or further `transfer` can ever be authorized.

Notably, the codebase already recognizes recipient risk for fungible payouts — `process_withdraw` routes the `to` recipient through `require_external_recipient` (`contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs:152-154`) — but no analogous guard exists on the far more dangerous NFT leg, where the `to` silently inherits the entire position (all collateral and all debt, per `contracts/position-nft/README.md:39-41`).

### Finding Description
`PositionNft::transfer` / `transfer_from` are inherited unmodified from OpenZeppelin `stellar-tokens` 0.7.1 (`contracts/position-nft/README.md:13-14`). They update `Owner(token_id)` and enumeration entries for any `to: Address` with no `require_auth` on the recipient and no exclusion of contract addresses. Meanwhile `mint` (`contracts/position-nft/src/contract.rs:68-76`) always targets the authorizing caller, so the lock is reachable not only at creation time (a contract caller that authorizes `supply` once but has no re-invocable entrypoint) but — more importantly — at any later point via a single `transfer` call to any arbitrary contract address, e.g. the pool, a SAC token contract, an Aquarius/Soroswap pool, or any contract lacking an NFT-management entrypoint.

Unlike the Solidity original there is no `safeMint`/`IERC721Receiver` equivalent in the SEP-token model, so the mitigation cannot be a receiver callback; it must be an explicit recipient restriction.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of user funds. Every asset supplied into the account (all `supply_positions`) becomes unreachable forever: `withdraw` requires `require_owner_or_delegate`, and the contract-owner can never satisfy `require_auth` unless it was purpose-built to invoke the controller. `transfer_hands_full_control_to_new_owner` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/position_nft.rs:46-64`) confirms that whoever holds the token holds full control — conversely, a holder that cannot sign holds a permanently frozen position. Third-party liquidation remains possible (`underwater_position_transfers_freely_and_stays_liquidatable`, same file), but that only lets liquidators drain collateral on an underwater account; a healthy account's collateral is simply bricked.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium-low but realistic. Users routinely move positions to smart wallets, escrow contracts, multisig contracts, or marketplace contracts for collateralized sale/lending of positions. Any integration that escrows the NFT into a contract without a release path — or a plain mistaken `transfer` to a token/pool address — permanently bricks the account in one irreversible transaction. This mirrors the original report's medium severity: the action is user-initiated but unrecoverable and silently accepted by the contract.

### Recommendation
Add a recipient guard to position ownership changes, analogous to `require_external_recipient` for withdraw payouts. Concretely: override `transfer`/`transfer_from` in `PositionNft` (rather than exporting the OZ defaults unchanged) to reject `to` addresses that are protocol contracts or otherwise flagged non-operable — at minimum the pool, hub assets' SACs, the controller itself, and governance — and consider requiring `to.require_auth()` on transfer so the recipient must demonstrably be able to authorize (a contract that cannot re-invoke cannot produce this auth). At minimum, document and enforce that `to` must not be a contract without a position-management entrypoint.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice calls `controller.supply(caller=alice, account_id=0, spoke_id, assets)`; `create_account_with` mints token `id` to Alice (`contracts/controller/src/account.rs:62-63`).
2. Alice calls `position_nft.transfer(from=alice, to=<POOL_CONTRACT or any SAC/AMM contract>, token_id=id)`. The OZ `transfer` succeeds; `owner_of(id)` now returns the contract address.
3. Alice (or anyone) calls `controller.withdraw(caller=alice, account_id=id, ...)`. `try_account_owner` resolves the contract as owner; `require_owner_or_delegate` fails because the pool/SAC contract never invokes `require_auth`. Collateral is permanently frozen; `borrow`/`repay`/`transfer` are equally unreachable.
