### Title
Bad-debt supply-index floor clamp resurrects wiped-out supply shares, letting already-socialized suppliers drain later deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
The kernel bug is a use-after-free: `__nf_register_net_hook` dereferences `new_hooks` after dropping `nf_hook_mutex`, so the hook array it still points at can be freed out from under it. The analog on XOXNO Lending is a use-after-free of supply shares: `clean_bad_debt`/`seize_positions` destroys the collateral backing of every supply share in a market by writing the supply index down, but `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps the index **up** at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`) instead of letting it reach zero. The scaled share balances — the dangling "references" — survive untouched, and are later dereferenced in `withdraw` against a supply index that no longer reflects the wipeout.

### Finding Description
Bad debt is socialized by lowering `supply_index`: `pool::ops::seize::apply` on the `Borrow` side computes `bad_debt = cache.unscale_borrow_ceil_ray(position)`, calls `interest::apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(&mut cache, bad_debt)`, then burns the debt shares (`contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:23-28`). The deposit side only reclassifies shares as revenue; `supplied` (total scaled shares) is never reduced. The write-down therefore frees the *backing* of all existing supply shares while the share counters stay allocated — exactly the shape of freeing `new_hooks` while a reader still holds the pointer.

When bad debt meets or exceeds the value of total supply, the computed post-write-down index falls below `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` and is clamped up to `RAY/1000`. Every wiped supplier's `scaled_amount` now redeems at the floor index, a phantom claim that no loss ever cancelled. The in-repo unit test `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:373-427`) demonstrates the full chain on the raw `Cache`: after a clamping write-down, `unscale_supply_floor(old_scaled)` is positive ("floor clamp leaves S_old a phantom claim"), a fresh deposit is minted, and the wiped position's `resolve_withdrawal`/`require_reserves`/`burn_supply`/`debit_cash` sequence pays it real tokens — `S_old drains exactly the fresh deposit` — leaving `cash` below the fresh supplier's claim. On the production path, `withdraw` computes the gross payout as `scaled × supply_index` and `require_reserves`/`transfer_out` settle it in tokens (`contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:57-88`), so the phantom claim converts directly into custody.

Reachability is permissionless: `clean_bad_debt` is open to anyone once `total_debt > total_collateral` and collateral is ≤ 5 WAD (math.md §Bad-debt socialization), and it routes through `check_bad_debt_after_liquidation`/seize entries into the same `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`. An attacker can manufacture the state on a thin market: supply a small amount, have a second account borrow nearly all of it against dust collateral, let the position go bad, liquidate/`clean_bad_debt` to trigger a clamping write-down, keep the wiped supplier shares, wait for (or provide via a second wallet) fresh deposits, then `withdraw` the phantom claim.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. Suppliers whose claims were meant to be zeroed retain `scaled × RAY/1000` of claim — up to 0.1% of pre-wipeout supplied value — redeemable against cash belonging to subsequent depositors. Each redemption debits `cash` without corresponding backing, so the market is left under-collateralized and later suppliers absorb the loss (cash < their claim, as the test asserts). The loss is bounded by the floor (0.1% of wiped supply value per wipeout), but it is a direct, permissionless transfer of custody to holders of invalidated shares.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a market wipeout severe enough that the socialized loss pushes the index below `RAY/1000` — i.e. bad debt ≥ ~99.9% of supplied value. On a mature deep market this is unrealistic, but on a thin or freshly-listed market an attacker can construct it deliberately: supply X, borrow ~X with near-worthless collateral, then trigger cleanup. The attacker then needs fresh capital to enter the market afterward, which happens organically on any live market. The exploit path uses only unprivileged entrypoints (`borrow`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `withdraw`), so likelihood is moderate-to-high whenever a thin market accumulates near-total bad debt.

### Recommendation
In `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, when the computed index would fall below `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, do not resurrect the index — instead zero out the claim itself: burn or reset `supplied` (and `revenue`) scaled shares so no `scaled_amount` can redeem afterward, or clamp the index to zero and handle the divide-by-zero in the scaling path explicitly. Alternatively, gate `withdraw`/`resolve_withdrawal` on a "wiped" flag set when the floor clamp engages, so surviving scaled balances cannot be dereferenced into payouts. Add a regression test asserting `unscale_supply_floor(old_scaled) == 0` after a clamping write-down.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker supplies `S` tokens into a thin market via `controller.supply`, then from a second account deposits dust collateral and `borrow`s ~`S` of the same asset.
2. Collateral price falls (or interest accrues) until `total_debt > total_collateral` and collateral ≤ 5 WAD, making the position permissionlessly cleanable.
3. Attacker calls `controller.liquidate` (seizes the dust collateral) and then `clean_bad_debt`; `pool::ops::seize::apply` on the `Borrow` side calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, which computes an index below `RAY/1000` and clamps it to the floor, leaving the attacker's `S` scaled shares live (`contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:23-28`; `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:388-393`).
4. A victim supplies `D` tokens into the market; `cash` is credited.
5. Attacker calls `controller.withdraw` on the wiped position; `resolve_withdrawal` returns `S × RAY/1000`, `require_reserves` passes against the fresh `D`, and the pool transfers real tokens (`contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:79-88`). `cash` now under-covers the victim's claim — the exact ending state the unit test asserts (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:414-425`).

Note: I verified the mechanics through `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:373-427`, `contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:18-35`, and `contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:57-88`; I did not read the body of `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs` or the `clean_bad_debt` controller path in `contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/bad_debt.rs` line-by-line, so the exact clamp expression and eligibility checks should be confirmed against source, though the test fixture pins the behavior.
