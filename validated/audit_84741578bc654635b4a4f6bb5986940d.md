### Title
Attacker-Caused Aquarius LP Price Outage Shields Insolvent Account From Liquidation and Bad-Debt Cleanup - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs)

### Summary
CVE-2016-5362 bypasses an intended anti-spoofing protection with a crafted DHCP message to deny service or intercept traffic. The analog here is bypassing the protocol's intended liquidation/bad-debt protection by crafting a state in which a required price read fails: a borrower supplies a dust leg of an Aquarius LP share as collateral, then withdraws liquidity from that very pool so its value falls below `min_pool_value_wad`, making every `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and `withdraw` on the account revert while the debt accrues to insolvency.

### Finding Description
The Aquarius LP price provider fails closed when the pool's measured value drops under a configured floor: in `providers/aquarius.rs::read`, after computing the fair LP price, the code returns `Err(OracleError::InsufficientAquariusLiquidity)` when `pool_value_wad < lp.min_pool_value_wad` (aquarius.rs:118-122). Any account holding even a dust position of that LP token as collateral then fails all paths that resolve its prices.

The bypass works because `supply` does not require a price for the supplied asset — a borrower can plant a dust LP leg cheaply at any time — while liquidation and bad-debt cleanup require every collateral leg's price to resolve. This asymmetry is confirmed by the harness test `audit_supply_setup_blocks_liquidation_via_stale_dust_leg`, which shows a dust supply leg accepted without a fresh feed, then `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and `withdraw` all reverting with `PRICE_FEED_STALE` on the account while a twin account liquidates normally. The threat model documents the same gate: "an indebted borrower can add a dust leg of any listed collateral and choose which feed outage shields the account. For an Aquarius LP leg, liquidity providers can cause that outage by withdrawing pool value below `min_pool_value_wad`."

Crucially, unlike a stale third-party feed (which the borrower cannot control), an Aquarius pool's reserve balance is directly manipulable by any LP holder: removing liquidity drops `pool_value_wad = price_wad * total_shares / share_unit` below the floor, so the attacker triggers the protection bypass on demand rather than waiting for an outage.

### Impact Explanation
Temporary freezing of funds and protocol insolvency. While the shield is active, no liquidator can repay the attacker's debt, and permissionless `clean_bad_debt` cannot socialize it — both revert on the unresolved collateral price. Interest keeps accruing on the debt, so an already-underwater position deepens. If the position is large relative to the market, the delay converts a recoverable underwater account into realized bad debt written down against suppliers. The collateral itself is also frozen (withdraw reverts), but that is the attacker's own dust stake and costs them little.

### Likelihood Explanation
The path is fully permissionless and uses only in-scope entrypoints: `supply` (dust LP leg, no price required), a normal Aquarius `withdraw_liquidity` call on the attacker's own LP position, and the resulting failure of `liquidate`/`clean_bad_debt`. Requirements: the account must hold an Aquarius-LP collateral listing, and the attacker must control enough of that pool's shares to push its value below `min_pool_value_wad` — plausible when the pool is thin or the attacker is a dominant LP. The shield is reversible (re-add liquidity to unbrick), giving the attacker fine-grained, repeatable control. This is a conditional, cost-bearing attack rather than free griefing, so Medium rather than High.

### Recommendation
- Do not let a single failing collateral price block liquidation of the whole account: allow liquidation plans that skip unpriceable collateral legs, or treat a leg below the liquidity floor as zero-value collateral for health computation instead of reverting.
- Alternatively, make the LP floor a valuation input (clamp the leg to 0) rather than an error: an LP share in a pool below `min_pool_value_wad` should contribute no collateral value, not poison the account.
- For cleanup, let `clean_bad_debt` ignore or zero-value unpriceable legs so bad debt can always be socialized.
- At listing time, bound `min_pool_value_wad` against realistic attacker-controlled LP shares, or require the LP feed to be backed by a non-LP-derived secondary source so pool drainage alone cannot silence the price.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance has listed an Aquarius LP share token as collateral in a spoke; the attacker is a liquidity provider in that pool.
2. Attacker opens an account, supplies real collateral (e.g., USDC), borrows the maximum debt (e.g., ETH/USDC) up to the LTV limit.
3. Attacker calls `supply` with a dust amount of the Aquarius LP share — accepted, since supply performs no price check (harness confirms the leg persists with nonzero scaled shares even when the feed is unusable).
4. Attacker (or colluding LPs) calls `withdraw_liquidity` on the Aquarius pool until `price_wad * total_shares / 10^decimals < min_pool_value_wad`.
5. Market moves: the account goes HF < 1. Every `liquidate(liquidator, attacker, ...)` reverts because resolving the LP leg returns `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` at aquarius.rs:120-122; `clean_bad_debt` reverts on the same leg; the attacker cannot even withdraw the dust leg (also price-gated), which costs them only the dust.
6. Debt accrues interest while unliquidatable. When the attacker restores liquidity, the position can be deeper underwater than when the shield engaged, leaving bad debt for suppliers.

Uncertain: whether liquidations can be executed per-leg via `debt_payments` such that a liquidator could avoid the poisoned collateral leg entirely — the pro-rata multi-leg plan iterates all collateral legs, and the threat model states the outage "blocks bad-debt cleanup and force-socialization," implying full blocking, but I did not verify the liquidation planner skips unpriceable legs in every code path.
