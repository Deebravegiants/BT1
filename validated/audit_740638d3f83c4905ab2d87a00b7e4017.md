I found a strong analog. The `SimplexPaymaster` stake mechanism has already been documented and tested as a live, exploitable defect matching the exact bug class in the report: a reachable function calls into an external protocol (the ERC-4337 `EntryPoint`) whose companion recovery function is unreachable because it's gated by logic that unconditionally reverts — permanently freezing funds, and additionally exposing a permissionless griefing vector.

### Title
Permanently Locked EntryPoint Stake via Unreachable `unlockStake`/`withdrawStake` and Permissionless `addStake` Griefing - (File: `evm/src/utils/SimplexPaymaster.sol`)

### Summary
`SimplexPaymaster` inherits `PaymasterERC20`, whose `unlockStake()`/`withdrawStake()` entry points are gated behind `_authorizeWithdraw()`, which `SimplexPaymaster` overrides to unconditionally revert with `UnauthorizedCall` [1](#0-0) . This is structurally identical to the GMX report: a reachable "harvest"/"recover" style function (here, `unlockStake`/`withdrawStake` on the `EntryPoint`) can never actually be invoked by any caller this contract exposes, because the gate it is wired through is permanently closed. Native stake placed with the `EntryPoint` via `addStake` — which is mandatory for bundlers to relay this paymaster's operations — becomes permanently unrecoverable, and until governance added `UnlockStake`/`WithdrawStake` request kinds, there was no `RequestKind` variant that could reach the stake at all.

### Finding Description
`_authorizeWithdraw` in `SimplexPaymaster` always reverts [1](#0-0) , and the inherited `PaymasterCore.unlockStake`/`withdrawStake` route through that authorization hook, so calling them from *any* address — treasury, host, or the `EntryPoint` itself — reverts. This is confirmed by a fork test against the live mainnet/BSC deployments showing every privileged identity refused and the stake permanently unchanged [2](#0-1) . Separately, `addStake` was originally ungated (permissionless), while the `EntryPoint` semantics mean every call to `addStake` resets any pending unlock and only ever grows `unstakeDelaySec` — so an unprivileged caller could push the delay to 136 years, permanently defeating any future recovery path [3](#0-2) .

The security-review changelog confirms this was a real, exploitable defect verified against live deployments (0.1 native stuck on Ethereum, BSC and Arbitrum before the fix) [4](#0-3) , and the fix added `RequestKind.UnlockStake`/`RequestKind.WithdrawStake` governance variants plus a treasury-only gate on `addStake` [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) .

### Impact Explanation
Exactly mirroring the GMX report's impact class ("impossible to claim/recover funds from a downstream protocol due to a caller-side gating mismatch"), any native staked with the `EntryPoint` was permanently frozen — a concrete, complete loss of funds with no recovery path, compounded by a permissionless griefing vector that could indefinitely extend the lockup delay against any future fix.

### Likelihood Explanation
High: staking is not optional — bundlers require a staked paymaster before they will relay its UserOperations, so the vulnerable path was exercised by design on every live deployment (Ethereum, BSC, Arbitrum), and the griefing call (`addStake{value: 1 wei}(type(uint32).max)`) requires no privilege and costs almost nothing to trigger.

### Recommendation
This has already been remediated in this codebase: add explicit governance-reachable `RequestKind` variants (`UnlockStake`, `WithdrawStake`) that call `entryPoint().unlockStake()` / `entryPoint().withdrawStake(treasury)` directly rather than through the always-reverting `_authorizeWithdraw` gate, and restrict `addStake` to a trusted caller (e.g., treasury) to prevent unstake-delay griefing, as implemented in the current `SimplexPaymaster.sol` [7](#0-6) .

### Proof of Concept
The live fork test `SimplexPaymasterStakeLockForkTest` demonstrates the pre-fix condition end-to-end: it forks BSC/Ethereum, confirms the deployed paymaster holds live stake, then shows every privileged identity (treasury, host, EntryPoint, and an arbitrary caller) reverts on both `unlockStake()` and `withdrawStake()`, leaving the stake unchanged [8](#0-7) , and a companion test shows an unprivileged `griefer` extending the unstake delay past a century via a permissionless `addStake` call [3](#0-2) .
