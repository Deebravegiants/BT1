### Title
Bad-debt floor leaves wiped suppliers residual shares that drain later deposits - ([contracts/pool/src/interest.rs](contracts/pool/src/interest.rs))

### Summary

Bad-debt cleanup socializes losses by lowering `supply_index`, but clamps the result upward to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead of zero. On a complete wipeout, suppliers retain the same scaled shares, each now worth at least `RAY/1000`. Those residual claims remain withdrawable, so the first such supplier can withdraw newly deposited cash even though the bad debt was already fully socialized.

### Finding Description

`clean_bad_debt` sends the account's borrow positions to `pool_seize_positions_call` as `AccountPositionType::Borrow` entries. [1](#0-0)  The pool converts those scaled borrow shares to `bad_debt`, calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, and burns the debt. [2](#0-1) 

When `bad_debt` equals or exceeds total supplied value, `remaining` becomes zero and the mathematical index reduction is zero. The subsequent `new_supply_index.max(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)` restores the index to `RAY/1000`, leaving all existing supply shares with a residual claim. [3](#0-2)  A later full withdrawal resolves those stale scaled shares against that floored index, burns them, checks only that tracked cash covers the payout, and debits that cash. [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

The in-tree regression test demonstrates the exact sequence: a 1000-share supply and 1000-share debt market is wiped out, the index clamps to the floor, a fresh deposit equal to the stranded claim is added, and the wiped supplier withdraws exactly that fresh deposit, leaving the depositor undercollateralized. [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) 

### Impact Explanation

A wiped supplier can steal funds deposited after bad-debt cleanup. The loss is proportional to the victim's fresh deposit, capped by the stranded residual claim; the victim's resulting position cannot be fully paid. This is theft of user funds and market insolvency.

### Likelihood Explanation

The triggering path is reachable by unprivileged users: `clean_bad_debt(account_id)` can clean an uncollateralized account, another user can call `supply`, and a former supplier can call `withdraw` with `i128::MAX`/full withdrawal. Complete bad-debt wipeouts are the highest-impact case because the floor guarantees every old share retains positive value rather than realizing a total loss.

### Recommendation

Do not clamp a completely consumed `supply_index` back to a positive claim value. When `remaining == 0`, set the index to zero and make full withdrawals resolve to zero, or explicitly burn/zero all stranded supply shares during the bad-debt write-down. If the floor is required to avoid division by zero, track that state separately so deposits can be reinitialized without giving pre-wipeout shares claims on post-wipeout cash.

### Proof of Concept

1. Existing supplier A holds scaled supply `S`; the market has debt whose ceil-scaled value is at least the full supplied value and no recoverable collateral.
2. A caller invokes `clean_bad_debt(account_id)` on the bad-debt account.
3. The controller emits a `PoolSeizeEntry` with `side = AccountPositionType::Borrow`, causing the pool to reduce the supply index and burn debt.
4. The write-down computes `remaining = 0`, but `.max(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)` sets `supply_index = RAY/1000`; supplier A's scaled shares are unchanged.
5. Victim B calls `supply` and transfers `D` tokens to the market.
6. Supplier A calls `withdraw` for the full `S` position. The pool unscale operation returns approximately `S * RAY/1000`; if `D` covers it, `require_reserves` passes, cash is debited, and A receives B's deposit.
7. B remains with newly minted supply shares but insufficient cash to redeem them.

This behavior is already encoded in `test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard`, which asserts that the stranded claim extracts exactly the fresh deposit and leaves the fresh depositor undercollateralized.

fn gate_and_debit(env: &Env, cache: &mut Cache, net_transfer: i128, skip_utilization_check: bool) {
    cache.require_reserves(net_transfer);

    if !skip_utilization_check {
        guards::require_utilization_below_max(env, cache);
    }
    guards::require_supply_for_debt(env, cache);
    cache.debit_cash(net_transfer);
}
