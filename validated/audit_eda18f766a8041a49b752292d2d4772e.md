No vulnerability found for this question.

The reported issue concerns a race condition between `repay` and `liquidate` in a lending protocol (Blueberry) after repayments are re-enabled from a paused state, allowing liquidators to front-run borrowers. Raydium AMM is a constant-product swap/AMM program with no lending, borrowing, collateral, repayment, or liquidation functionality of any kind.

The only comparable state-gating mechanism in this codebase is `AmmStatus`, which gates `deposit_permission`, `withdraw_permission`, and `swap_permission` [1](#0-0) , and is enforced in the swap handlers such as `process_swap_base_in` [2](#0-1) , `process_swap_base_out` [3](#0-2) , and their V2 counterparts [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) . These are single-flag permission checks with no analogous "repay vs. liquidate" dual-action race — there is no second privileged/unprivileged instruction pair whose ordering after an unpause event could be exploited to front-run a borrower, because there is no borrowing/liquidation concept in this AMM program at all. There is therefore no reachable unprivileged-instruction path in `Initialize2`, `Deposit`, `Withdraw`, or the swap instructions that reproduces this bug class.
