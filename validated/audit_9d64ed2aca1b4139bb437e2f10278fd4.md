### Title
Whole-unit liquidation seizure hides excess collateral extraction behind the quoted bonus - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/math.rs)

### Summary
The permissionless `liquidate` path can convert a solvent account’s quoted partial liquidation into a full close whenever one low-decimal collateral unit discounted by the configured bonus covers the debt plus one debt-unit rounding allowance. [1](#0-0)  On a full close, the low-decimal collateral seizure is rounded up to a whole unit rather than to the value represented by repayment plus the reported bonus. [2](#0-1)  The resulting effective bonus can exceed `bonus_bps`, while the emitted liquidation event still reports only the configured bonus. [3](#0-2) 

### Finding Description
`Controller::liquidate` is permissionless and accepts an arbitrary `liquidator`, target `account_id`, `debt_payments`, and `SeizeMode`. [4](#0-3)  Liquidation only authenticates the liquidator and requires the target’s health factor to be below one. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

For an account with one supply position in an asset below `MIN_BORROWABLE_ASSET_DECIMALS`, `whole_unit_repayment` computes the USD value of one whole collateral unit and promotes the quote to the full debt when `floor(unit / (1 + bonus))` covers total debt plus one base unit per debt leg. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7)  When that plan repays every debt leg, `calculate_seized_collateral` rounds the collateral amount upward to one whole unit and caps it only by the held balance. [2](#0-1) 

The plan’s `bonus_bps` remains the originally selected curve bonus and is exposed through `LiquidationResult` and `LiquidationEvent`; neither reports the additional collateral created by the whole-unit ceiling. [9](#0-8) [3](#0-2)  Project documentation confirms that this case pays an effective bonus of `U / D - 1`, not the reported `b`, and that the excess is not captured by the displayed bonus or protocol fee. [10](#0-9) 

### Impact Explanation
An unhealthy borrower can lose collateral worth materially more than `debt * (1 + bonus_bps)`, transferring the excess value to an arbitrary liquidator. [2](#0-1)  This is theft of user collateral beyond the liquidation bonus shown by the estimate and event, analogous to a truncated security indicator hiding the meaningful suffix of an outcome. [3](#0-2) 

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires an already-liquidatable account whose only collateral position is a whole-unit, below-three-decimal asset, and whose debt satisfies `LT * U < D < U / (1 + b)` after the small debt-unit allowance. [11](#0-10) [1](#0-0)  Once such an account exists, any unprivileged liquidator can trigger the behavior through `liquidate` with `SeizeMode::Transfer` and does not need governance access, privileged parameters, leaked keys, or an external manipulation path. [4](#0-3) [12](#0-11) 

### Recommendation
Do not report `bonus_bps` as the economic seizure rate when a whole-unit round-up creates collateral above `repaid * (1 + bonus_bps)`; either calculate and emit the realized bonus from measured repayment and seizure, or cap the seizure to the configured bonus and defer dust handling to a separately disclosed mechanism. [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2)  Alternatively, reject liquidation plans whose whole-unit payout exceeds the quoted collateralized value, and prevent positions whose single whole unit makes that gap economically material. [13](#0-12) 

### Proof of Concept
Assume a listed collateral asset has fewer than three decimals, a liquidation threshold of 80%, a liquidation bonus of 5%, and one whole unit valued at `U = $100`; the victim supplies exactly one unit and owes `D = $90`. [14](#0-13)  The account is unhealthy because `HF = 100 * 0.80 / 90 ≈ 0.889 < 1`. [6](#0-5) 

The discounted whole unit is `floor(100 / 1.05) = $95.23`, which exceeds the `$90` debt plus a sub-cent debt-token rounding allowance, so `whole_unit_repayment` promotes the quote to the full `$90` debt. [8](#0-7)  An attacker calls `liquidate(attacker, victim_id, [(debt_hub_asset, debt_amount)], SeizeMode::Transfer)`; because the plan repays all debt, the collateral seizure rounds up to the entire `$100` unit. [4](#0-3) [2](#0-1) 

The attacker repays `$90` and receives `$100`, an effective bonus of approximately 11.11%, while the estimate and `LiquidationEvent` still report only the configured 5% bonus. [3](#0-2) [10](#0-9)

**File:** docs/reference/formulas.md (L310-316)
```markdown
A rule-1 full close pays the liquidator one unit worth `U` for `D`. Its
effective bonus is `U / D - 1`, not `b`. With `k` held units and liquidation
threshold `LT`, `HF < 1` gives `k * U * LT < D`, so the effective bonus is at
most about `1 / (k * LT) - 1`. The borrower loses `U - D * (1 + b)` above the
normal bonus. `bonus_rate_bps` in the estimate and `bonus_bps` in
`LiquidationEvent` show only `b`. A listing below 3 decimals has no liquidation
fee, so no protocol fee applies to that excess.
```
