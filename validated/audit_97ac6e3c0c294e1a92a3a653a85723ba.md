### Title
`WrappedHyperFungibleToken.send()` credits the fixed requested amount instead of the actual tokens locked, allowing fee-on-transfer/rebasing tokens to mint unbacked cross-chain credit - (File: sdk/packages/core/contracts/apps/WrappedHyperFungibleToken.sol)

### Summary
`WrappedHyperFungibleToken.send()` (and its upgradeable twin, `WrappedHyperFungibleTokenUpgradeable.send()`) locks the underlying ERC20 via `safeTransferFrom(msg.sender, address(this), params.amount)` and then dispatches a cross-chain `Message` that always encodes `params.amount` as the amount to credit on the destination chain, regardless of how many tokens the wrapper actually received. For any underlying token whose `transferFrom` does not deliver exactly `amount` to the recipient — fee-on-transfer tokens, deflationary/rebasing tokens, or any ERC20 with non-standard transfer semantics — this creates a permanent mismatch between what is locked on the source chain and what is credited (minted/unlocked) on the destination chain.

### Finding Description
In `send()`: [1](#0-0) 
the contract does `IERC20(_underlying).safeTransferFrom(msg.sender, address(this), params.amount)` without ever measuring the actual balance delta, then builds the dispatch message with `amount: params.amount`: [2](#0-1) 

This is the exact bug class described in the external report: rewards/deficits from non-standard token transfer semantics accrue silently instead of being tracked pro-rata. Notably, the codebase already demonstrates awareness of this exact issue and fixes it elsewhere: `IntentGatewayV2.placeOrder()` explicitly snapshots balances before/after each transfer and uses the measured delta (`received`) rather than the caller-supplied `amount` for escrow accounting and commitment hashing: [3](#0-2) 
`WrappedHyperFungibleToken`/`WrappedHyperFungibleTokenUpgradeable` never received the equivalent fix — `send()` and its counterpart `onAccept()` both operate purely on the caller/message-supplied `amount`: [4](#0-3) 

Because `HyperFungibleToken` peers mint/burn a dedicated cross-chain wrapped token 1:1 against whatever `amount` value is embedded in the `Message`, a `WrappedHyperFungibleToken` deployed over a fee-on-transfer or negative-rebasing token will systematically under-collateralize the credits it issues on remote chains: the locked balance in the wrapper contract will always be less than the sum of `amount`s it has claimed to lock.

### Impact Explanation
Each `send()` call through a fee-on-transfer/rebasing underlying locks strictly less than `params.amount`, while the destination chain (either the peer `WrappedHyperFungibleToken` unlocking `message.amount`, or a peer `HyperFungibleToken` minting `message.amount`) is instructed to release/mint the full, uncollected `amount`. Over repeated use this drains the wrapper's real collateral relative to its cross-chain liabilities. Later legitimate users trying to bridge back and unlock their share of the underlying token from the source-chain wrapper will find the contract insolvent — `onAccept()`'s `safeTransfer(beneficiary, message.amount)` will fail or drain funds belonging to other depositors, i.e., permanent loss of user funds / unbacked cross-chain mint.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by any unprivileged user calling `send()` directly — no privileged role required. The only precondition is that the underlying token configured for a given `WrappedHyperFungibleToken` instance has fee-on-transfer, deflationary, or negative-rebasing semantics (a token-selection/deployment property, not an attacker capability), matching the "unprivileged token bridger" reachability bar. Given many real-world tokens implement such transfer semantics, and the contract provides no allow-list or measured-amount safeguard (unlike `IntentGatewayV2`), likelihood is elevated wherever such a token is wrapped.

### Recommendation
Mirror the pattern already used in `IntentGatewayV2.placeOrder()`: snapshot the wrapper's underlying-token balance immediately before and after `safeTransferFrom`, and use the measured delta as `params.amount` for both the locked collateral and the dispatched `Message.amount`. Apply the same fix to `WrappedHyperFungibleTokenUpgradeable.send()`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy `WrappedHyperFungibleToken` with `_underlying` set to a fee-on-transfer ERC20 that takes, e.g., a 1% fee on every transfer.
2. Alice calls `send({amount: 1000e18, dest: ChainB, to: Alice, ...})`. `safeTransferFrom` moves only 990e18 into the wrapper (contract balance increases by 990e18), but the dispatched `Message.amount` is `1000e18`. [1](#0-0) 
3. On Chain B, the peer contract processes the incoming request and mints/unlocks `1000e18` to Alice (10e18 more than was actually escrowed on Chain A).
4. Alice bridges the 1000e18 back from Chain B to Chain A. Chain A's `onAccept()` calls `safeTransfer(Alice, 1000e18)`, but the wrapper only ever received 990e18 net from Alice's original deposit — the extra 10e18 is paid out of other users' locked collateral, and repeating this leaves the wrapper permanently under-collateralized.
