The key question: after `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` floors the supply index, can a new depositor mint shares at the depressed index and later capture the orphaned cash once the index re-normalizes? Check the guards and mint math.### Title
Supplier entering after a bad-debt write-down mints shares at the floored supply index and drains orphaned pool cash once the index re-accrues - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` permanently compresses the supply index (floored at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW = RAY/1000`) while leaving the pool's residual cash untouched. Because share minting divides by the live index and `require_backed_market` measures the claim at the floored index, a depositor entering right after the write-down mints up to ~1000x the normal scaled supply. The index then re-normalizes through ordinary interest accrual — which the attacker can self-fund through an own borrow — and the attacker withdraws at the restored index, extracting far more than deposited from cash that no surviving claim legitimately owns.

### Finding Description
Bad-debt cleanup (`seize_positions` borrow leg in `contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:24-28`) calls `interest::apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, which multiplies `supply_index` by `(supplied_value − bad_debt)/supplied_value` and floors it at `RAY/1000` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89`). The `cash` book is never reduced — the seized collateral is reclassified as revenue shares (`absorb_supply_as_revenue`), so `cash` still reflects pre-write-down deposits while `supplied × supply_index` collapses.

Two facts make this exploitable:

1. `supply` is gated only by `require_backed_market` (`contracts/pool/src/guards.rs:52-66`), which compares the *floored* claim `supplied × supply_index` against `cash + debt`. After the write-down the claim is ~1/1000 of before, so shortfall is 0 and supply is admitted — no recapitalization is needed.
2. `calculate_scaled_supply` mints `floor(amount × RAY / supply_index)` (`contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:30-37`), so at the floor index each deposited unit mints ~1000x the shares it would at `RAY`.

The write-down is not restored by `recapitalize` (per the runbook, it only credits cash), but the index *does* recover through normal accrual: `update_supply_index` distributes borrower interest over `supplied × supply_index`, which is now tiny, so modest interest lifts the index sharply. The attacker can even finance the recovery themselves: borrow pool cash, let interest accrue (their dominant share count recaptures ~all of it, less `reserve_factor`), until the index approaches `RAY` again. At that point the attacker's claim is ~1000x their deposit, and `withdraw` pays it out of the residual cash orphaned by the write-down — cash that should otherwise accrue to written-down revenue shares and honest post-event suppliers pro rata.

This is structurally identical to the yBOLD report: a loss event depresses the share price (index write-down), the attacker enters at the discount, a later profit event restores the share price (interest-driven index recovery over a shrunken supply base), and the attacker redeems at the restored valuation, stealing residual pool value.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of pool funds: the attacker converts orphaned backing cash (created when claims were written down but cash was not) into a withdrawable claim of up to ~1000x their deposit. Losses land on protocol revenue and any supplier who entered at or after the write-down at fair terms. Magnitude scales with the residual cash in the affected market; at the `RAY/1000` floor the mint multiplier is up to 1000x (docs/reference/formulas.md:427 confirms the floor admits ~1000x share inflation).

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a market that suffers permissionless `clean_bad_debt` (debt > collateral, collateral ≤ $5 dust) — an event any unprivileged caller can trigger — leaving residual cash, plus attacker capital to deposit and optionally self-fund index recovery via an own borrow. All steps (`clean_bad_debt`, `supply`, `borrow`, `update_indexes`, `withdraw`) are permissionless entrypoints. The main uncertainty is how much residual cash a realistic dust-capped cleanup leaves behind; the attack is only profitable when `cash + debt` meaningfully exceeds the floored claim.

### Recommendation
On bad-debt write-down, either burn the orphaned excess cash backing (e.g., reduce `cash` or transfer the surplus to revenue in asset terms rather than share terms), or gate `supply` on a minimum `supply_index` / require recapitalization before new shares can be minted at a written-down index. Alternatively, mint shares against a hypothetical un-written-down index so post-loss entrants cannot capture the recovery.

### Proof of Concept
1. Market (hub H, token T) has suppliers and outstanding borrows; an insolvent account leaves dust collateral and T-denominated bad debt.
2. Attacker calls `controller::clean_bad_debt(caller, account_id)` — permissionless. Pool `seize_positions` writes T's `supply_index` down (toward `RAY/1000`); `cash` is unchanged.
3. Attacker calls `controller::supply` into (H, T) with amount X. `require_backed_market` passes (floored claim << cash + debt); `calculate_scaled_supply` mints ≈ 1000·X scaled shares.
4. Attacker calls `borrow` to draw pool cash, letting `update_indexes`/any op accrue interest; interest distributed over the tiny `supplied × supply_index` drives `supply_index` back toward `RAY`. The attacker's ~100% share dominance recaptures nearly all paid interest.
5. Once the index is restored, attacker calls `withdraw`/`repay`, redeeming shares for up to ~1000·X of pool cash, funded by the residual orphaned backing.
