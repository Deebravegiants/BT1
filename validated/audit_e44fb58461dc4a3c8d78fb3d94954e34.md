### Title
Relayer fee withdrawal zeroes `Fees` on Hyperbridge before destination payout succeeds, permanently freezing relayer funds if the destination host's fee-token balance is insufficient - (File: `modules/pallets/relayer/src/withdrawal.rs`)

### Summary
This maps to the OpenQ "insufficient-balance-permanently-freezes-a-claim" bug class: a claim/withdraw path marks the claim as settled (or zeroes the entitlement) *before* the actual token transfer is confirmed to succeed, and once that marker is flipped there is no way to re-attempt the payout for the original amount if the transfer later fails.

### Finding Description
`Pallet::withdraw` in `modules/pallets/relayer/src/withdrawal.rs` reads the relayer's accrued `available_amount` from `Fees::<T>::get(...)` [1](#0-0) , dispatches an ISMP `POST` request instructing the destination chain's `HostManager`/`EvmHost` to pay that amount out of the fee token, and then unconditionally zeroes `Fees` for that relayer/chain in the same extrinsic [2](#0-1) . The zeroing is not conditioned on the destination-side transfer ever completing — it is only conditioned on the local `dispatch_request` call succeeding.

On the destination side, `HostManager.onAccept` decodes the `Withdraw` action and calls `IHostManager(_params.host).withdraw(withdrawParams)` [3](#0-2) , which ultimately transfers `amount` of the configured fee token out of the host contract's own balance. If that balance is insufficient (e.g. protocol fee accrual on that chain is smaller than the amount now owed, due to legitimate collection lag or accounting drift across chains), the ERC20 transfer reverts, the whole `onAccept` call fails, and per the host's message-handling pattern the request receipt is deleted so it "can be retried" [4](#0-3) . However, retrying redelivers the exact same `PostRequest` body carrying the same fixed `amount` — it can never succeed until someone tops up the destination host's fee-token balance, and there is no unprivileged mechanism to do so from the ISMP request itself.

Meanwhile, back on Hyperbridge, the relayer's `Fees` entry for that (dest_chain, address) pair was already zeroed the moment the withdrawal extrinsic executed [5](#0-4) . The relayer cannot call `withdraw` again for the same amount since their tracked balance is now `0`. This is structurally identical to the OngoingBountyV1 pattern: a state flag/balance is committed to "claimed"/"zero" state ahead of (and independent from) confirmation that the actual asset transfer succeeded, so a transient or persistent balance shortfall on the paying side permanently stalls/loses the payout for the claimant, with no sweep or retry-with-adjusted-amount path.

### Impact Explanation
A relayer's legitimately earned fees become permanently unclaimable whenever the destination `EvmHost`/`HostManager` fee-token balance is smaller than the amount recorded in `Fees` at the time `withdraw` is called. Because `Fees` is zeroed unconditionally and irreversibly at dispatch time rather than at confirmed-delivery time, this is a permanent freezing of relayer funds reachable by any relayer's own unprivileged `withdraw` call — not an admin action.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate: it requires the aggregate amount owed to relayers on a given destination chain (accumulated via `accumulate_fees`) to exceed the fee-token balance actually held by that chain's `EvmHost` contract at the moment a `withdraw` extrinsic executes and its request is delivered. This can occur under normal operation whenever fee accrual and payout timing diverge across chains (e.g., a relayer batches many deliveries and withdraws before enough matching protocol fee revenue has landed on that specific destination), and does not require any malicious or compromised party.

### Recommendation
Do not zero `Fees` optimistically at dispatch time. Instead:
- Keep the relayer's `Fees` balance intact until Hyperbridge receives confirmation (e.g., a callback/response from the destination `HostManager` request) that the transfer actually succeeded, then zero it only on confirmed success; or
- On failure of the destination-side transfer, propagate a timeout/failure response back to Hyperbridge that restores the relayer's `Fees` balance so the withdrawal can be retried once destination liquidity is available, mirroring the "so it can be retried" pattern already used for receipts in `EvmHost.dispatchIncoming`/`dispatchIncoming`-style handlers, but wired all the way back into `Fees` accounting rather than only into request-receipt retry state.

### Proof of Concept
1. Relayer R accumulates `Fees[Kusama(dest), R] = X` via `accumulate_fees`.
2. Destination `EvmHost`'s fee-token balance is currently `< X` (e.g. because other relayers already withdrew most of the accrued protocol fee revenue, or fee collection on that route has been light).
3. R calls `Pallet::withdraw`; the extrinsic succeeds locally, dispatches the `Withdraw` POST request, and sets `Fees[Kusama(dest), R] = 0` at `modules/pallets/relayer/src/withdrawal.rs:177`.
4. A relayer delivers the request to the destination; `HostManager.onAccept` → `IHostManager.withdraw` attempts to transfer `X` fee tokens but reverts (insufficient balance); `EvmHost` deletes the receipt so delivery "can be retried."
5. Every retry redelivers the same fixed `amount = X`; if the host's fee-token balance never reaches `X` (e.g. because that route just doesn't accrue that much fee revenue), the request never succeeds.
6. R's `Fees` entry is permanently `0` on Hyperbridge — the fee is unrecoverable through any relayer-callable path.
