### Title
Debt-valuation overflow in accrual permanently freezes a market before the borrow-index cap can engage - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
The borrow-index growth cap `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` is intended to bound index growth, but accrual computes `borrowed * new_index` in `calculate_supplier_rewards` *before* the capped index is ever applied. When `scaled borrowed shares × new index` exceeds `i128::MAX`, `Ray::mul` panics with `MathOverflow`. Since `global_sync` runs at the head of every pool entrypoint, the panic bricks the entire market: supply, borrow, withdraw, repay, liquidate, and `update_indexes` all revert forever, permanently freezing every supplier's and borrower's funds.

### Finding Description
`accrue_chunk` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:39-53`) calls `accrue_step`, which splits accrued interest via `calculate_supplier_rewards` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:73-89`):

```rust
let old_total_debt = borrowed.mul(env, old_borrow_index);
let new_total_debt = borrowed.mul(env, new_borrow_index);
```

`Ray::mul` widens to `I256` only for the intermediate; the *result* must fit `i128`, otherwise it panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` (`common/src/math/fp_core.rs:148-159`). The borrow index itself is capped by `update_borrow_index` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19`), but that cap only limits the index value — it does nothing to keep `borrowed × index` inside the `i128` domain. For an 18-decimal asset, RAY-normalized `borrowed` scales as `amount × 10^9`, so a market with ~10^9 whole tokens borrowed reaches the `i128` ceiling at an index of roughly 170×RAY — far below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. The cap is effectively dead code on the path that matters.

The same overflow shape exists in `update_supply_index` at `supplied.mul(old_index)` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:34`) and in `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` at `cache.supplied().mul(..., supply_index)` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:74`), so once total supplied value crosses the ceiling even bad-debt cleanup panics.

The in-repo harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361`) demonstrates the end state: `update_indexes` fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, the index cap never engaged, and subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` calls fail with the same error because every verb accrues first.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds. Once the market crosses the ray-value ceiling, `global_sync` panics on every subsequent invocation, so no user can withdraw supplied tokens, repay debt, be liquidated, or claim revenue. There is no admin escape hatch on this path — accrual is unconditional — and the panic is deterministic, so the freeze is permanent rather than temporary. All supplier principal and accrued yield in that (hub, token) book is locked forever; borrower collateral positions that depend on the frozen market's index updates are also stuck.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by unprivileged addresses through ordinary `supply`/`borrow` flows — no privileged call is needed to push the market into the failing state. The trigger conditions are demanding: an 18-decimal asset market needs roughly 10^9 whole tokens of debt at sustained ~98% utilization on a steep rate curve, and accrual must compound long enough for `borrowed × index` to exceed ~1.7×10^38. For low-decimal assets the ceiling is proportionally further away (debt would need ~10^9 × 10^(18−d) tokens), so practical exposure concentrates on high-decimal, high-utilization whale markets. An attacker cannot force utilization alone, but once organic market conditions reach the cliff there is no way back — the first `update_indexes` call after crossing it bricks the market. Medium likelihood, high severity.

### Recommendation
Bound the *product*, not just the index. Options, in increasing order of invasiveness:

- Compute the effective accrual in `accrue_step` by capping `new_borrow_index` at `i128::MAX / borrowed` (clamped also by `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`) so `borrowed.mul(new_index)` can never overflow — interest simply stops accruing at the ceiling instead of panicking.
- Make `calculate_supplier_rewards` compute `accrued_interest` via a saturating mul (`mul_div_floor_saturating` on `borrowed × index`) so the split clamps rather than traps; suppliers stop earning past the bound but the market stays live.
- Apply the same product-bound to `update_supply_index` (`supplied × old_index`, line 34) and `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` (line 74), which share the identical overflow precondition.
- Alternatively, cap `borrowed`/`supplied` scaled shares at listing time via `require_cap_within_asset_domain` so that `cap_ray × MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` is guaranteed to fit `i128` for every market, making the overflow unreachable by construction.

### Proof of Concept
Reproduced by the existing harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361`):

1. Create a market for an 18-decimal asset with the XLM rate curve; lift caps.
2. `supply` ~10^9 × 10^18 base units (BOB); `borrow` ~98% of it (ALICE).
3. Advance ledger time in 1-year steps and call `update_indexes`.
4. `try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"])` returns `Error(Contract, MATH_OVERFLOW)` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` — the value ceiling is hit before the cap.
5. `try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1)` and `try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0)` both fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, confirming the market is permanently frozen.

Note: the cliff is documented by this test (which itself asserts the bound in `docs/reference/formulas.md` is wrong), so if the freeze at the documented boundary is treated as an accepted design limit, the residual issue is that `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`/`MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` are dead safety rails — the documented cap cannot ever engage on the paths where it was meant to protect the market.
