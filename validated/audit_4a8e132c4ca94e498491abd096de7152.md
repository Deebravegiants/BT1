### Title
EmporiumStack signature omits stealthAddressStructure/recipient binding, enabling front-run redirection of wallet-authorized withdrawal proceeds - (File: contracts/external-actions/emporium/upgradeable/EmporiumUpgradeable.sol)

### Summary
`EMPORIUM_SIGNATURE_TYPEHASH` only commits to `emporiumMessage`, `ops` (endpoint/invokeWallet/value/callData hash), `maxFee`, and `deadline`; it never binds `circomData.stealthAddressStructure` or any other `CircomData` field. [2](#0-1)  Since `emporiumMessage` (the replay-protection nonce) is only marked used on successful execution and any unprivileged EOA can call `Hinkal.transact()` directly with their own valid ZK proof, an attacker who observes a victim's pending signed `EmporiumStack` in the mempool can front-run it with an otherwise identical transaction but their own `CircomData`/proof carrying an attacker-controlled `stealthAddressStructure`, redirecting the resulting shielded UTXO to themselves while the victim's real wallet-authorized DeFi action still executes as signed.

### Finding Description
Broken equality: (destination encoded in the shielded output UTXO created by `handleOut`, i.e. `circomData.stealthAddressStructure.stealthAddress`) is **not** constrained to equal anything inside the value signed under `EMPORIUM_SIGNATURE_TYPEHASH`.

Trace:
1. `verifyWallet` recovers the signer solely from `keccak256(EMPORIUM_SIGNATURE_TYPEHASH, circomData.emporiumMessage, _hashEmporiumOps(stack.ops), stack.maxFee, stack.deadline)`, and only checks `usedMessages[circomData.emporiumMessage]` for replay protection — set to `true` on the **first** successful call, regardless of caller. [3](#0-2) 
2. In Case 1, `IHinkalWallet(stack.signerAddress).callHinkalWallet(op.endpoint, op.callData, op.value)` executes the exact signed operation regardless of who submitted the transaction. [4](#0-3) 
3. `handleOut` computes `balanceChange` from real wallet-balance deltas and builds `UTXO(amount, erc20Address, circomData.stealthAddressStructure, timeStamp)` — the stealth address comes straight from the caller-supplied `circomData`, not from anything the owner signed. [5](#0-4) 
4. `createOnchainCommitment` hashes exactly `utxo.amount`, `utxo.erc20Address`, `utxo.stealthAddressStructure.stealthAddress`, and `utxo.timeStamp` into the leaf inserted into the tree — again, no binding to the original signer/owner. [1](#0-0) 
5. Because the attacker submits their **own** `CircomData`/proof (with their own nullifiers/UTXO ownership), the ZK proof and `verifyProof`/`buildVerifierId` checks pass on their own terms — the circuit only proves internal consistency of the attacker's inputs (`inTotal+amountChanges===outTotal`, nullifier/root validity), it does not attest that the resulting `stealthAddressStructure` belongs to the original signer of the `EmporiumStack`. [6](#0-5) 
6. If the attacker sets `circomData.onChainCreation[i] = true` for the relevant token, `Hinkal.transact`'s balance equation reduces to `balanceDif == utxoAmount`, fully decoupling the payout amount/destination from any off-chain proof-committed `amountChanges`, and the on-chain leaf is inserted directly from the attacker's `UTXO`. [7](#0-6) 

Exploit flow: attacker observes victim's signed `(ops, maxFee, deadline, emporiumMessage, v, r, s)` in a pending mempool transaction, then submits their own `Hinkal.transact()` call with identical `stack` but their own `CircomData` (own proof, own nullifiers, `stealthAddressStructure` pointing at attacker key material, `onChainCreation=true`) before the victim's transaction is mined. The victim's authorized wallet operation (e.g., withdrawing funds from a DeFi position into their EIP-7702 wallet) executes verbatim, but the resulting shielded UTXO/commitment is created under the attacker's stealth address instead of the victim's, and the victim's original transaction subsequently reverts with `UsedMessage`.

Existing guards do not prevent this: `verifyWallet`'s signature only authorizes *which calls run*, not *who receives the resulting UTXO*; `usedMessages` prevents replay but not front-running by a different caller with a different `CircomData`; the circuit constraints validate the attacker's own shielded-balance arithmetic, not a binding between the wallet owner and the destination stealth address.

### Impact Explanation
The attacker steals the real, on-chain value that the wallet-authorized operation produces (e.g., withdrawn DeFi principal/yield), because the resulting UTXO/shielded note is claimable only by the attacker's stealth key. This is direct theft of in-flight, wallet-owner-authorized funds — value the wallet owner authorized to move but never authorized to be routed to the attacker. It is repeatable against any observable pending Emporium-stack signed transaction where Case 1 (`invokeWallet`) produces a positive balance change.

### Likelihood Explanation
Preconditions: a victim must sign and broadcast (or have broadcast on their behalf) an `EmporiumStack` invoking `invokeWallet=true` operations that produce a positive token/ETH balance change on their EIP-7702 wallet, visible in a public mempool before confirmation. The attacker needs only to observe this pending transaction, construct their own valid `CircomData`/ZK proof (using their own UTXOs/nullifiers, which they can freely generate as an unprivileged prover), and outrace the victim's transaction — a standard, low-cost front-running scenario requiring no privileged role, relayer collusion, or compromised keys. This is feasible and repeatable for every applicable Emporium-invoked wallet withdrawal.

### Recommendation
Bind the intended output destination and asset accounting to the EIP-712 signature. Include `circomData.stealthAddressStructure`, `circomData.erc20TokenAddresses`, and ideally a caller/relay-agnostic recipient commitment directly in the `EMPORIUM_SIGNATURE_TYPEHASH` payload (or require the signature to bind `msg.sender`/`originalSender` and the full `CircomData` hash), so that only the actual message signer's intended `CircomData` can be paired with a given signature.

### Proof of Concept
Hardhat test plan:
1. Deploy `Hinkal`, `EmporiumUpgradeable`, `HinkalWallet` (as a mock EIP-7702 delegate), and a mock DeFi endpoint that pays out ERC20 tokens on call.
2. Owner (EOA) signs an `EmporiumStack` with one op: `invokeWallet=true`, `endpoint=mockDefi`, `callData=withdraw(...)`, over `emporiumMessage=M`, `maxFee`, `deadline`.
3. Owner constructs `CircomData` A with `stealthAddressStructure = ownerStealth`, generates a valid proof, and prepares (but does not yet send) `Hinkal.transact(...)`.
4. Attacker independently builds `CircomData` B with identical `emporiumMessage=M`, the same signed `stack` (same `v,r,s`), but `stealthAddressStructure = attackerStealth`, `onChainCreation[i]=true`; attacker generates their own valid proof for `CircomData` B using their own dummy/self-owned nullifiers.
5. Attacker calls `Hinkal.transact(...)` with `CircomData` B first (simulating front-run); assert it succeeds, the DeFi withdrawal executes, and `NewCommitment` is emitted with a leaf computed from `attackerStealth`.
6. Assert owner's subsequent `Hinkal.transact(...)` call with `CircomData` A reverts with `UsedMessage`.
7. Assert `createOnchainCommitment`'s resulting leaf/UTXO is spendable only by the attacker's stealth key, confirming redirected fund ownership — i.e., assert `EMPORIUM_SIGNATURE_TYPEHASH`'s signed payload (`ops`, `maxFee`, `deadline`, `emporiumMessage`) is identical in both A and B while `stealthAddressStructure` (and thus the commitment) differs.
