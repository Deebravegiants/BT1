### Title
Missing re-validation of the HyperSwap V2 `uint112` reserve bound after launch permits a pre-seed donation to permanently brick `finalizeGraduation`, freezing all curve-raised LT and the 250M LP-reserve tokens - ([File: packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol])

### Summary
`Bonding._deployAndSeed` enforces the `uint112`-overflow guard on the HyperSwap V2 pair's reserve slot exactly once, at launch time, based on the LT's *then-current* `exchangeRate()`. The guard bounds only the curve-derived `virtualLtReserve`/`ltFromPair` quantity that the protocol itself will later deposit. It never re-checks the actual pre-existing balance of the destination HyperSwap V2 pair at graduation time. Because that pair is permissionlessly creatable and directly donatable by anyone (the exact "hostile pre-seed" threat model the codebase already documents at length), an attacker can push the pair's real `LT` balance close to `type(uint112).max` before `finalizeGraduation` runs its deposit leg. When `Bonding`'s own `ltFromPair` deposit is added on top, the combined balance can exceed the pair's `uint112` reserve slot, which the analog of libssh2's missing bound on `packet_length` maps directly onto: a size value from an attacker-influenceable field is never checked against the true destination capacity at the point of use.

### Finding Description
`Bonding._deployAndSeed` computes the launch-time virtual LT reserve and checks it once against a headroom-adjusted `uint112` bound: [1](#0-0) 

The natspec is explicit about the assumption baked into this check — that the LT amount ultimately deposited into the HyperSwap V2 pair "peaks at `3 * virtualLtReserve`" — and that assumption is what the `/4` headroom is calibrated against: [2](#0-1) 

That reasoning is sound *only* for `ltFromPair`, the amount the curve itself will later drain and deposit via `Router.graduate` / `_prepareGraduationLiquidity`. It says nothing about the pre-existing balance of the destination HyperSwap V2 pair — a contract that the protocol's own threat model treats as fully attacker-influenceable between phase 1 and phase 2 of graduation: [3](#0-2) 

The defense built for that threat model (`_seedUniswapV2Direct` → `_seedRebalancing` → `_pairRebalance` / `_routerDepositAndDispose`) is designed to neutralize *ratio* skew and to guarantee the deposit never reverts on the *shape* of a pre-seed: [4](#0-3) 

but nowhere does it re-derive or re-check the `uint112` capacity of the actual destination pair before depositing `ltFromPair` (or the swap-rebalance amount) on top of whatever balance the attacker has already parked there. The only `uint112` safety margin in the whole protocol is the one-shot, launch-time, exchange-rate-relative check in `_deployAndSeed` — a check that has no visibility into, and provides no protection against, the pair's *actual* balance at the moment of deposit.

### Impact Explanation
UniswapV2-family pairs revert their `_update`/`mint`/`swap` path if `balance0` or `balance1` would exceed `type(uint112).max` after the transfer. If an attacker (or several colluding wallets, or repeated pre-seeding across the graduation window) drives the HyperSwap V2 pair's real LT balance close enough to `type(uint112).max` that adding `ltFromPair` (which can itself be up to `~0.75 * type(uint112).max` per the `_deployAndSeed` bound) tips it over, every call to `finalizeGraduation` for that token permanently reverts. Because `_enterGraduating` has already frozen trading and moved all curve-raised LT and the full 250M `LP_RESERVE` tokens into `Bonding`'s custody, and because `LPLock.recordLock` is a one-shot call `finalizeGraduation` cannot skip, there is no alternate path to unlock these assets — this is a permanent freeze of trader-contributed LT and the creator/LP-bound token allocation, not merely a griefing delay. This satisfies the "permanent freezing of trader, creator, or LP funds" bar.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachability requires only unprivileged, permissionless actions already named in scope: pre-creating the HyperSwap V2 TOKEN/LT pair and donating/transferring LT into it before `finalizeGraduation` runs — exactly the primitive the codebase's own `AGENTS.md` "HyperSwap Pre-Seed Defense" section describes as freely available to any attacker (`factory.createPair` is permissionless; `transfer` + `mint`/`sync` against the pair requires no privilege). The only extra ingredient versus the already-mitigated ratio-skew attack is *magnitude* rather than *ratio* — pushing raw balance near `2^112 - 1` (~5.19e15 LT at 18dp, i.e. an astronomically large but not infinite LT quantity) rather than a cheap ratio skew. Whether this magnitude is realistically fundable depends on the LT's actual circulating supply and mintability, which is external to this repo and cannot be verified from the contracts in scope, so likelihood is best characterized as Medium: the code path has no defense at all against this specific vector (unlike the well-tested ratio-skew vector), but the capital/LT-mint requirement to reach `~2^112` may be nontrivial depending on the live BounceTech LT's supply constraints.

### Recommendation
Before depositing in `_routerDepositAndDispose` (and before the `_pairRebalance` swap), read the HyperSwap V2 pair's live `getReserves()` and explicitly bound the sum of existing reserve + inbound deposit/swap amount against `type(uint112).max`, reverting or truncating deterministically well before hitting the pair's own hard revert. This closes the gap between the launch-time, exchange-rate-relative assumption in `_deployAndSeed` and the actual, attacker-controllable balance of the destination pair at graduation time, preserving the "must never revert under any pre-seed shape" brick-resistance contract that the rest of `_seedUniswapV2Direct` already tries to uphold for ratio-based attacks.

### Proof of Concept
1. Creator launches token `T` against LT `L` via `Zap.createToken`; `Bonding._deployAndSeed` passes the `uint112` launch check for `L`'s exchange rate at that time (`virtualLtReserve ≤ type(uint112).max / 4`).
2. Attacker (unprivileged) calls `IUniswapV2Factory(uniswapV2Factory).createPair(T, L)` to pre-create the HyperSwap V2 pair before graduation.
3. Attacker acquires or mints a very large quantity of `L` (external BounceTech LT, permissionless `mint`) and repeatedly `transfer`s it directly into the pre-created pair, pushing the pair's stored `L` reserve close to `type(uint112).max`.
4. Normal trading drives token `T` through `Zap.buy` to the graduation threshold; `_enterGraduating` fires, draining the curve's LT into `Bonding` and caching `ltFromPair`/`tokensForLP` in `pendingGraduation`.
5. Anyone calls `Bonding.finalizeGraduation(T)`. `_seedUniswapV2Direct`'s Regime 3 path computes the rebalance/deposit and calls `pair.swap`/`router.addLiquidity`, which internally attempts to write a post-transfer balance exceeding `type(uint112).max` on the `L` side; the V2 pair's own overflow guard reverts the call.
6. `finalizeGraduation` has no fallback for this condition — the token is permanently stuck in `Lifecycle.Graduating`, with all curve-raised LT and the 250M `LP_RESERVE` tokens frozen in `Bonding`, and `LPLock.recordLock` never callable for this token again.

```markdown
### The exploit

A vanilla UniswapV2 pair is deployable by anyone: `factory.createPair(token, lt)` is permissionless, and after creation anyone can call `pair.mint(to)` against pre-transferred tokens. So between phase 1 (`_enterGraduating` flips lifecycle to `Graduating` and caches `tokensForLP / ltFromPair`) and phase 2 (`finalizeGraduation` mints LP via `pair.mint(lpLock)`), an attacker can:

1. Front-run by calling `factory.createPair(token, lt)` themselves
2. `transfer(pair, smallToken)` and `transfer(pair, smallLT)` at any ratio they choose
3. Call `pair.mint(attacker)` — they now own LP at a hostile reserve ratio

When our `pair.mint(lpLock)` runs in phase 2 against this non-empty pair, V2's mint formula picks up the existing reserves:

```
liquidity = min(amount0 · totalSupply / reserve0, amount1 · totalSupply / reserve1)
```

The `min(...)` arm whose denominator is bigger relative to its numerator wins, and the OTHER arm's "excess" deposit is donated pro-rata to existing LP holders — i.e. to the attacker. Two harms:

- **Wrong opening price.** Post-mint reserves are `(R_attacker + T_a, R_attacker + T_b)`, so the LP opens at `(R_a + T_a) / (R_b + T_b)`, NOT at the curve close `T_a / T_b`. A `$15` LT pre-seed at 50% off curve close opens the pool ~454 bps off.
- **LP capture.** The wasted-side excess goes to the attacker's LP claim. A `1 wei + 1 LT` pre-seed (~`$1` attack budget) captures ~34 bps of LP.

A cheaper variant skips step 3 entirely: `transfer(pair, dust) + pair.sync()` forces the stored reserves to the dust ratio without minting any LP, leaving the pair at `reserves > 0 && totalSupply == 0`. Regime 1 below covers both shapes by keying on supply rather than reserves.

This is the same exploit class as the four.meme Feb 2025 incident (~$183K loss).
```
