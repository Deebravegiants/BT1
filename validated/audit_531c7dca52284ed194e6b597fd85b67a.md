### Title
Self-inflicted Aquarius LP oracle outage shields an account from liquidation and bad-debt cleanup - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs)

### Summary
A borrower can make one of its own collateral legs unpriceable on demand — withdrawing Aquarius liquidity so the pool value falls below `min_pool_value_wad` — which makes every valuation-dependent path on that account revert: `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and `withdraw`. The in-repo harness test `audit_supply_stale_shield.rs` proves the analogous stale-feed variant end-to-end: `supply` succeeds while the leg's price is broken, then liquidation, bad-debt cleanup, and withdrawal all revert until the price recovers. Unlike a passive stale feed, the Aquarius LP source lets an unprivileged address toggle the outage itself through its own pool liquidity, so it can keep a position unliquidatable exactly while its debt goes bad.

### Finding Description
`AquariusLpSource` prices an LP share from live pool reserves and rejects the quote with `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` when `pool_value_wad < lp.min_pool_value_wad` [1](#0-0) . `read` is invoked during strict price resolution; the controller prices every supply leg of an account when computing its health factor for `liquidate` and `clean_bad_debt`, so one failing leg aborts the whole call.

The attack sequence for a single unprivileged address:

1. Supply real collateral, borrow against it (e.g., `supply(USDC)`, `borrow(ETH)`).
2. `supply` a dust amount of an Aquarius LP share token that is a listed collateral in the account's spoke. Supply requires no price, so the leg is accepted even while the oracle read would fail — proven by the stale-WBTC analog where `try_supply(ALICE, "WBTC", 0.001)` succeeds and persists a non-zero scaled share [2](#0-1) .
3. The attacker is an LP in that Aquarius pool. When it wants protection, it withdraws its own liquidity so `pool_value_wad` drops below `min_pool_value_wad`, making the LP leg's price return `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity`.
4. Every subsequent valuation-dependent call on the account reverts: `liquidate` with `PRICE_FEED_STALE`-class errors, `clean_bad_debt`, and even the borrower's own `withdraw` — mirroring the test's assertions at lines 51–58 [3](#0-2) .
5. To unblock (e.g., to withdraw remaining collateral later), the attacker redeposits liquidity, restoring the quote — the oracle outage is fully under the attacker's control at the cost of temporarily parking liquidity.

### Impact Explanation
While the leg's price is broken, the account cannot be liquidated and its bad debt cannot be socialized via `clean_bad_debt`, which also reverts [4](#0-3) . If the collateral value decays during the outage window, the position becomes bad debt that still cannot be cleaned — interest keeps accruing on debt no one can seize, socializing the loss onto suppliers: protocol insolvency. This maps directly to the "temporary freezing of funds / insolvency" acceptance criteria, and the threat model itself registers this exact boundary (DoS.1: "For an Aquarius LP leg, liquidity providers can cause that outage by withdrawing pool value below `min_pool_value_wad` … The same leg blocks bad-debt cleanup and force-socialization") [5](#0-4) .

### Likelihood Explanation
Requirements are modest: a listed Aquarius LP collateral market and enough LP position in that pool to push pool value below `min_pool_value_wad` at will (or being the dominant LP). No privileged role, no oracle compromise, and no third party is needed — the attacker uses only `supply`, `borrow`, and its own Aquarius `withdraw_liquidity`/`deposit` trades, all in the allowed reach set. The mechanism is deterministic: any `liquidate` or `clean_bad_debt` attempt while the pool is thin reverts rather than degrading.

### Recommendation
Price collateral legs independently of the strict-fail path used for borrow power: during liquidation and bad-debt cleanup, treat an unpriceable or stale supply leg as zero-value collateral (skip it for HF bonus purposes, or seize it last/none) instead of aborting the entire call. Alternatively, allow `SeizeMode::Transfer`/`Credit` plans that exclude the failing leg from the pro-rata seizure set. At minimum, enforce `min_pool_value_wad` at `supply`/`update_account_threshold` time so an already-broken leg cannot be added or kept as a shield, and document that LP-asset collaterals whose pool value can be driven below the floor by a single LP carry unliquidatable-position risk.

### Proof of Concept
Adapt `audit_supply_stale_shield.rs` [6](#0-5) : replace the stale Reflector WBTC feed with an Aquarius LP market whose pool value is manipulated by the attacker's own `withdraw_liquidity` below `min_pool_value_wad`. Steps: supply collateral + borrow → `supply(dust LP share)` → attacker withdraws its Aquarius liquidity → crash the market so HF < 1 → `try_liquidate` and `try_clean_bad_debt_by_id` both revert with the oracle error while a twin account without the LP leg liquidates fine → redeposit liquidity → liquidation succeeds, confirming attacker-controlled toggling of the shield.

use test_harness::{errors, usd, usd_cents, LendingTest, PositionType, ALICE, BOB, LIQUIDATOR};

#[test]
fn audit_supply_setup_blocks_liquidation_via_stale_dust_leg() {
    let mut t = LendingTest::new()
        .three_asset_usdc_eth_wbtc()
        .with_dust_disabled_all_markets()
        .build();

    t.set_oracle_single_spot("WBTC");

    t.supply(ALICE, "USDC", 10_000.0);
    t.borrow(ALICE, "ETH", 3.0);
    t.supply(BOB, "USDC", 10_000.0);
    t.borrow(BOB, "ETH", 3.0);

    let pre = t.try_liquidate(LIQUIDATOR, ALICE, "ETH", 1.0);
    test_harness::assert_contract_error(pre, errors::HEALTH_FACTOR_TOO_HIGH);

    t.advance_time(5_000);
    let now = t.env.ledger().timestamp();
    let wbtc = t.resolve_asset("WBTC");
    t.mock_reflector_client()
        .set_price_at(&wbtc, &usd(60_000), &(now - 3_600));

    let plant = t.try_supply(ALICE, "WBTC", 0.001);
    assert!(
        plant.is_ok(),
        "supply must accept the leg even though WBTC's feed is stale: {plant:?}"
    );
    t.assert_position_exists(ALICE, "WBTC", PositionType::Supply);
    assert!(
        t.supply_balance_raw(ALICE, "WBTC") > 0,
        "poisoned WBTC leg must persist with a non-zero scaled share"
    );

    t.set_price("USDC", usd_cents(50));

    assert!(
        t.can_be_liquidated(BOB),
        "twin account must be underwater so the crash — not the leg — drives HF<1"
    );
    t.liquidate(LIQUIDATOR, BOB, "ETH", 1.0);
    assert!(
        t.borrow_balance(BOB, "ETH") < 3.0,
        "twin liquidation must succeed with fresh feeds"
    );

    let alice_id = t.resolve_account_id(ALICE);

    let liq = t.try_liquidate(LIQUIDATOR, ALICE, "ETH", 1.0);
    test_harness::assert_contract_error(liq, errors::PRICE_FEED_STALE);

    let clean = t.try_clean_bad_debt_by_id(alice_id);
    test_harness::assert_contract_error(clean, errors::PRICE_FEED_STALE);

    let wd = t.try_withdraw(ALICE, "WBTC", 0.0001);
    test_harness::assert_contract_error(wd, errors::PRICE_FEED_STALE);

    t.set_price("WBTC", usd(60_000));
    let recovered = t.try_liquidate(LIQUIDATOR, ALICE, "ETH", 1.0);
    assert!(
        recovered.is_ok(),
        "once WBTC is fresh again, the identical liquidation must succeed: {recovered:?}"
    );
    assert!(
        t.borrow_balance(ALICE, "ETH") < 3.0,
        "post-recovery liquidation must reduce ALICE's debt"
    );
}
