### Title
JIT (Just-In-Time) Liquidity Sniping via Deposit-then-Withdraw Around Swaps Lets an Attacker Steal Pro-Rata Trading Fee Yield from Long-Term LPs - (File: `program/src/processor.rs`)

### Summary
Raydium's `Deposit` and `Withdraw` instructions carry no time-lock, minimum holding period, or fee/penalty on same-block redemption. Because `Withdraw` re-computes `calc_take_pnl` and redeems `total_pc_without_take_pnl` / `total_coin_without_take_pnl` strictly pro-rata to LP-token share at withdrawal time, an attacker who deposits liquidity immediately before a large swap and withdraws immediately after it captures a full pro-rata share of that swap's accrued trading-fee growth, without ever bearing the price risk or providing durable liquidity that long-term LPs take on. This is the same underlying bug class as the referenced report's "JIT deposit before yield distribution, withdraw right after" attack, applied to Raydium's constant-product pool fee/PnL accrual mechanism instead of Sturdy's `distributeYield`.

### Finding Description
`process_deposit` mints LP tokens based on the pool's current reserves (`total_coin_without_take_pnl`/`total_pc_without_take_pnl`, computed from live vault balances) relative to `amm.lp_amount`: [1](#0-0) 

`process_withdraw` burns LP tokens and pays out coin/pc strictly proportional to the withdrawer's LP-token share of `total_coin_without_take_pnl`/`total_pc_without_take_pnl` at the moment of withdrawal, after first calling `calc_take_pnl` to realize any invariant (`k`) growth since the last snapshot: [2](#0-1) 

`calc_take_pnl` measures growth of the pool's `x*y` invariant between the last recorded baseline (`target.calc_pnl_x`/`calc_pnl_y`) and the live reserves, and skims only `pnl_numerator/pnl_denominator` (12%) to the protocol, leaving the remaining ~88% of any invariant growth (driven by `swap_fee_numerator`, 25bps per swap) inside the pool, increasing the value of every outstanding LP token pro-rata: [3](#0-2) 

Because a swap's fee (`swap_fee_numerator`/`swap_fee_denominator`) is deducted from `amount_in` before the constant-product math and stays in the vaults, growing `total_coin_without_take_pnl`/`total_pc_without_take_pnl` for whoever holds LP tokens at the time of the next `Deposit`/`Withdraw`/`WithdrawPnl` call that reprices the pool: [4](#0-3) 

There is no mechanism anywhere in `Deposit`/`Withdraw` (no epoch/slot check tied to the depositor's own LP position, no withdrawal fee, no minimum holding duration) that prevents the same LP-token holder from depositing and withdrawing within the same block or an atomically-ordered set of transactions. The only state that changes on deposit besides `lp_amount` is the `target_orders.calc_pnl_x`/`calc_pnl_y` baseline, which is reset to include the depositor's newly added funds: [5](#0-4) 

This means an attacker (via a searcher/validator MEV bundle, exactly the "gas auction / Flashbots-style ordering" mechanism described in the referenced report, adapted to Solana's leader/Jito-bundle equivalent) can:
1. Detect a pending large `SwapBaseIn`/`SwapBaseOut` transaction (or any transaction expected to move significant volume through the pool).
2. Insert a `Deposit` instruction immediately before it, becoming a large fraction of `amm.lp_amount`.
3. Let the victim's swap execute, growing `total_coin_without_take_pnl`/`total_pc_without_take_pnl` via the swap fee.
4. Insert a `Withdraw` instruction immediately after, redeeming their LP tokens at the new, fee-inflated exchange rate.

The attacker earns their pro-rata share of the trading fee revenue generated by the sandwiched swap while holding the position for less than one block, with zero exposure to impermanent loss or genuine liquidity risk, at the direct expense of the pool's pre-existing LPs whose expected fee yield per unit of capital-time is diluted.

### Impact Explanation
This directly transfers value away from genuine, long-term LP-token holders in `AmmInfo`-governed pools to the transient MEV attacker, since every unit of the swap fee captured by the attacker's temporary share is a unit no longer distributed pro-rata to real liquidity providers. Across pools with meaningful swap volume relative to pool depth, this can be repeated on essentially every large swap, resulting in a systematic and repeatable siphoning of LP yield — a concrete leak/theft of LP funds. This mirrors the Medium severity assigned to the analogous Sturdy finding, since funds are not directly stolen from the vault in a single shot, but LP yield is unfairly and repeatably redirected under a reachable, permissionless MEV path.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires only standard permissionless instructions (`Deposit`, `Withdraw`, and knowledge of an upcoming swap) and MEV-style transaction ordering capability, which is a normal, unprivileged capability on any blockchain with searcher/bundle infrastructure (e.g., Jito on Solana), not a "malicious validator" exploit. No special permissions, account ownership, or protocol misconfiguration are needed — only capital and the ability to order or closely time transactions around a known/observed large swap. Given `Deposit`/`Withdraw` are open to any account holding the mint tokens and there is no cooldown, this is straightforward to execute repeatedly.

### Recommendation
Introduce an anti-JIT mitigation such as: (1) a minimum holding period (e.g., measured in slots/epochs) between a `Deposit` and a subsequent `Withdraw` for the same LP-token holder before full pro-rata redemption is allowed, (2) a withdrawal fee that decays over holding time so instantaneous round-trips are unprofitable, or (3) accruing swap-fee-driven pool growth into a snapshot-based accounting scheme so that only LPs who held their position across the relevant swap(s) receive the associated fee growth, rather than whoever holds LP tokens at redemption time.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker monitors for a large upcoming `SwapBaseIn`/`SwapBaseOut` instruction targeting a given `AmmInfo` pool.
2. Attacker submits (via a searcher bundle ordered ahead of / around the victim's swap):
   - `Deposit` with `max_coin_amount`/`max_pc_amount` sized to acquire a large fraction of `amm.lp_amount` (see `program/src/processor.rs:1200-1350`).
3. Victim's swap executes, paying `swap_fee_numerator/swap_fee_denominator` (25 bps) into the vaults, growing `total_coin_without_take_pnl`/`total_pc_without_take_pnl` (see `program/src/processor.rs:2372-2384`).
4. Attacker submits `Withdraw` for their full LP balance immediately after, which calls `calc_take_pnl` and redeems coin/pc pro-rata to the now fee-inflated reserves (`program/src/processor.rs:1719-1761`), capturing ~88% of the invariant growth attributable to the victim's swap that is proportional to the attacker's temporary LP share — despite holding the position for a negligible amount of time and bearing no market risk.
