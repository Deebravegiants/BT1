### Title
Borrow-index accrual overflows i128 before the RAY index cap, permanently freezing all operations on a high-utilization market — (contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs)

### Summary
Every pool verb accrues interest before acting, and accrual multiplies scaled debt shares by the RAY borrow index via `scaled_to_original`. On a very large book at sustained high utilization, the product `scaled_borrowed * borrow_index` exceeds `i128` capacity and panics inside `scaled_to_original` **before** the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` index cap engages. Once the index crosses this cliff, `MathOverflow` is raised on every subsequent accrual, so `borrow`, `repay`, `withdraw`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `flash_loan`, and `update_indexes` all revert for that market forever — a permanent freeze of all supplied and borrowed funds, analogous to the CVE class where an operation path crashes the system into a denial of service.

### Finding Description
`Cache::calculate_utilization` and all market operations unscale the book with `scaled_to_original(&env, self.borrowed, self.borrow_index)` [1](#0-0) . The RAY multiply is checked (`checked_add`/`MathOverflow` style helpers from `common::rates`), but there is no saturation: once `borrowed_scaled * borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX`, accrual panics. The harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` proves the sequence: a whale supplies `BILLION * 10^18` units of an 18-decimal asset, a borrower takes 98% utilization, and after enough elapsed time `update_indexes` fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` calls fail identically [2](#0-1) . The threat-model doc acknowledges the class but treats it only as a numeric-limit caveat [3](#0-2) .

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds and a market unable to operate: no supplier can withdraw, no borrower can repay, no liquidator can liquidate, and bad-debt cleanup cannot run, because all entrypoints accrue interest first. All tokens physically held by the pool for that market — including the attacker's own borrowed amount, which is never repaid — are locked indefinitely. This satisfies the accepted "permanent freezing of funds" / "contract unable to operate" impact classes.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by a single unprivileged address using only `supply` + `borrow` (or riding an existing large book) plus permissionless `update_indexes` calls; no privileged role is needed. However, exploitation requires whale-scale capital (a supply on the order of `10^27` raw units of an 18-decimal token on a steep-segment curve) and sustained high utilization over a long accrual horizon, so practical feasibility depends on a large-supply listing existing or the attacker funding one. Medium.

### Recommendation
Introduce a checked/saturation guard in accrual before `scaled_to_original` panics: e.g., cap `borrow_index`/`supply_index` at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`-style ceilings *before* the value multiply overflows, or clamp the unscaled result to `i128::MAX` and let the index cap logic freeze accrual gracefully. Ensure the cap engages strictly before the value-ceiling cliff so `repay`/`withdraw`/`clean_bad_debt` remain executable (at minimum a wind-down mode that skips accrual for exits).
