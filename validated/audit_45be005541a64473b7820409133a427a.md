### Title

Unpriceable Aquarius LP collateral dust blocks liquidation and bad-debt cleanup - (File: contracts/controller/src/risk/totals.rs)

### Summary

A borrower can add a small Aquarius LP token as an additional collateral position, then withdraw enough liquidity from the referenced Aquarius pool to make that LP token unpriceable. Liquidation and permissionless bad-debt cleanup price every supply position in the account, so the single unusable leg causes both entrypoints to revert even when the account is insolvent. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description

`liquidate` builds its plan by calling `calculate_account_risk_totals`, which iterates every supply position and calls `cache.cached_price(&hub_asset.asset)` before checking whether the account's health factor is below one. [1](#0-0) [3](#0-2) 

`Context::load_markets` fetches prices for all unique collateral and debt assets through the configured price aggregator, and `fetch_prices` propagates aggregator failures rather than substituting a zero or unusable collateral value. [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

An `AquariusLpSource` explicitly treats a pool whose computed value is below `min_pool_value_wad` as unpriceable. [6](#0-5)  The provider computes LP fair value and pool value, then returns `OracleError::InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` when the pool value is below that configured floor. [7](#0-6) 

The same all-position valuation is performed by standalone `clean_bad_debt`, so the poisoned collateral leg blocks both ordinary liquidation and the permissionless socialization path. [8](#0-7) 

The repository already has a regression test demonstrating the general bug shape: a borrower supplies a tiny collateral amount after its feed is stale, and subsequent liquidation, cleanup, and withdrawal all revert with `PRICE_FEED_STALE`. [9](#0-8) 

### Impact Explanation

An underwater borrower can temporarily prevent every liquidation and `clean_bad_debt` call against their account while the referenced Aquarius pool remains below its configured liquidity floor. This delays seizure of other, perfectly priceable collateral and can leave debt outstanding while interest continues to accrue, increasing eventual supplier losses or protocol insolvency. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10) 

The impact is stronger than blocking only the LP leg: the oracle error aborts valuation of the entire account before repayment sizing or collateral seizure can proceed. [12](#0-11) 

### Likelihood Explanation

The borrower controls the prerequisite account and can call `supply(caller, account_id, spoke_id, assets)` with a dust amount of an Aquarius LP collateral market; the owner/delegate is permitted to add a new supply leg. [13](#0-12) [14](#0-13) 

The borrower or another unprivileged liquidity provider can then reduce the referenced Aquarius pool below `min_pool_value_wad`, at which point every LP price read returns `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity`. [15](#0-14)  Mainnet configuration includes Aquarius LP oracle sources with nonzero `min_pool_value_wad` floors, including an AQUA/USDC LP configuration. [16](#0-15) 

The attack requires the borrower to hold or acquire only a dust amount of LP collateral, but making a pool unpriceable may require controlling or removing enough pool liquidity to cross its configured floor. Recovery requires pool liquidity to return above the floor or another valid price path, so the freezing window can persist for as long as the pool remains below the floor.

### Recommendation

Do not make account-level liquidation and bad-debt admission depend on successfully pricing collateral that cannot be seized or valued safely. For liquidation and cleanup, isolate unavailable-price collateral legs from the health-factor and proportional-seizure plan, or assign them an explicit conservative value of zero while retaining the normal strict-price behavior for borrow and withdrawal gates. Add regression coverage where an Aquarius LP collateral leg crosses `min_pool_value_wad`, then verify that `liquidate` and `clean_bad_debt` still execute on the remaining priceable positions.

### Proof of Concept

1. The attacker owns account `A` with normal collateral and outstanding debt.
2. The attacker calls:
   ```text
   Controller::supply(
       caller = attacker,
       account_id = A,
       spoke_id = S,
       assets = [(HubAssetKey { hub_id: H, asset: AQUARIUS_LP }, dust_amount)]
   )
   ```
   `process_supply` authorizes the caller, loads or creates the account, and deposits the LP leg without pre-pricing it. [17](#0-16) 
3. After prices move and `A` becomes liquidatable, the attacker withdraws their own liquidity from the Aquarius pool configured for `AQUARIUS_LP`, reducing its computed value below `min_pool_value_wad`.
4. A liquidator calls:
   ```text
   Controller::liquidate(
       liquidator = liquidator,
       account_id = A,
       debt_payments = [(HubAssetKey { hub_id: H, asset: DEBT }, amount)],
       seize_mode = SeizeMode::Transfer
   )
   ```
   `build_liquidation_plan` prices every supply position, including the unavailable LP leg, and the transaction reverts with `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` before checking `health_factor < 1`. [1](#0-0) 
5. A keeper calls `Controller::clean_bad_debt(caller, A)`; `socialize_bad_debt` repeats the same all-position valuation and reverts before reaching the insolvency/dust admission check. [18](#0-17) 
6. The existing stale-feed regression demonstrates the same externally reachable denial pattern: a dust collateral leg makes liquidation, cleanup, and withdrawal revert until the feed becomes usable again. [19](#0-18)
