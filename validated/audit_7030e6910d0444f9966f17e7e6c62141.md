### Title
Whale-scale debt book overflows i128 during accrual before `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` engages, permanently freezing the market - ([File: common/src/rates/index.rs](common/src/rates/index.rs))

### Summary
The ClamAV report is a crash-class bug: an edge condition (improper initialization → NULL read) panics the process and denies service. The XOXNO analog is the ray-value overflow cliff in interest accrual. `update_borrow_index` caps the *index* at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, but nothing caps the *value* product `scaled_shares * index`. On a large market at sustained high utilization, `scaled_to_original`/`Ray::mul` panics with `MathOverflow` long before the index cap engages. Since every pool mutator accrues first (`Cache::load` → `interest::global_sync`), once the state crosses the cliff the market is bricked: no withdraw, repay, borrow, supply, liquidation, or `update_indexes` ever succeeds again.

### Finding Description
`update_borrow_index` in `common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19` multiplies `old_index * interest_factor` and clamps the result to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` — but the debt *value* (`borrowed * borrow_index`, computed in `calculate_supplier_rewards` at lines 80-81 and in `scaled_to_original` via `fp_core` mul-div) is unbounded. With `borrowed` scaled shares large enough, `borrowed.raw() * index.raw()` exceeds `i128::MAX` while `index` is still far below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, so `Ray::mul` → `fp_core::try_mul_div_*` returns `None` → `panic_with_error!(MathOverflow)` (`common/src/math/fp_core.rs:117`).

Per `contracts/pool/README.md:159-168`, every mutation of an existing market runs `Cache::load → interest::global_sync → mutate → guards → commit`. The accrual is the first step, so the overflow panics before any state change — permanently, for every entrypoint, since accrual is monotone and the oversized product only grows.

An unprivileged attacker reaches this through ordinary entrypoints: `controller.supply` a large amount on a high-decimals market, `controller.borrow` to push utilization onto the steep segment of the rate curve, then `update_indexes` (permissionless) lets time compound the index until the cliff. The harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-361`) demonstrates exactly this: ~1 billion BIG18 tokens supplied, 98% borrowed on a 175%-APR curve, and within the tested horizon `update_indexes` reverts with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`; subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` revert identically.

### Impact Explanation
**Permanent freezing of funds** (accepted impact). Once the debt-value product crosses `i128::MAX`, every supplier's deposit, all outstanding debt, and all accrued revenue in that (hub, token) market are unrecoverable: `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `recapitalize`, and `claim_revenue` all funnel through the panicking accrual step. There is no rescue path — the cap on the index does not help because the panic is in the value multiplication, not the index update, and no privileged function can shrink `borrowed` without first accruing. Bad debt in the frozen market also cannot be cleaned, blocking any insolvency resolution.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium-to-low per-market, but non-trivial. It requires (a) a market listed at high decimals (up to 18 is permitted) with borrow caps lifted to `max_cap_for_decimals`, (b) whale-scale supply — the test uses ~10^9 tokens — held at high utilization on a steep rate curve, and (c) sustained accrual without deleveraging. An attacker with the capital can deliberately create the condition (supply + borrow own position to pin utilization near max) and then the freeze is purely time-driven and permissionless to trigger via `update_indexes`. No governance action, oracle manipulation, or privileged access is required. The capital cost and duration lower the probability, but the absence of any mitigation or recovery path makes the consequence absolute.

### Recommendation
Bound the value product, not just the index. Options:
- In `interest::global_sync`/`accrue_step`, clamp `new_borrow_index` to `min(MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY, i128::MAX / max(borrowed.raw(), 1))` so `scaled_to_original(borrowed, index)` can never overflow — debt stops accruing at the ceiling instead of bricking.
- Alternatively, cap total borrows so `borrowed * MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY < i128::MAX` at the `borrow` entrypoint (a derived per-decimals ceiling), making the cliff unreachable by construction.
- At minimum, make accrual saturate rather than panic: compute `new_total_debt` via `mul_div_floor_saturating` and derive `accrued_interest` from the saturated value, so the market freezes interest growth but stays withdrawable/liquidatable.

### Proof of Concept
Existing test: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-361` — `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap`. Steps it performs:

1. Create market `BIG18` (18 decimals, $1 price) with the steep `xlm_curve` (`max_borrow_rate = 175%`, `optimal_utilization = 75%`, `max_utilization = 100%`), plus `COL` collateral market; lift caps via `lift_caps`.
2. BOB supplies `1e9 * 10^18` BIG18; ALICE supplies COL collateral and borrows 98% of BIG18.
3. Advance time in 1-year steps calling `try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"])` — a permissionless call. Within the asserted bound it fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (asserted at line 350-353).
4. Assert the freeze: `try_withdraw_raw(BOB, ...)` and `try_repay(ALICE, ...)` both revert `MATH_OVERFLOW` (lines 355-356) — no exit, no repayment, no liquidation path remains.
