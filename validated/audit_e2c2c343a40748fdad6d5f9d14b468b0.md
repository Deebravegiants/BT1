### Title
Outbound delivery rewards are read at claim time rather than pinned at delivery time, letting governance retroactively reduce or zero out rewards already earned by relayers - ([File: modules/pallets/relayer/src/outbound_request.rs])

### Summary
`pallet-relayer` pays relayers for delivering hyperbridge-originated requests (`OutboundRequestDeliveryReward`, keyed by `module_id`) and for delivering mandatory consensus rotations (`OutboundConsensusDeliveryReward`, keyed by destination `StateMachine`). Both reward amounts are plain governance-settable `StorageMap` values with no snapshotting, and both are read live at claim time rather than being pinned to the value in effect when the relayer actually performed the (unprivileged, permissionless) delivery.

### Finding Description
An unprivileged relayer permissionlessly delivers a hyperbridge-originated request to a destination chain (e.g. via the tesseract messaging pipeline), and the delivery receipt is durably recorded in the destination's `RequestReceipts[commitment]` (or `EvmHost._epochs[set_id]` for consensus rotations). The relayer earns the right to claim a reward for this real, already-performed work, but the claim is a separate, later, unsigned transaction (`claim_outbound_request_delivery_reward` / `claim_outbound_consensus_delivery_reward`).

At claim time, `process_outbound_request_delivery_claim` reads the reward as: [1](#0-0) 

and `process_outbound_consensus_delivery_claim` similarly reads: [2](#0-1) 

Both maps are updated at any time by governance-permissioned calls with no restriction on already-delivered-but-unclaimed work: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

Nothing snapshots the reward rate at the moment the request/rotation was dispatched or delivered. So the amount a relayer receives for a given piece of already-completed, real delivery work depends purely on whichever reward value happens to be live in storage the moment they submit the claim — not the value that was advertised or that applied when they actually incurred the gas cost of delivery. This is the same root-cause pattern as the external report: a coefficient that determines a reward owed for a past, already-recorded action is read live rather than pinned at the time the entitlement was earned.

### Impact Explanation
If governance lowers or zeroes `OutboundRequestDeliveryReward[module_id]` (or `OutboundConsensusDeliveryReward[destination]`) after relayers have already delivered messages/rotations under the previous, higher rate but before they submit their claim, those relayers are retroactively shortchanged or, in the reward=0 case, permanently denied any reward for delivery work they already performed and expended real gas for (`OutboundRequestNoRewardConfigured` / `OutboundNoRewardConfigured` rejects the claim outright once the reward hits zero, since zero also means "off the allowlist"). Two relayers who deliver functionally identical requests for the same module get paid different amounts purely based on the order/timing of their claim submission relative to a rate change — an unfair and inconsistent economic outcome, and in the worst case a permanent loss of an already-earned reward for the relayer, since there is no other path to recover it once the on-chain reward is zeroed and the commitment sits unclaimed.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a legitimate governance/relayer-origin action to change the reward rate between a relayer's delivery and their claim submission — this is a normal operational action (rate tuning, disabling a module, treasury management), not an attacker exploiting the system. The relayer-side actions (delivering the request, submitting the claim) are fully unprivileged and permissionless, matching the "unprivileged relayer" reachability requirement. The window between delivery and claim can be arbitrarily long in the current design (claims are processed by an async off-chain task and can be retried later, per `tesseract/relayer/src/claim_rewards.rs`), so a rate change landing in that window is plausible during normal DAO/ops activity, not a contrived edge case.

### Recommendation
Pin the reward rate at the time the delivery is proven, not at claim time. Concretely: snapshot the current `OutboundRequestDeliveryReward[module_id]` / `OutboundConsensusDeliveryReward[destination]` into a per-commitment (or per-rotation) record as soon as the delivery becomes provable on-chain (e.g. at the point `RequestCommitments` is inserted, or via a dedicated "reward locked-in" storage item written the first time a valid delivery proof is seen), and pay out from that locked-in value on claim rather than re-reading the live governance-set map. Alternatively, apply rate changes only prospectively (e.g. effective after a delay/epoch boundary) so that all requests/rotations already dispatched under the old rate can still be claimed at that rate.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance calls `set_outbound_request_delivery_reward(module_id, 100)` (`modules/pallets/relayer/src/lib.rs:436-450`), enabling module `M` with reward 100.
2. Relayer A permissionlessly delivers a hyperbridge-originated `PostRequest` from module `M` to destination `D`; `RequestReceipts[commitment]` on `D` now attributes the delivery to Relayer A.
3. Before Relayer A submits their claim, governance calls `set_outbound_request_delivery_reward(module_id, 0)`.
4. Relayer A submits `claim_outbound_request_delivery_reward` with a valid proof of their delivery; `process_outbound_request_delivery_claim` reads `OutboundRequestDeliveryReward::get(module_id) == 0` and rejects with `OutboundRequestNoRewardConfigured` (`modules/pallets/relayer/src/outbound_request.rs:148-149`), permanently denying the reward for work already performed, even though the delivery happened while the reward was 100.
