Audit Report

## Title
Single-ParaId order spam can monopolize the shared bounded on-demand queue, starving unrelated paras with `QueueFull` - (File: `polkadot/runtime/parachains/src/on_demand/mod.rs`)

## Summary
`OrderQueue::pop_assignment_for_cores` enforces at most one assignment per `ParaId` per scheduling round via the `popped.insert(order.para_id)` dedup check, deferring all additional same-`ParaId` orders into `remaining_orders` regardless of `num_cores` availability. Since queue admission in `do_place_order` is gated only on aggregate queue length (`order_status.queue.len() < on_demand_queue_max_size`) with no per-`ParaId` quota, an unprivileged account can flood orders for one `ParaId` to fill the shared, finite queue faster than its fixed 1/round drain rate, causing `QueueFull` for unrelated paras' legitimate orders.

## Finding Description
The queue is a single shared bounded structure capped at `ON_DEMAND_MAX_QUEUE_MAX_SIZE` and configured at runtime via `on_demand_queue_max_size` [1](#0-0) . Any signed account can call `place_order_allow_death`, `place_order_keep_alive`, or `place_order_with_credits` for an arbitrary `para_id`, not just its own [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) .

The documented and implemented invariant caps assignment to one order per `ParaId` per round regardless of `num_cores`, deferring the rest to `remaining_orders`: [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) .

Admission control in `do_place_order` only checks the aggregate queue length against `on_demand_queue_max_size`, with no per-`ParaId` limit: [6](#0-5) . The spot-price mechanism (`update_spot_traffic` / `calculate_spot_traffic`) reacts only to aggregate queue utilization (`queue_size / queue_capacity`), not to per-`ParaId` outstanding order counts [7](#0-6) , so it taxes all demand uniformly rather than specifically penalizing single-para spam. Combined, these mean an attacker with sufficient funds/credits can enqueue up to `on_demand_queue_max_size` orders for one `ParaId`, which drain at only 1/round, saturating the shared queue and blocking `place_order_*` calls from unrelated accounts/paras with `QueueFull`, independent of `num_cores`.

## Impact Explanation
This is a griefing/DoS on a shared, permissionless resource: legitimate on-demand purchasers for unrelated paras can be locked out via `QueueFull` even when cores are idle, because queue admission has no fairness/per-para bound and drain rate for a spammed `ParaId` is fixed at 1/round regardless of core count. Recovery time scales with queue occupancy rather than core availability.

## Likelihood Explanation
The attack is triggerable by any unprivileged signed account through the standard `place_order_*` extrinsics targeting an arbitrary `para_id`, and does not require any privilege escalation or victim mistake. The only friction is the rising spot price from `calculate_spot_traffic`, which increases the attacker's cost but does not structurally prevent monopolizing the shared queue, since price scaling is based on aggregate utilization rather than a per-`ParaId` limit.

## Recommendation
Add a per-`ParaId` cap on outstanding queued orders (e.g., proportional to `num_cores` or a small fixed multiple), or make backpressure/spot pricing sensitive to per-`ParaId` queue depth so that spamming a single para's queue slot escalates cost specifically for that para rather than only reflecting aggregate demand, preserving fair access for unrelated paras.

## Proof of Concept
1. Set `on_demand_queue_max_size = M` and `num_cores = 2` (or more) in test configuration.
2. From a single unprivileged account, repeatedly call `place_order_allow_death` for `ParaId(X)` until queue length approaches `M`; verify acceptance each time (subject to rising spot price).
3. Advance blocks invoking `pop_assignment_for_cores`; verify queue length for `ParaId(X)` decreases by at most 1 per round irrespective of `num_cores`.
4. While the queue remains near capacity, have a different account call `place_order_allow_death` for `ParaId(Y)` and confirm it fails with `Error::QueueFull` despite idle cores.
5. Repeat with different `num_cores` values to confirm the bottleneck is the per-`ParaId` 1/round cap in `pop_assignment_for_cores`, not core count.
