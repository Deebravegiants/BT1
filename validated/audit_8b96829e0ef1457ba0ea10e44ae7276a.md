### Title
Truncated cross-decimal amount conversion in `pallet-hyper-fungible-token` permanently burns dust on inbound ISMP transfers - (File: modules/pallets/hyper-fungible-token/src/impls.rs)

### Summary
`convert_to_balance` in the hyper-fungible-token pallet performs integer division when scaling an incoming ERC20-denominated amount down to the local asset's decimal precision. Any remainder from that division is silently discarded and never credited to anyone, causing a permanent loss of value for the recipient — the same rounding-truncation bug class described in the external report, but here it hits the *receive* path (mint/refund) instead of a deposit path.

### Finding Description
`convert_to_balance` divides the incoming `value` by `10^(erc_decimals - local_decimals)` and truncates: [1](#0-0) 

This helper is invoked in the pallet's ISMP `on_accept` handler, which is executed whenever a relayer delivers a verified POST request originating from a registered `HyperFungibleToken`/`WrappedHyperFungibleToken` contract on a remote EVM chain. The message's raw `amount` (denominated in the remote ERC20's own `decimals()`, fully controlled by whichever address called `send()` on that EVM contract — an unprivileged sender) is converted with `erc_decimals` (from `Precisions`) and the local asset's decimals: [2](#0-1) 

If `erc_decimals > decimals` (any local asset registered with fewer decimals than its EVM counterpart, e.g. the shipped `BridgeToken` example itself uses 18 decimals on EVM vs. 12 on nexus, a 10^6 scale factor), any `message.amount` that is not an exact multiple of `10^(erc_decimals - decimals)` loses its remainder. That remainder is not minted, not escrowed-released, and not tracked anywhere — it disappears entirely. The same truncating conversion is reused on the timeout/refund path: [3](#0-2) 

Unlike the timeout path (where the amount being converted back was itself produced by `convert_to_erc20`'s exact scale-up from the same local amount, so no loss occurs there in the normal case), the `on_accept` path converts an amount that was chosen independently by a caller on the remote EVM contract, who can pick any `uint256` value including one that is not a clean multiple of the scale factor.

There is no on-chain validation in `register_token`/`update_token` enforcing that `erc_decimals` and local `decimals` are compatible (e.g., requiring the scale factor to always land on round increments the sender is guided to use), and no dust-tracking/carry-forward mechanism exists to accumulate truncated remainders for later credit.

### Impact Explanation
Every inbound cross-chain transfer whose EVM-side amount is not an exact multiple of the decimal scale factor permanently loses the truncated remainder — the value is neither credited to the beneficiary, refunded to the sender, nor retained by the pallet's escrow account. This is a direct, unrecoverable loss of user funds triggered purely by an ordinary token-bridge transfer, matching the report's root cause (integer division during decimal-precision conversion silently drops value). While the per-transaction loss is bounded by the scale factor (e.g. up to `10^6 - 1` units of BRIDGE at 12 decimals for the shipped example), the pallet is generic over `Config::Decimals` and `Precisions` (settable per registered asset by governance), so pairs with more extreme decimal mismatches lose proportionally larger dust per transfer, and losses accumulate across the volume of transfers processed by the bridge.

### Likelihood Explanation
This triggers on the ordinary, unprivileged happy path: any external account can call `send()` on the counterpart EVM `HyperFungibleToken`/`WrappedHyperFungibleToken` contract with an arbitrary `uint256 amount`, and once a relayer delivers the resulting POST request (a normal, permissionless relay operation), `on_accept` executes the truncating conversion automatically. No malicious relayer, admin, or governance action is required — only a sender choosing (or accidentally using) an amount that isn't a round multiple of the decimal scale factor, which is trivial to do since ERC20 `send()` accepts any `uint256`.

### Recommendation
Avoid truncating division when converting inbound amounts to local precision:
- Reject (revert) inbound messages whose `amount` is not an exact multiple of `10^(erc_decimals - local_decimals)`, refunding/erroring rather than silently dropping the remainder, or
- Track truncated remainders per-asset/per-chain and carry them forward, crediting them once they accumulate to a full local unit, or
- Enforce at `register_token`/`update_token` time that the local asset's decimals equal (or a documented minimum precision relative to) the EVM contract's decimals, eliminating any lossy division on the hot path.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance registers asset `X` as non-native with `Precisions::<T>::get(X, EVM_CHAIN) = 18` while the local asset `X` has `decimals = 12` (a 10^6 scale factor), mirroring the shipped `BridgeToken` example (`evm/src/apps/BridgeToken.sol:34-36`).
2. An unprivileged user calls `HyperFungibleToken.send()` on the EVM chain with `amount = 1_500_000` (i.e., `1.5 * 10^6`, not a multiple of `10^6`), burning that exact amount and dispatching a POST request. [4](#0-3) 
3. A relayer delivers the proven request; `pallet-hyper-fungible-token::on_accept` runs `convert_to_balance(1_500_000, 18, 12)` → `1_500_000 / 10^6 = 1` (integer division), silently dropping the `500_000` remainder. [5](#0-4) 
4. The beneficiary is credited `amount = 1` local unit instead of the value equivalent to `1.5`; the `0.5`-unit remainder is permanently lost — not minted, not escrowed, not recoverable by any subsequent action.

/// Converts an ERC20 U256 amount to a local balance type
///
/// Divides by 10^(erc_decimals - local_decimals) to scale down from ERC20 precision.
/// The target type must implement `FromStr`.
pub fn convert_to_balance<B: core::str::FromStr>(
	value: U256,
	erc_decimals: u8,
	local_decimals: u8,
) -> Result<B, B::Err> {
	let dec_str = (value /
		U256::from(10u128.pow(erc_decimals.saturating_sub(local_decimals) as u32)))
	.to_string();
	dec_str.parse::<B>()
}
