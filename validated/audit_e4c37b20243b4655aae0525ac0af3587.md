### Title
A borrower can self-shield an underwater account from liquidation and bad-debt cleanup by planting a dust collateral leg whose price feed they can break - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/plan.rs)

### Summary
Liquidation planning (`build_liquidation_plan`) computes account risk totals and a pro-rata seizure across **every** supply position, and each leg is priced strictly via `Context::cached_price`. A single unpriceable collateral leg makes the entire `liquidate` call revert, and the same strict pricing path makes permissionless `clean_bad_debt` revert too. `supply` requires no valid price for the supplied asset, so a borrower can plant a dust leg of an Aquarius-LP-collateral market while its price resolves, then — using only their own Aquarius liquidity position — withdraw pool liquidity below the oracle's `min_pool_value_wad` floor. From that point the LP price read returns `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity`, every liquidation of the account reverts, and bad-debt cleanup is blocked. This is the on-chain analog of the CVE-2023-31627 class: a single crafted input (one poisoned leg) aborts the whole operation that must process it.

### Finding Description
The root cause is the coupling of two facts:

1. **Liquidation iterates and prices all collateral legs.** `build_liquidation_plan` calls `risk::calculate_account_risk_totals` over `account.supply_positions`, and `calculate_seized_collateral` iterates every supply position calling `cache.cached_price(&hub_asset.asset)` — a strict price that propagates oracle errors. There is no collateral-selection argument for the liquidator and no mechanism to skip a failed leg. The seizure loop and flag enforcement (`FreezePolicy::SeizureLeg`) run over the full leg set. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

2. **An Aquarius LP source fails closed when pool value drops below `min_pool_value_wad`.** `providers::aquarius::read` computes `pool_value_wad = price_wad * total_shares / share_unit` and returns `OracleError::InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` when it is under the configured floor — a condition the borrower can induce at will by removing their own liquidity from the pool. [4](#0-3)  `Supply` does not gate on the supplied asset's price, so the dust leg can be planted cheaply (an LP token of that pool is itself natural to hold). The codebase's own audit tests pin this failure shape: a planted stale-feed dust leg makes `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and even the borrower's `withdraw` revert with `PRICE_FEED_STALE` while an identical twin account with all-fresh feeds liquidates fine. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5)  The threat model acknowledges the shape (DoS.1) but the Aquarius leg makes it unilaterally triggerable rather than dependent on third-party oracle liveness. [7](#0-6) 

### Impact Explanation
Permanent bad debt / protocol insolvency. While the shield leg is active, no liquidator can repay any of the account's debt — `liquidate` reverts at the risk-total/seizure stage before any leg executes, regardless of payment size or `SeizeMode` (Credit mode still prices every leg). `clean_bad_debt` also requires the same prices to establish `total_debt > total_collateral`, so even after the account is deeply insolvent the permissionless cleanup path reverts; only the privileged `force_socialize_bad_debt` remains. Meanwhile the debt keeps accruing interest. The attacker needs only keep the Aquarius pool below the floor (they control their own LP share) for as long as they want the shield, or re-trigger it whenever price recovery threatens them — a low, refundable cost versus their full debt. Suppliers ultimately absorb the written-down debt via the supply-index write-down on cleanup.

### Likelihood Explanation
Fully unprivileged and repeatable: `supply` of a dust amount of a listed LP-collateral asset plus the attacker's own `remove_liquidity` on Aquarius. `min_pool_value_wad` is a fixed configured floor (e.g. 500_000 USD WAD for `XLMAQUA_LP` on mainnet), so for pools where the attacker holds a meaningful share of liquidity the cost is bounded and the LP position is recoverable afterward. [8](#0-7)  Any listed collateral with a manipulable-failure feed works; Aquarius LP legs are the clearest since pool depth is attacker-controlled. Severity: Medium-High — insolvency outcome with modest prerequisites (an LP-collateral market exists and the attacker can hold or acquire enough pool share).

### Recommendation
Decouple liquidation liveness from per-leg price availability:
- In `calculate_seized_collateral` / `calculate_account_risk_totals`, treat a leg whose strict price resolution fails as zero-value (skip it) rather than aborting, so the remaining collateral can still be seized pro-rata; or
- Give `liquidate` an explicit collateral-subset argument letting the liquidator exclude failed legs (with `seize_all` only when all legs resolve); and
- Let `clean_bad_debt` use the same degraded pricing so a poisoned leg cannot block socialization once debt exceeds the priced collateral.

At minimum, disallow supplying an asset whose oracle currently fails to resolve, so the shield leg cannot be planted after the feed is already broken.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker supplies real collateral (e.g. USDC) and borrows near max LTV in ETH.
2. Attacker calls `supply` with a dust amount of a listed Aquarius-LP collateral asset (`XLMAQUA_LP`) while its price resolves — `supply` needs no price, so this succeeds.
3. Attacker withdraws their own liquidity from the underlying Aquarius pool until `pool_value_wad < min_pool_value_wad` (`aquarius.rs` returns `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` at line 120).
4. Price moves push the account below HF 1. Any `liquidate(liquidator, account_id, debt_payments, seize_mode)` reverts inside `build_liquidation_plan` → `calculate_account_risk_totals`/`cached_price` on the poisoned leg. `clean_bad_debt` reverts the same way. The in-repo tests `audit_supply_setup_blocks_liquidation_via_stale_dust_leg` and `audit_liquidate_and_clean_bricked_by_unpriceable_dust_leg` demonstrate the identical mechanism with a stale feed; the Aquarius variant substitutes an attacker-controlled failure for an exogenous one.
5. Debt accrues until insolvent; when the account is eventually socialized (only via privileged `force_socialize_bad_debt`), suppliers absorb the accrued loss.
