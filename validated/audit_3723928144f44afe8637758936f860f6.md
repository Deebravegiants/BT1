Based on my analysis, `circomData.externalActionData` (which contains `externalActionMetadata`) is hashed into `getHashedCalldata1` and checked via `circomData.calldataHash` in `HinkalHelper.performHinkalChecks` [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) . This means the attacker's own `externalActionMetadata` is a free field they choose at proof-generation time — the attacker generates their own proof for their own funds and can set `externalActionMetadata` to anything they want (including calldata that consumes only a fraction of `inputAmount`), since it's the caller who builds `circomData` for their own transaction.

Tracing the balance equality Hinkal actually enforces per transaction: `balanceDif == (onChainCreation? 0 : amountChanges[i]) + utxoAmount` [3](#0-2) . This only checks Hinkal's own balance movement and the UTXO(s) actually created by the external action — it never checks what happened to funds *inside* `LifiExternalAction` beyond the reported `swappedAmount`. `ExternalActionSwap.swap` computes `inputAmount = uint256(-deltaAmounts[0])` and passes it to `callRouter`, but `callRouter` never verifies that the router actually consumed `inputAmount`; it only measures `outputToken` balance delta [4](#0-3) . There is no check like `IERC20(inputToken).balanceOf(address(this)) == 0` after the call, and no refund of unconsumed input back to Hinkal/the depositor.

Combined with `approveUnlimited` leaving a persistent max allowance for `router` on `inputToken` [5](#0-4) , any unspent `inputToken` balance stranded in `LifiExternalAction` after one `transact` call becomes a shared, unrefunded pool that a **subsequent, unrelated caller** can drain: they deposit their own (possibly tiny) `inputAmount` via `_externalTransact`'s `transferERC20TokenOrETH` [6](#0-5) , then supply their own `externalActionMetadata` whose calldata instructs the router to pull the *entire* current `inputToken` balance of `LifiExternalAction` (their deposit + the stranded residual, since allowance is `type(uint256).max`). The resulting larger `swappedAmount` flows entirely into their own `utxoAmount`/UTXO via `transferERC20TokenOrETH(outputToken, msg.sender, amountToSendToHinkal)` and `createOnchainCommitment` [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7) . Hinkal's per-token equality still balances trivially for both transactions (since `utxoAmount` is computed post-hoc from actual balances, not independently constrained by the circuit for external-action outputs), so nothing in `performHinkalChecks`, `verifyProof`, or the balance/slippage requires catches the diversion of the first depositor's stranded principal into the second caller's UTXO.

This is a genuine, code-level flaw in `LifiExternalAction.callRouter`/`ExternalActionSwap.swap`: input funds sent into the external action are never guaranteed to be either fully consumed by the swap or refunded, and the unlimited allowance plus lack of a residual-balance check make any leftover balance (whether from a griefed/partial-fill legitimate swap or an attacker's own earlier transaction) claimable by the next arbitrary caller.

### Title
Unaccounted residual `inputToken` balance in `LifiExternalAction` can be stolen by a subsequent caller - (File: contracts/external-actions/swaps/LifiExternalAction.sol)

### Summary
`LifiExternalAction.callRouter` trusts caller-supplied `externalActionMetadata` to fully consume the `inputAmount` that `Hinkal._externalTransact` transferred to it, but never verifies that the router actually pulled that amount, nor refunds any unspent balance. Combined with `approveUnlimited` leaving a max allowance on `router`, any stranded `inputToken` balance becomes a shared pool that the next unprivileged `transact` caller can route through the same `externalActionMetadata` mechanism into their own output UTXO.

### Finding Description
The equality that should hold per transaction is: `inputAmount transferred to LifiExternalAction == amount consumed by router + amount refunded`. Nothing enforces the right-hand side. `ExternalActionSwap.swap` computes `inputAmount` from `deltaAmounts[0]` [9](#0-8)  and calls `callRouter(inputToken, inputAmount, outputToken, externalActionMetadata)`, but `callRouter` ignores `inputAmount` for the actual router call — it just does `approveUnlimited(inputToken, router)` and `router.call(externalActionMetadata)`, measuring success only by `outputToken` balance delta [4](#0-3) . Since `externalActionMetadata` is entirely attacker-chosen (it is only hashed into `calldataHash` to bind it to the attacker's *own* proof, not validated against `inputAmount` on-chain) [1](#0-0) , an attacker can submit calldata that has the router consume less than `inputAmount`, leaving the difference sitting in `LifiExternalAction`'s ERC20 balance with a live max allowance already granted to `router`.

Hinkal's per-token balance/UTXO equality check [10](#0-9)  only reconciles Hinkal's own balance movement against `amountChanges[i]` and the UTXO(s) minted in that same transaction — it has no visibility into, or requirement about, `LifiExternalAction`'s internal token balance. So the first (stranding) transaction passes all checks even though only a fraction of `inputAmount` was truly swapped.

In a later, unrelated `transact` call to the same `externalActionAddress`, a different (or the same) unprivileged caller deposits their own `inputAmount` and crafts `externalActionMetadata` for a router function that pulls the router's *entire* current allowance-backed balance (their fresh deposit plus the previously stranded residual, since `approveUnlimited` already set allowance to `type(uint256).max`). The larger resulting `swappedAmount` is transferred back to Hinkal and credited entirely into the second caller's own UTXO via `createOnchainCommitment`, while their own `amountChanges[0]`/`deltaAmounts[0]` only reflects their own (smaller) deposit. Hinkal's balance equality is still satisfied because `utxoAmount` is derived post-hoc from actual balances, not independently bound by the circuit to the swap's expected output for that specific principal.

### Impact Explanation
An unprivileged caller can extract another party's stranded `inputToken` (left behind by a prior partial/incomplete swap execution through `LifiExternalAction`) and convert it into shielded value credited to their own UTXO, at the expense of whoever's funds were stranded. This is theft of user funds routed through the protocol's own external-action mechanism, and it is repeatable for every subsequent stranding event on this action contract, matching the Critical category (theft of in-flight user funds via an action the depositor never counted on).

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires: (1) some residual `inputToken` balance in `LifiExternalAction` (achievable by any unprivileged user crafting `externalActionMetadata` for a partial-consuming router call, or naturally from partial-fill/slippage-driven leftover in a real LI.FI route), and (2) a subsequent unprivileged caller able to craft calldata pulling the enlarged balance via the pre-existing unlimited allowance. Both preconditions are fully within an unprivileged attacker's control (they build their own `CircomData`/proof and choose `router` calldata freely), making this straightforward and cheap to trigger and repeat.

### Recommendation
In `LifiExternalAction.callRouter` (and `ExternalActionSwap.swap`), assert that the router fully consumed the approved `inputAmount` — e.g. require `IERC20(inputToken).balanceOf(address(this)) == balanceBeforeInput` after the call — or explicitly compute and refund any unspent `inputToken` residual to `msg.sender`/Hinkal within the same transaction so no balance is left stranded. Additionally, avoid `approveUnlimited`'s persistent max allowance in favor of exact, per-call approvals reset after use.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry fork test: deploy `LifiExternalAction` with `router` set to a mock contract whose fallback/swap function only `transferFrom`s half of the caller-approved `inputToken` amount and mints a small amount of `outputToken` to the contract. (1) Run `Hinkal.transact` once with a valid proof for `deltaAmounts = [-inputAmount, +swappedHalf]`, and assert `IERC20(inputToken).balanceOf(address(lifiExternalAction)) > 0` after the call (residual not zero, and no revert). (2) Run a second `Hinkal.transact` from a different caller with a fresh proof for a small `deltaAmounts[0] = -dust`, using `externalActionMetadata` that instructs the mock router to pull `dust + residual` (using the surviving max allowance) and mint proportional `outputToken`; assert the second caller's minted on-chain commitment/UTXO amount for `outputToken` exceeds what `dust` alone would produce, and that `IERC20(inputToken).balanceOf(address(lifiExternalAction))` drops to 0, confirming the first caller's stranded principal was captured by the second caller's UTXO.
