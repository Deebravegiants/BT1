## Finding: A pre-graduation HyperSwap V2 pre-seed can permanently outsize `Bonding`'s rebalance budget, seeding the locked LP at an attacker-controlled ratio away from the curve-close price

### Title
LP-Ratio Manipulation via Oversized HyperSwap V2 Pre-Seed Exceeding the Rebalance Swap Budget - (File: `packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol`)

### Summary
`Bonding._seedRebalancing` / `_pairRebalance` defend against a hostile pre-seed of the post-graduation HyperSwap V2 (TOKEN, LT) pair by swapping toward the curve-close ratio before depositing, but the swap size is hard-capped at 99% of `Bonding`'s own graduation-inventory (`ltFromPair` / `tokensForLP`), not at whatever size is needed to fully correct the ratio. [1](#0-0)  An attacker who permissionlessly creates the pair and pre-seeds it with reserves large enough (relative to the graduation's own `ltFromPair`/`tokensForLP`) that the required correcting swap exceeds this budget forces `finalizeGraduation` to deposit the protocol's locked liquidity at a ratio that is still skewed away from the true curve-close price.

### Finding Description
`finalizeGraduation` is fully permissionless and calls `_ensureUniswapV2Pair`, which simply looks up or creates the V2 pair for `(tokenAddress, lt)` — nothing stops anyone from creating that pair, and seeding it with `pair.mint`, before `finalizeGraduation` is ever called. [2](#0-1) 

When the pool already has `totalSupply != 0` (an attacker minted LP against a self-seeded ratio), `_seedUniswapV2Direct` routes to `_seedRebalancing`, which computes the correcting swap size via `_noFeeSwapInput` and caps it at `maxSwap = _swapBudget(inventory)`, where `inventory` is `Bonding`'s own held balance of the input side (`_ltSwapInventory(lt, protectedLT)` or `IERC20(tokenAddress).balanceOf(address(this))`) — i.e., bounded by this graduation's own `ltFromPair`/`tokensForLP`, and `_swapBudget` further caps that at 99%. [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

The natspec on `_swapBudget` explicitly acknowledges the cap can bind for a pre-seed beyond the budget's capacity and accepts the tradeoff ("the alternative is bricking") rather than aborting or scaling the deposit to match. [5](#0-4)  `_pairRebalance` then executes only the capped swap and returns `true` as long as `getAmountOut` is non-zero — it never checks whether the post-swap ratio actually reached the target. [6](#0-5)  `_routerDepositAndDispose` then deposits whatever remains via `router.addLiquidity(..., 1, 1, lpLock_, ...)` at the *current* (still-skewed) pool ratio, since the router's `quote()` split simply matches live reserves, not the cached curve-close target. [7](#0-6) 

Because `ltFromPair` is bounded by the curve's total raised LT (at most `3 × VIRTUAL_LIQUIDITY_USD` = $9,000-equivalent) and `tokensForLP` is bounded by `LP_RESERVE` (250M tokens), an attacker can pre-fund the pair — by buying tokens on the curve and buying/holding LT externally — with reserves sized well beyond this graduation's own inventory in a deliberately skewed ratio, guaranteeing the 99%-of-inventory swap cap binds and the final deposit lands off the curve-close price.

### Impact Explanation
The permanently locked LP (`LPLock.recordLock`, a one-shot call `finalizeGraduation` cannot skip or retry at a corrected ratio) [8](#0-7)  is seeded at a price divorced from the price the curve actually closed at. The attacker, holding the dominant share of the pre-existing LP supply they minted themselves, captures the resulting mispricing (via arbitrage against the true curve-close price or by simply owning the disproportionate share of pooled value), extracting value from the protocol-owned locked liquidity — a direct, permanent value transfer from the launched token's LP/holders to the attacker. This satisfies the "LP seeded away from the curve close price" impact class.

### Likelihood Explanation
`finalizeGraduation` and pair creation/pre-seeding are both permissionless and reachable by any unprivileged address; the attacker only needs enough capital to out-seed a single graduation's `ltFromPair`/`tokensForLP` (bounded by the fixed $9K/250M-token caps), which is a modest, calculable amount, and the exact defense boundary (`_swapBudget`'s 99% cap) is fully deterministic from public contract constants and on-chain reserves, making the attack precisely computable rather than probabilistic.

### Recommendation
When the rebalance swap is budget-capped short of the ratio target, do not fall through to depositing at the residual skewed ratio — either (a) scale the deposit down proportionally to the corrected ratio and sweep/burn the true excess exactly as the "catastrophic pre-seed" comment anticipates but does not implement, or (b) revert and require a keeper/owner-mediated remediation path (e.g., pulling additional inventory) instead of unconditionally minting the locked LP at an uncorrected ratio.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker buys tokens on the curve (or waits for a token approaching graduation) and separately acquires LT externally.
2. Before `finalizeGraduation` is called, attacker calls `IUniswapV2Factory.createPair(tokenAddress, lt)` (permissionless — `Bonding._ensureUniswapV2Pair` would otherwise do this itself) and directly transfers a heavily skewed (TOKEN, LT) ratio to the pair, then calls `pair.mint(attacker)`, minting themselves the entire initial LP supply at their chosen ratio.
3. Size the pre-seed so that `_noFeeSwapInput`'s required correcting swap exceeds `99% × Bonding`'s inventory of the deficient side (bounded by `ltFromPair`/`tokensForLP`, i.e., at most ~$9K LT / 250M tokens) — sizing math is fully public per `_swapBudget`/`_noFeeSwapInput`.
4. Anyone calls (or the keeper calls) `Bonding.finalizeGraduation(tokenAddress)`. `_seedRebalancing` → `_pairRebalance` executes only the capped swap; `_routerDepositAndDispose` deposits the remainder via `addLiquidity(min=1,min=1)` at the still-skewed post-swap ratio into `lpLock`.
5. Attacker, owning the dominant share of the pool's total LP supply from step 2, extracts value from the resulting mispriced pool relative to the true curve-close price.

    /// @dev Cap the rebalance swap at 99% of the available side's budget,
    ///      so the subsequent `addLiquidity` always has a non-zero amount
    ///      of BOTH sides to deposit. Without this, an extreme hostile
    ///      pre-seed (massively imbalanced reserves) drives the
    ///      unconstrained `_noFeeSwapInput` past our per-side budget,
    ///      `_pairRebalance` clamps to the full budget, and the swap
    ///      consumes 100% of one side. `_routerDepositAndDispose` then
    ///      skips `addLiquidity` (`remToken == 0` or `remLT == 0`),
    ///      `finalizeGraduation` returns `liquidity = 0`, and
    ///      `LPLock.recordLock(...)` records a zero-sized lock — the
    ///      attacker's pre-existing LP becomes 100% of the pool. Reserving
    ///      1% guarantees the deposit leg always lands AND mints non-zero
    ///      LP at the post-swap ratio. The 1% comes off the swap, not the
    ///      deposit — for any realistic pre-seed `s_unconstrained` is
    ///      orders of magnitude below `maxSwap`, so the cap doesn't bind
    ///      and behaviour is unchanged. It only kicks in for catastrophic
    ///      pre-seeds beyond our budget capacity, where the alternative
    ///      is bricking.
    function _swapBudget(
        uint256 budget
    ) internal pure returns (uint256) {
        return (budget * 99) / 100;
    }

    function _pairRebalance(
        RebalanceParams memory p
    ) internal returns (bool) {
        uint256 s = _noFeeSwapInput(p.reserveIn, p.reserveOut, p.targetN, p.targetD, p.maxSwap);
        if (s == 0) return false;

        // Quote from the pair so the output tracks its live fee; a value
        // derived from a stale fee rate would trip the pair's K-check.
        uint256 expectedOut = IUniswapV2Pair(p.pair).getAmountOut(s, p.tokenIn);
        if (expectedOut == 0) return false;

        IERC20(p.tokenIn).safeTransfer(p.pair, s);
        (uint256 amount0Out, uint256 amount1Out) = p.tokenInIs0 ? (uint256(0), expectedOut) : (expectedOut, uint256(0));
        IUniswapV2Pair(p.pair).swap(amount0Out, amount1Out, address(this), new bytes(0));
        return true;
    }
