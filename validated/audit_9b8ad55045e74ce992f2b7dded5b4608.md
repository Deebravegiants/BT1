### Title
Hostile HyperSwap pre-seed beyond the rebalance budget forces the graduation LP to open away from curve-close price and diverts curve-raised LT to the owner instead of the locked LP - ([File: packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol])

### Summary
`Bonding.finalizeGraduation` seeds the graduated HyperSwap V2 TOKEN/LT pair via `_seedUniswapV2Direct → _seedRebalancing → _pairRebalance/_routerDepositAndDispose`. This defense assumes any hostile `pair.mint` pre-seed can be rebalanced back toward the cached curve-close ratio using a swap capped at the graduation's *own* inventory. When a front-runner pre-seeds the pair with reserves large enough relative to `tokensForLP`/`ltFromPair` that the capped rebalance swap cannot materially move the pool ratio, the subsequent `addLiquidity` call deposits Bonding's inventory at (nearly) the attacker-chosen ratio instead of the curve-close ratio, and the unmatched majority of the curve-raised LT — real user funds — is swept to the protocol `owner()` rather than entering the locked LP.

### Finding Description
`_seedRebalancing` only takes the safe direct-mint path when **both** sides of the pre-seed are below `DIRECT_MINT_PRESEED_BPS` relative to the cached target amounts [1](#0-0) . An attacker can front-run `finalizeGraduation` by calling `factory.createPair` and `pair.mint` with reserves skewed so that one side is small (passes the band check) while the other side is arbitrarily large relative to the graduation's own `tokensForLP`/`ltFromPair` (fails it), pushing execution into the rebalance branch [2](#0-1) .

The rebalance swap is capped at `_swapBudget(...)`, which is 99% of **Bonding's own** LT (or TOKEN) inventory for this graduation — not the attacker's pre-seed size [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) . When the pre-seeded reserve on the "deep" side dwarfs this budget, `_noFeeSwapInput`'s capped swap can only nudge the ratio a small amount toward target; the pool remains close to the attacker's chosen ratio. `_routerDepositAndDispose` then calls `router.addLiquidity` with `min0=1, min1=1` [5](#0-4) , which — per V2's `quote()`-based optimal split — only pulls the ratio-matched subset of Bonding's inventory into the pool. The unmatched majority of the *other* side is disposed of: TOKEN is burned, but LT (which cannot be burned) is left in `Bonding` and later swept in full to `owner()` by `finalizeGraduation`'s post-bookend `_sweepLTToOwner` [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) .

The net effect: (1) the LP opens at a price far from the curve's last marginal price (`tokensForLP / ltFromPair`), analogous to the flash-crash mechanic in the AutoShark Finance incident where an attacker distorted AMM reserves to blow out the price; and (2) most of the real LT that traders paid into the bonding curve never reaches the locked LP backing the token — it is siphoned out of the graduation flow entirely. `AGENTS.md`'s own rationale for `_swapBudget` acknowledges this: the cap "only kicks in for catastrophic pre-seeds beyond our budget capacity, where the alternative is bricking" [8](#0-7) , i.e. price-accuracy is knowingly sacrificed once the pre-seed exceeds the rebalance budget, and the documented "~50 bps" ceiling explicitly assumes the budget cap does **not** bind [9](#0-8) .

### Impact Explanation
- The graduated LP can open at a price severely divorced from the curve's true close price, so the first post-graduation traders (and the attacker's own pre-seed LP position) trade against a mispriced pool — the exact "flash crash" pattern in the AutoShark analog.
- Real LT raised from traders on the bonding curve, intended to permanently back the token's liquidity via `LPLock`, is redirected to the protocol `owner()` address instead of entering the locked LP, permanently reducing the LP's backing and the funds available to holders exiting through the graduated pool.
- This is reachable by any unprivileged address with sufficient LT/TOKEN capital and does not require any privileged role — only `factory.createPair` and `pair.mint`, both permissionless on HyperSwap V2, ahead of `finalizeGraduation`.

### Likelihood Explanation
`finalizeGraduation` is permissionless and has an observable window (the AGENTS.md keeper timing states ~60s) between `TokenGraduating` (phase 1, which caches `tokensForLP`/`ltFromPair`) and `finalizeGraduation` (phase 2), during which anyone can front-run the pair creation and mint. The band check in `_seedRebalancing` is designed for "reasonable" hostile pre-seeds and, by the code's own comments, degrades gracefully (not safely) once the pre-seed exceeds the graduation's own inventory-derived swap budget — this is a known, acknowledged edge case in the codebase's own documentation rather than a purely theoretical one.

### Recommendation
Bound the acceptable price deviation of the resulting LP explicitly (e.g., revert or fall back to burning the excess pre-seed / re-routing via `skim`+re-mint if the achievable post-rebalance ratio deviates from `tokensForLP/ltFromPair` by more than a hard bps ceiling), rather than silently depositing at whatever ratio the capped swap achieves. Alternatively, size `_swapBudget` relative to the attacker's pre-seed reserves (not solely Bonding's own inventory) so the rebalance can always fully correct the ratio, or require `_seedRebalancing` to detect the "budget-capped, ratio-uncorrected" case and defer/refuse finalization rather than proceeding with a degraded seed and sweeping undeposited LT to the owner.

### Proof of Concept
1. Creator launches a token; curve trades normally until `_enterGraduating` fires, caching `tokensForLP` and `ltFromPair` in `pendingGraduation[token]`.
2. Before the keeper calls `finalizeGraduation`, attacker calls `IUniswapV2Factory.createPair(token, lt)` then transfers a very large amount of `lt` (obtained via BounceTech `mint`) and a tiny amount of `token` (bought on the still-open curve, or acquired post-graduation trigger) to the pair, then calls `pair.mint(attacker)` — reserveLT ≫ `ltFromPair`, reserveToken ≪ `tokensForLP`.
3. `finalizeGraduation(token)` runs: the `DIRECT_MINT_PRESEED_BPS` band check fails (reserveLT too large relative to `ltFromPair`), so `_seedRebalancing` takes the rebalance branch; `_pairRebalance`'s swap is capped at `_swapBudget(_ltSwapInventory(...))` ≈ 99% of `ltFromPair`, far too small to correct the now heavily LT-skewed ratio.
4. `_routerDepositAndDispose` deposits only the small ratio-matched TOKEN/LT subset into the pool (opening at close to the attacker's skewed ratio, not the curve's close price); the large unmatched LT remainder is later swept via `_sweepLTToOwner` to `owner()` instead of entering `LPLock`.
5. Result: the graduated pool opens far from the curve's marginal price, and most of the curve-raised LT never backs the locked LP.

    function finalizeGraduation(
        address tokenAddress
    ) external nonReentrant {
        BondingStorage storage $ = _s();
        TokenInfo storage info = $.tokenInfo[tokenAddress];
        if (info.lifecycle != Lifecycle.Graduating) revert NotGraduating();

        address lt = info.ltAddress;
        PendingGraduation memory p = $.pendingGraduation[tokenAddress];

        // Anything in this contract beyond `p.ltFromPair` belongs to a
        // concurrent graduation on the same LT (Phase 1 transferred it
        // via `Router.graduate`) or to stray dust. Either way it is
        // off-limits to this graduation's deposit and sweep — see
        // `_routerDepositAndDispose` and `_sweepLTToOwner`.
        // Saturating subtract: a balance below `p.ltFromPair` shouldn't
        // be reachable in normal operation, but we keep finalize from
        // bricking on a Panic if any future code path or non-canonical
        // LT briefly violates the invariant.
        uint256 ltBalance = IERC20(lt).balanceOf(address(this));
        uint256 protectedLT = ltBalance > p.ltFromPair ? ltBalance - p.ltFromPair : 0;

        address lpPair = _ensureUniswapV2Pair(tokenAddress, lt);
        uint256 liquidity = _seedUniswapV2Direct(tokenAddress, lt, lpPair, p.tokensForLP, p.ltFromPair, protectedLT);

        _sweepLTToOwner(lt, protectedLT);

    ///      emitting `LTRescued`. Called at the end of
    ///      `finalizeGraduation` with `keep = protectedLT` (any escrow
    ///      that doesn't belong to this graduation), so only THIS
    ///      graduation's rebalance residue lands on the owner. No-op on
    ///      the empty-pair fast path (nothing to sweep).
    function _sweepLTToOwner(
        address lt,
        uint256 keep
    ) internal {
        uint256 bal = IERC20(lt).balanceOf(address(this));
        if (bal <= keep) return;
        uint256 amount = bal - keep;
        address recipient = owner();
        IERC20(lt).safeTransfer(recipient, amount);
        emit LTRescued(lt, recipient, amount);
    }

    /// @dev Cap the rebalance swap at 99% of the available side's budget,
    ///      so the subsequent `addLiquidity` always has a non-zero amount
    ///      of BOTH sides to deposit. Without this, an extreme hostile
    ///      pre-seed (massively imbalanced reserves) drives the
    ///      unconstrained `_noFeeSwapInput` past our per-side budget,
    ///      `_pairRebalance` clamps to the full budget, and the swap
    ///      consumes 100% of one side. `_routerDepositAndDispose` then
    ///      skips `addLiquidity` (`remToken == 0` or `remLT == 0`),
    ///      `finalizeGraduation` returns `liquidity = 0`, and
    ///      `LPLock.recordLock(...)` records a zero-sized lock — the
    ///      attacker's pre-existing LP becomes 100% of the pool. Reserving
    ///      1% guarantees the deposit leg always lands AND mints non-zero
    ///      LP at the post-swap ratio. The 1% comes off the swap, not the
    ///      deposit — for any realistic pre-seed `s_unconstrained` is
    ///      orders of magnitude below `maxSwap`, so the cap doesn't bind
    ///      and behaviour is unchanged. It only kicks in for catastrophic
    ///      pre-seeds beyond our budget capacity, where the alternative
    ///      is bricking.
    function _swapBudget(
        uint256 budget
    ) internal pure returns (uint256) {
        return (budget * 99) / 100;
    }

### Pool-open precision

Honest graduations open at 0 bps gap (Regime 1, exact direct mint). Dust mint pre-seeds also open at ~0 bps — they take the `_seedDirectMint` fallback, which deposits at the cached ratio with only a vanishing skew from the dust reserves. Larger mint pre-seeds (where the rebalance swap fires) open within ~50 bps — the structural ceiling is the V2 fee landing between rebalance and deposit (~30 bps) plus integer rounding in the router's `quote()`-based split (~10 bps). 50 bps is well inside "exploit denied" — arbitrage closes the gap within blocks and the attacker still loses pre-seed value.
