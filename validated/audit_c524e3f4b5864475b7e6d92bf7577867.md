### Title
Borrow-index accrual can overflow RAY valuation and permanently freeze a saturated market - (File: contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs)

### Summary
An unprivileged borrower can push a market’s aggregate debt close to the protocol’s representable RAY limit and wait for `borrow_index` to accrue. Once `borrowed * borrow_index / RAY` exceeds `i128::MAX`, every operation that refreshes or values the market reverts, permanently preventing withdrawals and repayments.

### Finding Description
`Controller::borrow` is permissionless subject to solvency and calls `process_borrow` with caller-selected `(hub_asset, amount)` entries. [1](#0-0)  The protocol permits token-to-RAY values up to approximately `i128::MAX / RAY`, so a sufficiently liquid market can admit aggregate debt shares near `i128::MAX`. [2](#0-1) 

Subsequent interest accrual is triggered through the permissionless `update_indexes` entrypoint, which forwards the selected markets to the pool. [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3)  The pool evaluates aggregate debt by multiplying scaled debt by `borrow_index`. [5](#0-4)  Fixed-point multiplication uses an exact intermediate but raises `MathOverflow` when the resulting `i128` value is unrepresentable. [6](#0-5) 

Repayment and withdrawal paths both load the market through interest-accruing operations before resolving the position. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7)  Because the borrow index is monotonically non-decreasing, the overflow cannot be reversed, and every later attempt repeats the overflowing accrual. [9](#0-8) 

### Impact Explanation
All suppliers’ claims in the affected market can become permanently frozen: withdrawals fail during interest refresh, and borrowers cannot repay to restore liquidity because repayment also accrues first. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10)  This is permanent freezing of user funds and makes the affected pool market unable to operate.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires a market cap, liquidity, and attacker collateral large enough to bring `total_borrowed` near the representable boundary, followed by enough elapsed time for index growth. Those prerequisites are economically substantial, but no privileged role, oracle manipulation, leaked key, or third-party failure is required; the resulting overflow is deterministic once the boundary is crossed.

### Recommendation
Maintain an explicit invariant that `total_borrowed * borrow_index / RAY <= i128::MAX` before committing accrual. Cap accrual at `min(configured_index_ceiling, i128::MAX / total_borrowed)` so interest can stop without making exit paths revert. Entry checks should also reject debt growth that would leave insufficient headroom to the configured index ceiling. Recovery-critical paths such as repayment should retain a bounded fallback that can burn debt without requiring an unrepresentable aggregate valuation.

### Proof of Concept
1. Victims supply a flash-loanable/borrowable asset whose cap admits near-maximum exposure.
2. An attacker supplies sufficient collateral and calls `borrow(caller, account_id, vec![(hub_asset, max_amount)], None)`, creating scaled debt close to `i128::MAX`.
3. After interest accrual time elapses, the attacker calls `update_indexes(caller, vec![hub_asset])`.
4. Pool accrual evaluates `total_borrowed * new_borrow_index / RAY`; for `total_borrowed = i128::MAX`, even `new_borrow_index = RAY + 1` produces approximately `i128::MAX + 170`, which overflows.
5. The transaction reverts without advancing state. Subsequent `withdraw`, `repay`, liquidation-related repayments, and `update_indexes` calls repeat the same accrual and revert indefinitely.
