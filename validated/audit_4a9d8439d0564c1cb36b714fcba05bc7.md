### Title
Batched withdraw-all reverts entirely when any single market leg fails its post-withdraw utilization gate - (File: contracts/controller/src/strategies/legs.rs)

### Summary
XOXNO Lending has the same bug class as the Union `AssetManager.rebalance` finding: a single transaction loops over multiple markets and unconditionally calls a withdraw leg on each, so a revert in any one leg reverts the whole batch. The concrete reachable analog is `execute_withdraw_all` (used by `repay_debt_with_collateral(close_position = true)`) and the multi-asset `settle_withdraw` path: in the pool, each normal withdraw leg runs `gate_and_debit` → `guards::require_utilization_below_max` and `require_reserves`, so a single market at/above max utilization (or with insufficient cash to pay the leg) bricks the entire multi-leg withdrawal.

### Finding Description
`process_repay_debt_with_collateral` with `close_position = true` calls `execute_withdraw_all`, which iterates **every** key in `account.supply_positions` and issues a `WITHDRAW_ALL_SENTINEL` withdraw for each (contracts/controller/src/strategies/legs.rs:143-160). Each leg goes through `execute_withdrawal` → `pool_withdraw_call` → `withdraw::apply` → `accounting` → `gate_and_debit`, which enforces `cache.require_reserves(net_transfer)` and, for non-liquidation, non-empty closes, `guards::require_utilization_below_max` (contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:111-118). There is no per-leg try/catch or skip-if-empty beyond the `empty_close` footprint case (withdraw.rs:78).

The same atomicity applies to `settle_withdraw` (contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs:181-208): all aggregated legs go into one `pool_withdraw_call` batch executed by `run_batch` (contracts/pool/src/ops/mod.rs:66-70), and any leg's failure reverts all legs — no partial withdrawal is possible.

Like the original report's "one `withdrawAll` revert fails the whole rebalance", a single position whose market is at max utilization, paused-equivalent (frozen flag), or cash-short makes the whole close-all / multi-asset withdraw revert.

### Impact Explanation
Temporary freezing of funds: a user who has fully repaid debt cannot execute `repay_debt_with_collateral(close_position = true)` nor a multi-asset `withdraw` whenever one of their supplied markets would breach the utilization gate or lacks liquidity. The withdraw-all must either fail entirely or the user must manually exclude the blocking asset — but `execute_withdraw_all` iterates all supply positions unconditionally, so the close-position convenience path has no asset exclusion mechanism at all. Funds remain locked until market conditions change (borrowers repay / utilization drops), which can persist during stressed markets — exactly when users most want to exit.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. Triggering conditions are ordinary market states, not attacker-crafted: high utilization is a normal, expected regime for a lending pool (it is precisely what `require_utilization_below_max` guards against). Any account holding supply in two or more markets where one leg is utilization-blocked cannot close atomically. The user retains a workaround — call `repay_debt_with_collateral(close_position = false)` then withdraw per-asset, skipping the blocked market — which caps severity at Medium rather than High. An unprivileged user reaches it via `controller.repay_debt_with_collateral(..., close_position = true)` on their own account.

### Recommendation
Skip legs that cannot pay out instead of reverting the batch: in `execute_withdraw_all`, check the market's withdrawable capacity before issuing the leg, or pass a flag to the pool to treat a zero-payout leg as a no-op rather than gating utilization on it. At minimum, for the close-all path, emit per-leg `PoolWithdrawEntry` calls with `is_liquidation`-style utilization exemption only for positions that would otherwise remain stuck, or document that `close_position = true` is best-effort and provide a `withdraw` fallback that lets users enumerate and exclude blocked markets.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice supplies to markets A (USDC) and B (ETH) in one account; market B is borrowed to max utilization by other users.
2. Alice repays all debt, then calls `controller.repay_debt_with_collateral(caller=alice, account_id, collateral=A, collateral_amount=x, debt=A, swap=empty, close_position=true)`.
3. `execute_withdraw_all` iterates both supply keys. The A leg succeeds; the B leg reaches `gate_and_debit` → `require_utilization_below_max` → panics → the entire transaction reverts, including the A leg.
4. Same for `controller.withdraw(caller, account_id, [(A,0),(B,0)], None)`: `settle_withdraw` batches both into one `run_batch`; the B leg's utilization failure reverts A's payout too. Alice cannot atomically exit until B's utilization falls.
