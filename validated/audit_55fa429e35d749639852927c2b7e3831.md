Based on my research, alt.fun's contracts have no analog to `maxColBorrowPerAccount` — there is no per-account cumulative limit that is enforced only on one action path (e.g. buy) while a companion action path (e.g. sell) fails to re-check it, allowing a state that violates the limit to persist. The closest conceptually related mechanism is `Bonding.canGraduate` (a global, per-token threshold, not per-account), and it is a `public view` re-derived from live/stored `Pair` reserves rather than a cached account-level counter, so it cannot go stale the way JUSDBank's `maxColBorrowPerAccount` check does.

Critically, `canGraduate` is checked consistently on *both* sides of trading, not just one:
- On buy, `Bonding._executeBuy` checks `canGraduate` after every trade and immediately enters graduation if true. [1](#0-0) 
- On sell, `Zap._sellInternal` checks `bonding_.canGraduate(tokenAddress)` *before* executing the sell and redirects into `triggerGraduation` instead of allowing the sell to proceed and drag the reserve back down. [2](#0-1) 

This symmetric enforcement is exactly what the JOJO report's remediation (`_isAccSafeAfterBorrow` on withdraw, mirroring the borrow-time check) is asking for — alt.fun already does the equivalent on its analogous surface. There is also no multi-collateral/per-account borrowing construct in Bonding/Router/Zap/Pair/FeeVault into which the "deposit A, borrow max, deposit B, borrow more, withdraw B" sequencing trick could be mapped; each bonding curve is a single-asset (Token/LT) pair with no shared per-account debt ledger across pairs. No valid analog exists within the allowed scope.
