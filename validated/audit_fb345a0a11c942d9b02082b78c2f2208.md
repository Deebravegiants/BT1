### Title
Rounding of `x2 = sqrt(x2_power)` in `calc_take_pnl` can exceed `x1`/`y1`, causing an `unwrap()` panic that permanently blocks Deposit/Withdraw/WithdrawPnl - (File: `program/src/processor.rs`)

### Summary
The reported USSD bug is a "same/near-equal amount" edge case where a subtraction between two closely-tracked quantities, followed by an integer division, can underflow/revert and leave the protocol permanently unable to rebalance. The structurally closest reachable analog in this AMM is `Processor::calc_take_pnl`, which performs an unchecked (`unwrap()`-based) subtraction between a freshly computed value (`x2`, `y2` — derived from an integer square root) and the pool's live token totals (`x1`, `y1`). Because `x2`/`y2` are derived through multiplication, division, and `integer_sqrt()` rounding rather than being provably `<= x1`/`y1` in all edge cases, a pool state where `current_k` is only marginally `>=` `last_k` (the guarding condition at line 190-191) can produce `x2 > x1` or `y2 > y1` after rounding, causing `checked_sub(x2).unwrap()` / `checked_sub(y2).unwrap()` to panic.

### Finding Description
`calc_take_pnl` is invoked from `process_deposit`, `process_withdraw`, and `process_withdrawpnl` — all instructions reachable by ordinary LPs/pool owners with attacker-influenced vault balances (an attacker can donate/skew token amounts directly to the vaults, similar to the "someone sends 1 wei of dai to the pool" trick in the source report) [1](#0-0) .

Inside `calc_take_pnl`, the guard only checks that `pool_pc_amount * pool_coin_amount >= calc_pc_amount * calc_coin_amount` before proceeding [2](#0-1) . This guarantees the *continuous* (real-number) invariant `x2 <= x1` and `y2 <= y1`, but `x2` is actually computed as an **integer square root** of `x2_power` via `Calculator::calc_x_power` followed by `.integer_sqrt()`, and `y2` is computed by another multiply/divide step: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

Integer division/sqrt truncation and rounding in this chain (multiply, divide, then integer sqrt, then multiply/divide again) means the discrete result can, for certain input ratios, round up past `x1` or `y1` — especially when the two operands being compared are equal or extremely close (mirroring the "same DAI and USSD amount" edge case in the source report). When that happens, the following unchecked subtractions panic instead of returning a graceful error: [5](#0-4) 

A panicking `unwrap()` in a Solana program aborts the transaction, so this doesn't corrupt state directly — but because `calc_take_pnl` is called unconditionally on every `Deposit`, `Withdraw`, and `WithdrawPnl` for a pool, if the vault balances are steered (by direct token transfers to the vault or by the natural drift of `total_pc_without_take_pnl`/`total_coin_without_take_pnl` versus `target.calc_pnl_x`/`calc_pnl_y`) into this boundary condition, **every subsequent Deposit/Withdraw/WithdrawPnl call for that pool will panic and revert**, since the state causing the rounding overflow is not itself mutated by the failed call.

### Impact Explanation
If reachable, this results in the pool's LP deposit/withdraw functionality being permanently disabled ("undesired state"/DoS matching the source report), and LPs could be indefinitely unable to withdraw their liquidity through the normal `Withdraw` path (though `process_swap_base_in/out` do not call `calc_take_pnl` and would remain functional). This maps to "permanent freezing of LP funds" per the validation rubric.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is **low-to-moderate and unproven**: the guarding inequality at lines 188-192 is designed specifically to make `x1 >= x2` and `y1 >= y2` hold, and in most realistic ranges of `x1, y1, calc_pnl_x, calc_pnl_y` the rounding error introduced by `integer_sqrt()` is at most 1 unit in the `U256`/normalized decimal space (`sys_decimal_value` scaled), which may never surface as `x2 > x1` because of the margin created by real trading fees accruing `pool_pc*pool_coin` strictly above `calc_pc*calc_coin`. I could not fully verify with a concrete numeric example (would require deep-diving `integer_sqrt` behavior on `U256` and constructing exact boundary values) whether a genuinely attacker-reachable input combination triggers `x2 > x1`/`y2 > y1` in practice — the code's existing test `test_calc_take_pnl`/`test_calc_pnl_precision` exercise nearby values without hitting the panic. This uncertainty is significant enough that I cannot assert the bug is concretely exploitable without further exact arithmetic proof.

### Recommendation
Replace the unchecked `checked_sub(x2).unwrap()` / `checked_sub(y2).unwrap()` at `program/src/processor.rs:213-214` with saturating subtraction (as already used in `get_max_buy_size_at_price`/`get_max_sell_size_at_price` in `math.rs:252-287`) or with a `checked_sub` that returns a typed `AmmError` (e.g., reusing `AmmError::CheckedSubOverflow`) instead of panicking, so that any rounding-induced edge case degrades to "no pnl taken this call" rather than a hard revert that can recur indefinitely for a stuck pool.

### Proof of Concept
Not constructed — a concrete PoC would require finding exact `(x1, y1, calc_pnl_x, calc_pnl_y)` values (within `AmmInfo`/`TargetOrders` state constraints) that satisfy `pool_pc*pool_coin >= calc_pc*calc_coin` yet cause `integer_sqrt(x2_power) > x1` or the derived `y2 > y1`, which is not proven here.
