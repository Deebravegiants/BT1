### Title
Debt-index accrual can permanently freeze an oversized high-utilization market - (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary
A market that grows to a very large scaled borrow balance can reach a state where the next accrual overflows `i128` before the borrow-index ceiling is reached. Because index accrual runs before pool mutations, repayment, withdrawal, liquidation, and permissionless `update_indexes` all revert, permanently freezing funds in that market.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` first unscales `borrowed` and `supplied` through `scaled_to_original`, then computes utilization, a new borrow index, and `calculate_supplier_rewards`. The reward calculation separately multiplies `borrowed` by both the old and new borrow indexes. [1](#0-0) 

`scaled_to_original` is just `scaled.mul(index)`, and the underlying fixed-point multiplication panics with `MathOverflow` when the result does not fit `i128`. [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

Although `update_borrow_index` clamps the index to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, that clamp is applied only after multiplication and does not bound `borrowed * borrow_index`. [4](#0-3)  The panic can therefore occur while the stored borrow index remains below the ceiling. [5](#0-4) 

The controller exposes `update_indexes` as an authenticated but permissionless maintenance call. [6](#0-5)  Pool accrual processes all elapsed chunks before committing the market state. [7](#0-6) 

### Impact Explanation
Once the overflowing state is reached, every market operation that accrues first traps. The regression test demonstrates that `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay` all fail with `MathOverflow`, meaning suppliers cannot exit, borrowers cannot repay, and liquidators cannot use the normal market paths. [8](#0-7) 

This is permanent freezing of funds in the affected market rather than a transient input rejection. It can also leave bad debt unrecoverable because liquidation and cleanup paths still need market accrual/index valuation before completing.

### Likelihood Explanation
The condition requires a very large representable market and sustained high utilization. The reproduced configuration supplies `1_000_000_000` whole 18-decimal tokens, borrows 98% of them, and lets interest compound for multiple years under a steep rate curve. [9](#0-8) 

Those amounts are economically expensive and require governance-admitted caps and token supply, so this is not an ordinary small-input denial of service. However, the cap is the protocol’s explicit maximum representable amount, and the failure is reached by normal `supply`, `borrow`, and permissionless `update_indexes` activity rather than privileged state corruption.

### Recommendation
Bound market scaled shares by both the asset-domain ceiling and a safe accrued-value ceiling. In particular:

- Calculate `borrowed * projected_borrow_index` and `supplied * projected_supply_index` with widened arithmetic before committing index growth.
- If a result exceeds `i128`, clamp the relevant index or stop further interest accrual before evaluating utilization/rewards rather than allowing a permanent trap.
- Alternatively, enforce market caps low enough that `scaled_amount * MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` and `scaled_amount * MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cannot overflow.
- Add a regression check that withdrawal and repayment remain executable at the maximum admitted scaled balance after index growth.

### Proof of Concept
The repository contains a reproduction in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs`:

1. Create an 18-decimal market using a steep rate curve and disable the utilization cap.
2. Raise supply and borrow caps to the protocol’s representable maximum.
3. Supply `1_000_000_000 * 10^18` base units and borrow 98% of the market.
4. Advance time year by year and call `update_indexes`.
5. A call fails with `MathOverflow` before `borrow_index` reaches `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`; subsequent withdraw and repay calls fail with the same error. [10](#0-9)
