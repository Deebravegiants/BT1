This alt.fun codebase already documents and partially tested a real analog to the "balanceOf() manipulation via large capital inflow" bug class in the external report: an attacker can permissionlessly pre-seed the HyperSwap V2 pair with an extreme reserve ratio before `finalizeGraduation` runs, and the protocol's bounded rebalance mechanism cannot always fully correct it, leaving the graduated LP seeded materially off the curve-close price.

### Title
Budget-capped hostile-preseed rebalance lets an attacker permanently skew the graduation LP price away from curve-close, bleeding value from the locked LP - (File: `packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol`)

### Summary
`finalizeGraduation`'s LP-seeding path (`_seedUniswapV2Direct` → `_seedRebalancing` → `_pairRebalance`) defends against a hostile pre-seeded HyperSwap V2 pair by swapping the pool back toward the cached curve-close ratio before depositing. That corrective swap is capped at 99% of `Bonding`'s own graduation inventory (`_swapBudget`), which is a fixed, protocol-controlled amount independent of how large a ratio the attacker pre-seeds the pair with. An attacker who mints LP against the pair with a sufficiently extreme (e.g. deposits far larger than `tokensForLP`/`ltFromPair`) ratio can make the required corrective swap exceed this budget, so the swap only partially rebalances the pool and the finalized LP opens materially off the last curve price.

### Finding Description
`Bonding._swapBudget` caps the rebalance swap at 99% of the LT/token inventory `Bonding` itself is depositing for this graduation: [1](#0-0) 

`_pairRebalance` computes the theoretically-needed no-fee swap input via `_noFeeSwapInput`, but the caller (`_seedRebalancing`) passes `maxSwap = _swapBudget(...)` as a hard clamp: [2](#0-1) 

Because `tokensForLP`/`ltFromPair` are fixed at phase 1 (derived from the curve's own state, not from the attacker's pre-seed), the budget available to correct the ratio never scales with how skewed the attacker makes the pool. An attacker who front-runs by calling `factory.createPair` (permissionless) and then `transfer + pair.mint(attacker)` with a reserve ratio far enough from the curve-close price forces the "M-02" regime: the corrective swap consumes its full 99% budget and still leaves the pool materially off-ratio when `_routerDepositAndDispose` deposits the remaining inventory via `addLiquidity(min0=1, min1=1, ...)`.

The team's own test explicitly measures and accepts this residual: [3](#0-2) 

and the natspec/AGENTS.md documentation frames "mass conservation prevents fixing both the price and the deposit" as an accepted tradeoff, but only asserts that the *attacker's own* LP claim ends up worth no more than what they deposited — it does not address the fact that the *protocol's locked LP* (held permanently in `LPLock`, which has no withdraw path in v1) opens at a mispriced ratio and is exposed to arbitrage extraction by unrelated third parties, not just the attacker: [4](#0-3) 

This is the direct analog of the external report's `getSupplyProportion()` issue: a live, attacker-influenceable on-chain balance/reserve (here, the pre-seeded HyperSwap pair's reserves) feeds a downstream capital-allocation calculation (the LP-seeding rebalance-and-deposit) whose correction capacity is bounded and independent of the manipulation's magnitude, so a sufficiently large capital inflow produces an incorrect, economically-damaging final state that the protocol has no way to walk back after the fact (`LPLock.recordLock` is one-shot and unrecoverable).

### Impact Explanation
The graduated pool's LP — locked permanently in `LPLock` with `finalizeGraduation` calling the one-shot `recordLock` — opens at a price materially different from the curve's true closing price: [5](#0-4) 

Any gap between the LP's opening price and the true market price is closed by arbitrageurs trading against the pool, which extracts real value from the pool's reserves — the bulk of which (`tokensForLP`, `ltFromPair`) is protocol/creator-owned curve-raised capital, permanently locked with no withdraw mechanism in v1. This is a permanent, unrecoverable value loss to the protocol's own locked liquidity, satisfying the "LP seeded away from the curve close price" impact class.

### Likelihood Explanation
`factory.createPair`, `pair.mint`, and `finalizeGraduation` are all permissionless, and the attack window is the entire `Graduating` phase between phase 1 (`_enterGraduating`) and phase 2 (`finalizeGraduation`), which can be extended by any address delaying the permissionless finalize call. Mounting the "M-02" regime does require the attacker to commit meaningful capital (their own LT/token, as the test uses a 200x-off ratio to exceed the 99% budget), and the attacker's own LP claim ends up capped at or below their deposit value — but the capital is not "burned"; it sits in the pool as reserves and is extracted via arbitrage from the pool as a whole, harming the protocol's much larger locked LP stake rather than the attacker.

### Recommendation
Scale the rebalance swap budget (or reject/hold back finalization) based on how far the pre-existing pool ratio deviates from the cached curve-close ratio, rather than capping strictly at 99% of `Bonding`'s own graduation inventory; alternatively, detect when the post-swap price gap exceeds a safety threshold and route the excess to a slower, permissioned remediation path (e.g., an owner-recoverable escrow) instead of depositing directly into the permanently-locked LP.

### Proof of Concept
This mirrors the already-present `testFuzz_hostilePreSeed_neverProfitable_neverBricks` / the M-02-regime test in `test/TwoPhaseGraduation.t.sol`: [6](#0-5) 
1. Launch and trade a token through `Bonding._enterGraduating` to reach `Lifecycle.Graduating`, caching `tokensForLP` / `ltFromPair`.
2. Front-run `finalizeGraduation`: call `factory.createPair(token, lt)`, `transfer` a token/LT amount at a ratio ~200x off the cached curve-close ratio into the pair, and call `pair.mint(attacker)`.
3. Call the permissionless `finalizeGraduation(token)`. The rebalance swap in `_pairRebalance` clamps at 99% of `Bonding`'s inventory and cannot fully correct the ratio; `_routerDepositAndDispose` deposits the remainder at the still-skewed ratio.
4. Observe (as the existing test does) that the final pool price is >20% off the curve-close price, and that the LP permanently locked in `LPLock` is now exposed to arbitrage extraction of that gap.

        (uint256 tokensForLP, uint256 ltFromPair,,) = bonding.pendingGraduation(tokenAddr);

        // Extreme LT-rich shape: TOKEN side at 1% of target, LT side at 200x.
        // The optimal TOKEN-in swap to reach the cached ratio is ~1.4x
        // `tokensForLP`, so the 99%-of-`tokensForLP` budget cap binds and the

        uint256 reserveToken = tokensForLP / 100;

        // M-02 precondition: the optimal swap exceeds the budget (this is the

        assertGt(

            (tokensForLP * 99) / 100,
            "setup: optimal rebalance swap must exceed the per-side budget (M-02 regime)"
        );

        deal(tokenAddr, griefer, reserveToken);
        address hyperPair = _grieferMintPreSeed(tokenAddr, reserveToken, reserveLt);
        uint256 grieferLp = MockHyperswapPair(hyperPair).balanceOf(griefer);
        uint256 ownerLtBefore = lt.balanceOf(bonding.owner());

        bonding.finalizeGraduation(tokenAddr);
        assertTrue(bonding.isGraduated(tokenAddr), "finalize must succeed despite an unrecoverable pre-seed");

        // The accepted residual: no bounded swap can correct a 200x LT-rich
        // pre-seed, so the pool opens materially off curve-close.
        assertGt(
            _poolPriceLtPerToken(hyperPair, tokenAddr),

        );

        // The over-funded LT side is arbed out by the rebalance swap and swept
        // to the owner — the pre-seeder cannot recover it.

            "the over-funded LT side must be confiscated to the owner"

        // cost-negative.

        assertLt(claimValue * 2, depositValue, "pre-seeder must lose materially, not merely break even");
