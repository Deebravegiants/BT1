### Title
Dust Aquarius-LP collateral leg + self-inflicted pool drain permanently shields an underwater account from liquidation and bad-debt cleanup - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs)

### Summary
A borrower can plant a negligible-value supply leg of an Aquarius LP share token and then, using only their own liquidity position, drain that Aquarius pool below the source's `min_pool_value_wad`. Every valuation-dependent path on the account — `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `withdraw`, `update_account_threshold` — requires a strict price for every collateral leg, and the LP read fails closed with `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity`. The account becomes unliquidatable for as long as the attacker keeps the pool drained, so its debt accrues into bad debt that cannot be permissionlessly socialized — mirroring the incident's "attacker-induced system shutdown" class.

### Finding Description
- `supply` performs no price read, so any listed collateral (including an Aquarius LP share token) can be added to an existing account at dust cost regardless of feed health. Confirmed by `test_stale_price_allows_supply_without_price_read` in `tests/test-harness/tests/oracle/tolerance/staleness.rs:10-19` and by `audit_supply_setup_blocks_liquidation_via_stale_dust_leg` in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_supply_stale_shield.rs:26-35`. [1](#0-0) 
- Required valuations fail closed: a missing/unusable price for any collateral leg aborts liquidation, withdrawal, and bad-debt cleanup (INV-ORACLE-01, INV-LIQ-04). The audit test shows `try_liquidate`, `try_clean_bad_debt_by_id`, and `try_withdraw` all reverting on a single unpriceable leg. [2](#0-1) 
- The Aquarius LP provider rejects any read when the pool's fair value falls below the configured `min_pool_value_wad`: [3](#0-2) 
- An LP in that pool can withdraw its own liquidity on Aquarius (an in-scope "own trades on Aquarius" action) to push `pool_value_wad` under the floor at will, and can keep it there indefinitely. The attacker cannot even remove the dust leg themselves without restoring the pool — `withdraw` reverts on the same price failure — but they have no need to.

### Impact Explanation
While the shield is up, the position accrues interest and can slide deep into insolvency: no liquidator can repay any debt leg (repayment in `liquidate` needs the collateral valuation), and `clean_bad_debt` reverts because it requires valid prices for the account. Once collateral value is exhausted the protocol is left with unsocializable bad debt (owner-only `force-socialize` also reads prices per INV-LIQ-04), and the attacker can restore the pool at any moment to unfreeze the account. Result: protocol insolvency plus temporary freezing of the account's funds, both attacker-controlled.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a listed Aquarius LP collateral market, an account with debt, and enough LP share in that pool to drop its value below `min_pool_value_wad` — all reachable by a single unprivileged address via `supply` (dust LP leg) plus ordinary Aquarius `withdraw_liquidity`. Cost is the dust deposit plus the capital temporarily parked in/removed from the pool. Medium severity: conditional on an eligible LP listing and pool size, but fully deterministic once preconditions hold.

### Recommendation
Decouple per-leg price availability from whole-account solvency actions: e.g., value unpriceable collateral legs at zero for liquidation eligibility/seizure instead of aborting, or allow `clean_bad_debt` to skip legs whose strict read fails (treating them as zero collateral). Alternatively, require a fresh strict price at `supply` time for newly opened collateral positions so a pre-bricked feed cannot be planted, though this does not help against self-inflicted LP drain — the zero-valuation fallback is the robust fix.

### Proof of Concept
Mirror of `audit_supply_setup_blocks_liquidation_via_stale_dust_leg` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_supply_stale_shield.rs`) with an Aquarius LP collateral market:
1. Attacker supplies real collateral, borrows to near-max LTV.
2. Attacker supplies ~1 unit of a listed Aquarius LP share token (`supply(caller, account_id, payments=[(lp_hub_asset, dust)])`) — accepted without any price read.
3. Attacker calls `withdraw_liquidity` on that Aquarius pool until `pool_value_wad < min_pool_value_wad` (`aquarius.rs:120-122`).
4. Collateral price drops; account goes underwater. `liquidate(liquidator, account_id, debt, amount, Transfer)` reverts (price failure on the LP leg); `clean_bad_debt` reverts likewise.
5. Debt accrues into bad debt until the attacker re-adds liquidity, at which point they choose whether to let liquidation proceed or repay.
