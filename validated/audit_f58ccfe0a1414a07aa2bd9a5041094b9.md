### Title
Dual-leg tolerance deviation in price aggregator permanently blocks liquidations while markets diverge - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs)

### Summary

The OpenLeverage report describes an anti-flashloan check that reverts liquidations when spot price deviates >5% from TWAP, freezing liquidations during sustained crashes. XOXNO Lending has the same bug class: `PriceSource::AquariusLp`/`AquariusStableLp` legs are compared against the second oracle leg via `within_tolerance_band`, and any deviation beyond `tolerance.upper_ratio_bps` (e.g. 10_500 = 5%) marks the outcome `deviation`, which `Outcome::failure` maps to `OracleError::UnsafePriceNotAllowed` and `force`/`resolve` panic on. Every controller entrypoint that prices the asset — including `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `withdraw`, `borrow` — resolves prices through this hard-fail path, so an unprivileged trader can halt liquidation of a deteriorating asset simply by trading on Aquarius, and natural market divergence does the same thing without any attacker.

### Finding Description

`compose` builds `Legs::Two { primary, anchor }` from the oracle's two configured sources. `blend` then computes `deviation = !within_tolerance_band(env, anchor.price_wad, primary.price_wad, &oracle.tolerance)` (`contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs:427-428`). `within_tolerance_band` rejects when `high * BPS / low > upper_ratio_bps` (`contracts/price-aggregator/src/tolerance.rs:21-25`). `Outcome::failure` returns `UnsafePriceNotAllowed` when `deviation` is set (`engine.rs:136-138`), and `resolve`/`compute_hard` call `force`, which `panic_with_error!`s on any failure (`engine.rs:181-189`, `engine.rs:286`). Aquarius LP sources are always `FeedNature::Market` (`engine.rs:577`), meaning their "price" is derived from live pool reserves — directly manipulable by any unprivileged swapper on Aquarius/Soroswap, and naturally divergent from Reflector/RedStone legs during volatility.

Unlike OpenLeverage's check (which the sponsor kept as wont-fix because TWAP was only 1-minute valid), the XOXNO deviation flag has no time decay: as long as the Aquarius pool price sits >`upper_ratio_bps` away from the second leg, the outcome is unusable. There is no fallback to the anchor leg or a degraded "use anchor for health check" mode — `Legs::Partial` also sets `deviation: true` (`engine.rs:117`), so even a missing leg bricks pricing rather than degrading.

### Impact Explanation

While the deviation flag is set, every call that resolves the affected `PriceKey` panics: `liquidate` cannot seize collateral, `clean_bad_debt` cannot write down the position, `borrow`/`withdraw`/`multiply`/`flash_position` all revert for markets touching that price key. An underwater borrower's position cannot be closed exactly when it most needs to be — during a fast market move or an active Aquarius manipulation window — so lenders absorb bad debt that timely liquidation would have prevented (protocol insolvency / temporary freezing of funds for all suppliers of the affected hub/spoke books). An attacker with an under-collateralized position can also keep their own liquidation frozen by repeatedly pushing the Aquarius pool outside the band, since deviation is re-evaluated per transaction rather than latched.

### Likelihood Explanation

Two independent triggers exist. (1) Natural: Aquarius constant-product pools move with every trade; during volatility, a single normal-size swap can push the pool more than the configured 5% (or tighter `upper_ratio_bps`) from a slower-updating Reflector/RedStone leg. (2) Adversarial: any unprivileged address can swap on Aquarius (explicitly in scope) to push the LP-derived leg outside the band, then submit `liquidate`/`clean_bad_debt` is impossible for anyone else until an arbitrageur restores the price — which the attacker's own position being under water does not incentivize. Severity is bounded by the rescue that arbitrage provides, hence Medium/High rather than Critical: funds are not stolen, but liquidation availability — the protocol's core solvency mechanism — is gated by an attacker-manipulable or naturally-divergent band, identical in spirit to the confirmed OpenLeverage finding.

### Recommendation

Do not treat leg deviation as a hard failure for risk-affecting paths. Options consistent with the codebase shape: return the `PriceStatus` deviation flag to the controller and let the risk module use the more conservative of the two legs (min for collateral valuation, max for debt valuation) instead of `panic_with_error!`; or latch the deviation only for value-increasing operations (`borrow`, `withdraw`, `multiply`, `flash_position`) while letting `liquidate`, `repay`, and `clean_bad_debt` proceed on the conservative leg. If the band must remain for manipulation protection, add a time-decay so a sustained real market move cannot block liquidation indefinitely.

### Proof of Concept

1. Governance configures an oracle `AssetOracle` for asset X with two sources: `PriceSource::AquariusLp` (pool P, X/USDC) as primary and `PriceSource::Feed` (Reflector) as anchor, `tolerance.upper_ratio_bps = 10_500`.
2. Borrower B opens a leveraged X-collateralized debt position near the liquidation threshold.
3. Adversary A (or B itself to self-protect, or simply a large organic trade) swaps on Aquarius pool P, moving the X price down 6%. `compose` now yields `Legs::Two` where `within_tolerance_band` returns `false` (`engine.rs:427-428`, `tolerance.rs:21-25`), so `Outcome.deviation = true`.
4. Any liquidator calling `controller.liquidate(...)` on B's position reaches `resolve` → `compute_hard` → `force`, which panics with `OracleError::UnsafePriceNotAllowed` (`engine.rs:136-138`, `engine.rs:183`), reverting the liquidation.
5. Interest keeps accruing (`update_indexes` for debt shares does not require the price to clear the band), the position drifts further under water, and `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` pricing paths are equally blocked; lender funds are effectively frozen behind a flag any unprivileged swapper can keep set.
