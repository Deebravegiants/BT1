### Title
Deposit LP-share minting uses raw SPL vault balances with no minimum-LP-out check, enabling a donation attack that steals depositor funds - (File: program/src/processor.rs)

### Summary
`process_deposit` computes the number of LP tokens to mint (`mint_lp_amount`) from `total_coin_without_take_pnl` / `total_pc_without_take_pnl`, which are derived directly from the live SPL token balances of `amm_coin_vault` / `amm_pc_vault` [1](#0-0) . Because these vault accounts are ordinary SPL token accounts, any unprivileged actor can transfer ("donate") tokens directly into them without going through the `Deposit` instruction, inflating the balances used in the exchange-rate math while leaving `amm.lp_amount` (the LP supply denominator/tracking variable) unchanged. `DepositInstruction` only exposes `max_coin_amount`, `max_pc_amount`, `base_side`, and an `other_amount_min` that bounds the *ratio* of the two deposited tokens - there is no parameter to enforce a minimum LP output [2](#0-1) . This mirrors the reported PRST-6 bug class: share-minting math based on manipulable pool balances with no "minimum shares out" protection.

### Finding Description
`mint_lp_amount` is computed as `deduct_coin_amount * amm.lp_amount / total_coin_without_take_pnl` (or the PC-side equivalent), floor-rounded, via `InvariantPool::exchange_token_to_pool` [3](#0-2) . `total_coin_without_take_pnl`/`total_pc_without_take_pnl` come straight from `amm_coin_vault.amount`/`amm_pc_vault.amount`, i.e. the actual SPL balances of the vault accounts owned by the pool authority [4](#0-3) . An attacker (or anyone) can:

1. Create a pool via `Initialize2` with the minimum amounts that just clear the `InitLpAmountTooLess` floor, so `amm.lp_amount` (tracked pool-wide LP units) stays low and the attacker holds nearly the entire LP supply [5](#0-4) .
2. Directly transfer (donate) a large amount of coin and PC tokens into `amm_coin_vault`/`amm_pc_vault` using a standard SPL `Transfer` instruction (no authority over the vault is required to transfer tokens *into* it — only the mint authority/owner is checked when transferring *out*).
3. Because the donation is proportional to the existing price ratio, the `other_amount_min` ratio check in `Deposit` still passes for the victim, but `total_coin_without_take_pnl`/`total_pc_without_take_pnl` are now inflated relative to `amm.lp_amount`, so a legitimate depositor's `mint_lp_amount` is rounded down disproportionately to the value they deposit — there is no parameter to reject this outcome, only a check that it isn't exactly zero [6](#0-5) .
4. The attacker then calls `Withdraw`, which computes `coin_amount`/`pc_amount` as `withdraw.amount * total_*_without_take_pnl / amm.lp_amount` [7](#0-6) . Since the attacker holds nearly all of `amm.lp_amount` and the vault balances now include both the donation and the diluted victim deposit, the attacker redeems a disproportionate share of the pool, recovering their donation plus a portion of the victim's newly-deposited funds.

This is architecturally identical to the classic ERC4626/vault "donation" inflation attack described in the report: minting math keyed off a manipulable balance-of-style total with no minimum-output slippage protection for the minted share token.

### Impact Explanation
A victim's `Deposit` can be silently diluted so that they receive far fewer LP tokens than their contribution is worth, while the pool creator/attacker (holding nearly all pre-existing LP supply) can withdraw a disproportionate share of the pool, including part of the victim's freshly deposited assets. This is a concrete theft of LP/user funds reachable from a single unprivileged transaction sequence with attacker-chosen accounts (create pool, donate, wait for victim deposit, withdraw).

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires the attacker to control (or newly create) a low-liquidity pool and front-run/interleave with a victim's deposit — feasible since pool creation (`Initialize2`) is permissionless, and Solana's transaction ordering allows an attacker to place a donation transfer immediately before a target deposit transaction lands. The `InitLpAmountTooLess` floor and existing rounding-down protections raise the capital cost somewhat but do not eliminate the exploit, particularly against newly created/low-liquidity pools that are the most attractive targets for depositors.

### Recommendation
Add an explicit `min_lp_amount_out` parameter to the `Deposit` instruction (analogous to `other_amount_min`, but bounding `mint_lp_amount` rather than the paired token amount), and reject the deposit if the computed `mint_lp_amount` falls below it. Optionally, track internal pool reserves in `AmmInfo` state (rather than relying solely on live SPL vault balances) so unsolicited direct transfers into the vault cannot silently be folded into share-price calculations at deposit/withdraw time.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual sequence (build with a background agent/test harness for exact numeric parameters given target token decimals and the `InitLpAmountTooLess` threshold):
1. Attacker calls `Initialize2` with minimal `init_coin_amount`/`init_pc_amount` just above the MINIMUM_LIQUIDITY threshold enforced at [8](#0-7) , receiving nearly all of `amm.lp_amount`.
2. Attacker issues a plain SPL `Transfer` (not via the AMM program) of a large amount of coin+pc tokens, in the current pool ratio, directly to `amm_coin_vault`/`amm_pc_vault`.
3. Victim submits `Deposit` with reasonable `max_coin_amount`/`max_pc_amount`/`other_amount_min`; because ratio is unchanged, the ratio check passes but `mint_lp_amount` (computed at [9](#0-8) ) is rounded down far more than the victim expects, since `total_coin_without_take_pnl` is inflated relative to `amm.lp_amount`.
4. Attacker calls `Withdraw` for their LP tokens, receiving `coin_amount`/`pc_amount` computed at [7](#0-6) , recovering their donation plus a disproportionate share of the victim's deposit.
