### Title
Bad-debt write-down clamps `supply_index` to the floor and leaves unbacked residual claims that drain fresh deposits - ([File: contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs](contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs))

### Summary
CVE-2023-6207 is a use-after-free: an object retains an owner who can keep using it after its backing storage is freed. The XOXNO Lending analog is the supply-index write-down in `seize_positions`: when bad debt is cleaned, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` socializes the loss by lowering `supply_index`, but clamps it at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`, `10^24`) instead of zero. Suppliers' scaled shares are never burned to match, so they keep a "freed" claim: scaled balances that unscale to real asset value while the market's `cash` is empty. When a later depositor supplies into the wiped market, the stranded holder's `withdraw` passes `require_reserves` against the fresh cash and extracts the new deposit — ownership over value that was already written off survives and consumes unrelated funds.

### Finding Description
The bad-debt path runs:

- Controller `clean_bad_debt` / liquidation `bad_debt` leg → pool `seize_positions` → `ops/seize.rs` → `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, which lowers `supply_index` but floors it at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs`, `contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs`; documented in `docs/reference/invariants.md` INV-IDX-02/03 and `contracts/pool/README.md`: "`supply_index` is **not** monotone … floored at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`").
- Because the floor clamps the index up rather than to zero, surviving supply shares still unscale to a positive residual claim (`unscale_supply_floor`).
- The pool's own test `test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:430-495`) proves the mechanics: after a full seize, `supply_index == SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, `cash == 0`, Alice's stranded claim `> 0`. Bob's fresh `supply` + `credit_cash` makes the book insolvent (`total_owed > cash`), and Alice's `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX)` → `require_reserves(gross)` → `debit_cash(gross)` pays her exactly Bob's deposit, leaving Bob's honest claim unpayable.
- No pool-side supply guard blocks a deposit into a floor-clamped wiped market: `supply`'s guards check reserves/utilization against the `cash` book, which is exactly what the deposit refills, and the doc note in INV-IDX-02 explicitly acknowledges "an unbacked residual claim" can be preserved.

Reachability is unprivileged: `clean_bad_debt` and `liquidate` are permissionless controller entrypoints; `withdraw` is a normal user call; the victim's `supply` needs no attacker privilege. The attacker only needs to hold supply shares in a market that accrues bad debt above its dust threshold (e.g., a market whose collateral is exhausted by a full seize while borrow remains), which can be self-arranged via two of the attacker's own positions.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency: a wiped-market residual claimant withdraws value backed only by a subsequent depositor's fresh cash, leaving the new supplier's claim permanently unpayable (the book shows solvency while custody is gone — the same "book overstates custody" failure demonstrated in `contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3404-3483`, where `require_reserves` passes and the SAC transfer fails). Each wiped market converts the next deposit into a payout to the stranded holder.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. It requires a market to undergo a bad-debt write-down severe enough to pin `supply_index` at the floor while unburned supply shares remain — reachable on small/long-tail markets where a liquidation leaves `supplied > 0` scaled shares against zero cash. Once pinned, the condition is persistent: any later supply into that market is capturable, and the attacker controls both the stranded claim and the timing of withdrawal. No privileged action is needed at any step.

### Recommendation
- On write-down, burn the residual: when the computed index would clamp at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, instead pro-rata reduce/burn `supplied` (or write remaining claims to a separate non-withdrawable ledger entry) so scaled claims equal the index-implied residual exactly.
- Add a supply guard rejecting `supply`/`recapitalize`-driven deposits into a market whose `supply_index` sits at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` with `cash == 0`, or require explicit admin re-initialization of the wiped market.
- Extend `require_reserves`/withdraw to bound `gross` by `min(cash, unscaled position)` is insufficient alone; the invariant to enforce is `unscale_supply_floor(supplied) <= cash` at floor state.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker supplies `S` into market M and borrows; a distressed position is liquidated with `SeizeMode`/bad-debt leg so `seize_positions` calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` and clamps `supply_index` to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` while `cash == 0`. Attacker's scaled shares now unscale to residual `R > 0`.
2. Victim calls `supply` on M with amount `R`; pool mints shares and credits `cash`.
3. Attacker calls `withdraw(amount = i128::MAX)`; `resolve_withdrawal` returns `gross = R`, `require_reserves` passes (cash was just refilled), `debit_cash` and `transfer_out` send the victim's `R` to the attacker.
4. Victim's claim unscale value `R` now exceeds `cash = 0`; any later withdraw fails inside the SAC transfer despite the book reporting funds. This is exactly the sequence pinned in `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:430-495`.
