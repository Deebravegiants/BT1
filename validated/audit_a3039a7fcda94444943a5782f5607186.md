### Title
Precision loss in `convert_to_balance` truncates incoming EVM amounts, permanently locking dust and desynchronizing escrow/mint accounting - (File: `modules/pallets/hyper-fungible-token/src/impls.rs`)

### Summary
When a user sends `BridgeToken` (18 decimals) from an EVM chain to nexus (12 decimals), the pallet's `onAccept`/receive path converts the incoming `U256` amount to the local `u128` balance using `convert_to_balance`, which divides by `10^(erc_decimals - local_decimals)` and truncates the remainder. This is the same class of bug as the `Bonding.sol` finding: an integer division by a decimal-scaling factor silently discards the remainder, so the amount credited/unlocked locally is systematically less than the amount that was burned/escrowed on the sending chain, permanently orphaning the truncated dust.

### Finding Description
`convert_to_balance` is defined as: [1](#0-0) 

It divides the raw `U256` ERC20-precision `value` by `10^(erc_decimals - local_decimals)` (here `10^6`, since BRIDGE is 18 decimals on EVM and 12 decimals on nexus, as documented in `BridgeToken.sol`): [2](#0-1) 

Any fractional remainder smaller than `10^6` wei (i.e. `value % 10^6 != 0`) is truncated by the integer division. The corresponding `convert_to_erc20` is the inverse operation (`value * 10^(erc_decimals - local_decimals)`), used when nexus sends tokens back out to an EVM chain: [3](#0-2) 

This mirrors exactly the `Bonding.sol._openTradingOnUniswap()` bug: a division to convert precision, followed later by a multiplication elsewhere on the other side, that does not round-trip cleanly. For `BridgeToken`, `pallet-hyper-fungible-token` escrows the native balance on nexus and the EVM contract mints the equivalent amount there, so the pallet's escrow record and the EVM's minted supply must stay in lock-step: [4](#0-3) 

Because `convert_to_balance` truncates on the "amount arriving from EVM into the pallet" direction, an amount like `1_000_000_000_000_000_001` wei (18-decimal) sent from EVM converts to `1_000_000` local units (truncating the trailing `1` wei), a difference below `10^6`. When this local balance is later converted back to EVM precision via `convert_to_erc20` and multiplied by `10^6`, the reconstructed EVM amount is `1_000_000_000_000_000_000` — permanently 1 wei less than what any user or relayer accounting expects, and that dust is stuck, unaccounted for, in whichever custody/escrow the value passed through.

This differs somewhat from the referenced Solidity report in that dust here is sub-unit wei amounts (bounded by `10^6 - 1` per transfer), but it is systemic: every single cross-chain transfer between BRIDGE's 18-decimal EVM representation and its 12-decimal nexus-native representation that does not land on an exact multiple of `10^6` wei loses funds to truncation, with no mechanism to track, refund, or reconcile the lost dust.

### Impact Explanation
Each affected transfer permanently loses up to `999,999` wei (just under `10^-12` of one BRIDGE token) to truncation with no recovery path — this is small per-transaction but is a deterministic protocol-level fund loss that accumulates over the lifetime of the token across every relayed transfer that isn't a round multiple of the local chain's smallest unit. Because BRIDGE's escrow model requires the EVM minted supply to always be fully backed by the nexus pallet's escrow balance, systematic truncation on one leg while the reverse leg does not correspondingly refund it can also drift the on-chain supply invariant asserted by the design (`the supply of this token is always backed by the pallet's escrow account`). This is a Medium-severity finding: it is a permanent, protocol-level loss of funds (dust), not a critical drain, and it requires no privileged actor — any user's own cross-chain transfer of BRIDGE (or any future token registered through this generic pallet with mismatched EVM/local decimals) triggers it.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is high for occurrence (any transfer amount not an exact multiple of `10^(erc_decimals - local_decimals)` triggers truncation) but the per-transaction loss is bounded and small, which is why this is categorized as Medium rather than High/Critical.

### Recommendation
Track the truncated remainder explicitly (e.g., accumulate dust in a pallet-owned reserve, or reject/round transfer amounts that are not exact multiples of the scaling factor and refund the sender the non-convertible remainder) rather than silently discarding it in `convert_to_balance`. At minimum, enforce on the EVM `send()`/message-encoding side that `amount % 10^(erc_decimals - local_decimals) == 0`, so a user cannot burn/lock a non-representable amount that the destination chain then arbitrarily truncates.

### Proof of Concept
1. Register BRIDGE with `erc_decimals = 18`, `local_decimals = 12` (as done for nexus in `BridgeToken.sol`, `modules/pallets/hyper-fungible-token`).
2. From an EVM chain, call `HyperFungibleToken.send()` (inherited by `BridgeToken`) with `amount = 1_000_000_000_000_000_001` (i.e., `10^18 + 1` wei), burning that exact amount on the EVM side.
3. On delivery, the pallet's `onAccept` handler calls `convert_to_balance(U256::from(1_000_000_000_000_000_001u128), 18, 12)`, computing `1_000_000_000_000_000_001 / 10^6 = 1_000_000_000_000` (integer division), truncating the final `1` wei.
4. The recipient on nexus is credited `1_000_000_000_000` local units, corresponding to exactly `1_000_000_000_000_000_000` wei of EVM-precision value — 1 wei less than what was burned. That 1 wei is permanently unaccounted for; no event or storage item tracks it. [5](#0-4)

/// Converts an ERC20 U256 amount to a local balance type
///
/// Divides by 10^(erc_decimals - local_decimals) to scale down from ERC20 precision.
/// The target type must implement `FromStr`.
pub fn convert_to_balance<B: core::str::FromStr>(
	value: U256,
	erc_decimals: u8,
	local_decimals: u8,
) -> Result<B, B::Err> {
	let dec_str = (value /
		U256::from(10u128.pow(erc_decimals.saturating_sub(local_decimals) as u32)))
	.to_string();
	dec_str.parse::<B>()
}
