### Title
Stale `account.owner` authorizes the previous NFT holder to move funds after the account is sold - (File: contracts/controller/src/account.rs)

### Summary
The controller tracks account ownership in two different places, and the fund-moving paths authorize against the wrong one. `Account.owner` is a stored field written once at account creation (`create_account_with`), while the actual ownership instrument is the transferable position NFT. Every value-moving entrypoint (`borrow`, `withdraw`, `swap_debt`, `swap_collateral`, `repay_debt_with_collateral`, `multiply`, `flash_position`, `migrate_from_blend`) gates on `require_owner_or_delegate(env, account_id, caller, &account.owner)` — i.e. the *recorded* owner — instead of the live NFT holder returned by `storage::account_owner`, which is what `require_account_owner` uses for delegate management. The position NFT is a stock OpenZeppelin enumerable token (`position-nft::transfer`, `transfer_from`); its transfer emits no callback into the controller, so `account.owner` is never re-synced after a sale or gift. The previous holder therefore retains full spending authority over an account they no longer own — the on-chain analog of a read-scoped/stale credential performing write operations.

### Finding Description
`create_account_with` bakes the minter into `Account.owner` and persists it (`contracts/controller/src/account.rs`, lines 44-74). `is_owner_or_delegate` / `require_owner_or_delegate` then compare `caller` against that stored `owner` field and the delegate list keyed under it (lines 115-140). In contrast, `require_account_owner` deliberately re-reads the live NFT owner via `storage::account_owner` (lines 143-148), which proves the codebase already treats NFT ownership as authoritative — but only for `add_delegate`, `remove_delegate`, and `renew_account`, none of which move funds. `position-nft::transfer`/`transfer_from` are pinned stock OZ methods with no controller hook (see `scripts/permissionless_entrypoints.txt` lines 108-114 and the checker’s `CONTRACTTRAIT_METHODS` table), so nothing updates `account.owner` or clears delegates keyed under the old owner. The result is an authorization scope mismatch: possession of the NFT (the tradable ownership proof) does not grant spending authority, and loss of the NFT does not revoke it.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds. Anyone who acquires a funded lending account NFT (secondary sale, OTC deal, compromised marketplace approval, or a gifted account) can have the entire position drained by the *seller*: the seller, still recorded as `account.owner`, calls `withdraw` with `to` set to their own address to pull all collateral, or `borrow` to max out debt and keep the proceeds, leaving the buyer with a worthless or deeply underwater NFT. The attack requires no privilege beyond having once owned the account — exactly the incorrectly-scoped-authorization class: a credential that should have been invalidated continues to authorize write operations.

### Likelihood Explanation
Fully reachable by an unprivileged address: `withdraw`/`borrow` are permissionless entrypoints where `caller.require_auth()` is the only signature needed, and the seller legitimately holds the signing key for the recorded `account.owner`. The precondition is only that an account NFT has changed hands at least once, which the NFT’s own transfer/approve surface exists to support. No oracle manipulation, flash loan, or governance action is required.

### Recommendation
Authorize fund-moving paths against the live NFT owner, not the stored field: replace `require_owner_or_delegate(env, account_id, caller, &account.owner)` in `process_borrow`, `process_withdraw`, `load_or_create_account` (Migrate/Multiply guards), and the strategy entrypoints with a resolution that first calls `storage::account_owner(env, account_id)` and then checks the delegate list under that current owner. Alternatively, persist `account.owner` as a cache that is refreshed from the NFT at the top of every position flow, and key delegate grants under the current owner so a transfer invalidates prior grants atomically.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice calls `supply(caller=alice, account_id=0, spoke_id=1, [(XLM, 1000e7)])`; `create_account_with` mints NFT `id=7` to Alice and stores `Account{owner: alice, ..}`.
2. Alice transfers NFT 7 to Bob via `position-nft::transfer(from=alice, to=bob, token_id=7)` — or Bob buys it on a marketplace via `transfer_from`. `account.owner` remains `alice`.
3. Alice calls `withdraw(caller=alice, account_id=7, [(XLM, 0)] /* withdraw-all sentinel */, to=Some(alice))`. `process_withdraw` executes `require_owner_or_delegate(env, 7, alice, &account.owner)` where `account.owner == alice`, so the check passes; `settle_withdraw` pays the pool’s full XLM position to Alice and solvency trivially holds (no debt).
4. Bob, the legitimate owner, cannot stop it: `remove_delegate`/`renew_account` require the NFT owner (Bob), but `withdraw` and `borrow` never consult the NFT.
5. Variants: Alice instead calls `borrow(caller=alice, account_id=7, [(USDC, max)], to=Some(alice))` to extract all borrowable value against Bob’s collateral, or uses `multiply`/`flash_position`/`repay_debt_with_collateral` against `account_id=7`, all gated by the same stale-owner check.

Note on certainty: I verified the two ownership sources and that no NFT→controller transfer hook exists in the pinned stock surface, but I could not exhaustively confirm whether `storage::get_account` re-resolves `owner` from the NFT on load; if it does not (the stored field is authoritative), the vulnerability holds exactly as described, and the existence of the separate `storage::account_owner` path in `require_account_owner` strongly suggests the stored field is known to diverge.
