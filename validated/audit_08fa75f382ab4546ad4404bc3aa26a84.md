### Title
Permissionless third-party `supply` top-up can keep an insolvent account's collateral above the $5 dust threshold, indefinitely blocking `clean_bad_debt` bad-debt socialization - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs)

### Summary
CVE-2021-26788's class — missing input validation on attacker-controlled input producing a persistent denial of service — maps onto `Controller::supply` in the XOXNO Lending controller. `supply` accepts arbitrary positive amounts and, crucially, lets *any* caller top up an *existing* supply position on an account they do not own (`account_id != 0`, asset already held). There is no validation that rejects top-ups on insolvent accounts or accounts eligible for `clean_bad_debt`. Combined with `clean_bad_debt`'s permissionless gate — collateral must be at or below the fixed `BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD` ($5 WAD) while debt exceeds collateral — an unprivileged attacker can perpetually push the account's collateral back above $5 with a small donation, keeping bad debt unsocializable.

### Finding Description
`supply(caller, account_id, spoke_id, assets)` is open to any authorized caller, and a third party may top up a hub asset the account already supplies; only opening a *new* asset slot is owner/delegate-gated. `supply` and `repay` skip the post-operation solvency gate, so nothing stops a top-up to an underwater account.

`clean_bad_debt` is the only permissionless path that writes off insolvent debt: it requires `debt > collateral` and `collateral <= BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD` ($5), reverting with `CannotCleanBadDebt` (#114) otherwise. The only bypass is the owner-gated `force_socialize_bad_debt`, which is privileged governance.

An attacker watching a deeply insolvent account can call `supply` with a few dollars of an already-held asset whenever collateral drifts at or below $5 (including after partial liquidations seize collateral). Each top-up restores `collateral > $5`, reverting every subsequent `clean_bad_debt` call while the condition holds. Because the added collateral is real, the attacker's cost is bounded but the account can never be cleaned up permissionlessly while the attacker funds it.

### Impact Explanation
Bad debt remains on the books indefinitely. The residual debt is never written down against the market, so the market's cash shortfall persists: suppliers at the tail of the withdrawal queue cannot be paid from cash plus outstanding debt until either governance force-socializes (a privileged, timelocked path — not guaranteed) or `recapitalize` donors cover the shortfall. This is a persistent freezing of supplier funds / sustained protocol insolvency produced entirely by unprivileged calls.

### Likelihood Explanation
Low-to-moderate cost, but ongoing: the attacker must keep collateral above $5 and loses each donation (it becomes account collateral). Requires an insolvent account to reach the cleanup regime and a motivated griefer (e.g., the account owner avoiding socialization, or a competitor). Both `supply` and `clean_bad_debt` are callable even during global pause, and no listing flag blocks the top-up side (a `frozen`/`paused` listing blocks entry, which partially limits the vector on flagged markets).

### Recommendation
Reject third-party `supply` top-ups on accounts eligible for `clean_bad_debt`, or exclude permissionless third-party top-ups from collateral when `debt > collateral`. Alternatively, evaluate the dust threshold on collateral excluding amounts supplied by non-owners after insolvency, or add a permissionless cleanup path that ignores collateral legs added by non-owners.

### Proof of Concept
1. Account A becomes insolvent: debt `D` exceeds collateral `C`, with `C <= $5`, so `clean_bad_debt` is callable.
2. Attacker (any address, no ownership needed) calls `supply(attacker, A, A.spoke_id, [(hub_asset_of_existing_supply_leg, amount)])` with `amount` worth >$5.
3. `C` now exceeds `BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD`; every `clean_bad_debt` reverts with `CannotCleanBadDebt` (#114).
4. Repeat step 2 whenever liquidation or price movement brings `C` back to <= $5. Only governance's force path or a recapitalizer can resolve the debt.

Caveat: `endpoints.md` and `permissionless_entrypoints.txt` document third-party top-up and the dust cap as deliberate design; whether the interplay (griefing cleanup) was considered is not stated. If this is deemed an accepted design tradeoff given the governance fallback, severity drops to informational. Permissionless cleanup requires ceil risk debt greater than half-up unweighted
collateral and collateral at or below the fixed $5 dust threshold. Owner-only
forced cleanup omits the dust cap. Both require debt, readable account and NFT
state, valid required prices and no active flash guard. Listing flags and
global pause do not block standalone cleanup.

Cleanup reclassifies all remaining collateral shares as revenue and writes off
all remaining debt against each debt's market. It releases spoke usage and
atomically removes account entries and the NFT. It does not net same-market
supply against debt. Standalone cleanup emits `CleanBadDebtEvent` with
pre-cleanup USD totals, without a controller position-update batch.

Ordinary liquidation and cleanup apply no final account-health or full-backing
assertion. The index floor can leave a shortfall. Recapitalization fills that
shortfall without restoring the lost index or deleted account.
