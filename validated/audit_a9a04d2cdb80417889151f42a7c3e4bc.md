### Title
Borrower self-shields from liquidation and bad-debt cleanup by draining an Aquarius LP collateral's pool below `min_pool_value_wad` — ([File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs])

### Summary
An unprivileged borrower can permanently block liquidation, `clean_bad_debt`, and `force_socialize_bad_debt` on their own underwater account by holding an Aquarius LP token as a collateral leg and withdrawing the underlying Aquarius pool's liquidity below the oracle source's configured `min_pool_value_wad`. Every valuation-dependent controller path prices **all** supply legs of an account, so one leg whose price cannot be resolved makes the entire account unpriceable. This mirrors the advisory's bug class — an attacker-reachable denial of service (availability impact only, CVSS A:H) — mapped onto XOXNO Lending's fail-closed pricing of collateral legs.

### Finding Description
- The Aquarius LP provider computes the pool's total value and rejects the read with `OracleError::InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` when `pool_value_wad < lp.min_pool_value_wad` (`contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs:118-122`). The observation timestamp and price come from the pool's live reserves and total shares, so any LP holder can push the pool under the floor simply by withdrawing liquidity — an "own trade on Aquarius", which is a permitted unprivileged action.
- `build_liquidation_plan` calls `risk::calculate_account_risk_totals` over `account.supply_positions` and `account.borrow_positions`, and asserts `health_factor < Wad::ONE` (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/plan.rs:34-44`). Each leg is priced via `cache.cached_price`, and `calculate_seized_collateral` iterates every supply leg calling `cached_price`/`cached_market_index` again (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/math.rs:383-385`). A leg that errors aborts the whole call — there is no collateral-selection argument for the liquidator (per `SpokeAssetSeizureHalted` semantics, "liquidation has no collateral-selection argument").
- The threat model documents this exact shape: "Supply needs no price, so an indebted borrower can add a dust leg of any listed collateral and choose which feed outage shields the account. For an Aquarius LP leg, liquidity providers can cause that outage by withdrawing pool value below `min_pool_value_wad`. The same leg blocks bad-debt cleanup and force-socialization" (`docs/explanation/threat-model.md:364`). The ops alerting confirms the impact: "Under 1.0x the LP price is rejected and accounts holding this leg become unliquidatable" (`services/lending-exporter/ops/alerts.yml:42-48`).
- `clean_bad_debt` eligibility (`is_socializable_bad_debt`) requires `total_debt > total_collateral`, which is computed from the same priced risk totals — so the account also cannot be socialized while the price is unavailable.

### Impact Explanation
Protocol insolvency / permanent freezing of liquidation on the attacker's account. While the shield holds, no liquidator can repay the attacker's debt or seize collateral, and governance cannot socialize the bad debt. The attacker can wait out favorable price moves (or even let the debt accrue past the collateral value), then restore pool liquidity to unshield — or leave the account permanently unliquidatable, converting the position into socialized bad debt borne by suppliers. Suppliers' funds are effectively frozen against an uncollectible debt.

### Likelihood Explanation
Fully attacker-controlled once an Aquarius LP token is listed as collateral:
1. The attacker supplies real collateral, borrows near max LTV, then supplies a small LP-share leg (supply is permissionless and price-free).
2. The attacker needs only enough LP share ownership (or coordination is not even needed — they can be the dominant LP) in the referenced Aquarius pool to withdraw reserves below `min_pool_value_wad`. The floor is set per-source and mainnet configs exist with nonzero floors.
3. The borrower's own positions make them likely to become liquidatable during volatility — exactly when they would trigger the shield. Cost is the LP capital temporarily withdrawn plus a dust collateral leg.

No privileged access, oracle dishonesty, or third-party cooperation is required — the "outage" is induced by the attacker's own permitted AMM withdrawal, not by assumed feed failure.

### Recommendation
- Make per-leg price failure non-fatal in liquidation accounting: skip or floor a collateral leg whose price cannot be resolved rather than aborting the whole `calculate_account_risk_totals`/`calculate_seized_collateral` pass (treat unpriceable collateral as zero value — it contributes nothing to seizure anyway).
- Alternatively, allow `liquidate`/`clean_bad_debt` to exclude unpriceable legs explicitly, and let `is_socializable_bad_debt` treat an unpriceable collateral leg as zero so bad-debt socialization cannot be blocked by a self-induced feed outage.
- Size `min_pool_value_wad` relative to expected withdrawal granularity, and alert before accounts can shelter behind the floor (the existing `LendingLpPoolValueNearFloor` alert covers detection but not prevention).

### Proof of Concept

Setup (single unprivileged address ATTACKER):
1. Governance has listed LP token S (share token of Aquarius pool P) as collateral
   in a spoke, with oracle source { pool: P, min_pool_value_wad: F > 0 }.
2. ATTACKER supply(USDC, $10_000); supply(S, dust); borrow(ETH) near max LTV.
3. ATTACKER is the dominant LP in P (deposited earlier). They call
   P.withdraw(...) removing reserves so that
   fair_price(S) * total_shares / 10^share_decimals < F.

Trigger:
4. Any liquidator calls controller.liquidate(LIQ, attacker_id, payments, Transfer).
   - build_liquidation_plan -> calculate_account_risk_totals -> cached_price(S)
   - aquarius::read -> pool_value_wad < F -> InsufficientAquariusLiquidity
   - price resolution fails closed -> liquidation reverts.
5. clean_bad_debt / force_socialize_bad_debt revert identically because
   total_collateral cannot be computed without S's price.
6. ATTACKER re-deposits into P whenever they want liquidation unblocked
   (e.g., after prices recover), or never — leaving bad debt socialized.
