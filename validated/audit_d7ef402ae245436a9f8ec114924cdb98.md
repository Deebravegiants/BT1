### Title
Borrower can push utilization to `max_utilization` to grief and revert every subsequent non-liquidation `withdraw` - ([File: contracts/pool/src/guards.rs])

### Summary
The pool enforces `require_utilization_below_max` on ordinary `withdraw` operations as an absolute post-state check on the whole market. A single unprivileged user can borrow (through `controller.borrow` on their own collateralized account) until market utilization sits exactly at `params.max_utilization`. After that, any victim's `withdraw` — which burns supply shares and therefore shrinks the denominator (`supplied × supply_index`) while debt stays constant — pushes utilization strictly above the cap and reverts with `UtilizationAboveMax` (error 127). The victim's withdraw is blocked for as long as the attacker keeps the debt open, a griefing-style front-run of the same class as the original `sendPortfolio`/`withdrawCollateral` DoS.

### Finding Description
`require_utilization_below_max` computes `borrowed_ceil / supplied_floor` and panics if it exceeds `params.max_utilization` (`contracts/pool/src/guards.rs:19-34`). In the withdraw path, `accounting` first burns the victim's supply shares (`burn_position`, `contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:104-107`) and then `gate_and_debit` calls `require_utilization_below_max` unless the call is a liquidation or an empty footprint close (`contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:111-119`). Burning supply lowers `supplied`, so the ratio `borrowed/supplied` rises monotonically with every withdrawal.

Meanwhile, the borrow path `mint_debt` enforces the same cap *post-mint* (`contracts/pool/src/ops/borrow.rs:78`), meaning the attacker is allowed to land exactly at `borrowed/supplied == max_utilization` but cannot exceed it. At exactly the cap, the smallest victim withdrawal of any positive amount tips the ratio above the cap and reverts. The victim cannot withdraw any amount — even dust — without first seeing debt repaid, because the check is on post-withdraw market state, not on the withdrawal size. The README confirms this behavior is intentional-looking but absolute: "once utilization reaches the cap no withdrawal of any size passes" (`contracts/pool/README.md:292-297`).

Only withdrawals are affected: liquidations skip the check via `is_liquidation || empty_close` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:79`), so there is no safety argument for gating ordinary exits.

### Impact Explanation
Temporary freezing of all suppliers' funds in the affected (hub, token) market. Every non-liquidation `controller.withdraw` reverts with `UtilizationAboveMax` while the attacker's borrow keeps utilization at the cap. Suppliers cannot exit even partially, cannot respond to adverse market moves, and cannot recover their principal until the attacker repays or liquidations reduce debt (which itself requires unhealthy positions to exist). The attacker can sustain the block indefinitely by topping up collateral on their own account to avoid liquidation.

### Likelihood Explanation
Fully reachable by a single unprivileged address: the attacker calls `controller.supply` (or `flash_position`/`multiply`) to post collateral and `controller.borrow` to bring utilization to `max_utilization`, which `mint_debt` permits since the check is `<=` (`contracts/pool/src/guards.rs:31`). From that ledger onward every victim withdraw reverts. The cost is the interest accrued on the attacker's own borrow plus the collateral at risk of liquidation; on markets with high configured `max_utilization` (e.g., close to RAY in `configs/mainnet/markets.json`), reaching the cap requires borrowing nearly all supplied value. No privilege, oracle manipulation, or leaked key is needed.

### Recommendation
Make the utilization gate proportional rather than absolute: allow a withdrawal when `borrowed_after / supplied_after <= max_utilization` OR when the withdrawal does not increase utilization beyond its pre-withdrawal level (i.e., skip the check when utilization is already at/above the cap before the call, since withdrawals can only make it worse by design). Alternatively, clamp withdrawals so that utilization is evaluated only when it improves, or exempt withdrawals up to the pro-rata share that keeps utilization constant. Interest accrual can also push a market past the cap with no attacker at all, so the fix benefits liveness independent of the griefing scenario.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker creates an account, supplies collateral in a second market, and calls `controller.borrow` on the target market for amount `B` such that `borrowed_ceil / supplied_floor == max_utilization` exactly (binary-search the amount; `mint_debt` only reverts when the post-state ratio exceeds the cap).
2. Victim calls `controller.withdraw` for any amount `w > 0` on their supply position in that market.
3. Inside `withdraw::accounting`, `burn_position` reduces `supplied` by the shares corresponding to `w`; `gate_and_debit` then evaluates `require_utilization_below_max`, where `borrowed_ceil` is unchanged but `supplied_floor` is smaller, so `borrowed/supplied > max_utilization` → panic with `UtilizationAboveMax` (127).
4. The victim's withdraw reverts for every `w > 0` until the attacker repays; the attacker keeps the position open and adds collateral as needed to avoid liquidation.
