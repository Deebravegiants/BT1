### Title
Unchecked RAY debt-value overflow permanently freezes a pool market - (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary
A large outstanding debt position can make `borrowed * borrow_index` exceed `i128` during interest accrual, causing every subsequent operation on that market to revert with `MathOverflow`. Because all mutating market operations synchronize interest before applying the requested action, the failure blocks repayment, withdrawal, liquidation, recapitalization, and explicit index updates. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` first computes `scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)` to determine utilization and later multiplies the same scaled debt by the updated index to calculate accrued interest. [3](#0-2)  `scaled_to_original` performs a checked RAY multiplication and reverts when the resulting token-scaled value exceeds `i128`. [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

Every pool leg loads the market through `synced_market` or `load_leg`, both of which invoke `interest::global_sync` before the operation-specific logic. [2](#0-1)  `global_sync` repeatedly calls `accrue_chunk`, which delegates to `accrue_step`, and only marks the market accrued after all chunks complete. [6](#0-5) 

Consequently, once `borrowed * borrow_index` becomes unrepresentable, the transaction aborts before `mark_accrued` or `commit` can persist progress. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7)  The borrow-index cap does not prevent this condition because `update_borrow_index` multiplies the old index by the interest factor before applying the cap, and the debt-value multiplication can overflow earlier. [9](#0-8) [10](#0-9) 

An unprivileged borrower can create the prerequisite state through `Controller::borrow` with `borrows=[(hub_asset, amount)]`, subject to having sufficient collateral and available pool liquidity. [11](#0-10) [12](#0-11)  The borrow path mints scaled debt and only validates reserves, the liquidation buffer, rounded shares, and configured utilization; it does not reserve enough `i128` headroom for the debt's future index-scaled value. [13](#0-12) 

### Impact Explanation
The affected market becomes permanently unable to process actions that first accrue interest, absent a privileged contract upgrade or manual state correction. [14](#0-13) 

This freezes suppliers' funds because `withdraw` calls `load_leg` before resolving or paying the position. [15](#0-14)  It likewise freezes borrower repayment and liquidation-driven debt reduction because `repay` accrues before burning debt, while liquidation withdrawals and debt repayment traverse the same synced market operations. [16](#0-15) 

Even the permissionless `update_indexes` remediation path cannot help because it calls the pool's `update_indexes`, which performs the same accrual. [17](#0-16) [18](#0-17)  `recapitalize` also loads a synced market before crediting cash, so adding liquidity cannot bypass the overflow. [19](#0-18) 

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires an unusually large debt book and enough elapsed interest for the product of scaled debt and borrow index to exceed `i128`. [3](#0-2) 

A single account must first obtain sufficient collateral and pool liquidity for a very large borrow, and configured borrow caps or utilization limits may prevent it in a particular deployment. [20](#0-19) [13](#0-12)  However, the code has no protocol-level invariant ensuring that admitted scaled debt remains representable after future accrual, and the failure affects all users of the market rather than only the position that created it. [21](#0-20) [2](#0-1) 

### Recommendation
Bound admitted scaled debt by the remaining representable debt-value capacity at the configured `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, rather than only by the current index and token-unit cap. [9](#0-8) [22](#0-21) 

In `mint_debt`, calculate a worst-case value bound such as `(position + minted) * MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` using an overflow-safe checked path and reject borrows that can cross the representable range before the index ceiling. [13](#0-12) 

For defense in depth, make accrual clamp `new_borrow_index` before calculating total debt when the multiplication result would exceed the representable domain, or carry a bounded index-only intermediate instead of calculating `new_total_debt` directly. [23](#0-22)  Any saturation should explicitly cap interest at the representable boundary and emit state indicating that accrual stopped growing, while preserving repayment and withdrawal paths. [24](#0-23) 

### Proof of Concept
1. As an unprivileged account owner, call `Controller::supply` with `account_id=0`, the target `spoke_id`, and collateral sufficient to satisfy post-borrow risk gates. [25](#0-24) 
2. In an already funded debt market, call `Controller::borrow(caller, account_id, [(hub_asset, amount)], None)` where `amount` is large enough that `scaled_debt * borrow_index` will eventually exceed `i128`. [11](#0-10) 
3. Wait for index growth, or periodically call `Controller::update_indexes(caller, [hub_asset])`. [17](#0-16) 
4. Once `borrowed * borrow_index` exceeds `i128`, `accrue_step` reverts while calculating `borrowed_original` or `new_total_debt`. [3](#0-2) [26](#0-25) 
5. Subsequent `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `recapitalize`, and `update_indexes` calls that touch the market all reach `synced_market`/`load_leg`, re-enter `global_sync`, and revert before their operation-specific logic executes. [2](#0-1) [6](#0-5) 
6. Because the accrual timestamp is advanced only after the full loop completes, every retry encounters the same overflowing calculation, leaving all supplier cash and outstanding debt operations frozen for that market. [7](#0-6)

    borrowed: Ray,
