### Title

Pull-based flash-loan repayment drains approved Wasm receivers - ([File: contracts/pool/src/ops/flash.rs]) [1](#0-0) 

### Summary

An unprivileged caller can select an arbitrary Wasm contract as `receiver` on `Controller::flash_loan`; the pool then pulls `amount + fee` from that receiver through its existing token allowance rather than requiring the receiver to push repayment. [2](#0-1) [1](#0-0) 

### Finding Description

`Controller::flash_loan` authorizes only `caller` and accepts caller-selected `receiver` and `data` arguments. [3](#0-2) 

The only receiver validation is that its address is a Wasm contract; there is no consent, authorization, or registration check proving that the receiver agreed to this loan. [4](#0-3) 

The pool transfers `amount` to the receiver, invokes `execute_flash_loan`, checks that the pool balance is unchanged, verifies `receiver -> pool` allowance for `amount + fee`, and pulls that repayment with `transfer_from`. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) [1](#0-0) 

Consequently, a compatible victim contract that already approved the pool and returns normally from `execute_flash_loan` receives `amount` but immediately loses `amount + fee`, for a net loss of `fee`. [5](#0-4) [1](#0-0) 

### Impact Explanation

Each successful attacker-initiated call transfers value equal to the flash-loan fee from the victim contract to the pool. [7](#0-6) [1](#0-0) 

The attacker can repeat the call while the victim has sufficient balance and remaining allowance, progressively draining the victim's token balance while the protocol books each fee as revenue. [8](#0-7) [1](#0-0) 

### Likelihood Explanation

The attack requires a deployed Wasm victim exporting the `execute_flash_loan` callback, a flashloanable market with sufficient reserves, a positive fee for the selected amount, and victim-to-pool allowance of at least `amount + fee` or a victim callback that creates that allowance. [9](#0-8) [7](#0-6) [6](#0-5) [1](#0-0) 

No victim signature, receiver authorization, attacker deposit, privileged role, or leaked key is needed; the attacker authorizes only the top-level controller call. [10](#0-9) 

### Recommendation

Do not settle flash loans by pulling from the receiver's token allowance. [1](#0-0) 

Require the receiver to explicitly transfer `amount + fee` to the pool during `execute_flash_loan`, replace the post-callback balance check with a final check for `initial_balance + fee`, and remove the allowance check and `transfer_from`. [5](#0-4) [1](#0-0) 

### Proof of Concept

1. Let victim contract `V` export `execute_flash_loan(initiator, asset, amount, fee, pool, data)`, hold at least `F`, and maintain token allowance `V -> pool >= P + F`. [6](#0-5) [1](#0-0) 
2. Attacker `A` calls `Controller::flash_loan(caller=A, asset=hub_asset, amount=P, receiver=V, data=...)`, choosing `P` such that the configured fee `F` is positive and `V`'s callback returns normally. [11](#0-10) [7](#0-6) 
3. The pool transfers `P` to `V`, invokes `V.execute_flash_loan`, verifies its own balance remains `B - P`, consumes `V`'s allowance, and pulls `P + F` back. [5](#0-4) [1](#0-0) 
4. `V`'s balance decreases by `F` even though `A` only authorized the controller call and did not control `V`. [10](#0-9) [1](#0-0) 
5. Repeat with amounts satisfying `allowance(V, pool) >= P + F` until `V`'s spendable balance or remaining allowance can no longer cover a positive fee. [7](#0-6) [1](#0-0)
