### Title
Sustained high-utilization debt overflow panics inside accrual and permanently freezes a market — (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs`)

### Summary
CVE-2015-2689 is a remote denial of service: under load, Tor hits an assertion failure and the daemon exits. The analog in XOXNO Lending is a user-reachable arithmetic panic in the pool's interest accrual path. Because every pool verb accrues first (`Cache::load` → `interest::global_sync` → mutate → guards → commit, per `contracts/pool/README.md:157-176`), once a market's borrow index grows past the point where `scaled_to_original` overflows `i128`, every subsequent call on that market panics with `MathOverflow` (33) — no repay, withdraw, liquidation, or cleanup can ever run again on that book. The `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap intended to bound index growth never engages because the RAY-scaled debt *value* overflows before the index itself hits the cap.

### Finding Description
`calculate_supplier_rewards` and the accrual path use `Ray::checked_add`/`checked_sub`/`mul`, which panic on overflow (`common/src/math/fp.rs:150-158`, `common/src/rates/index.rs:73-88`). An unprivileged borrower can drive utilization to the steep segment of the interest rate curve (e.g., 98% on the XLM curve) simply by calling `borrow`; sustained high utilization then grows `borrow_index` until `borrowed.mul(env, borrow_index)` overflows. The harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` demonstrates this end-to-end: after some years at 98% utilization, `update_indexes` fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and afterward `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` also fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW` because they accrue first (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-361`).

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds for every supplier and borrower in the affected `(hub, asset)` market: once the overflow state is reached, no exit or repayment transaction can ever succeed, since accrual runs before any guard or mutation. Liquidation and `clean_bad_debt` on that market are also bricked, so the bad debt cannot be socialized either — compounding into protocol insolvency. The trigger is reachable by any unprivileged address that can push and hold utilization near the curve's steep segment (a whale borrowing ~98% of a large market), matching the CVE's "attacker-induced load → assertion → dead process" shape.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a large borrow held at high utilization for an extended accrual period (the test bounds it under ~40 years even at extreme size, and on steeper curve segments far sooner), plus an 18-decimal or large-denomination asset. It is a deliberate design-tension overflow rather than a one-transaction exploit, but it is deterministic, needs no privilege, and is unrecoverable once crossed — there is no index-rollback or accrual-skip escape hatch.

### Recommendation
Apply the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` clamp *before* computing scaled values in `global_sync`, so the index cap engages before `borrowed.mul(borrow_index)` can overflow; alternatively, saturate the accrued index at the largest value for which `supplied/borrowed` multiplication stays within `i128` rather than letting `checked_*` panic. Add a regression test asserting every verb remains callable once `borrow_index` reaches the cap.

### Proof of Concept
`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361` — supply `BIG18` (18 decimals, XLM curve), borrow ~98% of a billion-unit principal, advance ledger time in yearly chunks until `try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"])`, `try_withdraw_raw`, and `try_repay` all revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
