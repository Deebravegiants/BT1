### Title
Bad-debt write-down clamps supply index to a nonzero floor, leaving wiped suppliers a phantom claim that drains fresh deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
The kernel bug class is an incorrect "is anything assigned/left" condition. The analog in XOXNO Lending is `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89`. When socialized bad debt meets or exceeds total supplied value, the function clamps the supply index up to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead of driving supplier claims to zero. Every supply share that survives the wipeout still unscales to a positive claim at that floored index, so the pool continues to owe real tokens to accounts whose claims should have been extinguished. The next honest deposit minted at the floor index becomes withdrawable by those stranded claims.

### Finding Description
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes `remaining = total_supplied_value - min(bad_debt, total_supplied_value)` and scales the index by `remaining / total_supplied_value`. A full wipeout yields a zero `reduction_factor`, but the result is floored: `cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)))` (line 88). After this clamp, `unscale_supply_floor(share)` returns a positive amount for every share still on the books — including revenue shares and any supplier positions the seize path did not absorb (`seize.rs` only touches `entry.position`, not the whole `supplied` total). The production path is reachable from `PoolSeizeEntry` with `side == Borrow`, which calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` then `burn_debt` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:24-28`), i.e., via the controller's `clean_bad_debt`/liquidation flow. The repo's own tests confirm the exploitation mechanics end-to-end at the raw cache layer: `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` and `test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` in `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:317-495` show a wiped residual paying out exactly a fresh depositor's cash and leaving the honest supplier unbacked. The defect is the condition that decides "suppliers still have value": clamping the index to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` asserts a nonzero residual claim when `remaining` is zero.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds. After a wipeout, stranded supply shares (surviving suppliers, or revenue shares created by `absorb_supply_as_revenue`/`accrue_revenue`) retain a positive floor claim while `cash` is zero — `require_reserves` merely masks this until liquidity returns. Any later `supply` minted at the floor index can be withdrawn in full by a stranded-share holder, and the fresh depositor's claim becomes unbacked. This is a direct, permanent loss for post-wipeout suppliers, reachable by unprivileged liquidation/`clean_bad_debt` calls that push bad debt over the supply total.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires bad debt at or above total supplied value — i.e., a position liquidated/cleaned after its collateral can no longer cover the debt, which is exactly the scenario `clean_bad_debt` exists for. An unprivileged caller can trigger the socialize-and-burn path; a single dust-priced or crashed spoke suffices. Once the clamp fires, any subsequent deposit into the market is immediately exposed. No privileged access, oracle manipulation beyond reaching genuine bad debt, or timing constraints are needed.

### Recommendation
In `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, when `capped == total_supplied_value` the market is fully wiped: burn the residual `supplied` shares (transfer them to revenue or zero them) rather than flooring the index, or track a "wiped" flag that forces `unscale_supply_*` to zero until shares are reconciled. Equivalently, ensure the seize path absorbs or burns all outstanding supply shares whenever the write-down saturates, so no share can unscale to a positive claim at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. Add a regression test asserting `supplied` shares have zero unscaled value after a saturating write-down.

### Proof of Concept
1. Account A supplies into spoke market M; account B borrows against other collateral.
2. Collateral value collapses (or B's collateral is exhausted by prior liquidation legs) so B's remaining debt exceeds M's total supplied value.
3. Any unprivileged address calls `clean_bad_debt` (or a liquidation that seizes B's residual borrow position). Pool `seize.rs` invokes `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, which clamps `supply_index` to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`; A's shares — never individually burned — still unscale to a positive amount.
4. Honest user C calls `supply` into M; `calculate_scaled_supply` mints C shares at the floor index and `credit_cash` records C's tokens.
5. A calls `withdraw`: `resolve_withdrawal` pays `gross == stranded claim > 0`, `require_reserves` passes because cash now exists, and A extracts exactly C's deposit, leaving `cash < C's claim`.
This sequence mirrors the assertions in `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:373-427`.
