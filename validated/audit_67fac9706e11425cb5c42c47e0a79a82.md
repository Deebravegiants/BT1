### Title
Withdrawal reverts when protocol revenue shares approach total supply, locking supplier funds to back revenue - ([File: contracts/pool/src/cache/shares.rs])

### Summary
In `contracts/pool`, protocol revenue is not a separate balance — it is a subset of the same scaled supply-share total (`revenue <= supplied` enforced by `require_revenue_backed` in `contracts/pool/src/cache/shares.rs:78-84`). Every withdrawal leg calls `burn_supply`, which subtracts the burned shares from `supplied` and then asserts `revenue <= supplied` (`contracts/pool/src/cache/shares.rs:20-23`). As protocol revenue shares grow relative to total supply — via reserve-factor interest accrual (`accrue_revenue`, `contracts/pool/src/cache/shares.rs:36-39`), flash-loan fees, liquidation fees (`withhold_liquidation_fee` → `interest::add_protocol_revenue`, `contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:141-145`), and seized deposits (`absorb_supply_as_revenue`, `contracts/pool/src/cache/shares.rs:45-48`) — an ordinary supplier's `withdraw` increasingly reverts with `GenericError::InternalError`, because burning their shares would leave `revenue > supplied`. The user is forced to leave shares behind whose floor value backs the protocol's revenue claim. This is the XOXNO analog of the Wagmi bug: accrued fee/revenue value competes with the user's own share balance at withdrawal time and the withdraw path underflows/reverts instead of paying out.

### Finding Description
`ops::withdraw::accounting` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:57-90`) resolves the request, burns position shares via `burn_position` → `Cache::burn_supply`, then commits. `burn_supply` does `supplied -= scaled` and immediately calls `require_revenue_backed`. There is no carve-out: a user's own deposit shares double as backing for the protocol's revenue shares. The codebase itself proves the revert path: `test_withdraw_rejects_burn_that_strands_revenue_above_supply` (`contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:1501-1516`) sets `revenue = 60% of supplied` and shows `try_withdraw` reverting with `InternalError`, and the same holds for the liquidation withdraw leg (`flows.rs:1518-1533`).

Revenue shares are only removed by `burn_claimable_revenue` (`contracts/pool/src/cache/shares.rs:54-75`), which is capped by `min(cash, floor(revenue_value))`. When the market is highly utilized, `cash` is small, so `claim_revenue` cannot shrink `revenue` even though it keeps accruing on outstanding debt interest. Meanwhile suppliers racing to exit hit the guard: each withdrawal makes `supplied − revenue` smaller for the next withdrawer, and the last suppliers find that a full close (`amount = 0` → `i128::MAX` sentinel per `skills/xoxno-lending/math.md:58`) always reverts. They can only make a partial withdrawal that leaves enough of *their own* shares in the market to satisfy `revenue <= supplied`, i.e., part of their principal is conscripted as revenue backing.

### Impact Explanation
Temporary-to-permanent freezing of user funds, plus theft-adjacent value transfer: suppliers cannot withdraw their full balance while revenue shares occupy a large fraction of `supplied`, and the locked portion is sized by the protocol's revenue claim, not the user's deposit. In a fully-lent-out market, `claim_revenue` pays nothing (`cash = 0` → `actual_amount = 0`, documented at `contracts/pool/README.md:337-346`), so the freeze persists until borrowers repay or revenue is otherwise burned. A withdrawal run is self-reinforcing: early withdrawers succeed, later ones are progressively more locked. This maps directly to the source report's impact class — yield/revenue growth renders withdrawal of principal impossible through the normal path.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by any unprivileged supplier calling controller `withdraw`. The precondition — revenue shares being a large fraction of `supplied` — accrues organically in any market with a nonzero `reserve_factor` and borrowing activity, and is accelerated by liquidation fees and deposit seizures (`absorb_supply_as_revenue`). Because `burn_claimable_revenue` is cash-capped, high utilization (the regime where suppliers most want to exit) is exactly the regime where `revenue` cannot be drained. It does not require `revenue ≈ supplied`; each user is locked once their burn would strand revenue above the remaining total, so the effective threshold for the marginal withdrawer arrives much earlier.

### Recommendation
Do not make user withdrawal burns contingent on `revenue <= supplied`. Track protocol revenue backing separately (e.g., book revenue's asset value as a claim on cash/debt rather than as shares inside `supplied`), or exempt the revenue-backing check for user-initiated burns by treating the last `revenue` shares of `supplied` as non-withdrawable protocol-owned shares that cannot be burned by `burn_supply` at all — i.e., enforce `supplied - burned >= revenue` only by capping which shares count as user-withdrawable, not by reverting. Alternatively, synchronously burn an equivalent amount of revenue shares during withdrawal and pay them later from repayments, so a supplier's full close never reverts.

### Proof of Concept
1. Market with `reserve_factor > 0`; supplier S deposits `X` tokens via controller `supply`.
2. Borrowers take most of the cash; time passes so interest accrues and `accrue_revenue` mints revenue shares until `revenue / supplied` is large (say ≥ 40%), while `cash` is small so `claim_revenue` cannot shrink `revenue`.
3. Other suppliers withdraw; each successful burn lowers `supplied` toward `revenue`.
4. S calls `withdraw` with amount `0` (full close). `burn_supply` subtracts S's shares, `require_revenue_backed` sees `revenue > supplied`, and the call reverts with `GenericError::InternalError` — the exact behavior pinned by `contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:1501-1516`.
5. S can only recover `floor((supplied - revenue) share of their position)` via partial withdrawal; the remainder of S's deposit stays locked backing protocol revenue until repaid cash lets `claim_revenue` burn revenue shares. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3)

    pub(crate) fn burn_claimable_revenue(&mut self) -> i128 {
        let treasury_actual = self.unscale_supply_floor(self.revenue);
        let amount = self.cash.min(treasury_actual);
        if amount <= 0 {
            return 0;
        }
        let scaled_to_burn = if amount >= treasury_actual {
            self.revenue
        } else {
            self.revenue
                .mul_ratio_ceil(&self.env, amount, treasury_actual)
        };

        assert_with_error!(
            self.env,
            scaled_to_burn != Ray::ZERO,
            GenericError::InternalError
        );
        self.revenue = self.revenue.checked_sub(&self.env, scaled_to_burn);
        self.supplied = self.supplied.checked_sub(&self.env, scaled_to_burn);
        amount
    }
