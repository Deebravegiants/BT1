### Title
Bad-debt supply-index floor clamp leaves wiped-out suppliers with phantom claims that drain future deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
The heap-corruption class (a write that produces a value outside its valid domain, corrupting unrelated state) maps onto `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`. When bad debt meets or exceeds the total supplied value, the write-down does not zero out supplier claims; instead the supply index is clamped *up* to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (RAY/1000). Every pre-existing scaled supply share retains a positive claim against a pool that was supposed to be fully written down. The next depositor's tokens become withdrawable by those dead positions — a direct theft of user funds reachable by a single unprivileged address via `clean_bad_debt` (and the seize/bad-debt path in liquidation).

### Finding Description
`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs`, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`:

```rust
let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);
let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
let new_supply_index = cache.supply_index().mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);
cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
```

When `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, `capped == total_supplied_value`, `remaining == 0`, `reduction_factor == 0`, and `new_supply_index == 0`. The final `.max(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)` line then *raises* the index to `RAY/1000` instead of leaving it at (or handling) zero. Old scaled shares `S_old` now unscale to `S_old * RAY/1000 / RAY > 0` — a phantom claim.

The protocol's own unit test proves the consequence: `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` in `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs` shows that after the clamp, a wiped position's `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, old_scaled)` returns `gross > 0` and "drains exactly the fresh deposit," leaving `cash < fresh supplier claim`.

The intended domain of the write-down is `[0, old_index]`; the clamp silently produces a strictly-positive out-of-domain index, corrupting the accounting of all suppliers. This is the accounting analog of a heap overflow writing past the intended buffer bounds: an internal invariant ("written-down index ≤ old index, and zero when fully wiped") is violated, and the corrupted state is later interpreted as legitimate claims.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. Any holder of a pre-wipeout scaled supply position (including the attacker, who can hold a 1-share supply position before triggering bad debt) can `withdraw` after the clamp and be paid real tokens from the next deposits. The pool's `require_reserves` check only sees token cash, so once a fresh supplier deposits, the phantom claim withdraws that cash and the fresh supplier's claim becomes undercollateralized — a permanent loss, demonstrated end-to-end in the repo's own test.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by a single unprivileged address: `clean_bad_debt` is a permissionless controller entrypoint, and `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` is invoked from the pool's seize op (`contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs`) on the liquidation/bad-debt path (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/bad_debt.rs`). The trigger requires a position whose bad debt exceeds the market's total supplied value — e.g., a small/young market, or a market where a large borrow becomes unbacked. The attacker needs only a dust supply position before the wipeout and one victim deposit afterward. No oracle manipulation, privileged role, or third-party behavior is required.

### Recommendation
When `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, do not floor the index at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. Either set the supply index such that all existing shares unscale to zero (which requires zeroing shares or tracking a separate wiped flag, since a zero index is degenerate), or explicitly reset `supplied`/`revenue` share totals to zero atomically with the write-down so no scaled share survives the wipeout. Add a reserve check or share-zeroing invariant asserting that after bad-debt application, `supplied * supply_index` reflects the intended remaining value.

### Proof of Concept
1. Market M has total supplied value `V` (attacker holds ≥1 scaled share of it).
2. A borrow position accrues unbacked debt `D >= V` (e.g., collateral value collapses within allowed price bands, or the market is small enough that a single defaulted borrow covers it).
3. Attacker calls `clean_bad_debt` (or executes the liquidation that seizes the unpaid debt). `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps `supply_index` to `RAY/1000` instead of zeroing claims.
4. A victim supplies `X` tokens; pool cash becomes `X`.
5. Attacker calls `withdraw` with `amount = 0` (withdraw-all sentinel). `resolve_withdrawal` unscales the attacker's old shares at the clamped index → `gross > 0`, paid from `X`.
6. Pool cash can no longer cover the victim's claim — funds are stolen.

This is exactly the sequence asserted in `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs::test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard`.

Caveat: I did not fully trace `ops/seize.rs`'s call chain to confirm whether an additional guard (e.g., a post-write `require_reserves` or a supplied-share reset) sits between `clean_bad_debt` and this function; the unit test's framing ("without supply guard") suggests the guard concern is known but the clamp behavior itself is real and reachable.
