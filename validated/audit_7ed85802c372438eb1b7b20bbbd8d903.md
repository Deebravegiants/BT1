### Title
Debt-value overflow during interest accrual permanently freezes a whale-sized market, blocking repay/withdraw/liquidate - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
Every state-changing pool operation runs `interest::global_sync` first, which calls `accrue_step` → `calculate_supplier_rewards`. There, `borrowed.mul(env, new_borrow_index)` and `borrowed.mul(env, old_borrow_index)` compute total debt as a product of two RAY-scaled values (`common/src/rates/index.rs:80-81`). When `borrowed * borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX` — reachable on a high-decimal market at sustained high utilization — `Ray::mul` panics with `MathOverflow` (33) *before* the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap in `update_borrow_index` can engage. Because accrual precedes every verb, the market is permanently bricked: `repay`, `withdraw`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and even `update_indexes` all revert. Like the Squid CVE, an attacker induces a denial of service through an unprivileged code path; here the "header" is ordinary supply/borrow activity that pushes the accounting invariant past the `i128` ceiling.

### Finding Description
The index cap `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` in `update_borrow_index` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19`) clamps the *index*, but not the *debt value* `borrowed_scaled_ray * borrow_index`. `calculate_supplier_rewards` evaluates `borrowed.mul(env, new_borrow_index)` unconditionally (`common/src/rates/index.rs:80-81`), so once the scaled-debt-times-index product overflows `i128` — the RAY-value ceiling — the call panics inside the widened `I256` mul-div conversion (`common/src/math/fp_core.rs:108-118`). The same product is also computed in `scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)` at `common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60` earlier in `accrue_step`. Neither path saturates or degrades; there is no fallback. The protocol's own harness test confirms it: `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-361`) shows `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay` all failing with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` — i.e., the cap never engages. The flaw is that the cap is applied to the index in isolation while the fatal overflow is in `shares × index` value terms; no mechanism bounds `borrowed` shares to `i128::MAX / borrow_index`.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds. Once the overflow condition is met, every entrypoint that touches the market — `controller.supply/borrow/withdraw/repay/liquidate/clean_bad_debt` (which all load the pool `Cache` and call `global_sync` per `contracts/pool/README.md` flow), plus `pool.update_indexes` — panics inside accrual. Suppliers can never withdraw, borrowers can never repay, and liquidators cannot clean positions; all tokens in that market's book are locked forever.

### Likelihood Explanation
An unprivileged attacker needs a large, high-decimal market at sustained high utilization (e.g., ~10²⁷ units × ~170× index on an 18-decimal asset at 98% utilization, per the harness test). Caps must be raised via governance, which limits feasibility, but on any market where caps are lifted for a whale asset the cliff is reachable purely through `supply`/`borrow` by ordinary users — no privileged call, oracle manipulation, or third-party cooperation needed. Once crossed, the state is irreversible since accrual always runs before any corrective action.

### Recommendation
Bound the market so the value ceiling is hit never, or degrade gracefully:
- Enforce `borrowed_scaled_ray ≤ i128::MAX / MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` as an invariant on `borrow`/`create_strategy` (a share cap, not just a value cap), since `borrow_index` is bounded.
- Alternatively, in `calculate_supplier_rewards`/`scaled_to_original`, use saturating math for the debt product so accrual completes and the index cap engages, turning the cliff into a (bounded) fee loss rather than a brick.
- Keep `update_borrow_index`'s cap but compute rewards against the capped index only, skipping the uncapped multiplication.

### Proof of Concept
See `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361`: on an 18-decimal market with lifted caps, supply `BILLION * 10^18`, borrow ~98% of it, advance time in yearly steps; `try_update_indexes_for` eventually returns `MATH_OVERFLOW` (33) while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and subsequent `try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1)` and `try_repay(...)` fail with the same error — demonstrating the permanent freeze via unprivileged calls.
