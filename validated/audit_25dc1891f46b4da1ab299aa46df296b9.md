### Title
Unprivileged `recapitalize`/`repay` refunds pay out of real custody without debiting the `cash` book, permanently overstating reserves and bricking withdrawals - (File: contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs)

### Summary
The H-4 bug class is: an unprivileged path mutates real state (modules on the Safe) while bypassing the bookkeeping counter (`enabledModuleCount`), so the stored count and reality diverge and every subsequent guard check reverts — a permanent brick. XOXNO Lending has the same shape: the pool keeps a `cash` book that is the sole record `Cache::require_reserves` consults, but two pool legs reachable via unprivileged controller entrypoints (`recapitalize`, `repay`) refund tokens out of real custody from a *declared* amount without debiting `cash` or calling `require_reserves`. After one such call, the book permanently overstates custody, every later withdrawal that exceeds real balance passes the pool's own liquidity/solvency guards and then reverts inside the SAC transfer — the market is bricked exactly like the HSG safe, and the refunded tokens are stolen besides.

### Finding Description
The pool's `cash` field is the bookkeeping counter analogous to `enabledModuleCount`. `ops::recapitalize::apply` refunds "the excess over the shortfall" and `ops::repay::apply` refunds "the excess over the debt", where both excesses are derived from the caller-declared inbound amount, not the cash book; "Neither refund debits `cash` or passes `require_reserves`" [1](#0-0) . A repay against a market with no debt makes the entire declared amount an overpayment: `net_repay` is zero, the `RepayRoundsToZeroShares` assert passes on its `net_repay == 0` disjunct, and the full declared amount is refunded out of real custody [2](#0-1) . The dedicated recapitalize test drains every token of pool custody via an unfunded refund while `cash` is untouched: "the pool's entire custody has left the contract" / "the cash book is untouched, so the pool still reports the paid-out funds as present" [3](#0-2) . This is the same desync as H-4: an alternate unprivileged path changes real balances while skipping the accounting write that every other path relies on. `Context::cached_pool_sync_data` shows the controller reads the pool's book (`PoolSyncData`) for all downstream risk checks [4](#0-3) , so the corrupted `cash` value propagates everywhere.

### Impact Explanation
Two impacts, both in the accepted set:

1. **Theft of user funds**: the refund transfers real pool custody to an arbitrary `payer`/`receiver` address chosen by the unprivileged caller — the test drains `token.balance(&t.pool)` to 0 into `payer` [5](#0-4) .
2. **Permanent freezing of remaining funds**: `Cache::require_reserves` reads the overstated book, so a supplier's exit "clears the pool's own liquidity guard ... and then fails inside the token transfer" — the SAC reports `BalanceError = 10`, not `InsufficientLiquidity` or `PoolInsolvent` [6](#0-5) . No unprivileged path rebuilds `cash` downward, so withdrawals against that market revert until someone donates tokens — the same "safe permanently bricked" outcome as H-4. A follow-up test confirms the claim is only bounded by live custody, i.e. the payout is capped at the pool's entire real balance [7](#0-6) .

### Likelihood Explanation
Fully reachable by a single unprivileged address with no timing, price, or governance precondition: `recapitalize` is a permissionless controller entrypoint, and `repay` accepts any `payer`. The attacker only needs to declare an amount exceeding their actual transfer (or repay a zero-debt market) on any initialized market holding supplier funds. The harness already demonstrates the drain succeeding against a market with a legitimate deposit [8](#0-7) . No victim interaction is required; the payer can be a fresh `Address::generate`.

### Recommendation
Derive both refunds from **measured** receipt rather than the declared amount, and route the refund through the same cash-book accounting as a normal payout (debit `cash` / pass `require_reserves`), so an unfunded declared amount refunds exactly `received - consumed` — zero when nothing arrived. As defense in depth, make `recapitalize` pull tokens via `transfer_amount_measured` before computing the shortfall (the controller already measures inbound transfers for this endpoint [9](#0-8) ), and revert the whole call when `received < declared` rather than refunding the difference out of custody.

### Proof of Concept
Sequence on any initialized market (e.g. the harness's standard pool holding `deposit` of supplier funds):

1. Attacker generates `payer` with zero token balance.
2. Attacker calls `recapitalize(hub_asset, payer, custody)` where `custody = token.balance(pool)` — or equivalently `repay(payer, ract(0, custody))` on a debt-free market.
3. Nothing transfers in; the entire declared amount is treated as refundable excess and the pool sends `custody` to `payer`. `cash` is unchanged [10](#0-9) .
4. Any honest supplier calls `withdraw(i128::MAX)`. `require_reserves` reads the stale `cash` book and admits the exit; the SAC `transfer` reverts with `BalanceError = 10`. The book still overstates custody after the rollback, so every subsequent withdrawal fails identically — the market is permanently bricked [11](#0-10) .

Working executable PoCs already exist in-tree: `test_unfunded_recapitalize_leaves_the_cash_book_overstating_custody` [12](#0-11)  and `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` [13](#0-12) .

*Uncertainty note:* the in-tree tests exercise the pool entrypoints directly; whether the controller's `recapitalize`/`repay` wrappers independently validate `received == declared` before delegating could not be fully verified in this pass — the fail-closed re-entry test shows the measured window exists for `recapitalize`, but the pool-side tests assert the refund bookkeeping gap itself is real.
