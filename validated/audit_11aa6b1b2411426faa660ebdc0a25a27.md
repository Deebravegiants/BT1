### Title
Stale dual-source oracle leg inflates collateral through midpoint blending - (`contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs`)

### Summary
The price aggregator accepts an old anchor observation as long as it remains inside that leg’s configured staleness bound, then returns the midpoint of the stale anchor and fresh primary price when their prices remain inside tolerance. [1](#0-0)  An attacker can therefore supply a dual-oracle collateral asset after one leg lags, borrow against the inflated blended valuation through `borrow`, and leave the protocol undercollateralized. [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
`read_source` marks a leg stale only when its age exceeds `oracle.max_price_stale_seconds`; a lagging observation below that bound remains usable. [3](#0-2)  Oracle validation permits every source-specific staleness bound up to the asset-level bound, so this can be a correctly validated configuration rather than a malformed oracle. [4](#0-3) 

For two readings, `blend` marks the result stale only if either leg is individually stale or two `Market`-nature legs are separated by more than `MAX_LEG_AGE_SPREAD_SECONDS`; otherwise it emits `midpoint_price_or_zero(anchor, primary)` as the final price. [1](#0-0)  Consequently, a delayed non-market anchor carrying an obsolete higher price can remain inside both its age bound and the configured price tolerance while still moving the accepted midpoint materially away from the fresh market price. [5](#0-4) 

The controller exposes `supply` and `borrow`, with `borrow` drawing funds against the account’s collateral and enforcing solvency using the prices loaded for that invocation. [6](#0-5)  The invocation cache retains fetched prices during the operation, so every collateral and debt valuation in the borrow check consistently uses the same inflated blended feed. [7](#0-6) 

### Impact Explanation
The existing audit fixture demonstrates the exploit economically: a Reflector primary reports the fresh `0.91` price while a RedStone anchor remains at `1.00` for `82_800` seconds under an `86_400`-second bound, and the resulting collateral valuation is more than 4% above the fresh-anchor valuation. [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8)  The attacker’s `70_000` USDC borrow succeeds under the lagging-anchor valuation, while the same borrow against a fresh anchor fails with `INSUFFICIENT_COLLATERAL`. [10](#0-9) 

This permits an unprivileged borrower to withdraw more debt assets than the fresh collateral value supports, causing theft of supplier funds or protocol insolvency if the borrowed assets are not recovered. [11](#0-10)  The issue is not limited to XLM or USDC; it applies to any collateral oracle that blends a fresh leg with a lagging leg whose stale value remains inside tolerance and its configured age bound. [12](#0-11) 

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation requires a delayed provider observation, but no privileged action, leaked key, malformed configuration, or direct control of the oracle is needed. [4](#0-3)  The vulnerable window exists whenever one configured leg lags enough to hold an economically meaningful obsolete price while staying below its staleness threshold and inside the deviation tolerance. [13](#0-12)  Once that window exists, the attacker only needs to call ordinary `supply` and `borrow` entrypoints on an account they control. [6](#0-5) 

### Recommendation
Reject a blended price when the timestamps of the two legs exceed a strict age spread for all dual-source configurations, not only when both legs have `FeedNature::Market`. [14](#0-13)  Prefer the fresher leg’s timestamp or require both legs to be current within a common freshness budget before midpoint blending. [15](#0-14)  Additionally, do not blend legs whose prices are near opposite ends of the tolerance band merely because neither leg is individually stale; treat an age-qualified leg as unavailable and fail the dual-source resolution rather than returning a partial or averaged price. [16](#0-15) 

### Proof of Concept
1. Configure collateral token `XLM` with a fresh Reflector primary and a RedStone anchor whose `max_stale_seconds` is `86_400`, with a 10% tolerance. [17](#0-16) 
2. Publish the fresh primary price as `0.91` while leaving the anchor at the prior `1.00` price with an age of `82_800` seconds. [18](#0-17) 
3. Supply `100_000` XLM to an attacker-controlled account. [19](#0-18) 
4. Call `borrow` for `70_000` USDC. [20](#0-19) 
5. The borrow succeeds because the midpoint is approximately `0.955`, while the identical borrow fails with `INSUFFICIENT_COLLATERAL` when the anchor is fresh at `0.91`. [21](#0-20)

    pub(crate) fn fetch_prices(&mut self, assets: &Vec<Address>) {
