Audit Report

## Title
Fee-refund conversion in `SwapAssetAdapter::correct_and_deposit_fee` uses a post-dispatch spot-price quote, allowing a manipulated pool price to alter the net asset fee charged - ([File: substrate/frame/transaction-payment/asset-conversion-tx-payment/src/payment.rs])

## Summary
`ChargeAssetTxPayment::withdraw_fee` locks in an asset amount at the pool's pre-dispatch spot price [1](#0-0) , while `correct_and_deposit_fee` re-quotes and swaps the refund back at the pool's post-dispatch spot price [2](#0-1) . Since the dispatched call executes between these two points and can itself swap on the same pool, the refund conversion can occur at a shifted price, changing the net `fee_asset_amount` relative to a "fair" single-price computation.

## Finding Description
The code path is accurately described: `withdraw_fee` quotes `asset_fee` via `S::quote_price_tokens_for_exact_tokens` against current reserves and swaps exactly that amount for `fee` native tokens [3](#0-2) . Later, `correct_and_deposit_fee` computes `refund_amount = fee_paid.peek() - corrected_fee` [4](#0-3)  and quotes/swaps that native refund back into `asset_id` at whatever price the pool has at that moment [5](#0-4) . `pallet_asset_conversion`'s `QuotePrice` trait explicitly documents that "the quoted price is only guaranteed if no other swaps are made after the price is quoted and before the target swap" [6](#0-5) , confirming the underlying spot-price mechanism is manipulable between two temporally separated quotes.

However, the magnitude of exploitable value here is bounded tightly by `refund_amount`, which is only the *weight-based correction* between the estimated fee (used at `prepare`) and the `actual_fee` computed from `PostDispatchInfo` after weight refunds [7](#0-6) . This refund is typically a small fraction of the total fee (the difference between declared and actually-consumed weight), not the full fee amount as the claim's PoC framing implies. The attacker's own dispatched call does not control the withdraw-time `asset_fee` (already fixed and consumed pre-dispatch, non-recoverable via price manipulation) — only the smaller refund leg is exposed to a shifted spot price. This is a real but narrow and low-magnitude price-consistency gap between two swap legs of the same fee cycle, not a mechanism to "under-pay the effective transaction fee" wholesale as characterized in the report; the bulk of the fee (`asset_fee` minus the small refund) is fixed at pre-dispatch price and cannot be reduced by the attacker.

## Impact Explanation
The realistic impact is a small discrepancy in the *refunded* portion of the fee (bounded by the weight-refund delta), extracted from AMM reserves/LPs via price movement the user itself causes within the same transaction. This is consistent with, and essentially a restatement of, the known, documented AMM spot-price manipulation caveat in `QuotePrice` — it is not a novel logic error unique to this adapter, and the magnitude of extractable value is inherently capped by the size of the weight-refund correction (usually small relative to total fee), making it a low-severity, bounded value-leakage issue rather than a mechanism for a user to broadly avoid paying fees.

## Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by any signed user submitting a self-crafted extrinsic whose call also swaps on the same fee-conversion pool, with a shallow pool magnifying the effect. However, exploitation profit is capped by the weight-refund magnitude and pool depth, which for realistic runtime configurations (deep pools, fixed-weight calls) yields negligible extractable value per transaction.

## Recommendation
Cache/reuse the withdrawal-time exchange rate for the refund-back conversion, or cap the refund `asset` amount to not exceed what the pre-dispatch rate would imply (`refund_amount * asset_fee / fee`), preventing the refund leg from benefiting from an intervening price shift.

## Proof of Concept
Not independently reproducible within the scope of the report's stated impact: the claim's PoC (batching a large price-shifting swap plus a fee-paying call) can at best affect the small weight-refund leg of the fee, not the bulk of the fee as described. No test in the repository demonstrates the significant fee-avoidance impact claimed; verifying the actual (bounded) magnitude would require a fork/integration test isolating the weight-refund delta under pool-price manipulation, which was not available to confirm materiality here.
