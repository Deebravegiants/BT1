### Title
RAY value overflow in `accrue_step` permanently freezes a large high-utilization market before the borrow-index cap engages - ([File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs](common/src/rates/simulate.rs))

### Summary
The analog of CVE-2022-38725 (integer overflow → remote DoS) lives in the interest-accrual path. `accrue_step` multiplies the scaled `borrowed`/`supplied` share totals by their indexes via `scaled_to_original` → `Ray::mul` → `mul_div_half_up`, which panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` when `scaled × index / RAY` exceeds `i128::MAX`. Because every state-mutating controller verb runs `global_sync` (which calls `accrue_step`) before touching position state, once `borrowed × borrow_index` crosses the i128 ceiling the market can never accrue again — `supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `flash_loan`, `update_indexes`, `claim_revenue` and `recapitalize` all revert permanently on that market, even though `update_borrow_index` would cap the index at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. The value ceiling (~`i128::MAX` ≈ 1.7e38 RAY-value) is reached before the index cap for whale-scale books.

### Finding Description
- `common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-61`: `accrue_step` unconditionally computes `scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)` and `scaled_to_original(env, supplied, supply_index)` before anything else.
- `common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`: `scaled_to_original` is `scaled.mul(env, index)` → `fp_core::mul_div_half_up` (`common/src/math/fp_core.rs:108-118`), which panics with `MathOverflow` when the quotient does not fit `i128`; the I256 fallback is exact, so `to_i128()` returns `None` past `i128::MAX`.
- `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33`: `global_sync` runs the chunked `accrue_chunk` → `accrue_step` loop before any mutation, so the panic is hit on every verb first.
- The in-repo regression test `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360` demonstrates exactly this: a 1-billion-token 18-decimal market borrowed to 98% on the XLM curve panics with `MATH_OVERFLOW` on `update_indexes` after a few years, `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (the cap never engages), and subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` also revert — a permanent freeze.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds: once the panic threshold is crossed, suppliers cannot withdraw and borrowers cannot repay on that market; liquidations cannot run, so positions cannot be exited at all. All deposited tokens on the affected `(hub, token)` book are bricked. This is not a fail-closed transient — no input or governance action that goes through `global_sync` can unblock it, because the panic occurs inside accrual itself.

### Likelihood Explanation
Low but reachable by unprivileged addresses: it requires a very large book — scaled shares × index must exceed ~1.7e38, i.e. roughly billions of whole tokens at 18 decimals, or proportionally more at lower decimals — sustained near the steep end of the utilization curve for multiple years of compounding. No privileged action is needed; ordinary `supply`/`borrow` volume and time suffice. Medium severity: high impact on that market's funds, demanding preconditions.

### Recommendation
Make accrual overflow-safe rather than panicking:
- In `accrue_step`, use a saturating/clamping variant when computing `borrowed_original`/`supplied_original` for utilization (utilization saturates at 100% anyway, so clamping the operands cannot change the rate).
- Let `update_borrow_index` engage `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` before the value computation overflows — e.g. check `scaled` against `i128::MAX / index` and clamp the index early, since at the cap no further interest accrues.
- Alternatively store/handle the accrual in the saturating domain so `global_sync` degrades to index-cap behavior instead of reverting every subsequent call.

### Proof of Concept
An unprivileged attacker/user path (already encoded as `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360`):
1. List a high-decimal market (18 decimals) on the steep XLM rate curve; lift supply/borrow caps (`lift_caps`).
2. `supply` ~10⁹ × 10¹⁸ units (BOB), then a second account `borrow`s ~98% of it.
3. Advance ledger time year by year, calling `update_indexes` (unprivileged entrypoint) each step.
4. After the borrow index has grown ~170×, `update_indexes` reverts with `MathOverflow` inside `accrue_step → scaled_to_original`.
5. Thereafter `withdraw(1)`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `claim_revenue`, `clean_bad_debt`, and `recapitalize` all revert on the same panic — the market's funds are frozen permanently while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
