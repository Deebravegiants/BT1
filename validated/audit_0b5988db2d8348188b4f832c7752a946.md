### Title
Bad-debt supply-index floor clamp resurrects wiped-out supply shares as a phantom claim that drains later deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs)

### Summary
The CVE-2022-4177 bug class (use-after-free: a resource that was logically destroyed remains usable) maps onto XOXNO Lending's bad-debt write-down path. When `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` socializes bad debt larger than total supply value, it clamps `supply_index` **up** to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (RAY/1000) instead of zeroing supplier shares. The old scaled supply — which was economically "freed" by the wipeout — keeps a nonzero claim. Once fresh cash enters the market, a holder of those dead shares can call `withdraw` and receive real tokens paid by new suppliers.

### Finding Description
`Cache` stores `supplied` (scaled shares) and `supply_index` separately (`contracts/pool/src/cache/mod.rs:30-42`). Bad-debt cleanup writes the loss into `supply_index`. The pool's own unit test `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` in `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-428` demonstrates that on a full wipeout (`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` with debt 5× the supply value), `supply_index` is clamped **up** to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` rather than shares being destroyed:

- `cache.supply_index().raw() == SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` — "wipeout clamps supply_index UP to RAY/1000 instead of resetting shares to 0"
- `unscale_supply_floor(old_scaled) > 0` — the dead shares still value positively
- After a fresh deposit, `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, old_scaled)` returns `gross == fresh_cash`, and `require_reserves` + `debit_cash` pay it out, leaving `cache.cash() < fresh_claim`

This is a use-after-free in accounting form: the write-down logically destroys the suppliers' claim, but the share object survives and is redeemed later against unrelated liquidity. The withdrawal path uses `Cache::resolve_withdrawal` (`contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:97-105`), which unscales `pos_scaled` at the post-clamp `supply_index` with no check that the shares predate the wipeout. The only barrier is `require_reserves` (a cash check), which passes as soon as any new liquidity exists — so the phantom claim is collectible by any unprivileged holder of pre-wipeout scaled supply via the controller's `withdraw` entrypoint after `clean_bad_debt` runs.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. An attacker holding (or acquiring, e.g., via position-NFT transfer) supply shares in a market that suffers a wipeout-level bad-debt event can wait for fresh deposits and withdraw against the clamped floor index, extracting value that belongs entirely to post-event suppliers. The test shows the drained amount equals the fresh deposit exactly, and the fresh supplier's claim is left unbacked.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a market to accumulate bad debt exceeding total supply value at the floor index — extreme but reachable in thin/illiquid hub markets with a price crash plus failed liquidations, after which `clean_bad_debt` (a permissionless controller path per the scope rules) performs the write-down. The attacker must hold scaled supply before the event; dust positions retained pre-crash suffice. Post-event, any deposit into the market becomes drainable.

### Recommendation
On write-down that hits `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, treat the market's scaled supply as extinguished: either record a per-market "wipeout epoch" that invalidates pre-event `pos_scaled` on withdrawal, or burn/flag the `supplied` shares in the same commit so `resolve_withdrawal` returns zero for them. Alternatively, revert `clean_bad_debt` when the required write-down would exceed the floor bound rather than clamping, forcing explicit `recapitalize` sequencing before new deposits are accepted.

### Proof of Concept
From `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-428` (in-contract unit test):

1. Seed market: `supplied = 1000 * RAY` scaled, `supply_index = RAY`, `cash = 0`.
2. `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(&mut cache, Ray::from(5_000 * RAY))` → `supply_index` clamps up to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`.
3. `unscale_supply_floor(old_scaled) > 0` — dead shares retain a claim.
4. Fresh supplier deposits `fresh_cash` → mints `fresh_scaled`, `credit_cash(fresh_cash)`.
5. Attacker withdraws the old position: `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, old_scaled)` → `gross == fresh_cash`; `require_reserves(gross)` passes; `burn_supply`/`debit_cash` pay out.
6. `cache.cash() < unscale_supply_floor(fresh_scaled)` — fresh supplier's funds were stolen by the freed-then-reused claim.

Caveat: I could not fully trace the production `clean_bad_debt` → `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` call chain and whether the controller applies an additional supply guard on withdrawal for wiped markets (the test name's "without_supply_guard" hints one may exist elsewhere); the pool-level accounting primitive demonstrably permits the drain as written.
