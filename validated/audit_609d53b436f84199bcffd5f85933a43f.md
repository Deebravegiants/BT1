### Title
Debt-value overflow in accrual permanently freezes a market - ([File: common/src/rates/index.rs](common/src/rates/index.rs))

### Summary
The pool accrues interest before every market mutation. During accrual, `calculate_supplier_rewards` multiplies the market's scaled debt by both the old and new borrow indexes using checked `i128` arithmetic. If that product overflows, the transaction panics before the timestamp is updated or any position is changed. Because repayment, withdrawal, liquidation, strategy repayment, recapitalization, and subsequent index updates all perform the same accrual first, the market becomes permanently unusable.

An unprivileged attacker can trigger this through `controller::update_indexes(caller, assets)` after creating a sufficiently large borrow position in a high-cap market. The borrow-index ceiling does not prevent the panic because the overflow occurs when calculating total debt value, before or independently of enforcing `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.

### Finding Description
Pool market operations load synchronized state through `ops::synced_market`, which calls `interest::global_sync` before executing the requested operation.

`global_sync` invokes `accrue_step` for every elapsed chunk. `accrue_step` first calls `update_borrow_index`, which caps only the index at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. It then calls:

```rust
let old_total_debt = borrowed.mul(env, old_borrow_index);
let new_total_debt = borrowed.mul(env, new_borrow_index);
```

in `common/src/rates/index.rs`.

`borrowed` and both indexes are RAY-scaled `i128` values. When `borrowed * borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX`, `Ray::mul` panics. The checked multiplication is necessary for correctness, but no caller handles the overflow or bounds the market's scaled debt to a representable value.

The failure happens inside `global_sync`, before `cache.mark_accrued()`. Consequently, `last_timestamp` is not advanced and the next call retries the same overflowing calculation. Since all repayment and exit paths synchronize the market first, users cannot reduce `borrowed` or withdraw supply to move the market below the overflow boundary.

The repository's own regression test demonstrates the condition: a one-billion-token, 18-decimal market at sustained 98% utilization reaches `MathOverflow` inside `scaled_to_original` before the borrow-index cap engages; afterward, both withdrawal and repayment fail.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of user funds.

Once the debt-value product crosses the `i128` boundary:

- Suppliers cannot withdraw the affected asset.
- Borrowers cannot repay the affected debt.
- Liquidators cannot liquidate positions touching the market.
- Bad-debt cleanup cannot process the market.
- Flash loans, revenue claims, recapitalization, and pool parameter updates that load the market also fail.
- The condition is self-perpetuating because every remediation path performs accrual before it can reduce debt or supply.

Any user liquidity already present in the affected market is frozen. In practical terms, governance cannot resolve it through ordinary market operations; recovery would require a contract upgrade or another privileged recovery mechanism outside the normal interface.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium likelihood, dependent on market scale and configuration.

The attack does not require privileged calls, malformed token behavior, oracle manipulation, or control of another account. A funded attacker can:

1. Supply enough of the target asset to create a large pool balance.
2. Supply sufficient collateral and borrow most of that liquidity.
3. Allow interest accrual to increase the borrow index until scaled debt times the index exceeds `i128::MAX`.
4. Call the permissionless `update_indexes` entrypoint to trigger and commit the condition.

The main constraints are economic and configurational: the market must admit enough debt for the RAY-scaled value to approach the `i128` ceiling, and the asset must have sufficient supply and decimals for that amount to exist. High-decimal assets are most exposed. Once those conditions exist, triggering the panic is reliable because `update_indexes` is permissionless and every accrual uses the same arithmetic.

### Recommendation
Bound and handle debt-value growth before calculating supplier rewards.

At minimum:

- Before `borrowed.mul(env, new_borrow_index)`, explicitly check whether the product can fit in `i128`.
- Enforce a scaled-debt ceiling such as `borrowed <= i128::MAX / new_borrow_index` before minting debt or accepting a state that can later exceed it.
- Prefer a non-panicking saturation path that advances `last_timestamp` and prevents further interest accrual while preserving repayment, withdrawal, liquidation, and bad-debt cleanup.
- Add the invariant to pool debt minting and utilization guards rather than only handling it during accrual.
- Add a permanent regression test showing that after reaching the boundary, `repay`, `withdraw`, `liquidate`, and `clean_bad_debt` remain executable and do not repeatedly panic during synchronization.

The index cap alone is insufficient; the market's total scaled debt multiplied by the index must also remain representable.

### Proof of Concept
The repository already contains a deterministic harness witness in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs`, `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap`.

Conceptual on-chain sequence:

1. In a listed, borrowable 18-decimal market with sufficiently high caps, call:

   ```text
   controller.supply(caller, account_id = 0, spoke_id, [
       (target_hub_asset, large_supply_amount)
   ])
   ```

   to create an account and provide liquidity.

2. Supply enough collateral in another listed collateral market and call:

   ```text
   controller.borrow(caller, account_id, [
       (target_hub_asset, approximately_98_percent_of_supply)
   ], to = None)
   ```

3. Leave the market untouched while high utilization compounds the borrow index.

4. Trigger accrual with the permissionless entrypoint:

   ```text
   controller.update_indexes(caller, [target_hub_asset])
   ```

5. `accrue_step` calculates:

   ```text
   borrowed_scaled_ray * new_borrow_index_ray / RAY
   ```

   through `calculate_supplier_rewards`. The product exceeds `i128::MAX`, causing `MathOverflow`.

6. Subsequent calls fail identically:

   ```text
   controller.repay(caller, borrower_account_id, [
       (target_hub_asset, 1)
   ])

   controller.withdraw(supplier, supplier_account_id, [
       (target_hub_asset, 1)
   ], None)
   ```

   Both load the market and execute `global_sync` before debt can be burned or supply withdrawn, so they repeatedly hit the same overflow.
