Let me verify the insolvent-account trim and leg-dropping behavior in the code.### Title
Insolvent account with collateral in ($5, one debt-token unit/(1+base)) can never be liquidated or cleaned — permanent bad-debt DoS - ([File: contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/math.rs](contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/math.rs))

### Summary
An attacker can open a position whose debt is denominated in a low-decimal (`asset_decimals < MIN_BORROWABLE_ASSET_DECIMALS`, i.e. < 3) borrowable asset and whose collateral sits just above the `BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD` of $5. Once interest accrual pushes the account insolvent, every `liquidate` call reverts: the collateral-backed quote is worth less than one native unit of the debt token, so the insolvency trim floors each kept repayment leg to zero, drops it, and `liquidate` panics with `InvalidPayments`. At the same time `clean_bad_debt` permanently refuses because `total_collateral > $5`. The result is bad debt that neither liquidation nor permissionless cleanup can remove — a direct analog of the reported "backend worker silently enters an unrecoverable error state" DoS class, mapped onto XOXNO Lending's liquidation/bad-debt pipeline.

### Finding Description
For an insolvent account (`total_collateral < total_debt`), `estimate_liquidation_amount` returns `quote = min(D, floor(C / (1 + base)))` at the base bonus (`curve.rs:117-123`). `normalize_repayment_plan` then calls `process_excess_payment` with `insolvent = true`, which floors each kept repayment leg to native token units via `to_token_floor` and removes a leg whose kept amount reaches zero (`math.rs:668-686`). If the debt token's smallest unit is worth more than the quote — e.g., a listed 0-decimal asset priced above ~$6 — the kept amount always floors to 0 for every leg. `liquidate` then reverts at `require_non_empty_payments` (`mod.rs:66`), and `get_liquidation_estimate` shows a zero payment for every possible offer. `one_unit_per_leg_usd`/`seize_all` rescue logic (`math.rs:209-212`) never engages because no leg survives to be summed. Permissionless `clean_bad_debt` requires `collateral <= BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD` ($5, `curve.rs:25-27`), which the attacker keeps violated by holding just over $5 of collateral. If the collateral market has no borrowers, its supply index does not grow (`supply`/`borrow` indexes only move on utilization), so `C` never drifts upward into the liquidatable band and the state is permanent absent an owner-only `force_socialize_bad_debt`.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds / protocol insolvency: the attacker's debt is never repaid and never socialized. The unbacked `borrowed` aggregate remains in the debt market's books indefinitely, so the pool's `cash` covers less than the booked debt and suppliers of that market cannot fully withdraw — the shortfall is frozen (not socialized, since the write-down path in `seize_positions` never runs). The attack can be repeated per account; each occurrence permanently locks up to roughly the borrowed amount (~LTV × ~$5–(1+b)·U per account) of supplier liquidity until governance manually intervenes. Unlike the documented transient "stuck band" for solvent accounts (which only delays a healthy liquidation until accrual/price movement), this state is insolvent and self-perpetuating: accrual increases `D`, never `C`, so time makes it worse, not better.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. Requires (a) a listed borrowable asset with `asset_decimals < 3` and unit price exceeding the quote band — feasible for a 0-decimal token priced ≥ ~$6/unit or a 2-decimal token priced ≥ ~$550/unit; and (b) a collateral market with negligible borrow demand so its supply index stays flat (any idle market qualifies). The attacker then simply supplies ~$5–$6 of collateral, borrows near the LTV limit in the low-decimal asset, and waits for millisecond-chunked interest accrual to push `D > C` — no price manipulation or privileged action needed. Cost per stuck account is roughly collateral minus borrowed proceeds (~$0.5–$1 at 90% LTV on $6).

### Recommendation
Do not floor insolvent kept repayments in a way that can drop the last surviving leg below executability. In `process_excess_payment` (`keep_within_quote` branch), if flooring the kept amount to token decimals yields zero while the leg is the only remaining repayment, either keep the leg at one unit (accepting the one-unit overshoot against the quote, mirroring the `seize_all` one-unit tolerance in `one_unit_per_leg_usd`) or promote `seize_all`/`full_close` semantics so the collateral-backed quote is taken at ceil rounding. Alternatively, relax `is_socializable_bad_debt` for the case where the collateral-backed quote is unexecutable — e.g., treat an insolvent account whose maximum executable repayment rounds to zero in every debt market as socializable regardless of the $5 cap — or lower the effective gate to compare collateral against `max($5, min whole-unit repayable value across its debt markets)`.

### Proof of Concept
Precondition: hub contains a borrowable asset `LOW` with `asset_decimals = 0` and oracle price $10/unit, and a collateral asset `COL` with nonzero supply but zero borrows (flat supply index), LTV 90%, liquidation bonus base ~10%.

1. Attacker calls `supply` for 1 unit of `COL` priced $6 (C = $6 > $5 dust cap).
2. Attacker calls `borrow` for 0.5 units of `LOW` (D = $5 ≤ LTV·C = $5.4).
3. Interest accrues on `LOW`'s borrow index until D > $6 (insolvent). No price movement needed.
4. Any liquidator calls `liquidate` offering `LOW`:
   - `estimate_liquidation_amount` → `quote = floor(6 / 1.1) ≈ $5.45`.
   - `process_excess_payment` trims the offered leg to the quote: kept `LOW` amount = `to_token_floor($5.45 / $10) = to_token_floor(0.545) = 0` → leg removed.
   - `require_non_empty_payments(&result.repaid)` → revert `InvalidPayments` (controller #16). Same for any offered amount and any `SeizeMode`.
5. Any keeper calls `clean_bad_debt(account_id)`: `total_collateral = $6 > $5` → revert `CannotCleanBadDebt` (#114).
6. Since `COL` has no borrows, its supply index stays at RAY; `C` remains $6 forever, so steps 4–5 revert permanently. The $5+ of `LOW` debt is never repaid and never written down — supplier funds in the `LOW` market are permanently short.
