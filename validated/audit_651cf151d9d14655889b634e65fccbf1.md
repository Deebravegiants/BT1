The analog exists. In Allo the late `fundPool` contribution was absorbed into `poolAmount` but became unreachable once payouts were marked `paidOut`. In XOXNO Lending the corresponding shape is: tokens transferred directly to the pool contract increase `token.balance(pool)` but never increase the `cash` book, and there is no sweep/recovery path — the excess over the book is permanently stranded. The `recapitalize` entrypoint only credits the *measured receipt of its own transfer* and caps it at `backing_shortfall`, so it cannot rescue a pre-existing donation. Per the project's own invariant docs, this is deliberate (`INV-ACCT-02`: "token donations alone do not increase it"), but unlike Allo's fix (a `recoverFund`), no function exists to release stray balance, so the funds are frozen permanently.

### Title
Tokens transferred directly to the pool are permanently locked — the cash book ignores real balance and no recovery path exists - (contracts/pool/src/ops/recapitalize.rs)

### Summary
The pool tracks reserves in a `cash` bookkeeping field, completely decoupled from the SAC token balance. The only operations that credit cash are `supply`, `repay` (net), and `recapitalize` — all gated behind the controller (`#[only_owner]`), and all basing the credit on the controller's *measured* inbound transfer within the same call. A direct `token.transfer(sender, pool, amount)` therefore raises the pool's real balance without raising `cash`, and no entrypoint can ever attribute or release that surplus. This is the exact analog of M-9: funds added to the pool outside the sanctioned accounting path are locked.

### Finding Description
- `contracts/pool/src/cache/cash.rs` (`credit_cash`/`debit_cash`/`transfer_out`) mutates only the `cash` book; nothing reconciles it to `token.balance`.
- `contracts/pool/src/ops/recapitalize.rs:52` applies `amount.min(backing_shortfall(&cache))` where `amount` is the *declared* figure — the controller passes the measured delta of its own just-executed transfer (`contracts/controller/src/markets.rs:154-163`, `transfer_amount_measured`). A donation sitting in the balance before the call is invisible: `backing_shortfall` reads the book (`floor(supply_value) − (cash + ceil(debt))`), not the balance.
- The pool's entire outbound surface (`withdraw`, `borrow`, `claim_revenue`, `repay`/`recapitalize` refunds, `flash_loan`) is bounded by the `cash` book or by owner-declared amounts (`INV-AUTH-01`). None can pay out more than `cash`, so balance−`cash` is unreachable.
- Worse: `flash_loan` is documented as the *only* strict-equality balance reconciliation (`contracts/pool/README.md:61-63`). A donation makes `balance > cash`-consistent expectations, biasing the flash-loan equality checks (fail-closed), but critically there is no `skim`/`recoverFund` equivalent — the recommended remediation in M-9 — anywhere in the surface (`contracts/pool/README.md:67-82`).

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds: any tokens sent directly to the pool contract (mistaken deposits, fee-on-transfer residual, third-party "donations," or a user funding the pool after a bad-debt write-down believing it restores backing) are unrecoverable by anyone, including the owner. Unlike Allo, where `poolAmount` at least grew recipient payouts, here the donation does not even reduce `backing_shortfall`, so suppliers are not compensated — the value is destroyed, not redistributed. This satisfies the "permanent freezing of funds" acceptance criterion and is reachable by a wholly unprivileged address via `token.transfer` (explicitly an in-scope vector: "direct token transfers to the pool or controller").

### Likelihood Explanation
Any holder of the market asset can trigger it with one SAC transfer — no privilege, timing, or multi-step setup. The same exposure applies to tokens sent to the controller address, which are likewise unreachable (the controller only forwards measured deltas during `claim_revenue`, `markets.rs:179-196`). The caveat that keeps this at Medium rather than High is that loss requires the *sender* to bypass the controller — the protocol itself never solicits direct transfers — matching the original finding's "anyone can call `fundPool`" self-lock profile, which was judged Medium.

### Recommendation
Add an owner-only `recover`/`skim` entrypoint on the pool that transfers `token.balance(pool) − cash` (per asset) to a treasury address, mirroring the M-9 fix (`recoverFund`). Alternatively, document and enforce the "no direct transfers" assumption by having `recapitalize` measure the pool balance itself rather than trusting the owner's declared `amount`, so any pre-existing surplus is credited to `cash` (up to the shortfall) or refunded.

### Proof of Concept
1. `token.transfer(victim, pool, X)` — pool balance becomes `cash + X`, `cash` unchanged (`INV-ACCT-02`).
2. `controller.recapitalize(payer, hub_asset, X)` — `transfer_amount_measured` pulls `X` *from the payer*; the pool applies only `min(X, backing_shortfall)` and refunds the rest to `payer`. The earlier `X` donation is untouched.
3. No entrypoint can move `X`: `withdraw`/`borrow`/`claim_revenue` are cash-bounded; refunds are owner-declared; `flash_loan` only enforces equality around its own loan.
4. `X` is permanently stranded — verified against the surface table in `contracts/pool/README.md:67-115`, which contains no recovery function.
