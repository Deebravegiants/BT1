### Title
RAY-denominated market totals can overflow during accrual, permanently freezing a market - ([File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs](common/src/rates/simulate.rs))

### Summary

The interest-accrual path multiplies RAY-scaled market totals by RAY indexes before applying the configured index ceiling. When `borrowed * borrow_index` or `supplied * supply_index` exceeds `i128::MAX`, accrual panics before state is committed. Because every pool mutation loads the market through `synced_market`, the same overflow blocks later repayments, withdrawals, liquidations, flash operations, recapitalization, and even permissionless `update_indexes` calls.

### Finding Description

`Controller::update_indexes(caller, assets)` is permissionless after caller authorization and forwards the selected markets to the pool [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) . The pool's mutating market paths call `ops::load_leg`, which calls `synced_market`, which always executes `interest::global_sync` before the requested operation [3](#0-2) .

`global_sync` invokes `accrue_step` for each elapsed compounding chunk [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) . `accrue_step` first computes `borrowed_original` and `supplied_original` with `scaled_to_original`, which is a checked `scaled * index` multiplication [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) . It subsequently multiplies `borrowed` by both old and new borrow indexes in `calculate_supplier_rewards`, and `supplied` by the supply index in `update_supply_index` [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8) .

The configured `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` ceiling does not prevent this condition because it is applied only after `old_index * interest_factor`, while other total-value products can overflow before or independently of that ceiling [10](#0-9) . An attacker can establish a very large market through `Controller::supply` and `Controller::borrow`, using attacker-controlled supplier and borrower accounts, and then any unprivileged caller can cross the overflow boundary with `Controller::update_indexes(caller, vec![hub_asset])` [11](#0-10) [1](#0-0) .

The repository contains an executable reproduction of this failure: `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` creates a one-billion-whole-token market, maintains 98% utilization, repeatedly calls `update_indexes`, observes `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and then confirms that withdrawal and repayment fail with the same error [12](#0-11) .

### Impact Explanation

Once the boundary is crossed, all users with supply or debt in the affected `(hub_id, asset)` market lose access to every operation that accrues first. `withdraw` loads a synced market before burning supply, `repay` loads one before burning debt, and `borrow` does the same before minting debt [13](#0-12) [14](#0-13) [15](#0-14) . This produces permanent freezing of supplier funds and borrower collateral recovery paths, while unclaimed protocol revenue also becomes inaccessible.

### Likelihood Explanation

Triggering requires an unusually large market or large scaled total whose RAY-valued multiplication exceeds `i128::MAX`, plus enough elapsed time at sufficiently high utilization for index growth to reach the boundary. It does not require privileged access, oracle manipulation, leaked keys, or malformed token behavior: the attacker can provide the required supply and collateral, borrow through the normal `borrow` entrypoint, and anyone can invoke `update_indexes`.

The practical barrier is capital and market configuration. Deployed supply caps, borrow caps, utilization limits, and available token supply determine whether the required scale is reachable. The test demonstrates the arithmetic boundary is real and occurs before the index ceiling protects the market, but it intentionally lifts caps and disables the max-utilization guard in the harness [16](#0-15) .

### Recommendation

Handle unrepresentable accrued totals without aborting the shared accrual path. Possible mitigations include:

- Bound `supplied`, `borrowed`, and derived RAY totals below the multiplication ceiling at market entry.
- Saturate total-value calculations used for utilization rather than panicking, while preserving conservative solvency semantics.
- Provide an emergency accrual path that caps the affected index and allows exits and repayments to proceed from the last valid committed state.
- Add a continuous invariant monitor that halts new exposure before `scaled_total * index` approaches `i128::MAX`.
- Enforce caps based on worst-case future index growth, not only current token-unit caps.

### Proof of Concept

1. An attacker-controlled address creates supplier and borrower accounts.
2. Through `Controller::supply`, the address deposits a very large amount into the target market and sufficient collateral into a supported collateral market.
3. Through `Controller::borrow`, the borrowing account draws enough target assets to keep the market at high utilization.
4. Time advances while utilization remains high, increasing the borrow and supply indexes.
5. Any unprivileged address calls `Controller::update_indexes(caller, vec![target_hub_asset])`.
6. `interest::global_sync` calls `accrue_step`; `scaled_to_original` panics on `scaled * index > i128::MAX`, so no updated state is committed.
7. Later `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, and other market mutators fail for the same reason because each loads the market through `synced_market` before executing its own logic.

The checked test at `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321` performs this scenario and confirms `MATH_OVERFLOW` on `update_indexes`, followed by identical failures for `withdraw` and `repay` [17](#0-16) .

pub(crate) fn global_sync(env: &Env, cache: &mut Cache) {
    if !cache.needs_accrual() {
        return;
    }

    let mut remaining = cache.elapsed_ms();
    while let Some(nonzero) = NonZeroU64::new(remaining) {
        let chunk = nonzero.get().min(MAX_COMPOUND_DELTA_MS);
        accrue_chunk(env, cache, chunk);
        remaining = remaining.saturating_sub(chunk);
    }

    cache.mark_accrued();
}

/// `i128::MAX`, 170 times that. At the XLM curve's steep segment the index
/// grows past 170x in a few years, and the next accrual panics inside
/// `scaled_to_original`. Every verb accrues first, so the market freezes:
/// no repay, no withdraw, no liquidation. The index cap never engages.
#[test]
fn a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap() {
    let mut t = LendingTest::new()
        .with_market(big("BIG18", 18, xlm_curve()))
        .with_market(col())
        .with_max_utilization_disabled_all_markets()
        .build();
    lift_caps(&t, "BIG18", 18);
    lift_caps(&t, "COL", 7);
    let principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18);
    t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);
    let debt = principal / 100 * 98;
    t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
    t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);

    let mut years = 0u32;
    let failure = loop {
        years += 1;
        assert!(
            years <= 40,
            "no cliff within 40 years; the bound in docs/reference/formulas.md is wrong"
        );
        t.advance_time(YEAR_SECS);
        if let Err(e) = t.try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"]) {
            break e;
        }

    assert_contract_error(failed, errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    let last = book(&t, "BIG18");
    assert!(
        last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY,
        "the index cap did not engage before the value overflow"
    );
    // The market is frozen: exits and repayments accrue first and hit the same panic.
    assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
