### Title
Borrows can be opened in a market that already carries a positive backing shortfall, draining residual cash owed to written-down suppliers - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/borrow.rs)

### Summary
The M-01 class — a guard that inspects only part of the settled state and admits new participants after the round has partially resolved — maps onto the pool's entry guards. `supply` runs `require_backed_market`, which rejects entry into an under-backed market, but `mint_debt` (used by `borrow` and `create_strategy`) omits that check entirely and only enforces cash reserves, the 2% liquidation buffer, and the utilization ceiling. After `seize_positions` socializes bad debt through `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, a market can hold a positive `backing_shortfall` while still holding cash. An unprivileged borrower with collateral in healthy markets can then draw that cash, deepening the shortfall borne by suppliers whose claims survived the write-down at the `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` residual.

### Finding Description
Guards are asymmetric in a way the codebase does not document as policy. `guards::require_backed_market` panics with `PoolInsolvent` when `backing_shortfall(cache) > 0`, where the shortfall is `floor(supplied*supply_index) − (cash + ceil(borrowed*borrow_index))`. It fires only in `ops::supply::apply` (`guards::require_backed_market(env, &cache)` at `contracts/pool/src/ops/supply.rs:26`). The debt-minting path `ops::borrow::mint_debt` runs only `cache.require_reserves(amount)`, `guards::require_liquidation_buffer`, and `guards::require_utilization_below_max` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/borrow.rs:63-78`). `require_reserves` and the buffer look only at `cash`; the utilization check compares ceiled debt to floored supply, which stays under the ceiling whenever cash itself is the missing backing (i.e., `supplied > cash + borrowed` with low utilization is exactly the bad-debt aftermath). The documented guard table (`contracts/pool/README.md`, Guards section) confirms `require_backed_market` fires on `supply` and nothing else, and the two documented intentional asymmetries concern withdraw vs supply and the utilization skip for liquidations — not borrow.

`seize_positions` on the `Borrow` side writes the loss down via `interest::apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` and burns the debt shares (`contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:24-28`). The index is floored at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`), which deliberately leaves unburned supply shares with a residual unbacked claim (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:317-369`, `430-495`). The resulting state — positive shortfall, cash possibly still positive — blocks new supply but admits new debt.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent loss for existing suppliers / deepening protocol insolvency. Each borrowed unit is transferred out of a market whose supplier claims already exceed `cash + debt`, so stranded suppliers' residual claims become even less recoverable; `recapitalize` would then require a larger donation to restore backing. The borrower's own collateral is booked in other `(hub, asset)` markets, so the extracted cash is never at risk from this market's books — economically equivalent to buying a ticket after prizes were distributed, except here the "ticket" withdraws cash outright rather than merely being worthless. Qualifies as theft of user funds / protocol insolvency.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a market that has suffered a partial bad-debt write-down while still holding cash — reachable through permissionless `clean_bad_debt` or liquidation `seize_positions`, and via the floor-residual mechanic the codebase explicitly documents as reachable. Any unprivileged user can then hit controller `borrow` (or `multiply`/`flash_position`, which reach `mint_debt` through `create_strategy`) with collateral elsewhere. The utilization ceiling partially self-limits the draw — when the write-down shrinks the floored supply value enough, additional debt trips `UtilizationAboveMax` — so the drainable amount is bounded and the issue is Medium rather than High.

### Recommendation
Run `guards::require_backed_market` (or an equivalent `backing_shortfall == 0` assertion) inside `mint_debt` alongside the existing reserve and utilization checks, so debt cannot be minted into an under-backed book. This mirrors the recommended fix in the original finding: freeze new entry for the market once a loss has been recorded, until `recapitalize` restores backing.

### Proof of Concept
1. Market `(hub, USDC)`: `supplied = 100` tokens of scaled shares, `borrowed = 50`, `cash = 80`, indexes at `RAY`.
2. A liquidation/cleanup calls `seize_positions` on the `Borrow` side for a 50-token position where only 30 tokens of collateral-backed value existed; `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` writes the supply index down so floored supply claim = 70, and burns the debt. Result: claim 70, cash 50 (liquidation paid 30 into cash), `backing_shortfall = 20 > 0`. `supply` now reverts with `PoolInsolvent` (per `ops/supply.rs:26`).
3. Alice has collateral supplied in healthy ETH markets. She calls controller `borrow(account_id, [(hub, USDC), amount = 25])` → pool `borrow` → `mint_debt` (`ops/borrow.rs:63-78`): `require_reserves` passes (cash 50 ≥ 25); `require_liquidation_buffer` passes (50 − 25 = 25 ≥ ceil(70 × 2%) = 2); `require_utilization_below_max` passes ((0 + 25)/70 ≈ 0.36 < 0.95). The 25 USDC is transferred to her (`ops/borrow.rs:32-34`).
4. Final state: claim 70, backing = cash 25 + debt 25 = 50, shortfall 20 → Alice holds debt obligating repayment into an already-short book; if she defaults or withdraws her collateral elsewhere, the 25 extracted tokens are an irrecoverable loss to the suppliers holding the floor-residual claims. The market entered this state through a guard (`mint_debt`) that, like `winningTickets[round][0].length == 0`, checked the wrong dimension of a partially resolved round.
