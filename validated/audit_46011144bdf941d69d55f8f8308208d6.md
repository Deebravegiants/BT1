### Title
Webhook `shop-domain` (and `topic`/`webhook-id`) headers are trusted without being covered by the HMAC signature, enabling cross-tenant webhook spoofing - (File: `lib/shopify_api/webhooks/request.rb`)

### Summary
`ShopifyAPI::Webhooks::Registry.process` authenticates an incoming webhook solely by validating the HMAC over the raw request body, then trusts the `shop-domain`, `topic`, `webhook-id`, and `api-version` values taken from unauthenticated HTTP headers to build the `WebhookMetadata` passed to the app's business-logic handler. Because Shopify signs webhooks with a single app-level `client_secret` shared across every shop that has the app installed, a body+HMAC pair that is valid for one shop is also a valid HMAC for that identical body regardless of which `shop-domain` header accompanies it. This breaks the binding `verified_bytes == acted_upon_bytes`: the bytes that are cryptographically verified are only the JSON body, while the bytes the handler actually acts on to determine *which merchant/tenant* the event belongs to (`request.shop`) are never covered by the signature.

### Finding Description
`Request#to_signable_string` returns only the raw body: [1](#0-0) 

while `shop`, `topic`, `webhook_id`, and `api_version` are read directly from HTTP headers with no cryptographic binding to the body: [2](#0-1) 

`HmacValidator.validate` verifies the signature strictly against `verifiable_query.to_signable_string`, i.e. the body only: [3](#0-2) 

`Registry.process` then dispatches to the app's handler using the *unverified* `request.shop` (and `topic`/`webhook_id`) once the body HMAC check passes: [4](#0-3) 

Because the app's webhook secret (`Context.api_secret_key`) is identical for every merchant that installs the app (it's the app's `client_secret`, not a per-shop secret), any party who legitimately receives one valid signed webhook payload/HMAC pair for their own shop (e.g. by installing the app in their own development or test store) can replay that exact body+HMAC to the app's webhook endpoint while substituting an arbitrary `shopify-shop-domain` header value. The signature check passes (the body byte-string didn't change), but the handler is invoked believing the event originates from the victim's shop — an identity binding break equivalent to the report's "value acted on but not covered by the HMAC/verification" pattern, here `shop` acted on ≠ `shop` covered by verification.

### Impact Explanation
This is a cross-tenant spoofing primitive: an attacker who controls one shop where the vulnerable app is installed can forge webhook events (order data, GDPR data-request payloads, uninstall notifications, etc.) that the app attributes to a different, victim shop, since `WebhookMetadata.shop` is populated from the unauthenticated header rather than anything covered by the signature. Depending on what the host application's webhook handlers do with `data.shop` (e.g., write data associated with that shop, trigger fulfillment/refund logic, or make decisions gating access to a session/token store), this can lead to cross-tenant data corruption or unauthorized actions on behalf of another merchant, satisfying the "cross-tenant access" criterion.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitability requires the attacker to first obtain at least one valid signed webhook body for *any* shop using the app — trivially achievable by an unprivileged party who installs the app on their own store (which any developer can typically do) and captures a delivered webhook. No access to `api_secret_key`, tokens, or victim credentials is needed; only reuse of a previously-observed valid signature with a substituted header.

### Recommendation
Bind the shop (and ideally topic/webhook id) into the verified material, e.g. include the `shopify-shop-domain` (and other identifying headers) in the HMAC-signed payload used by `to_signable_string`, or independently verify that the `shop` on the request corresponds to a shop with an active, known installation/session before trusting it, rather than relying purely on the body HMAC.

### Proof of Concept
1. Install the vulnerable app on attacker-controlled shop `attacker.myshopify.com`; trigger any webhook (e.g. `orders/create`) and capture the raw JSON body and its `x-shopify-hmac-sha256` value — this HMAC is valid because it is computed with the app's single shared `client_secret`.
2. Replay the captured `raw_body` and `x-shopify-hmac-sha256` to the app's webhook endpoint, but replace the `x-shopify-shop-domain` header with `victim.myshopify.com`.
3. `HmacValidator.validate` in `lib/shopify_api/utils/hmac_validator.rb` succeeds (body unchanged), and `Registry.process` in `lib/shopify_api/webhooks/registry.rb` invokes the handler with `WebhookMetadata.new(shop: "victim.myshopify.com", ...)`, causing the app to process attacker-controlled data under the victim shop's identity.
