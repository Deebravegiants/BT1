No vulnerability found for this question.

The reported issue (`AjnaToken.transferFromWithPermit`) is an EIP-2612-style ECDSA permit signature that can be extracted from a pending transaction and replayed by a third party with different destination/spender parameters, burning the signature and causing the original transaction to revert.

Raydium AMM has no analogous mechanism. All fund-moving instructions (`process_deposit`, `process_withdraw`, `process_swap_base_in/out`, `process_swap_base_in/out_v2`) authorize token movement exclusively through Solana's native transaction-level `is_signer` check on the owner account passed to the SPL Token CPI, not through a detachable, replayable cryptographic signature blob that a third party could intercept and resubmit with substituted parameters [1](#0-0) . The `token_transfer` and `token_transfer_with_authority` invokers require the actual `owner`/`amm_authority` `AccountInfo` as a co-signer or PDA-derived authority baked into the same instruction, so there is no way to lift and replay a signature to redirect funds or DOS a pending transaction the way permit signatures can be reused in the Ajna example [2](#0-1) . Solana's transaction model itself (single atomic transaction with all required signers bound to that specific instruction and account set) does not admit the "copy signature, front-run with different destination" pattern described in the report, since there's no separate `permit`-style delegated-approval primitive in this program's instruction set [3](#0-2) .
