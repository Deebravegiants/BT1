### Title
Self-liquidation on an insolvent account lets the owner capture the liquidation bonus while the residual debt is auto-socialized to suppliers - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs)

### Summary
XOXNO Lending's `liquidate` entrypoint explicitly permits self-liquidation: "Owners may self-liquidate but cannot credit seized shares back to the liquidated account" (`mod.rs:30-32`). On an insolvent account (collateral `C` < debt `D`), the repayment quote is capped at `floor(C / (1 + base_bonus))` while the seizure is sized to the *full* collateral, and `check_bad_debt_after_liquidation` immediately socializes whatever debt remains. A borrower who self-liquidates therefore pays strictly less than `C` in debt tokens, receives all `C` of their collateral back, and has the shortfall `D - repaid` written off against suppliers' supply index — pocketing the liquidation bonus themselves instead of an external liquidator.

### Finding Description
In `contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs:42-137`, `liquidate` only does `liquidator.require_auth()` — there is no check that `liquidator != account owner`. The plan builder caps an insolvent offer so the liquidator never pays more than the collateral backs (`skills/xoxno-lending-liquidations/SKILL.md:105-113`, confirmed by `test_insolvent_over_offer_repays_only_what_the_collateral_backs`, `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/liquidation.rs:270-296`, where repaying ~$4,761 of a $6,000 debt seizes the entire $5,000 collateral). After seizure, `apply::check_bad_debt_after_liquidation` (`apply.rs:301-315`) runs `is_socializable_bad_debt` and fires `bad_debt::execute_bad_debt_cleanup`, which seizes all remaining positions to protocol revenue, writes off residual debt via supply-index write-down, and deletes the account (`bad_debt.rs:14-60`). Because the remaining collateral is zero after a full seizure, the $5 dust gate always passes, so the residual debt is socialized atomically in the same transaction.

Analog to the StakeWise report: in StakeWise, a staker at LTV > 100% could self-liquidate to keep a fraction of osETH while withdrawing all staked ETH, accruing bad debt. Here, an insolvent borrower can self-liquidate via `liquidate(self, self_account_id, debt_payments, SeizeMode::Transfer)`, recovering their own collateral plus the effective bonus spread `C - C/(1+bonus)`, while the unpaid debt remainder is socialized to the pool's suppliers.

### Impact Explanation
Protocol insolvency / theft of user funds: the residual debt is written down against the debt market's supply index (`invariants.md` INV-LIQ-04, threat-model `docs/explanation/threat-model.md:302-308`), so other suppliers absorb the loss. The self-liquidating borrower extracts the bonus spread (e.g., ~$239 on a $5k/$6k position at 5% base bonus) plus avoids paying the difference an honest full `repay` would cost — value that, in a third-party liquidation, would at least go to an external liquidator who took market risk; here it goes to the very party who created the bad debt. On any position that drifts under water, the rational owner self-liquidates immediately, converting what could have been an orderly third-party liquidation into guaranteed self-dealing plus instant socialization.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium likelihood: requires the position to become insolvent (collateral < debt), but needs no privileged role — only `require_auth` by the owner (`mod.rs:43`) and a `Transfer` seize mode to their own wallet. The bad-debt gate is dust-threshold based, and a full seizure leaves zero collateral, so cleanup fires automatically every time (`liquidation.rs:348-373` shows auto-cleanup removing the account). The profit is bounded by the bonus spread on the seized collateral rather than the full collateral value (unlike StakeWise where all ETH was withdrawn against partial osETH burn), which is why the severity sits at Medium rather than High.

### Recommendation
Disallow the account owner (and its delegates) from acting as `liquidator` for their own `account_id`, mirroring the existing restriction that seized shares cannot be credited back to the liquidated account. Alternatively, charge a protocol liquidation fee taken from the seized collateral (beyond the current share-credit fee) so that a self-liquidation strictly loses money relative to simply letting a third party liquidate, removing the incentive to manufacture bonus capture on one's own bad debt.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice supplies 100,000 USDC and borrows 6 ETH ($12,000) — see `deep_underwater_account_still_liquidates_to_the_dust_gate`, `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/bad_debt_netting_and_exit_timing.rs:191-269`.
2. USDC price crashes 10x → collateral $10,000 vs debt $12,000 (HF < 1, insolvent).
3. Instead of repaying or waiting, Alice calls `liquidate(alice, alice_account_id, [ETH payment ≈ full debt offer], SeizeMode::Transfer)`. The plan caps her payment to `floor(10000/1.05)` ≈ $9,524 USD of ETH and seizes the full $10,000 of USDC back to her own wallet.
4. `check_bad_debt_after_liquidation` sees collateral = 0 ≤ $5 and `total_debt > total_collateral`, so `execute_bad_debt_cleanup` writes off the remaining ~$2,476 of ETH debt against the ETH market's supply index and burns her account.
5. Net: Alice paid ~$9,524 to extinguish $12,000 of debt and recovered her $10,000 collateral — a ~$476 self-captured bonus plus ~$2,476 socialized onto ETH suppliers, versus the honest alternative of repaying the full $12,000 or forfeiting collateral to an external liquidator.
