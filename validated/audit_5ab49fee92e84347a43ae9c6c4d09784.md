### Title
Accrual overflow permanently freezes an oversized lending market - (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary
A user can create a market state whose accrued RAY-denominated supply or debt value exceeds `i128` before the borrow-index ceiling is reached. Once that occurs, every mutating market operation first runs accrual and reverts on `MathOverflow`, permanently blocking withdrawals, repayments, liquidations, and further index updates. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` calculates `borrowed * borrow_index` and `supplied * supply_index` before it updates and clamps the borrow index. [1](#0-0)  Both conversions call `scaled_to_original`, which performs checked multiplication and panics on overflow instead of clamping or safely bounding the market value. [4](#0-3)  The intended `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` clamp only runs after this earlier multiplication succeeds, so it cannot protect the value-domain overflow. [5](#0-4) 

The repository’s own regression test demonstrates the reachable state: a one-billion-whole-token, 18-decimal market at 98% utilization eventually causes `update_indexes` to fail before the index cap, after which both `withdraw` and `repay` fail with `MathOverflow`. [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6)  The admitted token-to-RAY bound is approximately 170 billion whole tokens, so this condition is inside the protocol’s expressible input domain rather than a rejected argument. [8](#0-7) 

### Impact Explanation
All user funds in the affected market become permanently frozen because every state-changing pool path synchronizes the market before applying the requested mutation. [2](#0-1) [9](#0-8)  Suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot reduce risk, and permissionless debt cleanup cannot proceed once accrual overflows. [3](#0-2)  This is not merely a failed individual request: the persisted `last_timestamp` is not advanced, so every subsequent attempt repeats the same overflowing computation. [10](#0-9) 

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is constrained by the need for a very large listed market and sustained high utilization over an extended accrual interval. [11](#0-10)  However, an unprivileged account can establish the state through ordinary `supply` and `borrow` calls when governance-configured caps permit the size, and later any routine market interaction triggers the failure. [6](#0-5) [3](#0-2) 

### Recommendation
Bound market scaled totals so that their post-accrual RAY values cannot exceed the representable domain, including projected index growth rather than only current token amounts. [12](#0-11)  At minimum, make accrual cap the effective borrow index before materializing total debt, or use a saturating market-total calculation that permits the index clamp to engage instead of panicking. [5](#0-4) [4](#0-3) 

### Proof of Concept
1. Supply an allowed 18-decimal asset amount near one billion whole tokens to a market.
2. Borrow approximately 98% of that market against sufficient collateral.
3. Leave the market at high utilization until the borrow index has grown enough that `borrowed * borrow_index` no longer fits in `i128`.
4. Call `update_indexes`, `repay`, `withdraw`, or `liquidate`; each call reaches `interest::global_sync`, then `accrue_step`, and panics in `scaled_to_original`. [10](#0-9) [1](#0-0) [4](#0-3) 
5. The repository test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` reproduces the failure and confirms that both withdrawal and repayment remain blocked afterward. [13](#0-12) [14](#0-13)
