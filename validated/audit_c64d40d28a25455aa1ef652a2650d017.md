### Title
Unprivileged flash loans can consume a receiver contract’s standing pool allowance - ([File: contracts/pool/src/ops/flash.rs](contracts/pool/src/ops/flash.rs))

### Summary
`flash_loan` lets any authenticated caller select an arbitrary WASM receiver, while the pool never requires that receiver to authorize the invocation. If the selected receiver already approved the pool, the pool pulls `principal + fee` through `transfer_from` after paying only `principal`, forcing the receiver to lose the fee. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
`Controller::flash_loan` authenticates only `caller`, verifies that the supplied `receiver` is a WASM contract, and forwards caller-controlled `receiver` and `data` to the pool. [1](#0-0)  The pool transfers `amount` to `receiver`, invokes its `execute_flash_loan` callback, requires the pool balance to remain at the post-payout level, and then checks `receiver -> pool` allowance before calling `transfer_from(pool, receiver, pool, principal + fee)`. [3](#0-2) [2](#0-1) 

Because `receiver` never authorizes the controller call and the pool accepts any pre-existing allowance, an attacker can weaponize a receiver contract’s standing approval. The receiver temporarily receives `principal`, but the pool then removes `principal + fee`, leaving the receiver `fee` poorer while the protocol books the fee as revenue. [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

### Impact Explanation
A receiver contract with a standing `receiver -> pool` allowance and a callable `execute_flash_loan` implementation can be forced to pay unsolicited flash-loan fees. Repeating the call while allowance and balance remain can drain the receiver’s balance down below one fee increment; the stolen fees become protocol revenue rather than being credited to the victim. [6](#0-5) [5](#0-4) 

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack is permissionless because the attacker supplies `caller`, `receiver`, `amount`, and `data` to `Controller::flash_loan`, while authorization is required only from `caller`. [7](#0-6)  Exploitation requires a flash-loan-enabled market with a nonzero fee and a WASM receiver that has both a surviving allowance to the pool and a callback that returns successfully; those preconditions make this a Medium-severity analog rather than an unrestricted drain of every approved token holder. [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8) 

### Recommendation
Do not treat a pre-existing `receiver -> pool` allowance as consent to a caller-selected flash loan. Snapshot the allowance before `invoke_receiver` and require the callback to increase it by at least `total_repayment`, or replace allowance collection with a repayment transfer explicitly authorized and performed by the receiver during its callback, while preserving the exact post-repayment balance check. [10](#0-9) [6](#0-5) 

### Proof of Concept
A victim flash-receiver contract grants the pool an allowance of at least `principal + fee`; an attacker then calls `Controller::flash_loan` with the victim as `receiver`. [1](#0-0) 

```rust
// Victim previously executed:
token.approve(victim, pool, principal + fee, live_until_ledger);

// Attacker executes:
controller.flash_loan(
    attacker,
    market,
    principal,
    victim_contract,
    attacker_controlled_data,
);
```

The pool sends `principal` to `victim_contract`, invokes its callback, verifies that the callback did not repay by transfer, observes the standing allowance, and pulls `principal + fee` from the victim. [3](#0-2) [2](#0-1)  The victim’s net balance change is `-fee`, and repeating the transaction consumes additional allowance and balance until the victim cannot cover another fee. [5](#0-4)

fn collect_repayment(
    env: &Env,
    asset: &token::Client,
    pool: &Address,
    receiver: &Address,
    terms: &FlashTerms,
) {
    assert_with_error!(
        env,
        asset.allowance(receiver, pool) >= terms.total_repayment,
        FlashLoanError::InvalidFlashloanRepay
    );
    asset.transfer_from(pool, receiver, pool, &terms.total_repayment);
    require_balance(env, asset, pool, terms.balance_after_repayment);
}
