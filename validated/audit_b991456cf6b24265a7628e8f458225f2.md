### Title
Phantom-order bid weight and pool depth are read from a single-block ERC-20/vault balance, letting a solver flash-borrow inventory to win the price median and inflate published liquidity - ([File: sdk/packages/sdk/src/protocols/intents/phantom-aggregation.ts])

### Summary
Phantom order bids are weighted, and pool depth/route data is derived, from `getTotalSolverBalance`, a single point-in-time `eth_call` read of a solver's ERC-20 balance plus ERC-4626 `maxWithdraw`, taken at `blockTag = "latest"` (chain head) with no requirement that the solver held or continues to hold that balance for any duration. [1](#0-0)  This is structurally the same flaw as the referenced MuteAmplifier report: a reward/weighting mechanism trusts a balance snapshot at one instant instead of requiring sustained custody, so a party can borrow funds for one block, get measured, and return them.

### Finding Description
`getTotalSolverBalance` reads `balanceOf`/`maxWithdraw` via `eth_call` at a caller-supplied `blockTag` that defaults to `"latest"` [2](#0-1) . `memoizedSolverBalance` explicitly documents that this is a point-in-time read shared across the run and makes no attempt to sample multiple blocks or a minimum-holding window: "the snapshot is a point-in-time read either way" [3](#0-2) .

This single balance is used for two impactful things inside `runAggregation`:
1. It directly becomes the bid's `weight` in `quotesByLeg`, which feeds `weightedMedian` to compute the leg's published `medianPrice`/`lowestPrice`/`highestPrice` [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) .
2. The same balance, swept across every configured token/chain via `sweepSolverLiquidity`, becomes `LpBalance`/`PoolBidder.liquidity` and therefore `PoolChainLiquidity.depth`, `PoolRoute.depth`, and `LiquidityPool.sellDepth`/`buyDepth` — the numbers "a taker sizes an order against" per the indexer's own documentation [6](#0-5) , and the weights recorded on `PoolBidder.liquidity` in `updateLiquidityPools` [7](#0-6) .

Because the read is unconditionally "latest" with no minimum holding period and no sampling of prior blocks (exactly the flaw the external report calls out — "the contract only checks the user's balance at [one point], the user doesn't have to hold that balance at any time but at [that] block"), an unprivileged solver can:
- Flash-borrow (or otherwise transiently acquire, e.g., via an atomic multicall/aggregator, an uncollateralized momentary transfer, or a cooperating counterparty) a large ERC-20/vault balance of the leg's output token just before/at the block the phantom bid window closes,
- Submit (or already have submitted) a signed bid whose weight is computed from that inflated balance,
- Return the borrowed funds in the same or next transaction, keeping none of it.

The aggregation and pool-fold code do nothing to average, minimum-sample, or delay the balance read — see the design notes acknowledging the read is deliberately just "the chain head" or a caller-supplied single block [8](#0-7) .

### Impact Explanation
- **Price manipulation**: `weightedMedian` in `runAggregation` weights each solver's quote by exactly this single-block balance [9](#0-8) . A solver who transiently inflates its balance can dominate a leg's weight and set the published `medianPrice` used by `PhantomOrderPriceSnapshotV2` [10](#0-9) , degrading the price real takers/solvers rely on for that pool/leg.
- **Depth/route forgery leading to undeliverable routes**: The same balance is swept into `PoolBidder.liquidity`, `PoolChainLiquidity.depth`, and `PoolRoute.depth` [7](#0-6) , which downstream consumers use to size and route real orders. A solver can inflate its advertised depth with borrowed capital it does not actually retain, then be selected/relied upon for a route it cannot actually fill once the borrowed funds are gone — matching the allowed impact category "a route unable to deliver messages."
- This directly parallels the referenced M-02 bug class: reward/weight is granted for capital held for a single block/read rather than genuinely and continuously held capital.

### Likelihood Explanation
The read is `eth_call` at `"latest"` by default, and nothing in `aggregatePhantomBids`/`runAggregation`/`foldInventoryReadings` requires a minimum holding duration, multiple historical samples, or the minimum-of-several-blocks approach the original report recommends. Any account able to construct a signed UserOp bid (an unprivileged solver) and momentarily acquire tokens (flash loan, cooperating lender, or an atomic bundle) can time its balance inflation to coincide with the aggregation's `eth_call`. The main uncertainty is the exact timing/atomicity guarantee between "when a solver borrows funds" and "when the SDK issues the `eth_call`" in production, since the aggregation runs asynchronously (indexer/relayer triggered) rather than being invoked in the same transaction as the borrow; a determined actor would need to either predict/trigger the aggregation window or hold the inflated balance for a short but non-atomic window (still far short of genuinely capitalizing the position), which is a plausible but not proven-in-repo attack window.

### Recommendation
- Do not weight bids or pool depth purely from a single "latest"-block `eth_call`. Sample the solver's balance at multiple blocks (e.g., several blocks preceding the bid/snapshot) and use the minimum, or require a minimum holding-period average, before crediting it as weight/liquidity, mirroring the original report's suggested mitigation.
- Alternatively, bind the balance read to the block the solver's UserOp/bid was actually signed or submitted (not the aggregation's `"latest"`), and additionally require that balance to still be present some fixed number of blocks later before it can back a published price or depth figure.
- Consider requiring collateral lock-up or a decaying reputation-weighted balance (time-weighted average balance) instead of an instantaneous snapshot for anything that influences `medianPrice`, `PoolBidder.liquidity`, or route depth.

### Proof of Concept
1. Solver `S` prepares a signed phantom bid UserOp for leg `L` (output token `T`) with `S`'s own true balance of `T` at 0.
2. Immediately before/at the block when the SDK's `aggregatePhantomBids`/`foldInventoryReadings` will call `getTotalSolverBalance(evmRpcUrl, chain, T, S, yieldVaults, "latest")`, `S` (or a colluding "lender") transfers a large amount of `T` into `S`'s account (flash loan, aggregator-routed transient transfer, or cooperating counterparty who can revoke it).
3. `getTotalSolverBalance` reads `balanceOf(S)` at `"latest"` and returns the inflated amount [2](#0-1) .
4. This inflated value becomes `weight` for `S`'s quote on leg `L`, biasing `weightedMedian`'s result toward `S`'s quoted price [9](#0-8) , and is also swept into `PoolBidder.liquidity`/`PoolChainLiquidity.depth`/`PoolRoute.depth` for `S` [7](#0-6) .
5. `S` returns the borrowed `T` in the same or next transaction, retaining none of it, yet the published price/depth for that leg/pool now reflects capital `S` never actually held for the aggregation to be meaningful.

/**
 * Sums the solver's redeemable balance of a single token on one chain: the raw ERC-20 balance plus
 * any ERC-4626 vault positions wrapping it.
 *
 * This is THE definition of "a solver's balance" — the periodic sweep and any per-event re-read
 * must use it rather than a bare `balanceOf`, because simplex funds fills straight out of a vault
 * inside the fill transaction: the wallet ends the block roughly where it started while
 * `maxWithdraw` is what actually moved, so a wallet-only read misses such a fill entirely.
 *
 * `blockTag` reads the balance as of a specific block ("0x..." or a tag), so a caller replaying an
 * event records the balance as of that event rather than stamping today's balance onto a
 * historical row. It defaults to the chain head, which is what a periodic sweep wants.
 */
export async function getTotalSolverBalance(
	evmRpcUrl: string,
	chain: string,
	token: string,
	solver: string,
	yieldVaults: YieldVaultMap,
	blockTag = "latest",
): Promise<bigint> {
	const padded = solver.replace("0x", "").padStart(64, "0")
	const raw = await ethCallUint(evmRpcUrl, token, `0x70a08231${padded}`, blockTag) // balanceOf(address)
	const vaults = yieldVaults[chain]?.[token.toLowerCase()] ?? []
	const vaultBalances = await Promise.all(
		vaults.map((v) => ethCallUint(evmRpcUrl, v, `0xce96cb77${padded}`, blockTag)), // maxWithdraw(address)
	)
	return vaultBalances.reduce((acc, b) => acc + b, raw)
}

	// so consumers cannot read an outlier bid as if it were a tradeable bound.

	// chain, so a zero-weight quote is one its solver cannot deliver at any price. Those are
	// dropped outright rather than merely down-weighted: they must not reach weightedMedian (with
	// nothing to weight by it picks a quote by position, letting whoever quotes the extreme set the
	// rate on zero capital), and they must not reach bidCount or `bidders`, where they would inflate
	// the solver count behind a price and mint zero-capacity PoolBidder/PoolRoute rows downstream.
	// A leg left with no backed quote at all is therefore absent entirely, exactly as if nobody had
	// quoted it — no snapshot, and its depth zeroes out downstream.
	const legs = [...quotesByLeg.entries()]
		.sort(([a], [b]) => a - b)
		.flatMap(([legIndex, { outputToken, quotes, bidders }]) => {
			// quotes and bidders are pushed in lockstep above, so the same predicate keeps them aligned.
			const backedQuotes = quotes.filter((quote) => quote.weight > 0n)
			const backedBidders = bidders.filter((bidder) => bidder.weight > 0n)
			if (backedQuotes.length === 0) {
				logger?.warn(
					{ commitment, chain, legIndex, outputToken, quotes: quotes.length },
					"Dropping phantom leg: no bidder holds the output token on this chain, so no quote is backed",
				)
				return []
			}

			const medianPrice = weightedMedian(backedQuotes)
			return [
				{
					legIndex,
					outputToken,
					lowestPrice: medianPrice,
					highestPrice: medianPrice,
					medianPrice,
					bidCount: backedQuotes.length,
					bidders: backedBidders,

# 2026-09-01 — Refresh a pool's LP balances on every order fill

A pool's published depth is the sum of its bidders' output-token inventory, measured when the last phantom bid
window closed. A fill spends some of that inventory, so between windows the depth advertises capacity the
solvers no longer hold — and the depth is what a taker sizes an order against.

`OrderFilled` now re-reads the balances of every LP recorded as backing the pools the fill traded through and
republishes the depths from them: `PoolBidder.liquidity`, `PoolChainLiquidity.depth`/`bidCount`/unrestricted
slice, `PoolRoute.depth`/`bidCount`, and the pool's own `sellDepth`/`buyDepth`/bid counts. A bidder left holding
nothing loses its row, so the "every row is a bidder with capacity" invariant survives, and the routes it alone
declared go with it. Rates are untouched — nothing here observes a quote — though a pool's merged rate can still
move, because the chains are weighted by the depths that just changed.
```

# 2026-09-01 — `blockTag` is a parameter of the balance read, and a per-chain map on the memo (#1159)

Chosen: `getTotalSolverBalance` takes a `blockTag` defaulting to `"latest"`, and `memoizedSolverBalance` takes a
`Record<chain, blockTag>`.

Alternative rejected — a single `blockTag` on the memo. A refresh reaches across every chain a pool is quoted on,
while the event that triggered it happened on one; block numbers are per chain, so one tag applied to all of them
would read some other chain at an arbitrary point in its history. The map pins the event's chain and leaves the
rest at the head, which is the only correct reading available.

Alternative rejected — leave every read at the head. Simpler, and it is what a periodic sweep wants, but a
per-event re-read at the head is not replayable: reindexing an old fill would stamp today's balance onto it.
```
