### Title
Anyone can mint relayer reputation to an arbitrary address by front-running valid GetResponse proofs - (File: `modules/pallets/state-coprocessor/src/impls.rs`)

### Summary
`pallet_state_coprocessor::handle_unsigned` accepts `GetRequestsWithProof { requests, source, response, address }` from any origin (`ensure_none`) [1](#0-0) . `handle_get_requests` cryptographically verifies the source membership proof and destination state proof, then mints `ReputationAsset` to `address` — a caller-supplied field that is never checked against any signature or the actual submitter of the transaction [2](#0-1) . This mirrors the vp-toolkit bug class: the cryptographic proof (VP signature / state proof) is verified correctly, but the "owner" field embedded in the payload (`credentialSubject.id` / `address`) is never checked to belong to the party that actually produced the proof.

### Finding Description
`GetRequestsWithProof.address` is documented as the "Address that should be credited with fees" [3](#0-2) . The dispatchable is unsigned (`ensure_none(origin)?`), so the extrinsic sender's identity carries no authorization weight at all — the only actor-identifying field in the whole payload is `address`, and it is fully attacker-chosen [4](#0-3) . After proof verification succeeds, `total_bytes` (computed from the verified `GetResponse`s) is multiplied by the governance-set `MintPerByte` rate and minted directly to `address` with no signature, no binding to `msg.sender`/tx signer, and no relation to who actually did the work of fetching/assembling the state proofs [5](#0-4) .

Contrast this with the sibling reward paths in the codebase, which do bind the beneficiary cryptographically:
- `pallet-messaging-incentives::relayer_for` recovers the reward recipient from the sr25519 signature on the ISMP `Message.signer` field rather than trusting a caller-supplied address [6](#0-5) .
- `pallet-ismp-relayer`'s outbound-delivery-reward claim explicitly recovers the signer and checks it against the address proven in the receipt, rejecting mismatches (`OutboundRequestSignerMismatch`) [7](#0-6) .

The state-coprocessor path has no analogous check: it verifies that the GET requests/response data is real, but not that `address` is the entity entitled to the reward for delivering that data.

### Impact Explanation
Any unprivileged party who observes a valid, unclaimed `(requests, source, response)` triple (which is inherently public — the proofs are freely generated from public chain state, as documented in `docs/content/developers/explore/nexus/relayer.mdx:1-32`) can resubmit it via `Ismp::stateCoprocessor.handleUnsigned` with an `address` of their own choosing before the original submitter's transaction lands, or simply always frontrun/replace the field regardless of who assembled the proof. This diverts the `ReputationMinted` reward stream to an arbitrary attacker-controlled account. Because `MintPerByte` is governance-settable and can be nonzero on a live network, this is a mechanism for continuously siphoning the messaging/bandwidth incentive reward pool away from the relayers who actually do the GET-request delivery work — a direct, repeatable theft of protocol-designated rewards with no cryptographic barrier.

### Likelihood Explanation
High. The call is unsigned and its `address` field carries no authorization check whatsoever; exploitation requires only observing calldata (mempool or block data) of any legitimate `handleUnsigned` submission and resubmitting a variant with an attacker's address before it lands, or an attacker running their own bandwidth-purchasing GET flow and simply setting `address` to themselves regardless of who performed off-chain relaying labor. No privileged position, race against consensus, or cryptographic forgery is needed.

### Recommendation
Bind the credited `address` to a verifiable identity, mirroring `pallet-messaging-incentives::relayer_for` and the outbound-delivery-reward signer check: either require `address` to be authenticated by a signature over the batch's commitments (recovered and checked, not trusted), or eliminate the free-form `address` field entirely and mint to the actual extrinsic submitter (though this call is currently `ensure_none`, so this would require converting to a signed origin or checking a signature attached to the payload).

### Proof of Concept
1. Observe a pending or recently-submitted `stateCoprocessor.handleUnsigned(GetRequestsWithProof { requests, source, response, address: legit_relayer })` in the mempool/block — this data is inherently public since it embeds freely-obtainable chain state proofs.
2. Submit a new unsigned extrinsic with the identical `requests`, `source`, `response` proofs, but `address` replaced with the attacker's own 32-byte account, ahead of (or instead of) the legitimate submission.
3. `handle_get_requests` verifies the (unchanged, valid) membership and state proofs, computes `total_bytes` from the responses, and mints `rate * total_bytes` reputation tokens directly to the attacker's `address` [5](#0-4) , with the legitimate relayer's version now rejected as a duplicate response (`Error::DuplicateResponse`) since the response receipt has already been stored under the attacker's submission.
