### Title
Bad-debt supply-index floor clamp leaves wiped suppliers with phantom claims that drain fresh deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` socializes bad debt by scaling down `supply_index`, but clamps the result at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`) instead of zeroing or burning the affected scaled positions. After a full wipeout (bad debt ≥ total supplied value), every supplier's scaled balance still unscales to a positive claim backed by zero cash. When a fresh depositor adds cash to the market, the stale positions can `withdraw` real tokens against that cash, stealing the fresh deposit. This is the use-after-free analog of JLSEC-2026-346: a logically-freed (fully written-down) supply claim remains usable.

### Finding Description
- `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89`: `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` caps the write-down at `total_supplied_value`, computes a `reduction_factor` that can be 0, then clamps `new_supply_index` up to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` via `.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW))`. Total scaled supply (`cache.supplied()`) is left untouched — no shares are burned.
- `contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:111-119`: `gate_and_debit` enforces only `require_reserves(net_transfer)` (cash sufficiency) and `require_supply_for_debt`.
- `contracts/pool/src/guards.rs:69-73`: `require_supply_for_debt` only fires when `supplied == 0 && borrowed != 0`. In the wipeout scenario `supplied` is nonzero (the phantom shares) and `borrowed` was burned to zero, so the guard passes.
- Therefore a wiped supplier's `unscale_supply_floor(scaled)` returns a positive phantom claim; `resolve_withdrawal` mints a nonzero gross, `require_reserves` sees fresh cash, and the transfer succeeds.

The repo's own unit tests demonstrate both mechanics:
- `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-428` (`test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard`): after wipeout-clamp, the old position's stranded claim `pays out real tokens` equal to a fresh deposit, leaving `cash < fresh_claim`.
- `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:431-494` (`test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard`): seizure residual path, same outcome — "Alice extracts exactly Bob's fresh deposit".

The only thing standing between these tests and production is `require_reserves` checking raw cash — which is satisfied the moment any fresh supply lands.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. Any supplier holding scaled shares in a wiped market retains a phantom claim equal to `scaled × RAY/1000`. Each such withdrawal is paid out of subsequent depositors' cash, making the pool books insolvent (`total owed > cash`) and leaving last-out suppliers unable to withdraw. Reachable by an unprivileged address via `controller.withdraw` after the market wipeout; no privileged role needed.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a market to reach full bad-debt wipeout: a borrower position must become bad debt at least equal to total supplied value and be cleaned via `clean_bad_debt` / seizure while residual supply shares remain on the books (e.g., other suppliers, revenue shares, or dust positions the cleanup doesn't burn). Small or thin markets make this realistic — a single under-collateralized borrower wiped out faster than liquidators react (oracle jump, thin liquidity) produces exactly this state. The attacker simply needs to hold any supply position in that market before the wipeout, or even acquire one cheaply, then wait for fresh deposits.

### Recommendation
In `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, when the write-down caps at `total_supplied_value` (full wipeout), do not clamp to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` — instead set the index to zero and treat all remaining scaled supply as burned (or require the caller to burn total `supplied` in the same op). Alternatively, in `withdraw::gate_and_debit` / `resolve_withdrawal`, add `require_backed_market`-style accounting so a withdrawal cannot pay gross beyond the position's pro-rata share of `cash + debt` backing, or burn wiped-out scaled positions during `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` so no phantom claims survive.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice supplies X of token T in hub market; Bob borrows against a different collateral; oracle price crashes / interest accrues so Bob's debt becomes bad debt ≥ total T supplied.
2. Anyone calls `controller.clean_bad_debt` (or liquidation seizure path) → pool `seize` burns Bob's debt shares and calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` → `reduction_factor = 0`, `new_supply_index` clamps to `RAY/1000`; Alice's scaled shares remain.
3. Carol supplies fresh cash C to market T (normal `controller.supply`).
4. Alice calls `controller.withdraw(T, i128::MAX)` → `resolve_withdrawal` computes gross = `unscale_supply_floor(alice_scaled)` > 0 → `require_reserves` passes (cash = C) → `require_supply_for_debt` passes (supplied ≠ 0) → Alice receives Carol's deposit.
5. Carol's claim now exceeds pool cash: `cash < unscale_supply_floor(carol_scaled)` — funds stolen / pool insolvent.

This mirrors the harness test at `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:414-426` where `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, old_scaled)` yields `gross == fresh_cash` and leaves `cash < fresh_claim`.
