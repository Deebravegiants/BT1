### Title
Stale oracle reads survive governance execution inside `flash_position`, allowing debt mispricing - (File: contracts/controller/src/strategies/flash_position.rs)

### Summary
`flash_position` prefetches debt and collateral prices before invoking the attacker-controlled receiver, then performs collateral deposit and final solvency checks after the callback. [1](#0-0)  Because `Context` only fetches missing prices and retains previously cached feeds, a ready governance operation that reprices an asset can be executed inside the callback without invalidating the stale price used by the final risk check. [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
The flow adds the debt asset and all declared collateral assets to the price-prefetch list, invokes `prefetch_strategy_prices`, mints debt into controller custody, forwards it to `receiver`, and invokes `execute_flash_position` under `with_flash_guard`. [3](#0-2)  After the receiver returns, the controller measures declared collateral deltas, deposits them, and runs `strategy_finalize`, which performs the final LTV and health-factor checks. [4](#0-3) 

The account risk calculation reads `cached_price` for each collateral and debt asset, so its result is based on the pre-callback oracle snapshot rather than aggregator state at finalization. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

A ready governance operation can target the price aggregator through `price_aggregator_operation`, and ready operations are executable by an arbitrary caller when `executor` is `None`. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7)  Executing such an operation from the receiver changes the price used by later transactions, but not the price retained in this invocation’s `Context`.

### Impact Explanation
An attacker can make the controller finalize a position using a pre-change debt or collateral price after governance has already replaced that oracle. [9](#0-8)  If a ready oracle operation reprices the debt asset upward or the collateral asset downward, the minted debt can remain on an account whose fresh health factor is below one or already insolvent. [10](#0-9) 

Because the borrowed tokens are transferred to the receiver before the callback, the receiver can retain part of them and return only enough collateral to satisfy the stale valuation. [11](#0-10)  The resulting underwater account is later liquidated against the corrected price, leaving residual bad debt to suppliers and producing protocol insolvency.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation requires a price-changing aggregator operation to already be scheduled and within its execution window, plus sufficient market liquidity for the flash-funded swap. [7](#0-6)  Once those conditions exist, the ordering is deterministic: the receiver can call governance after the controller’s price prefetch and before finalization in every attempt. [12](#0-11) 

The attacker only needs an unprivileged account, a deployed receiver contract, and the public `execute` path for a ready operation; no controller reentry is required because governance and the price aggregator are separate call targets. [13](#0-12) [8](#0-7) 

### Recommendation
Invalidate or refetch all externally controlled inputs after `invoke_receiver` returns, before collecting deposits or running `strategy_finalize`. [14](#0-13) 

In particular, clear `token_prices`, spoke configuration, hub checks, and stale pool snapshots while preserving pool-returned indexes, usage deltas, and event buffers; alternatively, record an oracle-configuration epoch before borrowing and reject the call if that epoch changes during the callback. [15](#0-14)  The same post-callback invalidation should be applied to every strategy path that prefetches prices before an external receiver or router call. [16](#0-15) 

### Proof of Concept
1. Wait for governance to have a ready operation that changes the debt asset’s oracle from a lower price source to a higher price source.
2. Deploy receiver contract `R` whose `execute_flash_position` calls `governance.execute(None, price_aggregator, function, args, predecessor, salt)` for that ready operation and then returns the declared collateral to the controller.
3. Call `flash_position(caller=attacker, account_id=0, spoke_id=S, mode=Multiply, debt=D, amount=200_000, receiver=R, data=..., collaterals=[(C, 125_000)], refund_assets=[])`; `account_id=0` creates an attacker-owned account. [17](#0-16) 
4. Assume the controller prefetches `D` at `$0.50`, while the ready operation reprices `D` to `$1.00`; the 200,000-unit debt is therefore treated as `$100,000` during finalization instead of `$200,000`.
5. During the callback, execute the oracle update, buy or supply collateral `C` worth `$125,000`, and keep the remaining `$75,000` of borrowed `D`.
6. The controller measures and deposits `C`, then evaluates the `$125,000` collateral and `$100,000` stale debt value using the cached feeds, allowing a nominal health factor of `1.0` at an 80% liquidation threshold. [18](#0-17) 
7. After the transaction, fresh prices value the same account as `$125,000` collateral against `$200,000` debt, so liquidation cannot recover the retained `$75,000`; the deficit becomes supplier bad debt.
