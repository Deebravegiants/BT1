### Title
Attacker pins utilization at `max_utilization` via borrow, freezing all withdrawals in the market - (File: contracts/pool/src/guards.rs)

### Summary
The Origami finding is a cap-based exit DoS: an attacker fills a rolling-period exit cap via a same-transaction invest/exit, blocking legitimate `exitToToken` calls. XOXNO Lending has no rolling exit cap, but it has an equivalent absolute exit gate: `require_utilization_below_max` is applied to non-liquidation withdrawals as a **post-state** check. Burning supply shares raises utilization, so an attacker who pushes a market's utilization to exactly `max_utilization` makes every subsequent withdrawal — of any size — revert with `UtilizationAboveMax`. The freeze persists until the attacker repays or is liquidated, and unlike the Origami bucket cap it does not decay on its own. Only entry caps exist for exits: "Exits consume no cap" (docs/reference/invariants.md), so the utilization ceiling is the sole blocker.

### Finding Description
`require_utilization_below_max` computes `ceiled_debt_value / floored_supply_value` and requires it to be `<= params.max_utilization` (contracts/pool/src/guards.rs:19-34). It fires on `withdraw` (non-liquidation) via `gate_and_debit` (contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:111-119) and on `borrow`/`create_strategy` via `mint_debt` (contracts/pool/src/ops/borrow.rs:63-79).

Two properties make this exploitable:

1. **A borrow may land exactly on the ceiling.** The check is `<=`, confirmed by `withdraw_that_lands_exactly_on_max_utilization_passes_and_one_unit_more_fails` (tests/test-harness/tests/controller/max_utilization.rs:161-184).
2. **Withdrawals raise utilization.** Burning supply shrinks the denominator, so once utilization equals `max_utilization`, any positive withdrawal fails. The pool README states this explicitly: "once utilization reaches the cap no withdrawal of any size passes… It is released by repayment, or consumed by liquidation" (contracts/pool/README.md:292-297).

**Attack path (single unprivileged address):**
1. `controller.supply` collateral (or `multiply`/`flash_position` to leverage it) into any listed hub/spoke market.
2. `controller.borrow` on the target asset, sized so post-state utilization lands exactly on `max_utilization`. `mint_debt` admits the boundary value.
3. Every victim `controller.withdraw` on that market reverts `UtilizationAboveMax`, because any burn of supply pushes the ratio above the cap. `claim_revenue` is blocked identically.
4. The attacker holds the open borrow; the freeze lasts until they choose to `repay` (releasing it at will) or a third party liquidates the position — which requires waiting for the HF to decay below 1 or paying to push it there.

`require_reserves` and the 2% `LIQUIDATION_BUFFER_BPS` (`require_liquidation_buffer`, guards.rs:39-47) bound how much cash can be drawn, but they don't prevent reaching the utilization ceiling: the attacker can supply a large amount and borrow up to `max_utilization` of it while leaving cash above the buffer, or simply take over most of a thin market's supply first.

### Impact Explanation
Temporary freezing of funds: all suppliers in the targeted market are unable to withdraw any amount for the duration of the attack, and protocol revenue claims are blocked. The attacker controls the release condition (repayment), unlike a rolling window that decays automatically. Interest keeps accruing on the attacker's debt, which pushes utilization further above the cap, so the freeze is self-sustaining — even dust withdrawals continue to revert.

### Likelihood Explanation
Fully reachable by an unprivileged address using in-scope entrypoints (`supply`, `borrow`; optionally `multiply`/`flash_position` for leverage). No flash loan is needed — the borrowed position persists across transactions. Cost is the collateral posted plus borrow interest, and the attacker risks liquidation only if their HF decays; with a healthy HF on a different collateral market the position can be held indefinitely. Attack is cheapest on thin markets where `max_utilization` of supply is a modest absolute amount.

### Recommendation
Apply the utilization ceiling to withdrawals on a **relative** basis rather than absolute: allow a withdrawal if pre-state utilization is already at/above `max_utilization` and the withdrawal does not worsen it beyond the current ratio (i.e., only block withdrawals that *raise* utilization past the cap), or exempt withdrawals up to the pro-rata utilization-neutral amount. Alternatively, exempt full exits, or add a per-account cooldown/exit queue. The asymmetry should mirror the documented liquidation bypass: exits that are utilization-neutral or utilization-improving should not be gated by a ceiling the user did not create.

Mirroring `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/max_utilization.rs:161-184`:

```rust
let mut t = LendingTest::new()
    .standard_two_asset()
    .with_market_params("USDC", |p| p.max_utilization = RAY * 80 / 100)
    .with_min_borrow_collateral_disabled()
    .build();
t.supply(VICTIM, "USDC", 100_000.0);

// Attacker: supply ETH collateral, borrow USDC to land exactly on 80% util.
t.supply(ATTACKER, "ETH", enough_collateral);
t.borrow(ATTACKER, "USDC", 80_000.0);   // util == max_utilization, admitted

// Victim exit of any size now reverts.
assert_contract_error(
    t.try_withdraw_raw(VICTIM, "USDC", 1),
    errors::UTILIZATION_ABOVE_MAX,
);
// Freeze persists until ATTACKER repays or is liquidated.
```
