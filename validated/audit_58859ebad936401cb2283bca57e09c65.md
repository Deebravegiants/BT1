### Title
Supply-index floor clamp leaves stranded "phantom" supply claims that drain fresh deposits after a bad-debt wipeout - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` socializes unpayable debt by scaling the supply index down, but clamps the result upward at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY / 1000`) instead of letting it reach zero. When the written-down debt exceeds total supplied value, every pre-existing supply share retains a positive floor-valued claim that is larger than its pro-rata share of any real backing. Because `withdraw` only enforces `require_reserves` (cash sufficiency), `require_utilization_below_max`, and `require_supply_for_debt` — never "does this claim exceed the backing it should have" — a holder of such a stranded claim can withdraw real tokens deposited later by a fresh supplier, stealing them.

### Finding Description
The bug class of CVE-2018-7053 (a resource that should have been freed/zeroed is left referenceable and is later used) maps here onto supply shares that should have been written to zero but are kept alive by the non-zero index floor.

In `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` (contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89), when `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, `remaining` is zero, so `reduction_factor` is zero and `new_supply_index` is zero — but the code then applies `.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW))`, resurrecting every outstanding scaled supply share to a claim of `scaled_amount × RAY/1000`:

```rust
let new_supply_index = cache
    .supply_index()
    .mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);
cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
```

The pool's own test suite pins the consequence in `test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` and `test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` (contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:317-370, 373-495): after a wipeout clamp, a stranded position's `unscale_supply_floor` claim is positive; once any fresh cash enters the market, `resolve_withdrawal` + `require_reserves` lets the stranded holder extract exactly the fresh deposit, leaving the honest supplier's claim unbacked.

The withdrawal path (`contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:111-118`, `gate_and_debit`) performs no per-claim backing check — `require_reserves` only checks `cash >= draw`, so the phantom claim is paid out of other users' money. The asymmetry is even codified: `require_backed_market` gates only `supply` entry, not withdrawal exit (contracts/pool/README.md:284-288).

Reachability: utilization caps limit debt *at mint time*, but the borrow index grows without bound afterward, so outstanding debt value can exceed total supplied value through interest accrual alone; a subsequent liquidation or `clean_bad_debt`/`force_socialize_bad_debt` then clamps the index to the floor. The stranded holder calls `withdraw` through the controller after any new `supply` (which passes `require_backed_market` once `floor(supply_value) <= cash + debt` — trivially true when the floor-valued supply is tiny relative to fresh cash) lands in the market.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds: the holder of a wiped position receives a real payout minted from nothing — paid out of a later supplier's deposit. The fresh supplier is left with an unbacked claim, i.e., protocol insolvency. Both are in-scope impacts.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a market to suffer a near-total bad-debt wipeout (bad debt ≥ total supplied value), which needs debt to grow past collateral plus liquidation activity — plausible on stale/mani­pulated price moves or long-unutilized markets where interest compounds past 100% utilization. After that, the theft needs only one new deposit into the zombie market and one `withdraw` call. The behavior is deterministic once the floor clamp triggers; the pool's own tests reproduce it exactly.

### Recommendation
When `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, write the supply index to the true computed value (zero) or burn/zero the outstanding supply accounting rather than clamping the index up to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. Alternatively, gate withdrawals on per-market backing: skip `require_reserves`-only settlement when the market has a floor-clamped index, or track a "wiped" flag that voids pre-wipeout claims before admitting fresh cash.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker supplies collateral on spoke A and borrows asset X to near the utilization cap; interest accrues (or a price move) pushes X debt above total X supplied value.
2. Permissionless `clean_bad_debt` (controller `clean_bad_debt(caller, account_id)`) fires: `execute_bad_debt_cleanup` → pool `seize` → `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps `supply_index` to `RAY/1000`, leaving victim supplier V's shares with a positive stranded claim.
3. Victim B supplies `c` units of X; `require_backed_market` passes because the floor-valued total claim is small relative to the fresh cash.
4. V (or the attacker, if V's position was the liquidated one's surviving collateral-side claim, or any holder of pre-wipeout scaled shares) calls `withdraw` for the full position; `resolve_withdrawal` returns `gross = c`, `require_reserves` passes, and `debit_cash` pays out the entire fresh deposit.
5. B's claim `unscale_supply_floor(scaled_b) = c` now exceeds `cash = 0` — B's funds are stolen.

This mirrors the repo's own `test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` (contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:317-370), which asserts `gross == c` — the stranded position drains exactly the fresh deposit — demonstrating the full arithmetic end to end.
