### Title
Attacker-owned Aquarius LP dust collateral leg can be pushed below `min_pool_value_wad` to fail-close all health-factor paths, blocking liquidation and bad-debt cleanup - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs)

### Summary
XOXNO Lending prices every supply and debt leg of an account when computing health factor (`calculate_account_risk_totals_body` iterates `supply_positions` and `borrow_positions` and calls `cache.cached_price`). For a market priced via the Aquarius LP provider, `aquarius::read` returns `OracleError::InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` whenever the pool's total value in WAD falls below the configured `lp.min_pool_value_wad`. An unprivileged borrower can supply a dust amount of an Aquarius LP share as one collateral leg, then withdraw their own liquidity from that Aquarius pool (or let natural withdrawal do it) so the oracle read fails. Because risk totals are computed over *all* collateral keys, every liquidation, `clean_bad_debt`, and `update_account_threshold` touching that account reverts — the attacker's real debt cannot be seized and accrues into bad debt that socialization cannot clean up. [1](#0-0) 

### Finding Description
- `aquarius::read` computes `pool_value_wad = price_wad * total_shares / share_unit` and returns `Err(OracleError::InsufficientAquariusLiquidity)` when `pool_value_wad < lp.min_pool_value_wad`. Any LP token holder can reduce `total_shares`/`reserves` below that bound by redeeming their own share — no privilege required. [2](#0-1) 
- The controller's risk engine loads markets for the union of all supply and debt keys and reads `cache.cached_price(&hub_asset.asset)` per position with no error isolation; a single failing feed makes `calculate_account_risk_totals` panic for the whole account. [3](#0-2) 
- Every gated verb (`liquidate`, `withdraw`, `borrow`, `clean_bad_debt`, `update_account_threshold`) computes risk totals for the acting/target account, so a dust LP leg poisons them all. The project's own threat model acknowledges this: "an indebted borrower can add a dust leg of any listed collateral and choose which feed outage shields the account… The same leg blocks bad-debt cleanup and force-socialization." [4](#0-3) 
- Unlike a third-party feed going stale, the Aquarius outage is *self-inflicted and persistent*: the attacker controls the pool's liquidity and can keep the price unresolvable indefinitely, or restore it only when they want to exit.

### Impact Explanation
Protocol insolvency / theft of supplier funds. The attacker borrows up to their limit against collateral including the dust Aquarius LP leg, causes the collateral value to deteriorate (or simply lets interest accrue), and then suppresses the LP oracle by draining the pool below `min_pool_value_wad`. Liquidators cannot `liquidate` the account — the HF check reverts — and `clean_bad_debt` cannot socialize the growing bad debt for the same reason. Interest compounds on unseizable debt until it exceeds remaining collateral value; suppliers bear the loss. Additionally, all other users who hold that LP token as collateral suffer temporary freezing (cannot withdraw or have their positions liquidated), which qualifies as temporary freezing of funds. [5](#0-4) 

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate-to-high for any listed Aquarius LP collateral market. The attacker needs only (a) an existing LP position in the targeted Aquarius pool, (b) a dust supply of that LP share token into their lending account (supply requires no price oracle and no collateral quality gate beyond listing), and (c) enough pool share to push value under `min_pool_value_wad` — on thin pools this is cheap, and the attacker recovers most of the withdrawn liquidity's underlying value rather than burning it. The position limits (5 markets per side) do not help since one dust leg suffices. The attacker loses nothing while the shield is up, since liquidation is precisely what they are preventing. [6](#0-5) 

### Recommendation
Isolate per-asset price failures in the risk-totals loop instead of failing the whole account. Concretely: in `calculate_account_risk_totals_body` and `sum_debt_usd_loaded`, treat an unresolvable *supply* price as value zero for collateral-weighting purposes (conservative for HF — it can only make the account look *worse*, never better, so it cannot be used to dodge liquidation) while still failing closed for *debt* legs. Alternatively, allow `liquidate`/`clean_bad_debt` to proceed on legs whose prices resolve and skip seizure of the unpriced leg, since `SeizeMode`/pro-rata planning already iterates legs independently. Setting `min_pool_value_wad = 0` for Aquarius sources would also close the self-inflicted knob, at the cost of accepting pricing on very thin pools.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance has listed an Aquarius LP share `LPX` (constant-product pool `P` with configured `min_pool_value_wad = V`) as a collateral market on hub H.
2. Attacker (a large LP in `P`) calls `supply(caller=attacker, account_id=0, payments=[(H:LPX, dust)])` — creating an account with a tiny LPX supply leg plus meaningful collateral in other assets.
3. Attacker calls `borrow` up to the account limit in asset `D`.
4. Market moves / interest accrues until HF approaches 1. Attacker calls `P.withdraw(attacker, share_amount, ...)` on Aquarius, redeeming enough of their own liquidity that `price_wad * total_shares / share_unit < V`.
5. Any liquidator calling `liquidate(caller, account_id=attacker_id, payments=[(H:D, amt)], ...)` reverts: `calculate_account_risk_totals_body` hits `cache.cached_price(LPX)` → aggregator `read` → `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` → panic. Same for `clean_bad_debt` and `update_account_threshold(account_ids=[attacker_id])`.
6. Attacker's debt compounds unseizable; when the dust is strategically large relative to pool depth, the attacker may even restore liquidity later, repay at a discount of their choosing, or let the position decay into permanent bad debt borne by suppliers.
