### Title
Bad-debt socialization clamps `supply_index` upward, creating stranded claims that can steal future deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
Permissionless `clean_bad_debt` calls pool `seize_positions` for every remaining collateral and debt position. For a borrow-side entry, the pool computes the unpaid debt, reduces `supply_index`, and burns the debt. When the bad debt equals or exceeds the total supplied value, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps the resulting index to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead of reducing it to zero or rejecting the state transition. Because the corresponding `supplied` shares remain outstanding, wiped suppliers retain claims equal to approximately 0.1% of their original assets. The seize path commits without a post-transition supply/backing guard, so those residual claims can withdraw newly deposited cash.

### Finding Description
`Controller::clean_bad_debt` is permissionless and delegates to `process_clean_bad_debt`. [1](#0-0) 

The account is admitted when debt exceeds collateral and collateral is no greater than the configured USD dust threshold. [2](#0-1) 

Cleanup submits deposit-side entries followed by borrow-side entries to the pool. [3](#0-2) 

For a borrow-side entry, the pool calculates `bad_debt`, calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, burns the debt shares, and commits the market. [4](#0-3) 

The bad-debt routine caps the loss at the current total supplied value and computes a proportional reduction factor. If the loss consumes all supplied value, `new_supply_index` is zero, but the code stores `max(new_supply_index, SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)`. [5](#0-4) 

This raises the exchange rate from zero to `RAY / 1000` while leaving `supplied` unchanged. Every pre-wipeout supply share therefore retains a residual claim even though the loss should have consumed the whole supplied value. `ops::seize::apply` then calls `cache.commit()` without running `require_backed_market`, `require_supply_for_debt`, or an equivalent check that would reject the inconsistent state. [6](#0-5) 

Withdrawal converts those old shares through the floored supply index, checks only current cash, utilization, and non-zero supply, and debits cash for the payout. [7](#0-6) 

The test suite explicitly demonstrates that the floor clamp leaves a phantom claim and lets it consume fresh cash deposited afterward. [8](#0-7) 

### Impact Explanation
An unprivileged account can trigger `clean_bad_debt` on an insolvent account whose remaining collateral is under the dust cap. If the bad debt is at least the market’s total supplied value, the market commits with a floored nonzero supply index while all old supply shares remain recorded.

The resulting residual claims are backed by no original assets. Once new cash enters the market, holders of the wiped supply can withdraw up to that phantom residual value and drain the new deposit. This is theft of user funds and leaves the fresh depositor under-collateralized; if enough phantom claims exist relative to fresh liquidity, withdrawals freeze or exhaust the market.

### Likelihood Explanation
The trigger path is permissionless: `clean_bad_debt` only requires caller authentication, open debt, insolvency, and collateral at or below the dust threshold. No privileged action is required.

The severe condition requires bad debt to reach or exceed total supplied value in the debt asset. That can arise when a small or heavily utilized market accrues debt faster than supplier claims, or when collateral value falls sharply until the account is insolvent and dust-capped. Markets with low supplied value are especially exposed because reaching the wipeout threshold requires less bad debt.

### Recommendation
Do not clamp a fully consumed supply index upward while preserving the same `supplied` share count. Either:

- allow the index to become zero and burn or otherwise settle all outstanding supply shares atomically; or
- make `seize_positions` reject a borrow-side write-down that would exhaust total supplied value unless the operation also removes all residual supply claims; and
- run an explicit post-seize consistency guard, such as requiring `supplied == 0` when the effective supplied value is zero or requiring claims not to exceed backing after accounting for burned debt.

Add an end-to-end regression test that performs controller `clean_bad_debt`, deposits fresh cash, and verifies that pre-wipeout supply cannot withdraw from it.

### Proof of Concept
1. Create or select a hub market with existing supply shares.
2. Open an account whose debt position grows or becomes priced such that `total_debt > total_collateral` and `total_collateral <= BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD`.
3. Ensure the account’s remaining debt in that market is at least the market’s total supplied value.
4. Call:

   `controller.clean_bad_debt(caller, victim_account_id)`

   The controller emits deposit-side and borrow-side `PoolSeizeEntry` values. The pool’s borrow-side leg calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, which clamps the zeroed index to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` while `supplied` remains unchanged.

5. Supply fresh assets into the market.
6. Withdraw using the old supply position. The withdrawal resolves a nonzero amount from the floored index and pays from the fresh deposit, even though the socialization step should have consumed the old supply claim entirely.

```rust
pub(crate) fn apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache: &mut Cache, bad_debt: Ray) {
    let total_supplied_value = cache.supplied().mul(cache.env(), cache.supply_index());

    if total_supplied_value == Ray::ZERO {
        return;
    }

    let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
    let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);

    let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
    let new_supply_index = cache
        .supply_index()
        .mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);

    cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
```
