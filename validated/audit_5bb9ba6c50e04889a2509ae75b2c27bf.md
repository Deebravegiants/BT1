### Title
Bad-debt wipeout leaves phantom supply claims that freeze withdrawals and block new supply - ([File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs])

### Summary
When a borrow position becomes socializable bad debt, `clean_bad_debt` permits an unprivileged caller to burn the debt while `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps a fully wiped market's supply index upward to `RAY / 1000` instead of zero. The surviving scaled supply shares therefore retain a positive claim even though their economic value was fully socialized away. This creates a persistent backing shortfall: ordinary withdrawals fail once accounting cash is exhausted, while new supply reverts through `PoolInsolvent` until someone recapitalizes the market.

### Finding Description
`clean_bad_debt` eventually executes `execute_bad_debt_cleanup`, which sends every remaining borrow position to `pool_seize_positions_call` as `AccountPositionType::Borrow`. [1](#0-0)  On the pool side, a borrow-side seizure converts the position's scaled debt into ceiling-rounded underlying debt, passes it to `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, and then burns the debt shares. [2](#0-1) 

The bad-debt write-down caps the reduction at total supplied value, but then clamps the resulting index to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. [3](#0-2)  Consequently, a complete socialized wipeout cannot reduce `supply_index` to zero; every old scaled supply position retains approximately `supply_index / RAY` of its prior units, or about 0.1% at the floor. [4](#0-3) 

Those residual claims enter the market-wide backing calculation, which compares floored supplier claims with accounting cash plus ceiling-rounded outstanding debt. [5](#0-4)  New supply requires `backing_shortfall == 0`, so the phantom claims make subsequent deposits revert after available cash is insufficient to back them. [6](#0-5)  Withdrawals also cannot proceed once their resolved gross amount exceeds accounting cash because `require_reserves` is enforced before the cash debit. [7](#0-6)  The recovery mechanism is `recapitalize`, which credits only `min(amount, backing_shortfall)` as cash and refunds the remainder. [8](#0-7) 

### Impact Explanation
A complete bad-debt cleanup can leave a market with no collectible debt, little or no cash, and positive residual supplier claims created solely by the index floor. Suppliers' withdrawals are temporarily frozen once those claims exceed accounting cash, and suppliers cannot recapitalize the market by supplying normally because `supply` rejects the positive backing shortfall. Recovery requires an outside token transfer through `recapitalize`; the residual phantom claims can consume that newly credited cash even though their economic claims should have been zero after a complete wipeout.

This matches the availability component of the referenced bug class: an ordinary user-triggered state transition can make a core pool operation repeatedly fail. The user-facing path is `borrow` to create the position, followed by permissionless `clean_bad_debt` once `total_debt > total_collateral` and collateral is no more than the fixed 5 USD dust threshold. [9](#0-8) 

### Likelihood Explanation
The attacker needs an underwater account whose collateral has fallen to at most the dust threshold while the residual debt is large enough to consume the market's total supplied value. This can arise from severe collateral depreciation or prolonged interest accrual; no privileged role is needed for the final cleanup. The resulting failure is persistent rather than a one-transaction revert because the index floor remains until the shortfall is funded or the accounting is corrected.

### Recommendation
Do not preserve a nonzero supply index after a capped write-down consumes the full supplied value. When `remaining == 0`, either reset the market's supply accounting to zero together with all scaled supplier claims, or explicitly burn the surviving scaled supply shares as part of cleanup. If retaining `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` is required for share-minting math, separately track a wipeout flag or zero effective supplier claim so `backing_shortfall` does not recognize claims that were intentionally socialized away.

### Proof of Concept
1. Supplier accounts deposit asset `X`, producing positive `supplied` shares and pool cash.
2. An attacker account supplies volatile collateral, then calls `borrow` for a large amount of `X`; the pool mints debt shares and debits cash. [10](#0-9) 
3. Let collateral value fall below the debt and at or below 5 USD, while accrued debt reaches the market's total supplied value.
4. Any address calls `clean_bad_debt(account_id)`. The borrow leg is submitted as a pool `Borrow` seizure, converted at the current borrow index, and burned. [2](#0-1) 
5. `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes `remaining = 0`, but commits `supply_index = SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` rather than zero. [11](#0-10) 
6. Every surviving supply share still unscales to a positive underlying amount at that floored index, while the debt backing was burned and most cash was borrowed.
7. `withdraw` reverts with `InsufficientLiquidity` when the residual claim exceeds accounting cash. [12](#0-11) 
8. `supply` reverts with `PoolInsolvent` because the floored residual claims produce a positive backing shortfall. [13](#0-12) 
9. Only `recapitalize` restores operations, by crediting enough cash to cover claims that should have been eliminated by the complete write-down. [14](#0-13)

```rust
pub(crate) fn apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache: &mut Cache, bad_debt: Ray) {
    let total_supplied_value = cache.supplied().mul(cache.env(), cache.supply_index());

    if total_supplied_value == Ray::ZERO {
        return;
    }

    let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
    let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);

    let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
    let new_supply_index = cache
        .supply_index()
        .mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);

    cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
```
