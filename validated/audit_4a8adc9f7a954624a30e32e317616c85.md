### Title
Permanent market freeze from unchecked scaled-debt value overflow before borrow-index cap - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
An unprivileged borrower and supplier can build a sufficiently large, highly utilized market and let interest accrue until `borrowed * borrow_index` overflows `i128` before `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` is reached. Because every pool mutation accrues first, the market then permanently rejects repayments, withdrawals, liquidations, bad-debt cleanup, parameter replacement, and recapitalization.

### Finding Description
`global_sync` splits elapsed time into bounded chunks and calls `accrue_step` for each chunk. [1](#0-0)  The resulting accrual updates both indexes and revenue from the current scaled `borrowed` and `supplied` totals. [2](#0-1)  Although `update_borrow_index` caps the index itself at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, the accrued debt calculation separately multiplies the scaled debt by the old and new indexes and can overflow before that cap is reached. [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

Market accrual runs before mutation commit, and `update_indexes` exposes the same accrual path permissionlessly. [5](#0-4)  The controller exposes permissionless `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `update_indexes`, and `recapitalize`, while the account owner can `supply` and `borrow`. [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) 

### Impact Explanation
Once the scaled-debt value crosses the `i128` domain boundary, all state-changing operations for that market execute accrual and revert with `MathOverflow`. This permanently freezes supplier cash, borrower debt, liquidation proceeds, protocol revenue, and any account whose risk or settlement path must load that market. It also prevents recapitalization or parameter replacement because those paths accrue under the old state first.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires a high-decimal market with caps and available liquidity large enough for the scaled book value to approach `i128::MAX / borrow_index`, plus sustained high utilization. No privileged action is needed after market listing: one attacker can supply liquidity through one owned account, supply collateral through another owned account, borrow that market to high utilization, and then call `update_indexes` as time passes.

The repository’s own boundary test demonstrates the reachable cliff with a one-billion-token, 18-decimal market at 98% utilization: accrual fails below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, after which both withdrawal and repayment fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW`. [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8) 

### Recommendation
Do not rely on `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` alone to keep scaled debt within the `i128` domain. Before accrual, explicitly bound the product of scaled `borrowed` and the prospective index, and clamp or fail only the incremental accrual in a way that still permits repayment and withdrawal. Market caps should also be derived from the worst-case permitted index and token decimals so listed caps cannot admit a book whose risk value can overflow before the index cap engages.

### Proof of Concept
1. An attacker controls two accounts.
2. Account A supplies a very large amount of the high-decimal debt token.
3. Account B supplies sufficient collateral and borrows the debt token to sustained high utilization.
4. Anyone calls `update_indexes(caller, assets=[debt_hub_asset])` as ledger time advances.
5. Accrual eventually computes `borrowed * borrow_index` beyond `i128::MAX` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
6. Subsequent `repay`, `withdraw`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `recapitalize`, and market administration paths panic during mandatory accrual, leaving the market frozen.

This sequence is reproduced by `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap`. [10](#0-9)

#[test]
fn a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap() {
    let mut t = LendingTest::new()
        .with_market(big("BIG18", 18, xlm_curve()))
        .with_market(col())
        .with_max_utilization_disabled_all_markets()
        .build();
    lift_caps(&t, "BIG18", 18);
    lift_caps(&t, "COL", 7);
    let principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18);
    t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);
    let debt = principal / 100 * 98;
    t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
    t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);

    let mut years = 0u32;
    let failure = loop {
        years += 1;
        assert!(
            years <= 40,
            "no cliff within 40 years; the bound in docs/reference/formulas.md is wrong"
        );
        t.advance_time(YEAR_SECS);
        if let Err(e) = t.try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"]) {
            break e;
        }
    };
    let failed: Result<(), soroban_sdk::Error> = Err(failure);
    assert_contract_error(failed, errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    let last = book(&t, "BIG18");
    assert!(
        last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY,
        "the index cap did not engage before the value overflow"
    );
    // The market is frozen: exits and repayments accrue first and hit the same panic.
    assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    assert_contract_error(t.try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
