### Title
Wiped-out supply index floor leaves phantom supplier shares that drain fresh deposits — ([File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs](contracts/pool/src/interest.rs))

### Summary
When bad debt is socialized, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` caps the loss at total supplied value but floors the resulting `supply_index` at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`) instead of zeroing it. The scaled supply shares of wiped-out suppliers are never burned — the pool's `supplied` total and each account's `scaled_amount` survive intact. Each remaining share therefore retains a nonzero claim (`unscale_supply_floor(scaled) > 0`). Once anyone makes a fresh deposit, the stranded claims resolve against real pool cash and are paid out in full, leaving the honest depositor undercollateralized. This is the protocol-level analog of CVE-2020-36385's use-after-free: a position whose backing was destroyed remains reachable through the share ledger (`scaled_amount`/`supplied`) and is later dereferenced by `resolve_withdrawal`.

### Finding Description
- `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes `remaining = total_supplied_value - capped`, derives a `reduction_factor`, and then applies `new_supply_index.max(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)` at `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-88`. When bad debt meets or exceeds the whole supplied value, the index is clamped **up** to `RAY/1000`, so `scaled * index` still unscale to a positive amount for every share holder.
- The seize op burns only debt shares: `seize::apply` calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` then `cache.burn_debt(position)` for `Borrow` entries at `contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:24-31`. No supply shares are burned and `supplied` is not reduced.
- An unprivileged caller reaches this via `clean_bad_debt(account_id)` → `process_clean_bad_debt` → `clean_bad_debt_standalone` → `socialize_bad_debt(DustCapped)` → `bad_debt::execute_bad_debt_cleanup`, which emits the seize entries (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs:196-243`). The dust-cap gate admits cleanup whenever collateral ≤ dust threshold and debt > collateral, so a near-total wipeout is permissionlessly triggerable.
- Withdrawal pays out via `resolve_withdrawal` + `require_reserves`, which only checks pool cash — so stranded shares redeem against subsequent deposits. The pool test suite itself demonstrates the drain: `test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` and `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-494`) show a wiped position extracting exactly a fresh depositor's funds, leaving the pool insolvent. I did not locate a production "supply guard" that blocks withdrawal or re-supply once the index is at the floor; if such a guard exists elsewhere it was not evident in the reviewed code, and the tests' names imply the guard is the only thing standing between this and realized loss.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. After a wipeout socialization, the first fresh suppliers' deposits become redeemable by holders of stranded pre-wipeout shares, whose claims should be zero. The books then owe more than cash on hand (`unscale_supply_floor(supplied) > cash`), so honest depositors lose funds.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a market where socialized bad debt meets or exceeds total supplied value (full wipeout). That is reachable permissionlessly through `clean_bad_debt` once an insolvent account's residual collateral is at/below the dust threshold — a normal outcome in illiquid or crashed markets, exactly when socialization is intended to run. Afterwards any ordinary `supply` followed by the stranded holder's `withdraw` realizes the theft; no privileged role, oracle manipulation, or flash loan is needed.

### Recommendation
When the reduction would consume the entire supplied value, drive the supply index to an explicit "wiped" state (or zero index) rather than clamping to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, and treat stranded scaled supply shares as unwithdrawable: e.g., have `resolve_withdrawal`/withdraw op return zero when `supply_index` is at the floor, or zero out `supplied`/revenue shares alongside the index write-down so no residual claim remains reachable. Alternatively, gate new supply on `supply_index > SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` and reset the market (shares burned, index re-anchored at `RAY`) via `recapitalize` before reopening.

### Proof of Concept
1. Market X has supplier Alice (`scaled = 1000e27`) and a borrower whose collateral collapses; debt ≈ or > total supplied value.
2. Attacker calls `clean_bad_debt(account_id)` once the borrower's collateral ≤ dust threshold. `seize::apply` invokes `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, clamping `supply_index` to `RAY/1000`; Alice's `scaled_amount` and pool `supplied` are untouched.
3. Bob calls `supply` depositing `D` tokens of real cash into the now-claimable market.
4. Alice (or the wiped account's residual shares via `clean_bad_debt` leftovers) calls `withdraw`. `unscale_supply_floor(alice_scaled) = alice_scaled * RAY/1000 > 0`; `require_reserves` passes against Bob's cash; Alice receives real tokens.
5. Pool cash < Bob's claim → Bob's deposit is impaired; protocol is insolvent.

This mirrors `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-494` end-to-end through the controller's permissionless entrypoints.
