### Title
Ray-scaled market math overflows `i128` before the borrow-index cap engages, permanently freezing every supplier's funds in the market - ([File: tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs])

### Summary
The Poppler CVE is a NULL-pointer dereference: code assumes a value exists / stays within representable bounds and crashes when it does not. The direct analog in XOXNO Lending is the ray-scaled accrual path: `scaled_to_original` multiplies a raw ray amount by a growing borrow index and panics with `MATH_OVERFLOW` on `i128` overflow. Because *every* controller verb accrues interest first, once the index grows past the representable ceiling on a very large market, the market is bricked: no repay, no withdraw, no liquidation — permanently. The protocol's own regression test proves the index cap (`MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`) never engages before the value overflow, contradicting the bound in `docs/reference/formulas.md`.

### Finding Description
Interest accrual unscales ray-share amounts by multiplying `scaled_amount * index`. For a market holding ~1 billion whole tokens of an 18-decimal asset (`1e36` raw ray), the index only needs to grow ~170x before `scaled_to_original` overflows `i128`. On the steep segment of the XLM-style interest-rate curve at sustained ~98% utilization, that growth is reached well before `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` is hit, so the cap that is supposed to stop index growth never fires. The test at `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:315-361` documents the cliff explicitly: "Every verb accrues first, so the market freezes: no repay, no withdraw, no liquidation. The index cap never engages," and asserts `MATH_OVERFLOW` on `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.

All entrypoints in scope — `supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `flash_loan`, `multiply`, `update_indexes` — funnel into index accrual for that `(hub, token)` book, so a single panic point permanently DoS-es the entire market.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds. Once the ceiling is crossed, every supplier's principal and accrued yield in that market is unrecoverable, every borrower's position is uncloseable and unliquidatable, and `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` cannot operate because they accrue first. Unlike the NFT-`Owner`-TTL analog (a missing `Owner(token_id)` entry makes `get_account` panic `AccountNotFound` — `contracts/controller/src/storage/account.rs:146-149`), which is recoverable because `position-nft::renew` is permissionless (`contracts/position-nft/src/contract.rs:109-115`) and archived entries can be restored, the overflow freeze has no recovery path short of a contract upgrade.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. An unprivileged attacker cannot trigger it instantly: it requires a billion-scale position in a high-decimal asset and sustained high utilization so the index compounds ~170x. However, the conditions are market-driven rather than permissioned: anyone can `supply` and `borrow` up to lifted caps, and once utilization sits near the steep curve segment the growth is deterministic — the protocol test shows the cliff is reached sooner than the documented bound suggests. No governance action can intervene once it starts, since liquidation and repay are frozen too. High capital cost and long exposure window keep this below High.

### Recommendation
Check the overflow in `scaled_to_original`/index accrual and clamp the accrued index at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` *before* the multiplication can overflow `i128`, or widen the intermediate to a checked 256-bit-style computation so the index cap is the binding constraint rather than the integer width. At minimum, gate accrual so a market approaching the representable ceiling stops accruing (freezing only yield, not principal) instead of panicking on every verb.

### Proof of Concept
The protocol's own harness demonstrates it end-to-end:

```rust
// tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360
let principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18);          // 1e36 raw ray
t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);
let debt = principal / 100 * 98;                    // ~98% utilization
t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);
// advance time at steep-curve utilization until:
assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
assert_contract_error(t.try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
assert!(book(&t, "BIG18").borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY); // cap never engaged
```

A single funded address achieves the precondition with two permissionless calls (`supply` then `borrow` at ~98% utilization); thereafter passive interest accrual — which any `update_indexes` caller advances — drives the market into the permanent freeze.
