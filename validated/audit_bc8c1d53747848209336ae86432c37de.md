### Title
Refund legs pay the declared amount instead of the measured receipt, draining supplier custody while the cash book still reports the funds - ([File: contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs](contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs))

### Summary
Analogous to the Chakra report's "failed settlement with no safe refund handling," the pool's two refund-bearing legs — `ops::repay::apply` (overpayment) and `ops::recapitalize::apply` (excess over the backing shortfall) — compute the refund from the **declared** inbound `amount`, not from the tokens actually received, and pay it out of pool custody via `cache.transfer_out` **without debiting `cash` or passing `require_reserves`**. When the inbound transfer delivers less than declared (fee-on-transfer / deflationary tokens) — or, at the pool layer, when nothing arrives at all — the "refund" is funded entirely by other suppliers' deposits. The cash book is left unchanged, so the market still reports the stolen liquidity as present and subsequent withdrawals pass the pool's own liquidity guard and fail inside the SAC transfer.

### Finding Description
`recapitalize` credits only `min(amount, backing_shortfall)` to cash but sends `amount - applied` back to `payer`, with no check that `amount` was actually received:

`contracts/pool/src/ops/recapitalize.rs` [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

`repay` refunds `overpayment = amount - net_repay` the same way; when the market has no debt, `resolve_repay` takes the full-close branch, `net_repay` is 0, `RepayRoundsToZeroShares` passes on its `net_repay == 0` disjunct, and the **entire declared amount** is refunded out of custody:

`contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs` [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

The in-repo tests already demonstrate the invariant break end-to-end: a single unfunded `recapitalize`/`repay` drains the pool's whole token balance to the payer while `cash` is untouched, and a subsequent supplier `withdraw` clears `require_reserves` yet fails inside the SAC with `BalanceError = 10`:

`contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs` [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

This is reachable by an unprivileged address through the controller's open entrypoints `recapitalize(payer, hub_asset, amount)` and `repay(caller, ...)` — both documented as open/unauthenticated in `docs/reference/endpoints.md` [7](#0-6) . For a market whose underlying token is a fee-on-transfer or otherwise taxed SAC, the controller's inbound `transfer` delivers less than the declared `amount`, but both refund legs still pay out the full declared excess.

### Impact Explanation
Each call converts the refund into an unbacked withdrawal: the attacker receives `declared - applied` tokens that belong to suppliers, while `cash` still counts them as pool liquidity. Repeated calls (or one call sized to `custody`) drain the market's entire token balance. Suppliers then cannot withdraw — exits pass `require_reserves` (book-based) and revert inside the SAC — which is theft of user funds plus permanent freezing of remaining supplier claims, i.e., protocol insolvency of that market.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a listed market whose token delivers less than the declared transfer amount (fee-on-transfer/taxed SAC) — a supported scenario per the threat model's own receipt-tax discussion — or any path where measured receipt < declared `amount`. A single unprivileged caller can execute it via `Controller::recapitalize` or `Controller::repay` with `amount` above the shortfall/debt, no privileged role, oracle manipulation, or timing needed.

### Recommendation
- Base both refunds on the **measured** inbound delta (balance before/after the controller's transfer), not the declared `amount`: `refund = received - applied` and `overpayment = received - net_repay`.
- Alternatively, pass the measured `actual_amount` (as `transfer_amount_measured` does elsewhere) into `repay`/`recapitalize` so the declared parameter can never exceed what was funded.
- Optionally debit `cash` for the refund leg or route refunds through `require_reserves`, so a refund can never pay out liquidity the book still owes suppliers.

### Proof of Concept
1. List a hub market over a fee-on-transfer token (or any token where the pool's measured receipt is less than the transfer amount).
2. Attacker calls `Controller::repay(caller, payments=[(hub_asset, declared)], ...)` on a debt-free position: `resolve_repay` returns `net_repay = 0`, `overpayment = declared`, and `transfer_out(payer, declared)` sends supplier custody to the attacker while `cash` is unchanged (`contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs:44-57`, `repay.rs:32`).
3. Equivalently, `Controller::recapitalize(payer, hub_asset, amount)` on a healthy market: `applied = 0`, `refund = amount`, paid from custody (`contracts/pool/src/ops/recapitalize.rs:52-55`, `recapitalize.rs:34`).
4. Supplier `withdraw` afterwards clears `Cache::require_reserves` (which reads `cash`) and fails inside the SAC transfer with `BalanceError = 10`, exactly as asserted by `test_unfunded_recapitalize_leaves_the_cash_book_overstating_custody` and `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` in `contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3408-3610`.

```rust
/// The refund gap is not specific to `recapitalize`. Two pool legs refund an
/// excess derived from a declared inbound amount, not from the cash book:
/// `ops::recapitalize::apply` (the excess over the shortfall) and
/// `ops::repay::apply` (the excess over the debt). Neither refund debits `cash`
/// or passes `require_reserves`.
///
/// A repay against a market with no debt makes the entire declared amount an
/// overpayment: `current_debt_ceil` is zero, so `resolve_repay` takes the
/// full-close branch and `net_repay` is zero. The `RepayRoundsToZeroShares`
/// assert in `ops::repay::accounting` passes on its `net_repay == 0` disjunct,
/// and the whole amount is refunded out of real custody with the book untouched.
#[test]
fn test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody() {
    let t = TestSetup::new();
    let token = token::Client::new(&t.env, &t.asset);
    let payer = Address::generate(&t.env);

    let custody_before = token.balance(&t.pool);
    let before = t.state_snapshot();
    assert_eq!(
        before.cash, custody_before,
        "fixture guard: book and custody must start in sync"
    );
    assert_eq!(before.borrowed, 0, "fixture must carry no debt");

    // Nothing transferred in, no debt to retire: the whole amount is "excess".
    let credited = t
        .client()
        .repay(&payer, &t.ract(0, custody_before))
        .get_unchecked(0)
        .actual_amount;
    assert_eq!(credited, 0, "no debt was retired, so nothing is credited");
    assert_unfunded_refund_drained_custody(&t, &payer, custody_before, &before);
```
