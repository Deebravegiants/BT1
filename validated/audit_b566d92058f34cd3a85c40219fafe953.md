### Title
Permissionless, deterministic AMM pool creation via `Initialize2` allows front-running of legitimate pool creators, letting an attacker seize the pool address and dictate its initial price - (File: program/src/processor.rs)

### Summary
`process_initialize2` derives every account needed to create a new AMM pool (the `AmmInfo` account, LP mint, coin/pc vaults, and target-orders account) deterministically from the `market_info` pubkey alone, using `find_program_address`/`get_associated_address_and_bump_seed` with fixed seed constants (`AMM_ASSOCIATED_SEED`, `LP_MINT_ASSOCIATED_SEED`, `COIN_VAULT_ASSOCIATED_SEED`, `PC_VAULT_ASSOCIATED_SEED`, `TARGET_ASSOCIATED_SEED`). Because the market address for a given trading pair is public knowledge before the legitimate creator's transaction lands, and `Initialize2` has no signer-based authorization beyond "some fee-paying wallet," anyone can submit their own `Initialize2` instruction referencing the same market and win the race. [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

### Finding Description
This mirrors the Aave Lens "profile creation frontrun" bug class: the resource being created (there, a profile `handle`; here, an AMM pool for a specific market) has an address that is fully predictable ahead of time from public input (the market pubkey), and creation is open to any caller. There is no commit-reveal, no reservation mechanism, and no check that ties pool creation to the intended creator's identity or an expected price.

An attacker monitoring the mempool/pending transactions for a `createProfile`-equivalent `Initialize2` call for a specific market can submit their own `Initialize2` with the identical `market_info` account but attacker-chosen `init_pc_amount`/`init_coin_amount`, and a smaller/cheaper `user_wallet_info`. Because the PDA derivations only depend on `market_info.key`, the attacker's transaction creates the exact same `AmmInfo`, vault, and LP-mint addresses the victim intended to use. The victim's subsequent `Initialize2` call then fails at `generate_amm_associated_account`/`generate_amm_associated_spl_token` with `AmmError::RepeatCreateAmm` since the accounts are already owned/initialized. [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) 

### Impact Explanation
The attacker who wins this race controls:
- The initial price ratio of the pool (`init_pc_amount` / `init_coin_amount`), which can be set arbitrarily far from fair market value using minimal capital.
- 100% of the initial LP token supply (`user_lp_amount`), since they are the first and only depositor.
- The pool's `amm_owner`/`pnl_owner` semantics remain protocol-config-controlled, but the economic terms of the pool are entirely attacker-defined.

Because the pool address for that market is now permanently claimed by the attacker's `AmmInfo`/vault/LP-mint set, the legitimate project/team cannot create their own "clean" pool at that same deterministic address — they must either use the attacker-created pool (at the attacker's manipulated price) or be unable to deploy their intended pool for that market at all, enabling ransom demands or immediate profit extraction from unsuspecting swappers who assume the pool reflects a fair, freshly-seeded price.

### Likelihood Explanation
The `market_info` account for a new pool is a Serum/OpenBook market that must already exist and be known publicly before `Initialize2` can be submitted, so an attacker has ample opportunity to observe it and race a pending creation transaction. No special privileges or leaked keys are required — this is exploitable by any unprivileged actor who can submit a transaction, satisfying the "single submitted transaction, attacker-chosen accounts and data" bar.

### Recommendation
Bind pool creation to the intended creator, e.g., by requiring an additional expected-creator signature/allowlist check, or by introducing a commit-reveal step (reserve the market, then finalize price/amounts in a follow-up transaction only the reserver can execute) before allowing `Initialize2` to mint LP tokens against attacker-chosen `init_pc_amount`/`init_coin_amount`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Team A prepares an `Initialize2` transaction for market `M` with `init_pc_amount = 10_000_000_000` USDC-equivalent and `init_coin_amount` matching a fair price, deriving `amm_pool`, `amm_lp_mint`, `amm_coin_vault`, `amm_pc_vault` from `market_info = M` per `get_associated_address_and_bump_seed`.
2. Attacker observes the pending transaction (or simply knows market `M` was just listed) and derives the same PDAs.
3. Attacker submits their own `Initialize2` for market `M` first, with `init_coin_amount = 1` and `init_pc_amount = 1` (or any attacker-favorable skewed ratio), funded by a throwaway wallet.
4. Attacker's transaction lands first, initializing `AmmInfo`, minting nearly all LP supply to the attacker (`user_lp_amount` per [7](#0-6) ), and setting `amm.status`.
5. Team A's original transaction now fails with `AmmError::RepeatCreateAmm` at `generate_amm_associated_account` because `amm_info.owner` is already the program.
6. Attacker now holds the sole pool for market `M` at a self-chosen price and can demand ransom from Team A or immediately profit by swapping against later depositors/traders who assume the pool was fairly seeded.
