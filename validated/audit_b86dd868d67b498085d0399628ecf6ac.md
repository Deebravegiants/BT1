### Title
Bad-debt supply-index floor clamp leaves wiped suppliers with phantom claims that drain future deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary

`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` socializes bad debt by multiplying the supply index by a reduction factor, then clamps the result to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (= RAY/1000) via `.max(...)`. When bad debt meets or exceeds the total supplied value, every supply share still resolves to ~0.1% of its pre-wipe claim instead of zero. Those wiped positions remain withdrawable: a subsequent deposit supplies the cash that the phantom claim then pays out, so an unprivileged actor whose supply was already "socialized to zero" can steal the next depositor's tokens. This is the direct analog of the heap-buffer-overflow class: a bounded-write/clamp fallback that lets a crafted extreme input (bad debt ≥ total supply) leave stale state that is later read as a real balance.

### Finding Description

In `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89`, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes `remaining = total_supplied_value - capped`, derives `reduction_factor = remaining / total_supplied_value`, and sets `supply_index = supply_index * reduction_factor`, finally clamping with `new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW))` (line 88). When `capped == total_supplied_value` (full wipeout), the mathematically correct index is 0, but the floor clamps it up to `RAY/1000`. Because suppliers' `scaled_amount` shares are untouched, `unscale_supply_floor` on the old shares returns a positive asset claim (`contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:60` → `common/src/rates/scaling.rs:77`).

The path is reachable from the unprivileged `clean_bad_debt`/`liquidate` controller entrypoints, which dispatch a `PoolSeizeEntry` with `AccountPositionType::Borrow` into `pool::ops::seize::apply` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:24-27`), calling `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` directly. Withdrawal then goes through `Cache::resolve_withdrawal` (`scale.rs:97`), which burns the old shares and pays the floored claim; `require_reserves` only checks current cash, so any fresh deposit makes the phantom claim payable. The pool's own regression test proves the drain: `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-427`) shows `gross == fresh_cash` — the stranded wiped position withdraws exactly the fresh supplier's deposit, leaving cash unable to cover the fresh claim.

### Impact Explanation

Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. Any supply holder whose shares survive a full-wipe bad-debt event retains a withdrawable claim worth `shares × RAY/1000` even though 100% of the debt was supposed to be socialized. The first deposit(s) after the wipeout are immediately claimable by wiped suppliers; the fresh supplier's shares become unbacked (`cash < fresh_claim`), which is a direct loss of the new depositor's principal.

### Likelihood Explanation

Fully unprivileged: `clean_bad_debt`/`liquidate` can be invoked by any address on a dust-threshold bad-debt position, and the wipeout condition (bad debt ≥ total supplied value) occurs naturally for thin markets, freshly initialized spoke markets, or an attacker who deliberately opens an undercollateralized borrow against their own small supply and lets it go to bad debt. The attacker can hold the wiped supply themselves and then frontrun the next depositor's withdrawal, making exploitation self-contained.

### Recommendation

When `remaining == Ray::ZERO` (bad debt meets or exceeds total supplied value), zero out supplier claims explicitly — e.g., track a wipeout flag, or burn/migrate residual supply shares — rather than clamping `supply_index` to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. Alternatively, gate withdrawals: skip paying claims when the index sits at the floor after a full wipeout, or reset `supplied` to zero alongside the index clamp.

### Proof of Concept

1. Attacker supplies `S` tokens and borrows, then the position becomes bad debt (or attacker calls `clean_bad_debt` on a dust position) such that `unscale_borrow_ceil_ray(debt_shares) ≥ supplied × supply_index`.
2. `seize::apply` runs `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`; `reduction_factor = 0`, index clamps to `RAY/1000` — attacker's `S` shares still map to `unscale_supply_floor > 0`.
3. A victim deposits `fresh_cash`; `credit_cash` raises pool cash.
4. Attacker calls `withdraw` (amount `0` = withdraw-all sentinel): `resolve_withdrawal` pays `gross = fresh_cash`, `require_reserves` passes, `debit_cash` drains the deposit.
5. Pool cash is now below the victim's `fresh_claim` — depositor funds are stolen. Mirrored exactly by `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:414-426`.
