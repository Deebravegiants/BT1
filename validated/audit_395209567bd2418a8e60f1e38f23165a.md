### Title
Sub-3-decimal collateral leg below `MIN_WHOLE_UNIT_COLLATERAL` permanently gates all risk-checked operations, freezing the account while debt remains - (File: contracts/controller/src/risk/validation.rs)

### Summary
The controller enforces a whole-unit collateral floor (`MIN_WHOLE_UNIT_COLLATERAL`, i.e. a minimum size per supply leg) on every supply position denominated in an asset with fewer than `MIN_BORROWABLE_ASSET_DECIMALS` decimals. The check runs inside `require_post_pool_risk_gates`, which gates `withdraw`, `borrow`, and other risk-increasing entrypoints whenever the account carries debt. Because the floor is evaluated on the account's *current* positions rather than the post-operation delta, an account that ever falls below 2 whole units in such a leg can never pass the gate again — every withdraw and borrow reverts with `MinBorrowCollateralNotMet`. This mirrors the Perennial `minPosition` bug class: a minimum-position invariant applied uniformly can strand a position that no unprivileged action can resize back above the floor.

### Finding Description
`require_post_pool_risk_gates` (contracts/controller/src/risk/validation.rs:29-61) is invoked after withdraw/borrow legs settle, whenever `account.debt_free()` is false. Its final step is `require_whole_unit_collateral_floor` (lines 153-170), which iterates *every* supply position and, for any hub asset with `asset_decimals < MIN_BORROWABLE_ASSET_DECIMALS`, computes `position.scaled_amount * supply_index` floored to asset units and reverts unless `units >= MIN_WHOLE_UNIT_COLLATERAL`.

Two properties make this a freeze primitive:

1. The check is position-wide, not leg-scoped. A withdraw of a *different, healthy* collateral leg still reverts if any unrelated sub-3-decimal leg is below the floor. The account cannot fix the offending leg by withdrawing it (withdraw is gated), and cannot top it up to a compliant size and then exit cleanly without first satisfying the gate — although `supply` itself is not gated, topping up does not remove debt, so the gate still runs on the next withdraw.
2. The offending state is reachable without the victim's cooperation. `liquidate` reduces the victim's collateral via multi-leg pro-rata seizure (SeizeMode::Transfer) and does not run the victim's post-pool risk gate — the victim ends the call with debt and a truncated collateral leg. Similarly, `repay_debt_with_collateral` / liquidation plan legs can leave a sub-3-decimal leg below 2 whole units. Once that happens, all subsequent `withdraw` calls by the account owner revert with `CollateralError::MinBorrowCollateralNotMet` (error 126), including withdraws of unrelated assets.

This is the direct analog of the Perennial `minPosition` freeze: a minimum-position bound that blocks deallocation/exit paths and is applied to state the user cannot unilaterally repair while leveraged.

### Impact Explanation
Temporary freezing of user funds: the account owner cannot withdraw *any* collateral (even legs unrelated to the undersized one and even when the withdraw would improve health) until the account becomes debt-free via `repay` — which requires the owner to source the borrowed asset externally, or until further liquidations erase the position. For a user relying on collateral value to deleverage, this is economically equivalent to forced position-holding against adverse markets, matching the report's "account freeze" impact classification.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. Requires: a market listed with `asset_decimals < MIN_BORROWABLE_ASSET_DECIMALS` (the codebase explicitly supports such markets — `config/asset.rs` and `liquidation/math.rs` special-case sub-3-decimal legs), a victim holding that asset as collateral with debt, and a liquidation (or dust-reducing path) that trims the leg below `MIN_WHOLE_UNIT_COLLATERAL`. Liquidation plans in `positions/liquidation/math.rs` operate in whole units for sub-3-decimal assets, so boundary remainders below 2 units are a realistic outcome, not a corner case. One uncertainty I could not fully verify within the tool-call budget: whether `liquidate` itself runs `require_post_pool_risk_gates` on the victim's post-state — if it does and reverts, liquidators would simply choose leg sizes that don't strand the leg, reducing but not eliminating the path (e.g., remainder produced by index accrual between legs, or by `swap_collateral`/bad-debt flows).

### Recommendation
Scope `require_whole_unit_collateral_floor` to state-worsening transitions only: skip legs that already existed below the floor before the operation, or evaluate the floor on the delta (only enforce when an operation *creates* or *shrinks* a sub-floor leg). Alternatively, exempt full withdrawals of the offending leg and treat "close the leg entirely" as always permitted, so a user can exit the non-compliant position. At minimum, liquidation plans should round victim sub-3-decimal legs to either ≥ `MIN_WHOLE_UNIT_COLLATERAL` remainder or a full seize, so a liquidation never leaves the victim in a state its own withdraws cannot repair.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance lists asset `X` with `asset_decimals < MIN_BORROWABLE_ASSET_DECIMALS` (e.g., 2 decimals) as collateral-enabled, alongside USDC and a borrowable ETH market.
2. ALICE supplies `X` (worth ≥ `MIN_WHOLE_UNIT_COLLATERAL` = 2 whole units, plus margin) and USDC, then borrows ETH so the account has debt.
3. ETH price rises (or X falls); LIQUIDATOR calls controller `liquidate`, seizing X-collateral legs. The plan leaves ALICE's `X` supply leg at, e.g., 1.5 whole units — below `MIN_WHOLE_UNIT_COLLATERAL`.
4. ALICE calls `withdraw` for any amount of her USDC leg (or any asset). `settle_withdraw` (contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs:173-215) settles the pool legs, then `require_post_pool_risk_gates` runs; `require_whole_unit_collateral_floor` iterates her supply positions, computes `1 < MIN_WHOLE_UNIT_COLLATERAL` for the X leg, and panics with `MinBorrowCollateralNotMet` — the entire call reverts.
5. Every subsequent `withdraw`, `borrow`, `multiply`, `swap_collateral`, or `update_account_threshold` (any entrypoint routing through `require_post_pool_risk_gates`) reverts identically. ALICE's only escapes are repaying all debt from external funds or being liquidated further — she cannot withdraw the healthy collateral she still owns.
