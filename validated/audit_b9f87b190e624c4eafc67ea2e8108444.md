### Title
Public, unbounded-frequency `accrue()` calls let an attacker permanently round the DAO treasury's reserve fee to zero - (File: `mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-sbtc.clar` and equivalent vault contracts)

### Summary
The vault's `accrue()` function computes the protocol's reserve share (`reserve-inc`) with a floor-division (`mul-div-down`) on the *incremental* debt delta since the last accrual, rather than on the full accumulated interest. Because `accrue()` is a public function with no access control and no minimum-interval requirement, any user can invoke it every block, shrinking each `debt-delta` window until `reserve-inc` floors to zero on every call — permanently and cumulatively denying the DAO treasury its designed cut of interest income, while borrowers continue to pay full interest (which flows entirely to suppliers via the liquidity index).

### Finding Description
In `accrue()`: [1](#0-0) 

`reserve-inc` is computed as `(mul-div-down debt-delta (var-get fee-reserve) BPS)`, i.e. `debt-delta * fee-reserve / 10000`. `debt-delta` is only the *new* interest accrued since `last-update` (`new-debt - old-debt`), and each call to `accrue()` resets `last-update` and thus resets the accrual window to zero. There is no carry-over or dust accumulator for the truncated remainder — each period's rounding loss is dropped forever, not deferred to the next call.

`accrue()` is `define-public` with no caller authorization check (unlike `system-borrow`/`system-repay`, which call `check-caller-auth`), so any external account can call it at will: [2](#0-1) 

The math utilities confirm floor (round-down) semantics used for `reserve-inc`: [3](#0-2) 

Since `BPS = 10000` and typical `fee-reserve` is `1000` (10%, per production proposals such as `local-testing/contracts/proposals/proposal-set-stx-interest-rates.clar` and `proposal-set-usdc-interest-rates.clar`), `reserve-inc` floors to zero whenever `debt-delta < 10` base units. For an 8-decimal asset like sBTC (`DECIMALS u8`, `PRECISION u100000000`), 10 base units is a negligible fraction of a cent — trivially reachable within a single short block interval for low/medium utilization pools. By repeatedly calling `accrue()` at every block instead of letting interest compound over longer natural intervals, an attacker keeps every single `debt-delta` window below this threshold, so `reserve-inc` (and therefore the `treasury-lp` mint to `.dao-treasury`) is zero on every call, indefinitely.

Meanwhile, `idx` (the borrow index) still advances by the same total amount as it would without the attack, since `next-index` is independent of `reserve-inc`; borrowers still pay full interest into the vault's `assets` on `system-repay`. That interest is captured entirely by the liquidity index (`next-liquidity-index`, already reduced by `fee-reserve` in its own separate calculation) for suppliers, but the treasury's separate skim (`reserve-inc`/`treasury-lp`) — the DAO's designed share of yield — is thereby suppressed to near-zero permanently, since the floored remainder is never recovered in a later call.

This is the direct structural analog to the referenced Rubicon finding: a `fee = amount * feeBPS / BPS_DENOM` computation that rounds to zero for small enough per-call amounts, letting an unprivileged caller systematically avoid paying/forwarding the fee by shrinking the amount used in each individual fee computation.

### Impact Explanation
This breaks the identity "interest paid by borrowers == interest distributed to suppliers + protocol reserve fee". By keeping the reserve-fee term at zero on every accrual, an attacker permanently redirects value that was contractually earmarked as protocol yield (the reserve factor, e.g. 10%) entirely to suppliers instead, causing permanent loss of the DAO treasury's unclaimed yield — meeting the High severity bar ("permanent freezing/loss of unclaimed yield").

### Likelihood Explanation
`accrue()` is unauthenticated and callable by anyone at any time with negligible cost (no funds need to be deposited or borrowed by the attacker), and Stacks block times are short enough that debt-delta per block for many pools/rate regimes will regularly fall under the rounding threshold, especially for pools with lower TVL or during periods of low utilization. This makes the attack cheap, repeatable, and does not require any privileged role or governance compromise.

### Recommendation
- Accumulate a running remainder ("dust") of un-minted `reserve-inc` truncation across calls instead of discarding it each accrual, so no interest fee is permanently lost regardless of call frequency.
- Alternatively (or additionally), enforce a minimum elapsed time or minimum `debt-delta` before allowing `last-update` to reset, so an attacker cannot artificially shrink the accrual window to force rounding to zero.
- Consider computing `reserve-inc` with round-up (`mul-div-up`) rather than round-down, consistent with other places in the codebase (e.g. `calc-cumulative-debt`) that already round debt up in the protocol's favor.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker (or anyone) observes a vault (e.g. `v0-vault-sbtc`) with `fee-reserve = 1000` (10%) and nonzero outstanding debt.
2. Attacker calls `accrue()` on every new block (public, unauthenticated call), so `time-delta` between successive `last-update` values is minimized to one block.
3. For each call, `debt-delta = new-debt - old-debt` computed over that single-block window is small enough that `mul-div-down debt-delta fee-reserve BPS` floors to `0` (true whenever `debt-delta < 10` base units, easily achievable for a single block's worth of interest on many pools).
4. `reserve-inc = 0` ⇒ `treasury-lp = 0` ⇒ no shares minted to `.dao-treasury` on that call — see `mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-sbtc.clar` lines 847-857.
5. `idx`/`lindex` still update per normal, and `last-update` resets, discarding the truncated fractional remainder permanently (no dust carried forward).
6. Attacker repeats step 2 indefinitely; over the vault's lifetime, the DAO treasury accumulates negligible-to-zero reserve share versus the full reserve-factor-based share it would have received under normal, less frequent accrual cadence.
