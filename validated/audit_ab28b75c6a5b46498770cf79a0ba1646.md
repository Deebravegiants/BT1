### Title
Webhook shop/topic identity not bound to HMAC signature enabling cross-tenant webhook spoofing - (File: lib/shopify_api/webhooks/request.rb)

### Summary
`ShopifyAPI::Webhooks::Request` computes its HMAC-verifiable signable string from the raw request body only. The `shop-domain`, `topic`, `webhook-id`, and `api-version` values — which are the fields host applications use to route/act on webhooks per-tenant — come from unauthenticated HTTP headers that are never included in the HMAC computation.

### Finding Description
`Registry.process` gates webhook processing on `Utils::HmacValidator.validate(request)` [1](#0-0) . `HmacValidator.validate_signature` computes the signature over `verifiable_query.to_signable_string` [2](#0-1) , and `Request#to_signable_string` returns only `@raw_body` [3](#0-2) . However `Request#shop`, `#topic`, `#webhook_id`, and `#api_version` are all read directly from headers [4](#0-3) , and none of those header values are covered by the HMAC. `Registry.process` then hands `request.shop` straight to the app's handler as the tenant identity for the event, with no separate authentication of that field [5](#0-4) .

The broken binding, as an equality that should hold but doesn't:
`bytes_covered_by_hmac (raw_body) == bytes_the_app_trusts_for_tenant_routing (shop header)` — these are disjoint sets. The HMAC only proves "this body was produced with knowledge of `client_secret`"; it proves nothing about which shop the header claims to represent.

Because many webhook payloads are shop-agnostic or highly predictable (e.g. `app/uninstalled`, `shop/redact` with empty or templated bodies, or any topic whose body content is identical/guessable across installs), an attacker who operates their own legitimate install of the app can obtain a validly-HMAC-signed `(raw_body, hmac)` pair for their own shop, then replay that same body+hmac to the app's webhook endpoint while substituting the `shopify-shop-domain` (and optionally `shopify-topic`/`shopify-webhook-id`) header with a victim shop's domain. `HmacValidator.validate` will still pass because it only checks the untouched raw body against the signature — it never re-derives or binds the shop from the signed content. The handler then executes shop-scoped side effects (data deletion/redaction, session invalidation, business logic tied to the shop, etc.) attributing them to the victim shop, achieving cross-tenant impact without ever possessing the victim's credentials or the app's `client_secret`.

### Impact Explanation
This crosses a tenant boundary: an attacker with only their own legitimate app installation can cause the host application to process and act on a forged event for another merchant's shop, since the field the app treats as the authenticated tenant identity (`shop`) is not covered by the cryptographic signature that supposedly authenticates the message. This matches the Critical "cross-tenant access" category — the attacker never needs the victim's access token or the app's `client_secret`, only a self-signed payload from their own shop replayed with altered headers.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitability depends on the attacker being able to produce or capture a `(raw_body, hmac)` pair whose body content is not shop-specific (or is otherwise attacker-controlled/predictable), which is realistic for several standard mandatory compliance topics (e.g. `app/uninstalled`) and many app-specific low-entropy payloads. The attacker only needs their own legitimate shop install to generate a validly-signed sample; no secret material or victim access is required to mount the header-substitution replay.

### Recommendation
Bind the tenant/topic identity into the HMAC-verified material: include `shop`, `topic`, and `webhook_id` in `Request#to_signable_string` (or verify them via a MAC that mirrors what Shopify actually signs), rather than trusting header values that sit outside the signed body. At minimum, document and enforce that `shop`/`topic` must be treated as verified only insofar as they are cryptographically bound, and reject requests where header-derived identity cannot be corroborated against the signed payload.

### Proof of Concept
1. Install the app on attacker-controlled shop `attacker.myshopify.com`; trigger a webhook with a shop-agnostic/predictable body, e.g. `app/uninstalled` with body `{}`.
2. Capture the resulting `POST` with headers `x-shopify-hmac-sha256`, `x-shopify-topic: app/uninstalled`, `x-shopify-shop-domain: attacker.myshopify.com`, and raw body `{}`.
3. Replay this exact request to the app's webhook endpoint, but replace `x-shopify-shop-domain` with `victim.myshopify.com` (raw body and HMAC header untouched).
4. `Registry.process` calls `HmacValidator.validate(request)` → passes, because validation is computed only over `raw_body`, which is unchanged [1](#0-0) .
5. `handler.handle` receives `WebhookMetadata` with `shop: "victim.myshopify.com"` [6](#0-5) , and the app performs the `app/uninstalled` side effects (e.g. deleting the shop's stored session/data) against the victim tenant.
