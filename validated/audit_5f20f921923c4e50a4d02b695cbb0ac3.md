### Title
Reward-per-epoch shares distribution lets a late-epoch staker capture rewards earned entirely by pre-existing stakers - (File: staking-pool/src/internal.rs)

### Summary
The Acala finding describes incentive rewards for a fixed period being split among *whichever* shares are present when the accumulation runs, rather than being scaled by how long the shares were actually deposited, enabling deposit→claim→withdraw sandwiching of the reward pool. The NEAR staking-pool contract has the same root-cause pattern in its epoch-reward distribution: `internal_ping()` computes the reward earned since the last epoch and mints/attributes it to the price of **every** currently outstanding "stake" share, without regard to when those shares were added relative to the reward-earning period.

### Finding Description
`internal_ping()` computes `total_reward` as the delta between the current account balance (including `env::account_locked_balance()`) and `last_total_balance`, then adds `remaining_reward` directly to `self.total_staked_balance`, which raises the price of **all** currently outstanding stake shares uniformly: [1](#0-0) 

Crucially, `stake()`/`deposit_and_stake()` add new shares to `total_stake_shares` immediately at the current price and then call `internal_restake()` to submit a new stake amount to the protocol: [2](#0-1) 

Because NEAR validator stake-weight changes take effect only in a following epoch (a `stake` action submitted late in epoch `N` does not contribute to the validator's actual locked/validating stake until epoch `N+1`), a share added right before an epoch boundary does not participate in earning the reward that gets credited to the pool's balance at the *start* of the next epoch. Yet when `ping()` (or any action, since every entrypoint calls `internal_ping()` first, e.g. `deposit`, `withdraw`, `stake`, `unstake`) is next invoked, the newly-credited reward is split pro-rata over `total_stake_shares` **as it exists at that moment**, which already includes the late-added shares: [3](#0-2) 

This mirrors exactly the Acala root cause: a fixed reward for a period is distributed over whatever total-shares figure exists at accumulation time, with no mechanism to scale rewards by actual deposit duration or to exclude late-added shares from a reward-earning window they didn't contribute to.

An unprivileged user can therefore:
1. Call `deposit_and_stake` with a large NEAR amount just before an epoch boundary (epoch height is public/deterministic, so timing is straightforward) — `staking-pool/src/lib.rs:227-236`.
2. Wait for the epoch to roll over; the protocol credits the pool with the epoch's validator reward (earned solely by the other, pre-existing delegators' locked stake).
3. Call `unstake`/`unstake_all` immediately (or trigger it via any call, since `internal_ping()` runs first) to convert the attacker's shares back to unstaked balance at the newly inflated share price — `staking-pool/src/lib.rs:289-314`, `staking-pool/src/internal.rs:194-249`.
4. Wait out the standard 4-epoch unbonding period like any other user to withdraw — the unfair gain was already locked in at step 3, the withdrawal delay does not prevent it.

### Impact Explanation
Long-term delegators' rewards are diluted by capital that was economically at risk for a much shorter window (roughly one epoch boundary) than the reward-earning period, letting an unprivileged user over-credit value and withdraw more than their fair entitlement, directly matching the in-scope impact "Share, reward,... balance-accounting divergence that lets an unprivileged user over-credit value... or withdraw more than fair entitlement."

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitability depends on the attacker being able to acquire and briefly commit a large amount of NEAR (their own or borrowed) at a predictable, publicly known epoch boundary, and on execution ordering being controllable enough to be "first" to trigger `ping()`/any action right after the boundary. These are realistic assumptions on NEAR (epoch height is deterministic and public), though the practical profitability depends on the size of the attacker's stake relative to the pool's total stake and on gas/timing competition with other transactions in the same block — comparable to the "Medium, with stated assumptions" characterization the Acala judge applied to the analogous finding.

### Recommendation
Track when shares are added relative to the reward-accumulation boundary (e.g., only allow newly staked shares to start accruing rewards starting from the next full epoch after they are submitted to `internal_restake()`, or checkpoint a "pending" stake bucket that is merged into `total_stake_shares` only after it has actually participated in validation for a full epoch). Alternatively, delay a newly staked amount's eligibility for the very next reward distribution by one epoch, similar to how unstaked funds are subject to a withdrawal delay.

### Proof of Concept
Given the emulator-based unit test harness already in the contract (`staking-pool/src/lib.rs` test module, e.g. `test_stake_with_fee`), a PoC could be structured as:
1. Alice deposits and stakes `X` NEAR at epoch `N-1` and lets it accrue normally (long-term staker).
2. At the very end of epoch `N` (just before `emulator.skip_epochs`/epoch boundary), Bob (attacker) calls `deposit_and_stake` with `Y` NEAR, `Y >> X`.
3. Simulate epoch reward accrual (`emulator.locked_amount += reward`) as in `test_stake_with_fee` (`staking-pool/src/lib.rs:663-673`).
4. Bob calls `unstake_all()` immediately after the simulated epoch turnover.
5. Compare Bob's realized gain (`unstaked_balance - Y`) against what Bob would be "fairly" owed if rewards were scaled by actual staking duration (near zero, since Bob's stake did not participate in earning that epoch's validator reward) — demonstrating Bob captures a share of Alice's/other delegators' earned reward.
