### Title
Accrual overflow permanently freezes large utilized markets - (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary
A market can enter a state where every state-changing operation panics with `MathOverflow`. Interest accrual multiplies the stored scaled borrow and supply balances by their current indexes before applying the index cap. For a sufficiently large market, ordinary index growth causes `scaled * index` to exceed `i128::MAX` while the borrow index is still far below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.

Once the elapsed accrual interval produces that overflow, `last_timestamp` is never advanced. Every later operation attempts the same accrual and fails again, permanently freezing repayment, withdrawal, liquidation, recapitalization, and other pool mutations.

### Finding Description
The pool stores `borrowed`, `supplied`, and `revenue` as scaled RAY balances in `contracts/pool/src/cache/mod.rs`. Every market mutation loads a `Cache` and calls `ops::synced_market`, which invokes `interest::global_sync` before performing the operation.

`global_sync` calls `accrue_step` for each elapsed chunk. In `common/src/rates/simulate.rs`, `accrue_step` computes:

```rust
let borrowed_original = scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index);
let supplied_original = scaled_to_original(env, supplied, supply_index);
```

`scaled_to_original` is a checked fixed-point multiplication in `common/src/rates/scaling.rs`. The market totals are approximately `token_amount * 10^(27 - decimals)`. Therefore, an 18-decimal market with one billion whole tokens stores about `1e36` RAY of scaled supply. A 98% utilized book stores about `9.8e35` RAY of scaled debt.

When the borrow index grows beyond roughly 173.6×, `9.8e35 * borrow_index / RAY` exceeds `i128::MAX`. That occurs well before `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, whose cap is `1e9` times the initial index. The checked multiplication aborts before `update_borrow_index`, `commit`, or `mark_accrued` can persist progress.

Controller-facing paths such as `supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `flash_position`, `recapitalize`, and `update_indexes` all eventually enter the pool's synced-market path for the affected market. Consequently, once the overflow point is reached, there is no unprivileged or governance market operation that can advance `last_timestamp` or repair the market without an upgrade.

### Impact Explanation
This is permanent freezing of user funds and protocol insolvency risk:

- Suppliers cannot withdraw principal or accrued interest.
- Borrowers cannot repay or reduce risk.
- Liquidators cannot liquidate underwater positions.
- New supply, further borrowing, flash loans, recapitalization, revenue claiming, and parameter updates touching the market all revert during the mandatory accrual step.
- Governance cannot recover through `update_params` because it also accrues under the old model before writing a safer model.

The impact persists indefinitely because the failing accrual never commits `last_timestamp`. Time passing only increases the required accrual work; it cannot make the multiplication smaller.

### Likelihood Explanation
An unprivileged account can create the required state using only `supply` and `borrow`:

1. Supply a very large amount of an 18-decimal asset.
2. Supply collateral in another market.
3. Borrow as much of the first asset as the configured utilization and collateral limits permit.
4. Leave the market to accrue.

The exploit does not require oracle manipulation, callback reentry, token misbehavior, administrator assistance, or malformed parameters. Larger markets and higher utilization reach the overflow sooner, but the structural limit is `scaled_balance * index`, not an attacker-controlled rate parameter. Repository tests explicitly reproduce the condition with a one-billion-token 18-decimal market and demonstrate `MathOverflow` on `update_indexes`, followed by the same failure on `withdraw` and `repay`.

### Recommendation
Avoid multiplying the full scaled market totals by an unbounded index in the accrual hot path. At minimum:

- Use saturating or widened arithmetic for market-total projections before index multiplication.
- Detect that the resulting capped borrow index would overflow and cap the index before evaluating `scaled * index`.
- Persist a safe accrual boundary when overflow is detected so the market remains operable.
- Add an explicit invariant that `scaled_balance * MAX_*_INDEX` fits in the required fixed-point domain, or reject/list positions that can violate it.
- Cover the cliff with tests asserting that `repay`, `withdraw`, `liquidate`, `recapitalize`, and `update_params` remain executable after long elapsed intervals.

### Proof of Concept
The repository already contains a reproduction in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs`:

1. Create an 18-decimal market.
2. Supply `1_000_000_000 * 10^18` units to the market.
3. Supply sufficient collateral in another market.
4. Borrow `98%` of the first market.
5. Advance ledger time and call controller `update_indexes` for the market.
6. Accrual reaches `accrue_step`, where `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` overflows `i128`.
7. The transaction reverts with `MathOverflow` before the borrow-index cap engages.
8. Subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` calls fail with the same error because they must accrue first.
