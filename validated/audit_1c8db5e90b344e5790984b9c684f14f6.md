### Title
Flash-loan repayment is pulled from an attacker-chosen `receiver`'s standing allowance to the pool — any contract that leaves a residual approval to the pool can have tokens drained as flash fees - ([File: contracts/pool/src/ops/flash.rs](contracts/pool/src/ops/flash.rs))

### Summary
`flash_loan` lets the caller pick the `receiver` address. The pool pays principal to that receiver, invokes its `execute_flash_loan` callback, and then repays itself via `transfer_from(pool, receiver, pool, principal + fee)` gated only by `asset.allowance(receiver, pool) >= principal + fee`. Nothing binds `receiver` to the caller, to the initiator, or to this specific loan — the analogue of the PledgeManager bug where `data.signer` was never checked against `msg.sender`. A victim flash-receiver contract that leaves a standing/residual allowance to the pool can be named as `receiver` by anyone, and the pool will consume that allowance.

### Finding Description
In `apply`, the only validation of `receiver` is `require_wasm_receiver` (it must be a Wasm contract). The pool then transfers `amount` to `receiver`, calls `execute_flash_loan(initiator, asset, amount, fee, pool, data)` on it — with `initiator` and `data` fully attacker-controlled — and finally calls `collect_repayment`, which asserts `allowance(receiver, pool) >= total_repayment` and pulls via `transfer_from` [1](#0-0) . The repayment pull is authorized by the *stored allowance*, not by any per-call consent for this loan [2](#0-1) . Every other token pull in the protocol (`supply`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `recapitalize`, `multiply` initial payment) moves funds from the authenticated `caller` via `transfer`, which enforces `from.require_auth()`; the flash path is the only place a caller-supplied address's funds are spent on the strength of an allowance alone.

### Impact Explanation
The victim contract loses the flash fee on every invocation (principal is paid out to it and pulled back, netting zero; only `principal + fee` exceeds what it received). An attacker can repeat `flash_loan` calls naming the victim as `receiver` until the victim's residual allowance to the pool is exhausted, transferring those tokens to the pool as booked protocol revenue. The attacker also gains a free invocation of the victim's `execute_flash_loan` callback with attacker-chosen `data` and `initiator`, plus a forced temporary inflow of principal during that callback. This is theft of user funds (fee per call, cumulative) reachable by any unprivileged caller, exactly matching the "stranger spends a user's open approval" class.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a Wasm flash-receiver contract with a positive residual allowance to the pool. Receivers that approve exactly `amount + fee` inside their callback leave no residue, but any receiver that over-approves (e.g., approves a max or buffered amount to avoid per-call approvals, or a previous repayment consumed less than approved) leaves an allowance any third party can burn through this path. Conditional on that integration pattern, the attack is fully permissionless and repeatable.

### Recommendation
Do not let the initiator choose whose allowance is consumed. Options:
- Repay via balance check only: drop `transfer_from` and require the receiver to have pushed `principal + fee` back during the callback, then assert `balance(pool) == balance_after_repayment`.
- Or pull repayment from `initiator`/the caller-authenticated address rather than `receiver`.
- At minimum, require `receiver` (or `initiator`) `require_auth` equivalence — e.g., make the receiver's callback the only path that can establish the approval, and verify the allowance was created within this call's ledger (not satisfiable on-chain; prefer the balance-check design).

### Proof of Concept
1. Victim contract `V` implements `execute_flash_loan` and holds a residual token allowance of `A` to the pool (from an earlier over-approval).
2. Attacker calls `controller::flash_loan(caller=attacker, asset, amount=X, receiver=V, data=arbitrary)`.
3. Pool transfers `X` to `V`, invokes `V.execute_flash_loan(attacker, asset, X, fee, pool, data)`, then `collect_repayment` sees `allowance(V, pool) >= X + fee` and executes `transfer_from(pool, V, pool, X + fee)`.
4. `V` nets `X - (X + fee) = -fee`; the fee is booked as protocol revenue. Attacker repeats until `allowance(V, pool) < amount + fee`, draining up to `A` in fees — no signature or auth from `V` is required beyond the pre-existing allowance.

```rust
/// Pulls principal + fee via `transfer_from` after verifying allowance.
fn collect_repayment(
    env: &Env,
    asset: &token::Client,
    pool: &Address,
    receiver: &Address,
    terms: &FlashTerms,
) {
    assert_with_error!(
        env,
        asset.allowance(receiver, pool) >= terms.total_repayment,
        FlashLoanError::InvalidFlashloanRepay
    );
    asset.transfer_from(pool, receiver, pool, &terms.total_repayment);
    require_balance(env, asset, pool, terms.balance_after_repayment);
```
