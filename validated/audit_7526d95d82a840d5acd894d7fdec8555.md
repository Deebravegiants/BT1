### Title
Unchecked aggregate debt valuation overflow permanently freezes a mature market - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
`calculate_supplier_rewards` multiplies total scaled debt by both the old and newly accrued borrow indexes. Once `borrowed * new_borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX`, accrual panics before the borrow-index cap can be applied, permanently blocking operations that sync the market. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
Every accrual invokes `accrue_step`, which computes old and new aggregate debt through `borrowed.mul(...)`; `Ray::mul` ultimately panics with `MathOverflow` when the exact product does not fit `i128`. [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3)  An unprivileged caller reaches this through `controller::update_indexes(caller, assets)` with a `Vec<HubAssetKey>` containing the affected market; all other pool mutations also load and sync the market before proceeding. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

### Impact Explanation
After the market crosses the representable aggregate-debt boundary, every accrual reverts and the committed timestamp never advances, so the same overflow repeats on every subsequent attempt. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7)  The in-repo regression demonstrates that withdrawals and repayments both revert with `MathOverflow`, permanently freezing supplier funds and preventing borrowers or liquidators from clearing debt. [9](#0-8) 

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires an unusually large, long-lived market: the regression uses 1 billion tokens at 18 decimals, approximately 98% utilization, elevated caps, and repeated annual accruals. [10](#0-9)  No privileged call, oracle manipulation, malformed account state, or external attacker contract is required once such a market exists; any address can trigger the fatal accrual through the permissionless index-update path. [6](#0-5) 

### Recommendation
Bound `new_borrow_index` before computing `borrowed * new_borrow_index`, using the largest index for which the market's scaled debt remains representable, or widen aggregate debt/reward calculations to an internal representation that cannot overflow before applying `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. Ensure the accrual timestamp and capped index still commit so repayments, withdrawals, liquidations, and bad-debt cleanup remain executable. [11](#0-10) [12](#0-11) 

### Proof of Concept
1. A single unprivileged account supplies a very large amount of an 18-decimal borrowable asset and sufficient separate collateral.
2. The account calls `controller::borrow(caller, account_id, [(hub_asset, debt)], None)` to establish sustained high utilization.
3. After enough time elapses, any caller invokes `controller::update_indexes(caller, [hub_asset])`.
4. `calculate_supplier_rewards` panics while computing `borrowed * new_borrow_index`; the accrual rolls back and `last_timestamp` remains unchanged. [3](#0-2) [8](#0-7) 
5. Subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` calls hit the same pre-operation accrual and fail, as demonstrated by the existing regression test. [9](#0-8)
