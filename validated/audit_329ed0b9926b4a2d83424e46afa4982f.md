### Title
Borrower can pin a market at `max_utilization` to freeze all supplier withdrawals - (File: contracts/pool/src/guards.rs)

### Summary
The pool applies `require_utilization_below_max` to non-liquidation withdrawals, not only to borrows. An unprivileged borrower who pushes utilization to `max_utilization` makes every subsequent `withdraw` revert with `UtilizationAboveMax`, freezing supplier funds in that market until the borrow is repaid or liquidated.

### Finding Description
`pool::withdraw` burns supply shares and pays out cash, but runs the utilization guard on non-liquidation calls (`contracts/pool/README.md:141`, error table; `contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs` invokes the guard in `contracts/pool/src/guards.rs`). Because a withdrawal removes cash without removing debt, utilization only rises as suppliers exit — so once utilization is at or above `max_utilization`, *no* ordinary withdrawal can ever pass the check. The guard is therefore self-locking: it permits borrows up to the cap and then forbids the exits that would relieve the condition.

Reachable path for a single unprivileged address:
1. Supply collateral and call `Controller::borrow` (`contracts/controller/src/lib.rs`) in one or several legs until the target market's utilization equals `max_utilization` (`pool::borrow` permits borrows up to that cap — `contracts/pool/README.md:140`).
2. Every `Controller::withdraw` → `pool::withdraw` call in that market now reverts with `UtilizationAboveMax` (127).
3. The attacker maintains the state by keeping the position healthy (self-funded interest or overcollateralization). The documented escape routes are third-party `repay`/`liquidate`/`recapitalize`, all of which the attacker can contest: they can repay a slice and re-borrow in the next transaction, or simply hold HF ≥ 1.

This is the on-chain analogue of the report's connection-slot exhaustion: a bounded shared resource (headroom below `max_utilization`) is monopolized by one actor, and the defensive check placed on the exit path converts saturation into a freeze rather than a queue.

### Impact Explanation
Temporary freezing of funds: all suppliers of the affected market are unable to withdraw any amount while utilization is pinned at the cap. The attack is per-market and reversible, matching the report's "network unreachable only while the attack is ongoing" shape. The attacker's cost is borrow interest on a large position, but interest accrues to the pool, not to the victims, and does not unlock withdrawals — utilization stays ≥ max regardless of accrual since debt grows together with the index.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. The attacker needs sufficient collateral to borrow the market's free liquidity up to the cap, and pays borrow interest for the duration. However, on a thin market or one already near high utilization, the marginal borrow needed to reach `max_utilization` can be small, and the attacker risks only liquidation-avoidance maintenance. No privileged role, price manipulation, or timing race is required. Partial mitigation exists: liquidation of the attacker (if their HF drops) and third-party `repay`/`recapitalize` both relieve utilization, so persistence depends on the attacker's solvency maintenance.

### Recommendation
Do not apply `require_utilization_below_max` to withdrawals. A withdrawal is risk-reducing for the supplier and, given the code's own invariant that `net_settle`/repay-style reductions only move utilization in the safe direction, gating exits on a cap designed for entries serves no protective purpose — it only converts a full-utilization state into a freeze. Restrict the utilization guard to `borrow`, `create_strategy`, and other cash-outflow entries, and let `withdraw` be limited solely by `InsufficientLiquidity` (available cash) and `PoolInsolvent` (backing).

### Proof of Concept
1. Market M lists `USDC` with `max_utilization` = 95%. Suppliers have deposited S; cash ≈ S.
2. Attacker supplies ETH collateral and calls `controller.borrow(caller, acct, [(hub_asset(M, USDC), amount)])` repeatedly until `B/S ≥ 0.95` (each borrow passes `require_utilization_below_max` pre-check and `InsufficientLiquidity`).
3. Any supplier's `controller.withdraw(caller, acct, [(hub_asset(M, USDC), x)])` reaches `pool::withdraw`, which for a non-liquidation call runs `require_utilization_below_max` → post-call utilization is even higher → revert `UtilizationAboveMax` (127).
4. Suppliers remain frozen until the attacker repays, is liquidated, or someone else repays/recapitalizes; the attacker can re-borrow after any such relief since borrows are only gated *up to* the cap boundary.

Note on confidence: I confirmed the guard's presence on the withdraw path via `contracts/pool/README.md:141` and its implementations in `contracts/pool/src/guards.rs` / `contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs`, but I could not fully verify in this pass whether the guard evaluates pre- or post-withdrawal utilization, or whether the withdraw path has an early exemption when the market is already over the cap. If the guard only fires when the withdrawal itself crosses the cap, the freeze requires the attacker to keep utilization strictly above it, which the borrow step already achieves — the finding stands either way, but the exact gating semantics should be confirmed in `contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs` and `contracts/pool/src/guards.rs` during review.
