### Title
Bad-debt supply-index write-down leaves a phantom claim that drains fresh deposits — ([File: contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs](contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs))

### Summary
Analogous to the FastNetMon IHL bug — where a field below its protocol minimum (IHL < 5) causes the parser to read TCP/UDP data out of the IP header itself — the pool's bad-debt write-down clamps `supply_index` down to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` even when the write-down exceeds total supplied value. The stranded supply shares are thereafter valued at a floor index that no longer reflects real backing, so a holder of pre-wipeout shares can withdraw against a *future* depositor's cash. The in-repo test `test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` demonstrates the drain end-to-end at the cache layer.

### Finding Description
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` (contracts/pool/src/interest.rs, exercised by `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:298-369`) reduces `supply_index` by `bad_debt / supplied` and clamps the result up to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` when the write-down is larger than the total supplied claim. The clamp bounds how far the index falls, but it does **not** bound the residual claim: shares that should have been wiped out entirely retain `floor(scaled × SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW / RAY / 10^(27−d))` tokens of claim value.

The demonstrated sequence (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:317-369`):

1. Market has `supplied = 1_000_000 RAY` shares, `supply_index = RAY`, `cash = 0`.
2. `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` is applied with `2_000_000 RAY` of bad debt — twice the entire supplied value. The index clamps to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, leaving userA a positive `unscale_supply_floor` claim ("floor clamp leaves userA a phantom claim").
3. UserB supplies `c` tokens via `calculate_scaled_supply` / `mint_supply` / `credit_cash`; B's honest claim equals `c`.
4. UserA calls `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, scaled_a)`, `require_reserves` passes against B's fresh cash, and `debit_cash(gross)` pays A exactly `c` — the entire deposit. Pool cash ends at 0 while B's claim `c` is unbacked.

Reachability: the write-down path is driven by the permissionless liquidation/`clean_bad_debt` flow (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/bad_debt.rs` → pool bad-debt op) whenever residual debt remains after seizure; a price collapse or whole-unit seizure rounding can leave debt exceeding the supplied claim. The attacker only needs to (a) hold supply shares in the affected market, (b) let an insolvent account's bad debt be socialized past the floor, and (c) wait for a fresh deposit before withdrawing — all unprivileged entrypoints (`liquidate`/`clean_bad_debt`, victim `supply`, attacker `withdraw`).

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency: a stale supplier's phantom claim is paid out of the next depositor's principal, leaving the honest supplier's claim unbacked (cash = 0, claim > 0). This is a direct wealth transfer from future suppliers to pre-wipeout shareholders, sized up to the floor-residual claim.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a wipeout-scale bad-debt event (bad debt ≥ total supplied value after floor clamp) plus a subsequent deposit before the stranded holder exits — an uncommon but reachable ordering, and the attacker can themselves be the fresh depositor is not required; any victim deposit suffices. Medium likelihood.

### Recommendation
After clamping `supply_index` to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, reconcile the residual: either burn/scale down outstanding `supplied` shares so `floor(supplied × floor_index)` does not exceed real backing, or gate withdrawals in floored markets against a backing check that accounts for the write-down shortfall (e.g., a `require_reserves` that compares against claims valued at the unclamped index). At minimum, mark the market bad-debt-impaired so new `supply` is rejected until `recapitalize` restores backing.

### Proof of Concept
See `test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` at `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:317-369`, which executes the full sequence — floor-clamped write-down, honest deposit `c`, attacker withdraw-all paying exactly `c`, ending with `cash() == 0` and the depositor's claim unbacked.
