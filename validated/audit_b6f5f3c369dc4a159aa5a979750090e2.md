### Title
Attacker-shielded bad debt: a dust Aquarius-LP collateral leg plus an LP withdrawal makes an account unliquidatable and uncleanable - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs)

### Summary
The price aggregator refuses to price an Aquarius LP share when the underlying pool's value falls below the oracle's `min_pool_value_wad` floor, returning `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity`. Because the controller prices every collateral leg strictly during `liquidate` and `clean_bad_debt`, while `supply` requires no price at all, a borrower can plant a dust LP-token collateral leg and then — as an ordinary Aquarius liquidity provider — withdraw pool liquidity below the floor. From that point every liquidation and every permissionless bad-debt cleanup of the account reverts, so the position's debt accrues interest while collateral cannot be seized and bad debt cannot be socialized.

### Finding Description
- `aquarius::read` computes `pool_value_wad` and returns `Err(OracleError::InsufficientAquariusLiquidity)` when it is below `lp.min_pool_value_wad` (contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs, lines 118-122). This is a runtime check on live pool reserves and share supply — not an admission-time constant.
- Supply-side entry does not require a readable price: the threat model states "Supply needs no price, so an indebted borrower can add a dust leg of any listed collateral and choose which feed outage shields the account," and explicitly notes "liquidity providers can cause that outage by withdrawing pool value below `min_pool_value_wad`. The same leg blocks bad-debt cleanup and force-socialization" (docs/explanation/threat-model.md, DoS.1, line 364).
- Liquidation prices all collateral strictly, so one unpriceable leg aborts the whole pro-rata plan; `clean_bad_debt` likewise requires "valid required prices" (docs/reference/invariants.md, INV-LIQ-04, lines 465-469). The test `audit_supply_setup_blocks_liquidation_via_stale_dust_leg` proves the general shape: a dust leg planted while its feed is bad makes `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and even the borrower's own `withdraw` revert with `PRICE_FEED_STALE` until the feed recovers (tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_supply_stale_shield.rs).
- Unlike a stale Reflector feed (which recovers when the provider pushes), the LP variant is attacker-controlled: the borrower can keep the pool under the floor indefinitely by maintaining a large LP share and withdrawing whenever the pool value approaches `min_pool_value_wad`, or by sandwiching any liquidation attempt with a just-in-time withdrawal. Mainnet LP markets are configured with floors of 200k–1M USD WAD (e.g., `configs/mainnet/markets.json` AQUAUSDC_LP, `min_pool_value_wad: 200000000000000000000000`), so draining below the floor is feasible only for a whale LP — but nothing requires the attacker to hold all pool liquidity; they need only supply enough liquidity beforehand to withdraw below the floor at will.

### Impact Explanation
Protocol insolvency: the shielded account's debt keeps accruing interest while it cannot be liquidated at any health factor and cannot be bad-debt-cleaned (cleanup also reads all collateral prices). Suppliers' claims on that market grow unbacked; when the borrower eventually lets the price resolve (or the pool naturally stays under the floor), the account is already deeply insolvent and cleanup writes the loss down against suppliers via supply-index reduction. This is theft-adjacent freezing of collateral seizure plus forced socialization of a larger loss than timely liquidation would have produced.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires: (a) a listed collateral whose oracle is `AquariusLp`/`AquariusStableLp` with a `min_pool_value_wad` floor (mainnet has several), (b) the attacker controls enough of that pool's liquidity to push `pool_value_wad` under the floor by withdrawing, and (c) an open borrow. All actions — `supply` of a dust LP leg, `borrow`, and `withdraw` on Aquarius — are unprivileged. The cost is the LP position plus a dust deposit; the attacker can re-add liquidity at any time to unbrick their own `withdraw`/`repay` if desired. Severity is bounded by the size of debt the attacker can open against the LP collateral listing's LTV and caps.

### Recommendation
- Price LP collateral with a graceful degradation path: e.g., treat an `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` leg as zero-value collateral for health-factor and liquidation purposes (it is dust anyway), rather than reverting the entire transaction. The leg would then be seized/credited at zero value or skipped in the pro-rata plan.
- Alternatively, cap permissionless planting: require a minimum USD value (priced at supply time) for any new collateral leg, and/or allow liquidation/cleanup to exclude collateral legs below a dust threshold whose price is unavailable.
- At minimum, keep cleanup viable: let `clean_bad_debt` tolerate a single unpriceable leg by valuing it at zero, since the dust-threshold check already bounds collateral at ≤ $5.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance lists `AQUA_USDC_LP` (hub 3) as collateral with `AquariusLp { pool: P, min_pool_value_wad: F }` (already configured in `configs/mainnet/markets.json`).
2. Attacker deposits a large amount of liquidity into Aquarius pool `P` so they control > `F` worth of pool value.
3. `controller.supply(attacker, AQUA_USDC_LP, dust)` — succeeds; supply does not read the LP price.
4. `controller.supply(attacker, USDC, X); controller.borrow(attacker, ETH, D)` — normal healthy borrow.
5. Attacker calls `P.withdraw(...)` on Aquarius until `pool_value_wad < F`.
6. Any `controller.liquidate(liquidator, attacker, ...)` reverts: collateral pricing hits `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity`. `controller.clean_bad_debt(attacker_id)` reverts on the same price read. Even `attacker.withdraw` reverts, which is irrelevant — the attacker keeps debt unseizable for as long as the pool stays under the floor.
7. While shielded, interest accrues; when the attacker finally restores liquidity, HF is far below 1 and the residual loss is socialized at cleanup.

Mirrors `audit_supply_setup_blocks_liquidation_via_stale_dust_leg` and `audit_liquidate_and_clean_bricked_by_unpriceable_dust_leg` (tests/test-harness/tests/controller/), but with the outage caused by the attacker's own Aquarius withdrawal instead of oracle staleness — i.e., permanently attacker-controlled rather than dependent on a provider missing a heartbeat.
