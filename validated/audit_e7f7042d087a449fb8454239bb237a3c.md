### Title
Mixed-nature oracle sources can blend a 16-hour-stale fundamental price with a fresh market price - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs)

### Summary

The price aggregator bounds the timestamp difference between two sources only when both are labeled `FeedNature::Market`. If one source is labeled `Fundamental`, each leg only has to satisfy its own independent staleness window, allowing a very old fundamental observation to be averaged with a fresh market observation. An attacker can use the resulting skewed collateral price through `supply` followed by `borrow`, potentially withdrawing more debt than the true collateral value supports.

### Finding Description

`blend` computes `spread_bounded` as true only when both legs are `Market`; therefore, a `Market`/`Fundamental` pair skips `MAX_LEG_AGE_SPREAD_SECONDS` entirely. The outcome is stale only if either source individually exceeds its configured `max_stale_seconds` or the oracle-wide `max_price_stale_seconds`. The accepted result is then the midpoint of the two legs. [1](#0-0) 

Each feed independently checks `observation.timestamp` against its own staleness budget before becoming a `Reading`. [2](#0-1)  For RedStone/Xoxno-style feeds, the observation timestamp is the earlier of the package and write timestamps, so an old package cannot masquerade as fresh—but it remains usable for its entire configured window. [3](#0-2) 

This is reachable in production configuration. The executed mainnet USDT oracle combines a Reflector `Twap(3)` source with `max_stale_seconds: 3600` and a RedStone source explicitly labeled `"nature": "Fundamental"` with `max_stale_seconds: 57600`. [4](#0-3)  Consequently, a RedStone observation nearly 16 hours old can be averaged with a fresh Reflector observation, even though the two observations may describe materially different market prices.

The existing regression test demonstrates the exact exploit shape: a $1.00 anchor that is 82,800 seconds old is blended with a fresh $0.91 price under 10% tolerance, inflating reported collateral by more than 4% and enabling a 70,000 USDC borrow that fails with `INSUFFICIENT_COLLATERAL` when the anchor is fresh. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) 

### Impact Explanation

An overvalued collateral feed lets a borrower pass the controller’s post-action collateral and health-factor checks while borrowing against stale valuation. The borrower can withdraw the borrowed asset and leave the pool with debt whose true collateral value is below the accepted accounting value, causing lender loss or protocol bad debt if the position cannot be profitably liquidated.

### Likelihood Explanation

Exploitation does not require oracle compromise or privileged access. The attacker only needs a configured mixed-nature pair whose slower leg remains within its long freshness window while the fresh market leg has moved by up to the configured tolerance. Waiting for a delayed fundamental feed and a market move is externally dependent, but the deployed USDT configuration permits a timestamp disparity of roughly 15 hours between the two sources.

### Recommendation

Apply the two-leg timestamp-spread check to every blended pair, regardless of `FeedNature`, or configure an explicit smaller `max_age_spread_seconds` for mixed `Market`/`Fundamental` pairs. A fundamental anchor may warrant a longer absolute freshness window, but it should still be rejected when its timestamp diverges materially from the market leg used to form the final price. Regression coverage should keep the stale-anchor borrow scenario and additionally assert that mixed-nature pairs fail with `PriceFeedStale` once their timestamps exceed the spread limit.

### Proof of Concept

1. Configure a collateral price key with:
   - Source 0: Reflector market feed, `Twap(3)`, `max_stale_seconds = 3600`.
   - Source 1: RedStone feed with `nature = Fundamental`, `max_stale_seconds = 57600`.
   - Tolerance wide enough to accept roughly 10% divergence.
2. Publish a fresh market price of `0.91` while the fundamental leg retains a valid-but-old price of `1.00` from approximately 82,800 seconds ago.
3. As an unprivileged account, call `supply` with collateral amount `100_000 XLM`.
4. Call `borrow` for `70_000 USDC` from the same account.
5. The aggregator averages `1.00` and `0.91`, reports approximately `0.955` per XLM, and marks the result fresh because neither leg exceeded its own budget.
6. The borrow succeeds even though the equivalent transaction using a fresh `0.91` fundamental observation fails with `INSUFFICIENT_COLLATERAL`, demonstrating borrowing capacity inflated by the stale observation.

/// Evaluates `source` into a `Reading`, if it produces one. Combines the
/// source's own component-level staleness with staleness computed against
/// `oracle.max_price_stale_seconds`.
fn read_source(
    session: &mut Session,
    key: &PriceKey,
    oracle: &AssetOracle,
    source: &PriceSource,
    depth: u32,
) -> Result<Option<Reading>, OracleError> {
    let Some((observation, component_stale)) =
        evaluate_source(session, key, source, oracle.asset_decimals, depth)?
    else {
        return Ok(None);
    };
    let timestamp = observation.timestamp;
    let stale = component_stale
        || is_stale(
            session.now_secs(),
            timestamp,
            oracle.max_price_stale_seconds,
        );
    Ok(Some(Reading {
        price_wad: observation.price_wad,
        timestamp,
        stale,
        nature: source_nature(source),
    }))
