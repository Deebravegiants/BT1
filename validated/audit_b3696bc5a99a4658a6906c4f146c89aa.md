### Title
Stale pre-callback Aquarius LP valuation can collateralize fee-free flash debt - (File: contracts/controller/src/strategies/flash_position.rs)

### Summary
`flash_position` caches all account, debt, and declared-collateral prices before invoking an attacker-controlled receiver. The cached prices are not invalidated after the callback, while an Aquarius LP quote is derived from mutable pool reserves and share supply. A receiver can therefore reverse a temporary Aquarius reserve manipulation during the callback, transfer LP collateral, and have the final solvency check value it using the stale pre-callback price. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

### Finding Description
`process_flash_position` loads the relevant assets and calls `prefetch_strategy_prices` before creating and forwarding the debt. [4](#0-3) 

`Context::fetch_prices` only fills missing entries, and subsequent valuation reads use `cached_price`, so a price fetched before an external call remains fixed for the rest of the operation. [5](#0-4) 

The receiver callback runs after those prices are cached, and external contracts such as the router or an AMM may be called from that callback even though re-entry into the controller is prohibited. [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) 

For Aquarius LP collateral, the aggregator resolves both legs and then reads the pool’s current reserves and total shares before computing the LP’s fair value. [3](#0-2) 

After the callback, the controller measures the collateral balance delta, deposits it, and calls `strategy_finalize`, but the risk calculation still uses the retained pre-callback `Context` prices. [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8) [10](#0-9) 

Consequently, an attacker can inflate the LP quote before `flash_position`, restore the Aquarius pool’s reserves inside the receiver callback, and pass collateral checks based on a valuation that no longer exists. [5](#0-4) [11](#0-10) 

### Impact Explanation
`mint_and_forward` creates the debt and forwards the borrowed asset to the receiver before the callback returns, while finalization only requires the declared debt and supply positions to remain open and pass the cached-price risk gates. [2](#0-1) 

If the restored Aquarius reserves make the delivered collateral worth less than the borrowed amount, the account is economically undercollateralized and liquidation cannot recover the full forwarded value, leaving the protocol with bad debt or insolvency. [10](#0-9) 

### Likelihood Explanation
An unprivileged caller can open an account through `flash_position`, select a flashloanable debt market, provide a deployed Wasm receiver, and declare collateral minima. [12](#0-11) [13](#0-12) 

The exploit additionally requires an Aquarius LP token to be listed as collateral and priced through `AquariusLpSource`, and requires the attacker’s own Aquarius operations to move its fair value within the configured oracle bands before restoring it during the callback. [14](#0-13) [15](#0-14) 

The attack uses only the public `flash_position` entrypoint, an attacker-controlled receiver, and allowed trades against the pricing venue, without controller re-entry, privileged roles, leaked keys, or malformed token behavior. [16](#0-15) [17](#0-16) 

### Recommendation
Invalidate or reload `Context.token_prices` after `invoke_receiver` and before collateral deposit/final solvency checks, so valuation reflects post-callback Aquarius reserves rather than the pre-callback snapshot. [2](#0-1) [5](#0-4) 

Apply the same cache-boundary review to every strategy path that prefetches prices before a router, receiver, token, or Blend call, and preferably compare the refreshed required prices against the prefetched values within the protocol’s configured tolerances. [18](#0-17) [19](#0-18) 

### Proof of Concept
Prerequisites: market `D` is flashloanable, LP token `L` is collateralizable, and `L` is priced through a configured `AquariusLpSource`; the attacker controls receiver contract `R` holding enough `L` to satisfy the declared minimum. [20](#0-19) [3](#0-2) 

1. Temporarily change the configured Aquarius pool’s reserve state through attacker-owned liquidity or swap operations so the LP quote used for `L` is inflated while remaining acceptable to the oracle configuration. [21](#0-20) 

2. Call `flash_position(caller=attacker, account_id=0, spoke_id=S, mode=Multiply, debt=D, amount=B, receiver=R, collaterals=[(L, min)], refund_assets=[])`; the controller fetches and caches `L`’s inflated quote before forwarding `B` units of `D` to `R`. [4](#0-3) [22](#0-21) 

3. Inside `R.execute_flash_position`, call the Aquarius pool to reverse the reserve manipulation and then transfer at least `min` units of `L` to the controller. [6](#0-5) [23](#0-22) 

4. The controller measures the received `L`, deposits it, and finalizes the account, but `cached_price` still returns the inflated pre-callback quote rather than the restored post-callback quote. [8](#0-7) [24](#0-23) 

5. A successful transaction leaves positive debt backed by collateral whose fresh Aquarius-derived value is lower than required, demonstrating undercollateralized protocol debt. [25](#0-24) [26](#0-25)

## Flash position

`flash_position` creates or reuses a `Multiply`, `Long`, or `Short` account,
mints debt, transfers the measured receipt to the receiver, and invokes:

```rust
fn execute_flash_position(
    env: Env,
    initiator: Address,
    account_id: u64,
    asset: Address,
    amount: i128,
    fee: i128,
    amount_received: i128,
    controller: Address,
    data: Bytes,
);
```

The controller passes `fee` as `0`. The callback does not repay or approve
debt. It transfers purchased collateral from the receiver to the controller.
After return, the controller:

1. measures each declared collateral balance delta
2. requires every minimum and at least one positive deposit
3. deposits positive declared deltas on `account_id`
4. refunds positive deltas for declared refund assets to the caller
5. requires debt and collateral to remain open
6. runs LTV, health-factor, and minimum borrow collateral checks

An undeclared token left on the controller is neither deposited nor refunded.
Refunding unused debt tokens does not repay the minted debt.
```

## Reentrancy

The Soroban host rejects a call into a contract that is already on the call
stack with `Error(Context, InvalidAction)`. A callback therefore cannot call
the controller, including its views, and a `flash_loan` callback cannot call
the pool. The controller flash guard is a second layer: it blocks user
position verbs, strategy verbs, keeper updates, revenue claims, liquidation,
bad-debt cleanup, and recapitalization. Do not design a callback that calls
the controller.

The router is a separate contract and may be called inside a callback with its
own exact token authorization.
```

```markdown
`flash_position` requires a debt market with flash loans enabled and a deployed Wasm receiver other than the controller or pool. Its collateral declarations must meet all of these conditions:

- The list is nonempty and does not exceed the maximum supply-position count.
- Markets and underlying tokens are unique.
- All minimum amounts are nonnegative, with at least one positive minimum.
- Measured controller receipts from the callback meet every minimum.

Pool supply measures receipts again. Borrow and supply caps are checked when each pool result merges into the account. Supply and the declared debt position must remain open after finalization.

Refund assets must be unique, listed in the debt hub and account spoke, disjoint from collateral declarations, and bounded by the maximum supply-position count. Refund eligibility requires an active spoke and an existing listing; it does not check collateralizable, borrowable, paused or frozen flags. Only positive balance changes above pre-callback balances return to the caller. The debt token can be a refund asset, but refunding it does not repay the minted debt.
```
