### Title
Bad-debt wipeout clamps `supply_index` at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead of zeroing unbacked supplier claims, letting wiped depositors drain fresh deposits - ([File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs](contracts/pool/src/interest.rs))

### Summary
When seized bad debt equals or exceeds the total supplied value, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` floors the new supply index at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (RAY/1000) rather than zeroing or confiscating the now-unbacked supply shares. Surviving suppliers of a wiped market retain a residual claim worth 0.1% of their original deposit. The market keeps operating, and the next depositor's cash backs that phantom claim: the wiped supplier can withdraw real tokens, directly stealing the new deposit. The pool's own unit test demonstrates exactly this sequence and asserts that the fresh depositor's cash cannot cover his claim.

### Finding Description
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89` caps the socialized loss at `total_supplied_value`, computes a `reduction_factor` that reaches zero, then clamps the result with `new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW))`. The clamp exists to avoid a zero index, but it resurrects a claim: every unburned scaled share is re-valued at 0.1% after a 100% loss.

The borrow-side seize in `contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:24-28` calls this and then burns the debt with `cache.burn_debt(position)`, leaving `supplied` untouched and `cash = 0`. No path burns or compensates the residual shares (`absorb_supply_as_revenue` is only used for deposit-side seizes, seize.rs:29-31).

Attack path (all unprivileged):
1. Market has supplier Alice (1,000 units) and a fully uncollateralized/dust bad-debt position ≥ total supplied value (reachable via price moves into deep bad debt or `clean_bad_debt` on dust accounts below `BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD`).
2. Any unprivileged address calls controller `clean_bad_debt`/`liquidate`; the controller emits `PoolSeizeEntry{side: Borrow}` → pool `seize_positions` → `ops/seize.rs::apply` → index clamps to floor, cash = 0.
3. Bob supplies a fresh deposit into the still-live market (shares minted at the floored index via `calculate_scaled_supply`).
4. Alice calls `withdraw` with `amount = i128::MAX`; `resolve_withdrawal` unscales her shares at the floored index and `require_reserves`/`debit_cash` pay her out of Bob's cash. Bob's claim can never be filled.

The test `test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` in `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:430-495` executes steps 2–4 verbatim: `gross == deposit` (Alice extracts exactly Bob's deposit) and `cache.cash() < bob_claim` (Bob loses funds). The recapitalization test `pool_loss_floor_recapitalization_returns_only_unused_funding` confirms `recapitalize` only restores up to the floored claim value, not the general drain case.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency: any user depositing into a market after a full bad-debt socialization (or any attacker racing to be the first victim-depositor, then front-running by depositing to enable the exit) loses funds to legacy suppliers whose claims were already written off economically but not in the books. There is no storage flag, share burn, or supply guard distinguishing post-wipeout residual shares from honest ones.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a market to reach ≥100% bad debt (bad_debt ≥ total supplied value). This is an extreme but reachable state: low-liquidity markets, severe price crashes on collateral with outlier debt, or accumulation of dust bad debts cleaned via `clean_bad_debt` can push the write-down to the cap. Once reached, exploitation is a single ordinary `supply` by a victim and a single `withdraw` by the residual holder — no privileged role, oracle manipulation, or timing beyond deposit ordering is needed.

### Recommendation
On a capped write-down (bad debt ≥ total supplied value), do not leave a floored residual claim: either set the supply index to a true zero-equivalent state and mark the market as wiped (block `supply`/`withdraw` until `recapitalize` or governance reopens it), or atomically burn all remaining scaled supply shares (`supplied` → 0, `revenue` → 0) so no unbacked claim survives. Alternatively, gate `supply` in `contracts/pool/src/ops/supply.rs` when `supply_index == SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW && supplied > 0` and require `recapitalize`/admin reset before reopening the book.

### Proof of Concept
The existing test already proves the drain (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:430-495`):

```rust
// state: supplied = borrowed = 1000*RAY, cash = 0 (bad debt wiped via seize)
apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(&mut cache, bad_debt); // supply_index -> SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW
cache.burn_debt(borrow_scaled);

// Bob deposits `alice_stranded` fresh cash; his shares mint at the floor
cache.mint_supply(bob_scaled);
cache.credit_cash(deposit);

// Alice withdraw-all: resolves against floored index, pays out Bob's deposit
let (burn, gross) = cache.resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, alice_scaled);
cache.burn_supply(burn);
cache.debit_cash(gross);
assert_eq!(gross, deposit);           // Alice took Bob's entire deposit
assert!(cache.cash() < bob_claim);    // Bob's claim is unpayable
```

End-to-end via the controller, the reachable sequence is: `borrow`/price deterioration → `liquidate` or `clean_bad_debt` (unprivileged, emits `PoolSeizeEntry{side: Borrow}`) → victim `supply` → attacker `withdraw(account, i128::MAX)` on the hub/spoke leg.
