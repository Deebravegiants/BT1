### Title
Paused spoke asset blocks user repayment while only `no_seize` gates liquidation — users can be liquidated after flag relaxation before they can heal their position - ([File: contracts/controller/src/positions/mod.rs])

### Summary
Each spoke market carries `paused`, `frozen`, and `no_seize` flags in `SpokeAssetConfig` [1](#0-0) . The halt policy distinguishes three cases: `BlockOnEntry` (rejects `paused` and `frozen`), `AllowOnExit` (rejects `paused`), and `SeizureLeg` (rejects only `no_seize`) [2](#0-1) . User-initiated repayment (`repay`, and the prefunded repay used by flows such as `repay_debt_with_collateral`) enforces `AllowOnExit` and therefore reverts on a `paused` asset [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) . Liquidation seizure legs enforce only `SeizureLeg` and deliberately tolerate `paused`/`frozen` collateral (ADR-0008) [5](#0-4) .

### Finding Description
When governance pauses a debt market via `set_spoke_asset_flags` / `edit_asset_in_spoke` (ratcheting flags tight is immediate, no timelock) [6](#0-5) , borrowers holding that debt cannot call `repay` — `settle_repay` rejects the paused asset [7](#0-6) . They also cannot `supply` additional collateral of a paused asset, since supply uses `BlockOnEntry` via `require_can_supply` [8](#0-7) . If prices move against the account while the flag is set, its health factor can fall below 1 with the user unable to cure it.

When governance later clears the flag through the timelocked `relax_spoke_asset_flags` path [9](#0-8) , there is no grace period before `liquidate` becomes executable for the now-undercollateralized account. Any unprivileged liquidator can submit `liquidate` in the same ledger window as — or before — the victim's `repay`/`supply` transaction, and because a liquidation's debt-repay leg is itself subject to the `paused` flag, liquidation is impossible during the pause and fully enabled the moment the flag clears, forcing all queued liquidations into a race against the user's cure.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds: an account that became liquidatable purely during the pause window (through price movement it could not respond to) can be seized with the liquidation bonus before its owner has a realistic chance to restore solvency. The user loses collateral plus bonus despite being willing and able to repay.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a governance pause of a debt asset coinciding with adverse price movement — an infrequent but plausible operational event (pauses exist precisely for emergencies, which correlate with volatility). Once it occurs, the loss is near-certain: liquidator bots monitor flag epochs and HF and will win the race on relaxation. On Soroban there is no gas-price mempool bidding like Ethereum, which reduces classic frontrunning, but transaction ordering contention in the first ledger after relaxation still favors automated submitters over humans.

### Recommendation
Introduce a grace period between flag relaxation and liquidation eligibility for accounts that became liquidatable while a flag was set — e.g., record the flag epoch/timestamp at which an account first became liquidatable and require a minimum delay before `liquidate` accepts it, or temporarily set `no_seize` together with `paused` and release it on a delay after `paused` clears. Alternatively, allow `repay` on `paused` debt assets (repayment only reduces risk), changing the repay policy from `AllowOnExit` to a policy that tolerates `paused`.

### Proof of Concept
1. User A has a borrow of asset D collateralized by asset C; HF ≈ 1.1.
2. Governance calls `edit_asset_in_spoke`/`set_spoke_asset_flags` setting `paused = true` on D's spoke market (immediate ratchet, no timelock) [10](#0-9) .
3. User A submits `repay(account_id, [(D, amount)])` → reverts with `SpokeError::SpokeAssetPaused` at `enforce_spoke_asset_flags(.., AllowOnExit)` [11](#0-10) .
4. Price of C drops (or D rises); HF < 1. During the pause, liquidation is also infeasible on the D debt leg, so the position sits liquidatable-but-untouchable.
5. Governance executes the timelocked `relax_spoke_asset_flags` clearing `paused` [9](#0-8) .
6. In the first ledger after relaxation, liquidator B submits `liquidate` targeting User A; User A's `repay` lands in a later ledger or is dropped under contention. User A's collateral is seized with bonus despite the intent and funds to repay.

Uncertainty: I did not fully confirm whether the liquidation repay leg invokes `repay_prefunded_position` (which enforces `AllowOnExit`) — the grep hits in `liquidation/apply.rs` and `plan.rs` suggest flag enforcement there, but if liquidation repays via an ungated path, the scenario still holds: liquidation remains enabled on the collateral/seizure side while the user's `repay` is blocked, making the asymmetry even stronger (users liquidatable *during* the pause, not just after).

/// Listing halt policy for each leg.
///
/// Seizure uses `no_seize`: applying `paused` to pro-rata seizure would block
/// liquidation of every account holding the paused collateral (ADR-0008).
#[derive(Clone, Copy, PartialEq)]
pub(crate) enum FreezePolicy {
    /// New exposure: rejects `paused` and `frozen`.
    BlockOnEntry,
    /// User-initiated exit: rejects `paused`, tolerates `frozen`.
    AllowOnExit,
    /// Liquidation seizure: rejects `no_seize` only, tolerates `paused` and `frozen`.
    SeizureLeg,
}

fn settle_repay(
    env: &Env,
    account: &mut Account,
    payer: &Address,
    aggregated: &AggregatedPayments,
    cache: &mut Context,
) {
    let pool_addr = cache.cached_pool_address();
    let mut actions: Vec<PoolAction> = Vec::new(env);
    for (hub_asset, amount) in aggregated.iter() {
        enforce_spoke_asset_flags(
            env,
            cache,
            account.spoke_id,
            &hub_asset,
            FreezePolicy::AllowOnExit,
        );
        let position = get_debt_position_or_panic(env, account, &hub_asset);
        let amount_in = payments::transfer_amount_measured(
            env,
            &hub_asset.asset,
            payer,
            &pool_addr,
            amount,
            GenericError::AmountMustBePositive,
        );
        actions.push_back(make_pool_action(&position, amount_in, hub_asset.clone()));
    }
