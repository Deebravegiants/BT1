### Title
Bad-debt supply-index floor preserves wiped supplier claims and can socialize later cash to them - ([File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs])

### Summary
The bad-debt write-down clamps the supply index to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead of allowing a fully wiped market’s index to reach zero. The scaled shares of wiped suppliers remain in `supplied`, so after the write-down they retain a positive residual claim that was not backed by the debtor’s collateral.

### Finding Description
`clean_bad_debt` is permissionlessly reachable through `process_clean_bad_debt`, subject only to caller authorization, the flash-loan guard, debt existence, insolvency, and the collateral dust cap. The cleanup calls pool seizure logic that unscales the borrower’s debt, calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, and burns the debt shares. `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` caps the loss at total supplied value, computes the proportional reduction, but then raises the new index to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. A complete wipeout therefore produces index `RAY / 1000`, not zero, leaving every historical supply share with a nonzero claim even though the write-down treated the market as fully consumed.

The repository’s own regression test demonstrates the unsafe end state: after a total write-down, an old supplier has a stranded positive claim, fresh cash can make that claim payable, and withdrawing the stranded claim can take a later depositor’s cash while leaving the depositor’s booked claim under-backed. Production `supply` does check `require_backed_market` before minting, so this residual normally blocks direct supply until recapitalization; however, the stranded accounting state still causes recapitalized cash to be allocated to a claim that should have been fully written off, and any path that admits cash while the floor-created residual is backed by still-outstanding market debt can transfer the residual loss to later liquidity.

### Impact Explanation
A permissionless `clean_bad_debt` call can permanently corrupt market accounting by preserving unbacked supplier claims after a complete write-down. Those claims remain withdrawable once cash becomes available, diverting recapitalization or later admitted liquidity away from current suppliers. This can cause theft of user funds or protocol insolvency: the pool can satisfy a claim that the bad-debt write-down was supposed to eliminate, while current suppliers remain with shares whose value depends on insufficient or subsequently depleted backing.

### Likelihood Explanation
The trigger requires a borrower account to become deeply insolvent so its remaining collateral is below the dust threshold, after which any unprivileged caller can invoke `clean_bad_debt(caller, account_id)`. Volatile collateral price movement or accrued debt can create that state without privileged action. The attacker does not need control of governance, the pool, or the oracle; they need only submit the permissionless cleanup at the appropriate time. Exploiting the stranded claim additionally requires cash to enter or be booked in the affected market after the write-down, such as recapitalization or another allowed liquidity path while the market passes the pool’s backing guard.

### Recommendation
Do not clamp a bad-debt write-down to a nonzero supply index when the capped loss consumes the entire supplied value. Represent complete supplier wipeout explicitly by burning or otherwise zeroing the affected supply claims during cleanup, or set the index to zero and ensure every conversion, division, and account readout treats a zero-index market as fully wiped without panicking. If a nonzero floor is retained for arithmetic reasons, remove the residual claims from total `supplied` or maintain a separate tombstone/shortfall amount so wiped shares cannot later receive new cash. Add an end-to-end controller test covering `clean_bad_debt` followed by `recapitalize` or `supply`, then attempted withdrawal by the wiped supplier and a new supplier.

### Proof of Concept
1. An honest supplier calls `supply(caller, account_id, spoke_id, [(hub_asset, amount)])`, creating a scaled supply position in the market.
2. A borrower obtains debt in that same market and later becomes insolvent with remaining collateral below the cleanup dust threshold.
3. Any unprivileged caller invokes `clean_bad_debt(caller, borrower_account_id)`. `socialize_bad_debt` admits the account, and pool `seize_positions` invokes `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, burning the debt.
4. If the debt write-down consumes all supplied value, `new_supply_index` is raised to `RAY / 1000`. The wiped supplier’s scaled shares remain in `supplied`, so `unscale_supply_floor` still returns a positive stranded claim.
5. Once cash is credited to the market through recapitalization or another permitted path, the wiped supplier calls `withdraw(caller, supplier_account_id, [(hub_asset, 0)], Some(recipient))`. The zero amount requests full withdrawal; pool withdrawal resolves the stranded shares to a positive gross amount, checks cash reserves, debits cash, and transfers it to the recipient.

pub(crate) fn apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache: &mut Cache, bad_debt: Ray) {
    let total_supplied_value = cache.supplied().mul(cache.env(), cache.supply_index());

    if total_supplied_value == Ray::ZERO {
        return;
    }

    let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
    let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);

    let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
    let new_supply_index = cache
        .supply_index()
        .mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);

    cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
}

/// Authorizes permissionless dust-gated cleanup outside flash loans.
pub(crate) fn process_clean_bad_debt(env: &Env, caller: &Address, account_id: u64) {
    caller.require_auth();
    validation::require_not_flash_loaning(env);
    clean_bad_debt_standalone(env, account_id);
}

/// Admission condition for bad-debt socialization.
#[derive(Clone, Copy, PartialEq)]
enum BadDebtGate {
    /// Permissionless: insolvent *and* collateral at or below the dust threshold.
    DustCapped,
    /// Owner-only: insolvent alone, with no cap on the collateral left behind.
    InsolventOnly,
}

/// Requires open debt and the selected insolvency gate, then cleans up the account.
fn socialize_bad_debt(env: &Env, account_id: u64, gate: BadDebtGate) {
    let mut cache = Context::new(env);
    let account = storage::get_account(env, account_id);

    assert_with_error!(
        env,
        !account.borrow_positions.is_empty(),
        CollateralError::DebtPositionNotFound
    );

    let totals = risk::calculate_account_risk_totals(
        env,
        &mut cache,
        &account.supply_positions,
        &account.borrow_positions,
    );

    let admits = match gate {
        BadDebtGate::DustCapped => {
            is_socializable_bad_debt(totals.total_debt, totals.total_collateral)
        }
        BadDebtGate::InsolventOnly => totals.total_debt > totals.total_collateral,
    };
    assert_with_error!(env, admits, CollateralError::CannotCleanBadDebt);

    bad_debt::execute_bad_debt_cleanup(env, &mut cache, account_id, &account, &totals);
}

/// Socializes insolvent debt when remaining collateral is at or below the dust cap.
pub(crate) fn clean_bad_debt_standalone(env: &Env, account_id: u64) {
    socialize_bad_debt(env, account_id, BadDebtGate::DustCapped);

/// Asset units by which supplier claims exceed cash + debt (0 if solvent).
pub(crate) fn backing_shortfall(cache: &Cache) -> i128 {
    let supplied_claim = cache.unscale_supply_floor(cache.supplied());
    let outstanding_debt = cache.unscale_borrow_ceil(cache.borrowed());
    let backing = cache.cash().saturating_add(outstanding_debt);
    supplied_claim.saturating_sub(backing).max(0)
