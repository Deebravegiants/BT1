### Title
Supply-index floor clamp resurrects wiped suppliers' freed claims to drain fresh deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` socializes unpaid debt by scaling down `supply_index`, but clamps the result at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY / 1_000`). When bad debt meets or exceeds total supplied value — a full wipeout — the index is clamped *up* to the floor instead of zeroing every scaled supply share. Surviving scaled positions therefore retain a phantom claim analogous to a use-after-free: value that was "freed" (socialized away) is still reachable through the stale share balances the controller continues to hold. Once a fresh depositor brings cash into the emptied market, any wiped supplier can withdraw real tokens against the floored index and drain the new deposit.

### Finding Description
In `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89`, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes `new_supply_index = supply_index * reduction_factor` and then applies `.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW))` at line 88. `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` is `RAY / 1_000` (`common/src/constants/pool.rs:9`), and the constant's own docs admit the floor only applies to bad-debt write-downs. In a wipeout (`capped == total_supplied_value`), `reduction_factor` is 0, so the index should become 0 — instead it becomes `RAY/1000`, leaving every scaled share worth ~1/1000 RAY of asset.

This state is reachable permissionlessly: `seize::apply` invokes it for `AccountPositionType::Borrow` entries (`contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:24-28`), which the controller calls during bad-debt cleanup. `process_clean_bad_debt` is permissionless (dust-gated insolvency, `contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs:196-200`, `240-243`), and `process_liquidation` triggers the same path via `check_bad_debt_after_liquidation` (line 135). Cleanup only adjusts the pool index — the wiped suppliers' scaled positions remain in their accounts.

On the withdrawal side, `gate_and_debit` in `contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:111-119` enforces `require_reserves`, `require_utilization_below_max`, and `require_supply_for_debt` — none of which detects the floored-index resurrection (borrowed is 0 after a wipeout, so utilization and supply-for-debt checks pass; reserves pass once any new cash exists). The codebase's own tests encode the attack mechanics: `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` and `test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-496`) show a stranded claim paying out exactly a fresh depositor's cash, leaving the honest depositor's claim unbacked.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. After a wipeout, each surviving scaled share claims `scaled * RAY/1000` of underlying that was already socialized away. The first wiped supplier to withdraw after fresh liquidity arrives extracts the fresh depositor's tokens; total obligations (`unscale_supply_floor(supplied)`) exceed cash, so last-out depositors lose funds. An attacker can position for this by holding a supply position in a risky market and racing to withdraw after a bad-debt event once new supply lands — or even by supplying the "fresh" cash themselves is not required; any victim deposit enables the payout.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a full bad-debt wipeout in a market (debt ≥ total supplied value), which is an extreme but reachable condition — `clean_bad_debt` is permissionless and an attacker can engineer insolvency through borrowed positions on volatile collateral within oracle bands, or simply wait for organic insolvency. The attacker must (a) hold or acquire scaled supply shares before/through the wipeout, and (b) withdraw before or alongside fresh deposits. No privileged role is needed.

### Recommendation
When `reduction_factor` would push `supply_index` below the floor, zero out the market correctly instead of clamping: e.g., set `supply_index` such that the residual is honored only up to actual remaining value, or explicitly burn/flag stranded scaled shares during cleanup. Alternatively, add a withdraw-time guard that rejects claims against a supply index sitting exactly at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` after a wipeout, or track a per-market "wiped" flag so positions' scaled balances are invalidated rather than left dereferenceable.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice supplies `S` to market M; Bob borrows against collateral that becomes worthless (or via an engineered insolvent account).
2. Any caller invokes `clean_bad_debt(account_id)` (permissionless, dust-gated) or a liquidation triggers `check_bad_debt_after_liquidation`. `seize::apply` → `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` caps the write-down at total supplied value and clamps `supply_index` to `RAY/1000`.
3. Alice's scaled shares remain on her account with claim `S * RAY/1000 > 0`.
4. Bob (or any victim) deposits `D` fresh units into M; cash = D.
5. Alice calls `withdraw` for her full position: `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, S)` → `gross = S * RAY/1000`, `require_reserves` passes against D, `require_utilization_below_max`/`require_supply_for_debt` pass (borrowed = 0), and the pool transfers `gross` to Alice — draining Bob's deposit while Bob's own claim is now unbacked.

This mirrors the existing test at `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-428` (`test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard`), which demonstrates `gross == fresh_cash` and `cash < fresh_claim` after the sequence.

**Uncertainty noted:** I could not fully verify whether the controller's bad-debt cleanup also deletes the wiped suppliers' deposit positions from their accounts (which would close the attack), or whether an additional production guard beyond the three checks in `gate_and_debit` blocks stranded withdrawals. The exploit stands if, as the test names imply, the only protection is the absence of a dedicated supply guard in this path.
