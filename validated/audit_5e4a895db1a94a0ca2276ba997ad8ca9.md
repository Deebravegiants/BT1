### Title
Emporium wallet-authorization signature omits `relay` and `feeStructure.feeToken`, letting an unprivileged party reroute/redenominate signer-authorized fees - (`contracts/external-actions/emporium/upgradeable/EmporiumUpgradeable.sol`)

### Summary
For Emporium-only transactions (`externalActionId == HINKAL_EMPORIUM_ACTION_ID` with `erc20TokenAddresses.length == 0`), Hinkal verifies a trivial ZK proof (`MainEVMCircuitMin`) that performs no real cryptographic authorization, so the *only* authorization for the wallet-funded `ops` execution and the associated fee payment is the EIP-712 signature checked in `verifyWallet`. That signature commits only to `(emporiumMessage, opsHash, maxFee, deadline)` — it never binds `circomData.relay` or `circomData.feeStructure.feeToken`. Anyone who obtains a valid, not-yet-consumed `(v, r, s)` blob for a signed `EmporiumStack` can resubmit it inside a self-crafted `circomData` that redirects the relay-fee recipient and/or the token the flat fee is paid in, exactly as in the referenced report where a signature not binding the full request payload is replayed with different parameters.

### Finding Description
`EmporiumUpgradeable.verifyWallet` recovers the signer from: [1](#0-0) 

The digest covers `emporiumMessage`, the hash of `stack.ops`, `maxFee`, and `deadline` only — it does not include `circomData.relay` or `circomData.feeStructure.feeToken`, and the only numeric constraint applied afterwards is: [2](#0-1) 

`payRelayFees` then pays `feeStructure.flatFee` in whichever token equals `feeStructure.feeToken`, to whichever address is `circomData.relay`, when the wallet signer path is used: [3](#0-2) 

Normally, `circomData.relay` and `circomData.feeStructure` are protected because they are folded into `calldataHash`: [4](#0-3) 

and `calldataHash` is in turn folded into the EdDSA `signedMessageHash` that the shielded-pool circuit verifies: [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

However, when `erc20TokenAddresses.length == 0`, `CircomDataBuilder.formInputForCircom` routes to the minimal-input builder and the minimal circuit is used instead: [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7) 

`MainEVMCircuitMin` never instantiates `SignatureVerifier`, never checks `spendingPublicKey`/`nullifyingPrivateKey`, and its only output (`message`) is an unconstrained function of a free private witness (`messageSeed`). Consequently a Groth16 proof for this circuit can be generated by anyone for any chosen public inputs (`outTimeStamp`, `calldataHash`) without knowledge of any secret — `getHashedCalldata` only needs to be internally consistent with the attacker's own crafted `circomData`, which is trivially satisfiable since the attacker controls the whole struct. `performHinkalChecks` accepts this proof as long as the hash matches: [9](#0-8) 

The `originalSender`/`relay` check permits either a relay-based call or a direct self-call by the caller (`circomData.originalSender == sender && circomData.relay == address(0)`), so no privileged relay role is required — any unprivileged EOA can be `msg.sender` of `Hinkal.transact`: [10](#0-9) 

Put together: the wallet-owner's off-chain EIP-712 signature is the *sole* real authorization for the Emporium-only, wallet-funded flow, yet it doesn't bind `relay`/`feeToken`. Anyone who has seen that signature (e.g., in a mempool transaction, or from a prior attempt whose `usedMessages[emporiumMessage]` write got reverted because a later `ops` call failed) can wrap it in their own `circomData`/proof pair that sets `circomData.relay` to their own address and/or `circomData.feeStructure.feeToken` to whichever token in the signer's wallet is most valuable, capping only the numeric `flatFee`, not its denomination or destination.

### Impact Explanation
This lets an unprivileged third party divert a wallet signer's pre-authorized flat fee to their own address, and/or force that fee to be paid in a token the signer never intended (up to the numeric `maxFee` cap but in the most expensive asset available), i.e. an asset movement out of the signer's wallet that the signer never actually authorized in those terms. This matches the "High" bucket: executing calls or moving assets a wallet owner or prover never authorised / theft of relay fees.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitability depends on: (1) obtaining a signed-but-unconsumed `EmporiumStack` (visible from a pending mempool transaction, or recoverable after a reverted attempt that never flipped `usedMessages`), and (2) the ability to front-run/resubmit before the legitimate transaction consumes the `emporiumMessage` nonce. Producing the accompanying trivial proof for `MainEVMCircuitMin` requires no secret and is cheap for any EOA to compute off-chain. This makes the likelihood moderate — it is a MEV/front-running-style race but requires no privileged role, unlike the "malicious relayer" assumptions this scan explicitly excludes.

### Recommendation
Include `circomData.relay` and `circomData.feeStructure` (feeToken, flatFee, variableRate) directly inside `EMPORIUM_SIGNATURE_TYPEHASH` so the wallet signer explicitly authorizes who is paid and in what token, rather than relying on `calldataHash`/EdDSA binding that is absent for the `MainEVMCircuitMin` path.

### Proof of Concept
1. Wallet owner signs an `EmporiumStack` (`v,r,s`, `signerAddress`, `ops`, `maxFee`, `deadline`) intending `circomData.relay = relayA` and `circomData.feeStructure.feeToken = USDC`.
2. Attacker observes this signed payload before it lands on-chain (mempool) or recovers it from a reverted prior attempt.
3. Attacker crafts their own `circomData` with `erc20TokenAddresses = []`, `externalActionData = {externalAddress: Emporium, externalActionId: HINKAL_EMPORIUM_ACTION_ID, externalActionMetadata: abi.encode(sameStack)}`, but `circomData.relay = attacker`, `circomData.feeStructure.feeToken = WETH` (or another valuable token the wallet holds), and `circomData.feeStructure.flatFee` at or below `stack.maxFee`.
4. Attacker computes `calldataHash = CircomDataBuilder.getHashedCalldata(circomData)` and generates a trivially-satisfiable Groth16 proof for `MainEVMCircuitMin` using the resulting public inputs (no secret knowledge required).
5. Attacker calls `Hinkal.transact` themselves (`originalSender = attacker`, `relay = address(0)` is also fine, or route via `relay = attacker` in the relayed branch) ahead of the legitimate submission.
6. `performHinkalChecks` and the proof verification pass; `EmporiumUpgradeable.verifyWallet` recovers `stack.signerAddress` successfully (the EIP-712 digest still matches because it never included `relay`/`feeToken`); `payRelayFees` pays the flat fee, in WETH, to the attacker's address instead of `relayA`/USDC as the signer intended.
