### Title
Direct token donations to AMM vaults let a pool creator + attacker inflate a fresh pool and dilute the next depositor's LP mint - ([File: program/src/processor.rs])

### Summary
Raydium's `process_deposit` computes minted LP tokens from the *live* SPL token balances of `amm_coin_vault`/`amm_pc_vault` rather than from an internally tracked reserve variable. Because those vaults are ordinary token accounts, anyone can transfer ("donate") tokens into them outside of any AMM instruction. Combined with `process_initialize2`, which lets an unprivileged caller create a pool with an arbitrarily small (but valid) initial liquidity, this reproduces the "donation to LiquidityPool manipulates share calculation" bug class from the external report: a creator can seed a pool with minimal LP supply, donate large amounts to the vaults to inflate the LP-token value, and cause a subsequent legitimate depositor's LP mint to round down disproportionately, effectively transferring value to the manipulator upon withdrawal.

### Finding Description
`process_initialize2` mints LP supply as `liquidity - 10^lp_decimals`, where `liquidity = sqrt(pc_vault.amount * coin_vault.amount)`, and stores the *full* `liquidity` value (including the unminted "dead" portion) as `amm.lp_amount`: [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

While this dead-share mechanism raises the bar compared to a naive first-depositor-inflation setup, it does not prevent the attack: the pool creator (a normal, unprivileged instruction caller) only needs to choose `init_coin_amount`/`init_pc_amount` large enough to clear the `10^lp_decimals` floor (`InitLpAmountTooLess` check), which can still leave them holding almost the entire (tiny) circulating LP supply: [3](#0-2) 

After initialization, `process_deposit` reads the *current* vault balances via `unpack_token_account` and derives `total_coin_without_take_pnl`/`total_pc_without_take_pnl` from them: [4](#0-3) 

The LP mint amount for a depositor is then computed purely from that live balance and the current `amm.lp_amount` (a floor-rounded ratio): [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

Because these vault balances are read directly from the SPL token accounts rather than from an internally-tracked reserve, a third party can inflate `total_coin_without_take_pnl`/`total_pc_without_take_pnl` at will via a plain SPL `Transfer` to `amm_coin_vault`/`amm_pc_vault` — no AMM instruction is required for this step. If this donation happens after a pool is created with a very small `amm.lp_amount`, a subsequent depositor's `mint_lp_amount = deduct_amount * amm.lp_amount / total_without_take_pnl` is pushed toward the rounding floor, so a legitimate depositor providing proportionally large token amounts receives far fewer LP shares (in the worst case very close to zero) than their deposit is worth, while the manipulator — who holds nearly the entire pre-donation LP supply — captures a disproportionate share of the pool's value (including the victim's freshly deposited tokens) on withdrawal.

The code does guard against the case where `mint_lp_amount == 0` by reverting before any token transfer occurs: [7](#0-6) 
This prevents the exact "victim loses all deposited tokens for zero shares" scenario from the report, but it does not prevent the "first depositor inflation" style dilution, since `mint_lp_amount` can round to a small-but-nonzero, disproportionately low value rather than exactly zero.

### Impact Explanation
An attacker who creates a pool and controls a large fraction of its (deliberately tiny) LP supply can, via ordinary token transfers to the vault, manipulate the LP-share-to-reserve ratio experienced by the next legitimate liquidity provider, causing that provider to receive fewer LP tokens than their deposited value warrants. Upon withdrawal, the attacker extracts a share of the victim's deposit. This constitutes value theft from an LP and unbacked/imbalanced LP-share accounting, matching the Medium/High severity criteria (concrete theft of LP funds via manipulated accounting) — though bounded in practice by the `10^lp_decimals` minimum-liquidity floor enforced at `Initialize2`, which raises (but does not eliminate) the cost of the attack for tokens with typical decimal counts.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is low-to-moderate: the attacker must (1) be the pool creator or otherwise control the pool immediately after creation with a very small `amm.lp_amount`, (2) have capital sufficient to donate large token amounts to skew the vault balances, and (3) get a victim to deposit into the still-shallow, price-manipulated pool before others provide meaningfully more liquidity. This is realistic for fresh/low-liquidity pools, which are common immediately after `Initialize2`, but requires deliberate timing and capital outlay, and does not work once a pool has substantial organic `amm.lp_amount`.

### Recommendation
Track pool reserves internally (e.g., store `coin_reserve`/`pc_reserve` fields on `AmmInfo` updated only by AMM instructions) instead of trusting the live SPL token account balances for LP-mint math in `process_deposit`/`process_withdraw`, so that external donations cannot influence share issuance. Alternatively/additionally, enforce a materially larger minimum LP supply at `Initialize2` (beyond the current `10^lp_decimals` dead-share floor) and/or reject deposits whose `mint_lp_amount` falls below a sane minimum ratio of the requested deposit value, not just `mint_lp_amount == 0`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker calls `Initialize2` with `init_coin_amount`/`init_pc_amount` chosen so that `liquidity = sqrt(init_coin_amount * init_pc_amount)` is only slightly greater than `10^lp_decimals`; attacker receives `user_lp_amount = liquidity - 10^lp_decimals` LP tokens (e.g., 1 LP token), while `amm.lp_amount` is set to the full `liquidity` value. [1](#0-0) 
2. Attacker performs a plain SPL `Transfer` (no AMM instruction) sending a large amount of the coin/pc mint tokens directly into `amm_coin_vault_info`/`amm_pc_vault_info`, inflating their balances without changing `amm.lp_amount`.
3. Victim calls `Deposit` with a legitimate, proportionally large amount matching the now-inflated vault ratio; `mint_lp_amount` is computed from `total_coin_without_take_pnl`/`total_pc_without_take_pnl` (now inflated by the donation) divided into the small `amm.lp_amount`, yielding a disproportionately small number of LP shares for the victim's deposit. [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8) 
4. Attacker withdraws their LP position, redeeming a share of the pool (including part of the victim's deposit) proportional to their now-relatively-larger share of `amm.lp_amount`, extracting value contributed by the victim.
