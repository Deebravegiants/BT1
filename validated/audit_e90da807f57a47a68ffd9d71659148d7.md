### Title
Unprivileged permanent market freeze: accrual panics on i128 overflow inside `scaled_to_original` before the borrow-index cap can engage - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
The ncurses CVE is an illegal-address-access crash (remote DoS). The analog on XOXNO Lending is an arithmetic-overflow panic in the interest-accrual path: once a large market's borrow index crosses the i128 RAY-value ceiling, every state-mutating verb on that pool panics inside `scaled_to_original` during `global_sync`, permanently freezing supplier funds — and the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap never gets a chance to stop it.

### Finding Description
`global_sync` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33` runs chunked accrual before every pool operation. Each chunk calls `accrue_step` (`common/src/rates`), which computes `scaled_to_original` on `borrowed × borrow_index` as a RAY value. Ray values are i128; a billion-token market at 18 decimals is ~`1e36` raw ray, so the ceiling (~`1.7e38`) is only ~170x of principal. When `borrowed × borrow_index` exceeds i128::MAX, `scaled_to_original` panics with `MathOverflow`.

The protocol defines a borrow-index cap `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (`common/src/constants/pool.rs`), but the overflow happens inside the value computation *before* the new index is ever compared to the cap — the existing test-harness test explicitly asserts `last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` when the panic fires (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:349-353`).

Because `global_sync`/`needs_accrual` runs first in `withdraw`, `repay`, `borrow`, `liquidate`-related settlement, and `update_indexes`, once the cliff is reached the panic is unrecoverable: `Err` paths never skip accrual. The test confirms `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` both fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW` after the cliff (`large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:354-356`).

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds / insolvency-grade DoS on the affected market: suppliers can never withdraw, borrowers can never repay, liquidators can never liquidate, and `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` paths that touch the same cache accrual also revert. All tokens held by that pool's (hub, token) book are bricked. This matches "permanent freezing of funds."

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by unprivileged addresses: any user can `supply` and `borrow` on the controller. The driver is market size × utilization × time on a steep rate curve (XLM-style curve at ~98% utilization, 18-decimal token). `lift_caps`/supply caps are governance parameters, not attacker barriers per se — but reaching the cliff requires a whale-scale market sustained at high utilization for years, and caps/interest-rate curves are configurable, so exploitability depends on deployed parameters. That makes it Medium rather than High: conditional on configuration and scale, but once triggered it is irreversible and requires no privileged role.

### Recommendation
In `accrue_step` (and the shared simulate path), compute the accrual cap check before unscaling: clamp `borrow_index` to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` and short-circuit when the cap is hit, and/or perform the `borrowed × borrow_index` product with explicit overflow handling that saturates instead of panicking. Alternatively, bound the per-chunk index growth so `scaled_to_original` can never overflow while `borrowed` is nonzero, and add a regression test that a market at the cliff still allows repay/withdraw.

### Proof of Concept
The repository already contains an executable PoC: `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361`. It supplies `1e9 × 10^18` units of an 18-decimal token, borrows 98% of it under the XLM curve, advances time year-by-year, and observes `update_indexes` fail with `MathOverflow` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`; subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` fail identically, demonstrating permanent market freeze.
