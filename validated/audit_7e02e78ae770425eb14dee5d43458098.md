### Title
Unchecked RAY-value multiplication can permanently freeze an oversized high-utilization market - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
Pool interest accrual computes total supplied and borrowed RAY value with non-saturating `Ray::mul` before enforcing the configured borrow-index cap. A sufficiently large market can therefore cross the `i128` value ceiling before `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, after which every operation that accrues that market panics. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

### Finding Description
The pool splits elapsed time into bounded accrual chunks and calls `accrue_step` with the market’s scaled supply, scaled debt, and current indexes. [4](#0-3) 

Inside index accounting, `update_supply_index` multiplies `supplied` by the old supply index, while `calculate_supplier_rewards` multiplies `borrowed` by both old and new borrow indexes. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

Those calls resolve to `Ray::mul`, which calls the checked fixed-point multiplication helper and converts an overflow into `GenericError::MathOverflow`; they do not saturate the computed RAY value. [3](#0-2) [7](#0-6) 

The intended cap is applied only to `new_index` after `old_index * interest_factor`, and does not protect the separate `scaled * index` value calculations used to calculate utilization, supplier rewards, or supply-index growth. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

The in-repository long-horizon test demonstrates this boundary explicitly: a one-billion-token, 18-decimal market at 98% utilization reaches a `MATH_OVERFLOW` below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and subsequent withdraw and repay calls fail with the same error. [8](#0-7) 

### Impact Explanation
This creates a permanent market freeze once scaled principal times the interest index exceeds the `i128` domain. Because controller-visible supply, borrow, repay, withdraw, liquidation, and index-update paths load or synchronize the market before executing their specific leg, users cannot exit collateral or reduce debt in that market once the overflow condition is reached. [9](#0-8) [10](#0-9) 

The regression test proves the practical consequence: after accrual fails, both `withdraw` and `repay` revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, while the stored borrow index remains below its intended maximum. [11](#0-10) 

The resulting impact is permanent freezing of supplier funds and borrower repayment/liquidation processing for the affected market, matching an allowed impact category. [12](#0-11) 

### Likelihood Explanation
The path is permissionless in the sense that ordinary users create the prerequisite state through `Controller::supply`, `Controller::borrow`, and later `Controller::update_indexes`, while all pool mutations are internally invoked by the controller. [13](#0-12) [14](#0-13) [9](#0-8) 

Exploitation is economically difficult rather than a one-call trigger: the market must represent an extremely large token position and sustain high-utilization interest accrual until `scaled_amount * index` exceeds `i128::MAX`. The checked-in scenario uses one billion whole tokens at 18 decimals and 98% borrow utilization, and bounds the failure to occur within 40 simulated years. [15](#0-14) 

The attacker does not need privileged configuration, leaked keys, malformed storage, or an external oracle failure; the state can be formed by supplying the target asset, borrowing it under a separate collateralized account, and waiting for interest to compound. The high capital requirement reduces likelihood, but the resulting freeze is deterministic once the numerical boundary is crossed. [16](#0-15) 

### Recommendation
Make aggregate value calculations domain-aware before applying interest. In particular:

1. Treat `scaled * index` as a potentially overflowing aggregate value wherever utilization, supplier rewards, and supply-index distribution are computed.
2. Prefer saturating aggregate-value multiplication for accounting decisions, or use a wider intermediate representation that cannot overflow `i128`.
3. Enforce `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` and `MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` before any multiplication that can panic on the stored scaled totals.
4. Add a regression path proving that after the cap engages, `repay`, `withdraw`, `liquidate`, and `update_indexes` continue to operate rather than reverting in accrual.

The existing failing-boundary test at `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs` should be changed from documenting the freeze into asserting that the index cap is reached first and the market remains usable. [11](#0-10) 

### Proof of Concept
A concrete reproduction is already encoded in the repository’s long-horizon harness:

1. Create a market with an 18-decimal token and the XLM rate curve, while disabling the utilization ceiling for the test.
2. Supply `1_000_000_000 * 10^18` raw units of the target asset.
3. Supply enough collateral in another market and borrow 98% of the target asset.
4. Advance ledger time year by year and call `Controller::update_indexes` for the target market.
5. Observe `GenericError::MathOverflow` while the stored borrow index is still less than `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
6. Attempt `Controller::withdraw` and `Controller::repay`; both accrue first and fail with the same overflow. [16](#0-15)

        .with_market(col())
        .with_max_utilization_disabled_all_markets()
        .build();
    lift_caps(&t, "BIG18", 18);
    lift_caps(&t, "COL", 7);
    let principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18);
    t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);
    let debt = principal / 100 * 98;
    t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
    t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);
