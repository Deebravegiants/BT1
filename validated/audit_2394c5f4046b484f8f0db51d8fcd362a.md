### Title
Attacker-sized HyperSwap pre-seed reserves can overflow `Math.mulDiv` in `_noFeeSwapInput`, permanently reverting `finalizeGraduation` - ([File: packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol])

### Summary
The GIMP CVE roots in an unchecked 16-bit×16-bit multiplication (`num_colors * num_cluts`) that overflows and produces undefined behavior/abort. The closest reachable analog in this codebase is the unchecked-in-intent but checked-in-effect multiplication chain inside `Bonding._noFeeSwapInput`, called from the permissionless `finalizeGraduation` → `_seedUniswapV2Direct` → `_seedRebalancing` → `_pairRebalance` path [1](#0-0) . `_noFeeSwapInput` computes `Math.mulDiv(reserveIn * reserveOut, targetN, targetD)` where `reserveIn`/`reserveOut` are attacker-controllable HyperSwap V2 reserves (up to `uint112.max` each, via a front-run pre-seed) and `targetN`/`targetD` are the protocol's own `tokensForLP`/`ltFromPair` values [2](#0-1) . When the denominator side of the target ratio is small relative to `reserveIn * reserveOut * targetN`, `Math.mulDiv`'s 512-bit intermediate result no longer fits in `uint256` and the OZ implementation reverts rather than truncating.

### Finding Description
The pre-seed defense in `_seedUniswapV2Direct`/`_seedRebalancing` is explicitly documented as needing to "never revert under any pre-seed shape" so that `finalizeGraduation` — a **permissionless** function anyone can call — always succeeds [3](#0-2) . The rebalance path reads the live HyperSwap pair reserves `(r0, r1)` via `getReserves()` and feeds them directly as `reserveIn`/`reserveOut` into `_noFeeSwapInput` [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) .

An unprivileged attacker can front-run `factory.createPair(token, lt)` and seed the pair with reserves as large as `type(uint112).max` on one side and a comparatively arbitrary small ratio on the other (the documented "mint pre-seed" attack, regime 3, already acknowledged in `AGENTS.md`) [6](#0-5) . `_noFeeSwapInput`'s own natspec concedes the failure mode: "Constructed adversarial inputs that violate this would `revert` rather than silently truncate, which is the correct failure mode" [7](#0-6)  — directly contradicting the "must never revert under any pre-seed shape" brick-resistance guarantee stated for the same code path elsewhere in the docs.

The test suite only validates overflow-safety at deliberately narrow bounds (`uint64` reserves, and one hand-picked realistic-max case with `targetN == targetD`) [8](#0-7) , not the general case where `reserveIn`/`reserveOut` sit at the real `uint112` ceiling (attacker-chosen) combined with a small-denominator `targetN/targetD` ratio arising from the token's own curve state (e.g. a thin curve paired with a high-`exchangeRate` LT, where `ltFromPair` or `tokensForLP` can be small).

### Impact Explanation
If the intermediate product `reserveIn * reserveOut * targetN / targetD` exceeds `uint256`, `Math.mulDiv` reverts and `_pairRebalance`/`_seedRebalancing`/`_seedUniswapV2Direct`/`finalizeGraduation` all revert with it. Since `finalizeGraduation` is the only path that drains `pendingGraduation[token]`'s cached LT/tokens out of `Bonding` and mints the locked LP, a permanent revert freezes: the entire curve-raised LT (`ltFromPair`) and the 250M `tokensForLP` parked on `Bonding` for that token, the creator's/traders' ability to ever reach the graduated, tradeable state, and the LP-lock step (`LPLock.recordLock`) can never fire for that token. This matches the rules' definition of permanent freezing of trader/creator/LP funds.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires only permissionless actions any wallet can take: front-run `factory.createPair`, `transfer`+`mint`/`sync` dust or skewed reserves into the HyperSwap pair before `finalizeGraduation` runs (already the documented threat model for this exact code), and target a token whose curve state (`tokensForLP`/`ltFromPair` ratio) happens to produce a small denominator. No special privileges, upgrades, or off-chain assumptions are needed — it is a direct extension of the pre-seed attack the protocol already defends against, just pushed to the numeric edge the defense's own math wasn't proven safe against.

### Recommendation
Bound `_noFeeSwapInput`'s inputs defensively before calling `Math.mulDiv`, or use a formulation that cannot exceed `uint256` regardless of adversarial `reserveIn`/`reserveOut` (e.g. clamp attacker-supplied V2 reserves to a sane multiple of `tokensForLP`/`ltFromPair` before rebalancing, or fall back to `_seedDirectMint` whenever the discriminant would overflow instead of letting `Math.mulDiv` revert). Add fuzz coverage that uses real `uint112`-max reserves crossed against small-denominator target ratios drawn from realistic thin-curve/high-exchangeRate scenarios, not just the `targetN == targetD` case currently tested.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual PoC (exact numeric thresholds would need to be derived against `Math.mulDiv`'s 512-bit overflow boundary in a Devin session with the ability to run Foundry):
1. Launch a token against an LT with a very high `exchangeRate` so that reaching the `$9K` USD graduation threshold requires only a small amount of real LT (`ltFromPair` small in wei terms).
2. Front-run `HyperSwapFactory.createPair(token, lt)` before `finalizeGraduation`, then `transfer` + `pair.mint(attacker)` (or `transfer`+`sync`) to seed reserves close to `type(uint112).max` on the LT side and a comparatively large but not-max amount on the token side, producing a `reserveIn * reserveOut` product near `2^224`.
3. Trigger graduation phase 1 normally (buy through the curve to cross the threshold), then call the permissionless `finalizeGraduation`.
4. Inside `_seedRebalancing` → `_pairRebalance` → `_noFeeSwapInput`, `Math.mulDiv(reserveIn * reserveOut, targetN, targetD)` computes a quotient that exceeds `uint256`, reverting the entire `finalizeGraduation` transaction and permanently stranding the token in `Lifecycle.Graduating` with its LT/tokens locked in `Bonding`.

```text
    function _noFeeSwapInput(
        uint256 reserveIn,
        uint256 reserveOut,
        uint256 targetN,
        uint256 targetD,
        uint256 maxSwap
    ) internal pure returns (uint256) {
        if (reserveIn == 0 || reserveOut == 0 || targetN == 0 || targetD == 0 || maxSwap == 0) {
            return 0;
        }
        uint256 product = Math.mulDiv(reserveIn * reserveOut, targetN, targetD);
        uint256 newIn = Math.sqrt(product);
        if (newIn <= reserveIn) return 0;
        uint256 s = newIn - reserveIn;
        return s > maxSwap ? maxSwap : s;
    }
```

```markdown
- **Two-phase split.** Graduation is split across two transactions to fit HyperEVM's small-block (~2M gas) ceiling.
  - **Phase 1: `_enterGraduating`**, fired inline by the threshold-crossing buy (~150-200k of additional gas on top of the buy). Drains the curve, computes the LP-bound amounts, caches them in `pendingGraduation[token]`, flips `lifecycle: Curve → Graduating`, freezes trading. Emits `TokenGraduating`.
  - **Phase 2: `finalizeGraduation`**, **permissionless** big-block tx (~2.5M gas). Creates the HyperSwap pair if needed, seeds liquidity across the empty, donation, and hostile mint-pre-seed regimes, locks LP, flips `lifecycle: Graduating → Graduated`. Emits `TokenGraduated`. A Cloudflare Worker keeper handles the happy path; anyone can call to rescue a stuck token.
- **Brick resistance.** Phase 2 must never revert under any pre-seed shape. Empty/donation pairs use direct pair calls; hostile mint pre-seeds use direct `pair.swap` for rebalance plus router `addLiquidity` for the canonical quote-based deposit. Tested by `test_brick_resistance_frontRun_dust_seed` in [`test/TwoPhaseGraduation.t.sol`](test/TwoPhaseGraduation.t.sol).
- **Virtual token reserve.** At launch, `Pair.reserve0 = totalSupply (1B)` while only `curveSupply = 75%` (750M) of real tokens are transferred to the pair. The other 250M (`LP_RESERVE`) sit in `Bonding` for graduation. This extends the curve beyond the sellable supply, which is what makes dynamic LP seeding work cleanly.
- **Dual trigger.** Phase 1 fires on whichever hits first: `(storedAssetReserve - virtualLtReserve) × exchangeRate ≥ $9K` (USD, for LT pumps) or `IPair.tokenBalance() == 0` (supply, for flat/bear markets). The USD trigger reads STORED reserves so direct LT donations to the pair don't count toward the threshold; the launch-time `virtualLtReserve` is recovered on-the-fly as `Pair.k() / Token.TOTAL_SUPPLY()` (K is set once at mint and never modified by `Pair.swap`). The supply trigger reads live `tokenBalance()`, which is donation-resistant in the opposite direction: token donations only INCREASE the balance and can never satisfy `== 0`, and any donated tokens are unconditionally burned by `_prepareGraduationLiquidity`.
- **Zero-gap LP seeding.** `_prepareGraduationLiquidity` computes `ltFromPair = storedAssetReserve - virtualLtReserve` (the real LT raised by the curve, donation-immune; `virtualLtReserve` is derived from `Pair.k() / Token.TOTAL_SUPPLY()`) and `tokensForLP = ltFromPair × storedTokenReserve / storedAssetReserve` at end-of-phase-1, caching the result. Phase 2 uses the cached value verbatim, so the curve→LP price match is invariant under the tx split. Donated LT stays in the curve pair under the trust assumption that `BONDING_ROLE` is only ever held by `Bonding` and `Bonding` won't call `Router.graduate` again post-graduation.
- **Parabola invariant.** With `V_t_init = totalSupply` and `curveSupply = 75%`, the function `tokensForLP(sold) = sold·(S−sold)/S` peaks at `S/4 = LP_RESERVE`. The cap in `_prepareGraduationLiquidity` is defensive — it can never bind in normal operation.
- **Overflow buy cap.** `Router.buy` caps `tokensOut` at the pair's real balance and back-calculates the LT consumed, so the last buy cannot exceed remaining supply. `Zap.buy` returns the unused LT (`ltMinted - amountInUsed`) directly as LT — not redeemed, to avoid re-incurring the LT redemption fee on dust — while unconverted USDC and the fee over-charge are refunded in USDC. `Bonding.buy` returns `(tokensOut, amountInUsed)` for this reason.
```

```markdown
## HyperSwap Pre-Seed Defense (Read This Before Touching `_seedUniswapV2Direct`)

The whole sub-system inside `_seedUniswapV2Direct` exists to defuse one specific attack class. It's the most subtle code in the package. Read this before touching any of the helpers (`_seedRebalancing`, `_pairRebalance`, `_routerDepositAndDispose`, `_noFeeSwapInput`).

### The exploit

A vanilla UniswapV2 pair is deployable by anyone: `factory.createPair(token, lt)` is permissionless, and after creation anyone can call `pair.mint(to)` against pre-transferred tokens. So between phase 1 (`_enterGraduating` flips lifecycle to `Graduating` and caches `tokensForLP / ltFromPair`) and phase 2 (`finalizeGraduation` mints LP via `pair.mint(lpLock)`), an attacker can:

1. Front-run by calling `factory.createPair(token, lt)` themselves
2. `transfer(pair, smallToken)` and `transfer(pair, smallLT)` at any ratio they choose
3. Call `pair.mint(attacker)` — they now own LP at a hostile reserve ratio

When our `pair.mint(lpLock)` runs in phase 2 against this non-empty pair, V2's mint formula picks up the existing reserves:

```
liquidity = min(amount0 · totalSupply / reserve0, amount1 · totalSupply / reserve1)
```

The `min(...)` arm whose denominator is bigger relative to its numerator wins, and the OTHER arm's "excess" deposit is donated pro-rata to existing LP holders — i.e. to the attacker. Two harms:

- **Wrong opening price.** Post-mint reserves are `(R_attacker + T_a, R_attacker + T_b)`, so the LP opens at `(R_a + T_a) / (R_b + T_b)`, NOT at the curve close `T_a / T_b`. A `$15` LT pre-seed at 50% off curve close opens the pool ~454 bps off.
- **LP capture.** The wasted-side excess goes to the attacker's LP claim. A `1 wei + 1 LT` pre-seed (~`$1` attack budget) captures ~34 bps of LP.
```
