### Title
Dual-source midpoint pricing allows a manipulated market leg to trigger wrongful liquidations - ([File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs])

### Summary
When an asset is configured with two price sources, the price aggregator accepts them if their ratio is inside the configured tolerance and then uses their arithmetic midpoint as the authoritative USD price. An attacker who can move one market-derived leg while the anchor leg remains near its prior value can therefore shift the accepted price by up to roughly half the tolerance without making the oracle unsafe. The inflated or deflated midpoint is cached by the controller and used for collateral valuation, health-factor checks, and liquidation sizing.

### Finding Description
`compose` collects two readings when both sources are readable and passes them to `blend`. `blend` checks only that their high/low ratio does not exceed `tolerance.upper_ratio_bps`, then sets `price_wad` to `midpoint_price_or_zero(anchor, primary)`. [1](#0-0)  The midpoint helper is a plain `(anchor + primary) / 2`, so a 9% discrepancy between the sources produces a price approximately 4.5% away from either side. [2](#0-1) 

The controller loads the resulting aggregator feed into its invocation-local cache and later retrieves it through `cached_price` during risk calculations and liquidation planning. [3](#0-2)  `liquidate` is permissionless apart from the liquidator's authorization, builds the plan from those cached prices, applies the repayments, and then seizes collateral according to the plan. [4](#0-3) 

The repository's own regression test demonstrates the issue: after ETH's primary source is moved from `$2,000` to `$1,820` while the safe source remains `$2,000`, the blended price becomes `$1,910`; the account is treated as liquidatable and the liquidator receives more than 2.73 ETH for a `$5,000` repayment. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

### Impact Explanation
An attacker can create or use a position whose collateral is valued at the manipulated midpoint rather than the more reliable source value. When the manipulated midpoint crosses the liquidation boundary, the attacker can call `liquidate` and extract excess collateral from the borrower. More generally, midpoint valuation changes every health-factor and liquidation calculation that consumes the affected asset, so repeated use across accounts can cause protocol undercollateralization and loss of user funds.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack only requires a discrepancy inside the configured tolerance, not an oracle outage or administrative action. A market-nature source can be influenced through the attacker's own trades on the referenced venue, while the second source remains near its prior observation. The included test uses a 9% source discrepancy under a loose tolerance and shows that liquidation succeeds rather than returning `UnsafePriceNotAllowed`. [7](#0-6) 

### Recommendation
Do not use an unconditional arithmetic midpoint as the risk price for two disagreeing sources. At minimum:

- Use a direction-aware conservative policy for collateral and debt valuation, or fail risky operations when independent sources disagree beyond a much tighter bound.
- Give the designated anchor source veto power over liquidations when a market source diverges.
- Bound liquidation eligibility and seizure sizing using a price selected for the direction that protects the protocol and borrower, rather than blending away half of a detected discrepancy.
- Add regression coverage for both downward collateral manipulation and upward debt manipulation through `liquidate`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, and `multiply`.

### Proof of Concept
The existing test `test_liquidation_collateral_extraction_via_averaging` is a direct proof:

1. Configure ETH with two sources and loose tolerance.
2. Supply 10 ETH and borrow `$18,175`.
3. Keep the safe source at `$2,000`, but move the primary source to `$1,820`.
4. The accepted price becomes `(2,000 + 1,820) / 2 = $1,910`.
5. Call `liquidate` with a `$5,000` USDC repayment.
6. Liquidation succeeds and awards more than 2.73 ETH, demonstrating excess collateral extraction through midpoint pricing. [8](#0-7)

/// toward zero. Returns `0` if the sum overflows `i128`.
pub(crate) fn midpoint_price_or_zero(anchor_price: i128, primary_price: i128) -> i128 {
    anchor_price
        .checked_add(primary_price)
        .map(|sum| sum / 2)
