### Title
Relayers cannot invalidate a previously signed fee-withdrawal or beneficiary-redirect message before it is executed - (File: `modules/pallets/relayer/src/withdrawal.rs`)

### Summary
`pallet-ismp-relayer`'s fee withdrawal and beneficiary-redirect flows authenticate the caller with a per-`(address, StateMachine)` nonce embedded in a signed payload, exactly like the `ExecutionModule::executeBySig` pattern in the referenced report. The nonce is only ever advanced as a side effect of successfully executing `withdraw_fees`/`accumulate_fees`; there is no function letting a relayer (or an admin) bump the nonce to unilaterally invalidate an outstanding signature. Since these calls are submitted as permissionless unsigned extrinsics and the payout amount is read live from storage rather than being bound into the signature, a stale-but-unconsumed signature remains a standing, uncancellable authorization to move whatever balance has since accrued.

### Finding Description
`Pallet::withdraw` reads the current nonce, builds `message(nonce, dest_chain, beneficiary)`, verifies the caller's signature against it, and only then increments `Nonce::<T>`: [1](#0-0) 

The same pattern is used for the beneficiary-redirect signature checked inside `accumulate`: [2](#0-1) 

Both `withdraw_fees` and `accumulate_fees` are dispatched as unsigned extrinsics validated in `ValidateUnsigned::validate_unsigned`, meaning any party holding a copy of the signed payload (e.g. observed in the transaction pool, in tesseract's local persistence, or leaked in logs) can submit it — not only the signer: [3](#0-2) 

Critically, the signed message binds only `(nonce, dest_chain, beneficiary)` — not the amount. The actual amount transferred is whatever `Fees::<T>::get(dest_chain, address)` currently holds at execution time: [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

There is no `increment_nonce`/`cancel` extrinsic anywhere in the pallet (confirmed by searching the module for such a function), so once a relayer signs a withdrawal or beneficiary-redirect message at a given nonce, that authorization stays valid — and executable by anyone — indefinitely until it happens to be consumed. The relayer has no way to preemptively invalidate it, e.g., after discovering the destination `beneficiary` address is wrong, compromised, or after wanting to stop an in-flight but not-yet-delivered withdrawal.

### Impact Explanation
Because the signature is amount-agnostic, a relayer who signs a withdrawal/beneficiary-redirect message while the accrued balance is small, then continues accruing fees before that signed request happens to be submitted (or is deliberately delayed), later finds the full, now much larger, balance withdrawn or redirected under the old signature — with no recourse to cancel it. If the relayer's off-chain key material is later suspected compromised, or a beneficiary address is entered in error, the relayer cannot invalidate the previously signed nonce; anyone in possession of it can still execute it and redirect/drain the full accumulated relayer fee balance. This is a Medium-severity freezing/misdirection-of-funds risk within the relayer fee and reward accounting subsystem.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is Medium: it requires either (a) a relayer's signed-but-unsubmitted withdrawal/redirect payload to leak or be retained longer than intended (plausible given tesseract persists pending withdrawals to a local DB and retries them), or (b) a relayer needing to change/cancel a stale beneficiary designation before it executes. Both are realistic operational scenarios for a permissionless-relaying incentive system, but require an outstanding signed message to exist and be reachable by a third party or to remain un-superseded.

### Recommendation
Add a signed/root-gated extrinsic (e.g. `cancel_pending_withdrawal` or `bump_nonce`) that lets the relayer address (verified via the same signature scheme) advance `Nonce::<T>` for a given `(address, dest_chain)` pair without executing a transfer, invalidating any outstanding signed withdrawal or beneficiary-redirect message at the old nonce. Additionally, consider binding the authorized amount (or a maximum) into the signed payload so a stale authorization cannot later be executed against an inflated balance.

### Proof of Concept
1. Relayer accrues 10 fee-tokens on `dest_chain` in `Fees::<T>`.
2. Relayer signs `message(nonce=N, dest_chain, beneficiary=B)` intending to withdraw the 10 tokens, but the resulting `withdraw_fees` extrinsic is delayed (e.g., stuck in a mempool, persisted in tesseract's pending-withdrawal DB) — see `modules/pallets/relayer/src/withdrawal.rs` lines 88-133.
3. Before that extrinsic lands, more fees accrue via `accumulate_fees`, bringing the balance to 500 tokens.
4. Meanwhile, the relayer wants to cancel because beneficiary `B` was mistyped/compromised — but no function exists to bump `Nonce::<T>` without executing a transfer (only `withdraw`/`accumulate` do so, per `modules/pallets/relayer/src/lib.rs` lines 467-476 and 484-490).
5. Anyone holding the original signed payload submits `withdraw_fees` with the still-valid nonce `N`; `Pallet::withdraw` reads the *current* `Fees::<T>::get` (500 tokens, not the 10 originally intended) and dispatches the full amount to beneficiary `B`, which the relayer never approved for that amount and could not cancel.
