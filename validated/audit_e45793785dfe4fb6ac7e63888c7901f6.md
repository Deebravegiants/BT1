### Title
Unprivileged borrower can push utilization to the cap and freeze all withdrawals in a hub market - (contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs)

### Summary
CVE-2019-0564 describes a denial of service caused by improper handling of requests. The analog in XOXNO Lending is the max-utilization gate applied to ordinary withdrawals: `withdraw::apply` → `accounting` → `gate_and_debit` calls `guards::require_utilization_below_max` whenever the withdrawal is not a liquidation and not a footprint-only close [1](#0-0) . A single unprivileged borrower who supplies collateral and borrows a hub market up to its max-utilization boundary causes every subsequent `withdraw` call on that (hub, token) book to revert, temporarily freezing lender funds. Because markets are separate books per `(hub, token)` over one physical pool balance, only the pushed market's withdrawals stall, but all suppliers in that book are affected [2](#0-1) .

### Finding Description
In `contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs`, `accounting` resolves the close/partial, burns shares, withholds the liquidation fee, then `gate_and_debit` enforces `cache.require_reserves`, `guards::require_utilization_below_max` (skipped only for liquidations and empty closes), `require_supply_for_debt`, and `debit_cash` [1](#0-0) . Any withdrawal requested while `borrowed / supplied >= max_utilization` reverts after shares are burned in-cache (the revert rolls back the whole tx, so no shares are lost — the request simply fails). There is no withdrawal queue or partial-exit fallback: a supplier at 100% utilization of an exhausted market cannot exit at all until utilization drops.

An unprivileged address reaches this via `controller.borrow` (supply collateral in one asset, borrow the target asset in the same spoke to push `total_borrowed` to the cap). The borrower's own cost is the interest on their position; they can alternatively combine with `flash_loan`/`multiply`/`swap_debt` leverage loops to reach high utilization with modest capital, since `multiply` mints debt before the callback settles. The freeze persists for as long as utilization stays at/above the cap — the attacker can keep it there by keeping their borrow open or repeatedly topping it up after repayments/liquidations.

Notably, liquidation withdrawals skip the gate (`is_liquidation || empty_close`), so the protocol can still be liquidated — this is a freeze of honest suppliers' exits, not a freeze of risk operations [3](#0-2) .

### Impact Explanation
Temporary freezing of funds: every supplier in the targeted `(hub, token)` market cannot withdraw any amount while utilization is at or above the max. With duration controlled by the attacker (who can re-push utilization after each partial unwind), suppliers' assets are locked for the duration of the manipulation; in a stressed market this coincides exactly with when exit demand peaks.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. Requires the attacker to post collateral and take a real borrow position paying interest, so it is not free, but it needs only one unprivileged address, uses only documented entrypoints (`supply`, `borrow`, optionally `multiply`/`flash_position` for leverage), and no special permissions or timing. Pools with thin liquidity or low `max_utilization` headroom are cheap to saturate.

### Recommendation
- Allow withdrawals to proceed when utilization is already at/above the cap (withdrawing only *reduces* free liquidity but also shrinks the denominator base of lenders; alternatively gate only new borrows/`transfer_out` beyond a hard solvency floor via `require_reserves` + `require_supply_for_debt`, which are already enforced) — i.e., drop or relax `require_utilization_below_max` for the non-liquidation withdraw path.
- If the gate must remain for supply-side solvency, add a pro-rata/queued exit or a cooldown-based partial withdrawal so a saturated market degrades gracefully rather than reverting outright.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker calls `controller.supply(hub=H, collateral=C)` then `controller.borrow(hub=H, asset=T, amount=up_to_cap)` until market `T` reaches `max_utilization`.
2. Victim supplier calls `controller.withdraw(hub=H, asset=T, amount, receiver)` → pool `withdraw::apply` → `gate_and_debit` → `require_utilization_below_max` panics; tx reverts, victim exits nothing.
3. Attacker keeps the borrow open (re-borrowing after any repayments) to keep utilization pinned; all victim `withdraw` calls continue to revert while liquidation flows remain unblocked.
4. Unwind: attacker repays and withdraws; freeze ends — impact realized is the window of frozen lender liquidity.

Note on scope verification: I read `withdraw.rs`, `seize.rs`, `account.rs`, and the ops/controller layouts directly; I did not fully trace `guards::require_utilization_below_max`'s threshold source (whether the cap is a per-market config or hard-coded) or the exact borrow-side utilization check, so the precise triggering boundary should be confirmed against `contracts/pool/src/guards.rs` and the borrow path during review.

```rust
fn gate_and_debit(env: &Env, cache: &mut Cache, net_transfer: i128, skip_utilization_check: bool) {
    cache.require_reserves(net_transfer);

    if !skip_utilization_check {
        guards::require_utilization_below_max(env, cache);
    }
    guards::require_supply_for_debt(env, cache);
    cache.debit_cash(net_transfer);
}
```
