### Title
Consensus-incentive reward misattribution lets a single relayer claim rewards for state-machine updates delivered by other relayers' consensus messages in the same `handle_unsigned` batch - (File: `modules/pallets/consensus-incentives/src/impls.rs`)

### Summary
`pallet-consensus-incentives`'s `FeeHandler::on_executed` determines the relayer to reward for an *entire* batch of ISMP messages by decoding the signer of only the **first** message in the batch (`messages.get(0)`), then pays that single relayer for every `StateMachineUpdated` event produced across the whole batch, regardless of which message/consensus proof (and therefore which relayer's signature) actually produced each event. This mirrors the Symmio-core M-14 pattern: whoever "goes first" in a shared, multi-step process collects the entire reward, while other legitimate contributors to the same batch get nothing.

### Finding Description
`pallet_ismp::Pallet::<T>::execute` (`modules/pallets/ismp/src/impls.rs:40-87`) accepts an arbitrary `Vec<Message>` via the unsigned, permissionless `handle_unsigned` extrinsic (`modules/pallets/ismp/src/lib.rs:373-382`), processes every message, aggregates all resulting ISMP `events` (including possibly several `StateMachineUpdated` events for different state machines), and hands the whole batch plus the whole event list to `T::FeeHandler::on_executed(messages_with_weights, events)` in one call.

`pallet-consensus-incentives`'s implementation of `on_executed` (`modules/pallets/consensus-incentives/src/impls.rs:108-163`) does the following: [1](#0-0) 

It looks only at `messages.get(0)` to recover a single relayer signer from a `Message::Consensus` signature, then: [2](#0-1) 

collapses **all** `StateMachineUpdated` events in the batch (which can span many distinct `state_machine_id`s, each potentially produced by a *different* `Message::Consensus` entry with its own independent signature) into a `BTreeMap`, and pays the reward for every one of them to the single relayer recovered from message index 0.

Nothing in `execute()` or `handle_unsigned` enforces that every message in the batch shares the same signer or even originates from the same submitter — `handle_unsigned` is a public, unsigned extrinsic, and `Vec<Message>` is caller-controlled. A submitter can therefore construct a batch containing:
1. Their own trivially-cheap `Message::Consensus` entry as index 0 (a signed proof they authored themselves, potentially producing a minimal or even a "no state update" `StateMachineUpdated`), and
2. Additional, already-signed `Message::Consensus` entries copied verbatim from other relayers (BEEFY/GRANDPA/sync-committee/etc. proofs are public data with signatures that verify independent of who submits the transaction — the docs explicitly note "these proofs can be generated by anyone, as they are freely provided by its connected chains" [3](#0-2) ).

Because consensus-proof validity and signature verification are per-message and do not depend on the submitting account, the attacker's bundled batch executes successfully end-to-end (each consensus client verifies its own proof), producing `StateMachineUpdated` events for every chain whose proof was included — but `on_executed` attributes **all** of those rewards to the signer recovered from `messages[0]`, i.e., the attacker, not to the relayers who actually produced and signed the copied consensus messages.

This is structurally identical to the M-14 root cause: a shared multi-part delivery process (here, a batch of independently-signed consensus updates) is rewarded to whichever party's contribution happens to occupy the first slot, even though the reward calculation (`calculate_reward`/`process_message`) subsequently pays for the block-span progress achieved by *every* state machine in the batch, not just the one the rewarded party actually advanced.

### Impact Explanation
This misattribution directly diverts `BRIDGE`-denominated treasury funds (`T::TreasuryAccount`) and reputation-asset mints (`T::ReputationAsset::mint_into`) away from the relayers who performed the actual consensus-proof relaying work to an attacker who merely repackaged already-public signed proofs and prepended a low-cost message of their own. At scale this is a systematic siphon of the consensus-incentive treasury: an attacker can front-run/observe pending consensus messages in the mempool (their signatures are visible once broadcast) and always land their own batch first, capturing 100% of the reward for state-machine advances performed by other relayers while the original submitters' transactions become no-ops (already-applied height) and they receive zero compensation despite paying gas/incurring cost to produce the proof. This is a concrete misallocation/theft of protocol treasury funds intended as relayer compensation, and — as in M-14 — discourages honest relayers from doing the work at all once they realize their submissions are routinely captured by front-runners, threatening the liveness of the consensus-relay incentive mechanism across all "mandatory"/cost-per-block-configured state machines.

### Likelihood Explanation
`handle_unsigned` is a completely permissionless, unsigned extrinsic that accepts any `Vec<Message>`; there is no validation anywhere in `execute()`, `validate_unsigned`, or `on_executed` that constrains a batch to messages signed by a single relayer or originating from a single source. Consensus proofs are inherently public (any node can extract a valid, signed `ConsensusMessage` from the mempool or a finalized block and re-submit it verbatim), so constructing an exploiting batch requires no special access — only monitoring the mempool/chain for other relayers' consensus submissions and packaging them behind a cheap message of one's own. This is straightforward to automate and requires no privileged role, collusion, or governance access, making exploitation highly likely once a treasury-funded `StateMachinesCostPerBlock` is configured for any chain of value.

### Recommendation
Attribute the reward for each `StateMachineUpdated` event to the signer of the specific `Message::Consensus` entry that produced it, rather than to the signer of `messages[0]`. Concretely, `on_executed` should pair each processed message with the events it individually generated (the underlying `MessageResult::ConsensusMessage(events)` per-message mapping already exists in `execute()` before events are flattened — see `modules/pallets/ismp/src/impls.rs:59-70`), recover the relayer signer per message, and call `process_message` with that message's own relayer account for the `StateMachineUpdated` events it caused. Preserve the existing per-state-machine "highest height" collapsing logic, but key it by `(state_machine_id, relayer_account)` derived per-message rather than a single batch-wide relayer.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual PoC (cannot be executed without a live simnode/testnet, but the code path is deterministic):
1. Relayer A submits a valid, signed `handle_unsigned([Message::Consensus(proof_A_for_chain_X)])` to the transaction pool; it advances `StateMachineUpdated` for chain X.
2. Attacker observes this pending/just-included extrinsic and extracts the fully-signed `ConsensusMessage` (signature included) for chain X.
3. Attacker crafts their own cheap `Message::Consensus(proof_attacker_for_chain_Y)` (e.g., a chain with negligible verification cost, or one producing a trivial height advance) and submits `handle_unsigned([Message::Consensus(proof_attacker_for_chain_Y), Message::Consensus(proof_A_for_chain_X)])`, ensuring their transaction is prioritized ahead of/instead of Relayer A's (e.g., resubmitting before A's is finalized, or A's tx failing due to replay if attacker's lands first).
4. `execute()` processes both messages successfully (each consensus client independently verifies its own proof), producing `StateMachineUpdated` events for both chain Y and chain X.
5. `on_executed` (`modules/pallets/consensus-incentives/src/impls.rs:112-156`) decodes the signer from `messages[0]` (the attacker's message for chain Y) and pays the attacker `calculate_reward` for **both** chain Y's and chain X's block-span advances, crediting `RelayerRewarded` and minting reputation entirely to the attacker's account — even though chain X's proof was authored and signed by Relayer A.
6. Relayer A's original submission (if it lands after) fails or becomes a no-op (state already advanced), and A receives no `RelayerRewarded` event and no treasury payout for their work.
