### Title
Unchecked index-scaled debt valuation overflows and permanently freezes a saturated market - ([File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs](common/src/rates/simulate.rs))

### Summary
A sufficiently large and highly utilized market can drive `borrowed * borrow_index` beyond `i128::MAX` before the borrow-index ceiling is applied, causing every accrual-dependent operation on that market to revert. This permanently blocks repayment, withdrawal, liquidation, bad-debt processing, and index updates, freezing supplier and borrower funds in that market.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` computes `borrowed_original` and `supplied_original` by calling `scaled_to_original` before calculating utilization, the borrow rate, or the bounded new borrow index. [1](#0-0)  `scaled_to_original` is a direct fixed-point multiplication and does not cap or recover from overflow. [2](#0-1)  Although `update_borrow_index` later clamps the newly grown index to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, the earlier valuation multiplication can already exceed `i128::MAX`, so that protective bound is never reached. [3](#0-2)  The pre-existing harness test demonstrates this exact ordering: the market fails with `MathOverflow` while its stored borrow index remains below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. [4](#0-3) 

All ordinary pool mutation legs load a market through `synced_market`, which calls `interest::global_sync`; consequently, a failed accrual prevents the operation from reaching its own repayment or withdrawal logic. [5](#0-4)  `global_sync` invokes the same `accrue_step` for each elapsed compounding chunk and commits indexes only after all chunks complete. [6](#0-5)  The permissionless controller `update_indexes` path reaches the same pool accrual routine. [7](#0-6) 

### Impact Explanation
Once the scaled-debt product crosses the `i128` domain, the affected market cannot process a subsequent accrual. Since repay and withdrawal begin by syncing the market, borrowers cannot reduce the dangerous debt position and suppliers cannot exit, even with a nonzero payment or withdrawal request. [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8)  The regression test confirms that after the first accrual failure, both a one-unit withdrawal and a repayment fail with the same `MathOverflow`, establishing a permanent freeze rather than a temporary oracle or liquidity outage. [10](#0-9) 

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires an exceptionally large listed market and sustained borrowing at the steep part of its configured rate curve, so it is not practical for every market. However, it requires no privileged call after market deployment: an unprivileged borrower can supply collateral and borrow through the public controller entrypoints, and anyone can later invoke `update_indexes` to trigger the overflow. [11](#0-10)  The tested shape uses a one-billion-token market at 18 decimals and approximately 98% utilization; whether real deployed markets admit that exposure depends on their token supply, caps, collateral capacity, and configured rate model. [12](#0-11) 

### Recommendation
Bound the index-scaled values before multiplication, or treat a value-domain overflow as saturation to the configured index/valuation ceiling rather than reverting. In particular, `accrue_step` should use a checked or saturating `scaled_to_original` calculation that can safely reach `update_borrow_index`'s cap, and market-creation/runtime validation should enforce `scaled_debt * MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY <= i128::MAX` and the equivalent supply-side bound. Regression coverage should assert that repayment and withdrawal remain executable after the borrow index reaches its ceiling.

### Proof of Concept
1. On a market whose configured caps and token supply permit it, an attacker supplies a very large amount of an 18-decimal asset and borrows approximately 98% of it through `controller::supply` and `controller::borrow`.
2. The attacker or any unprivileged caller waits while interest compounds, then calls `controller::update_indexes` with that market's `HubAssetKey`.
3. `update_indexes` invokes pool accrual; `accrue_step` evaluates `borrowed * borrow_index` before `update_borrow_index` can clamp the index.
4. The multiplication exceeds the RAY value domain and returns `MathOverflow`, reverting without committing `last_timestamp`.
5. Subsequent `update_indexes`, `repay`, `withdraw`, liquidation, and bad-debt paths repeat the same accrual and revert, leaving market funds permanently inaccessible.

This sequence is reproduced by `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap`, which first fails `update_indexes` and then confirms that both withdrawal and repayment remain blocked. [13](#0-12)

pub(crate) fn global_sync(env: &Env, cache: &mut Cache) {
    if !cache.needs_accrual() {
        return;
    }

    let mut remaining = cache.elapsed_ms();
    while let Some(nonzero) = NonZeroU64::new(remaining) {
        let chunk = nonzero.get().min(MAX_COMPOUND_DELTA_MS);
        accrue_chunk(env, cache, chunk);
        remaining = remaining.saturating_sub(chunk);
    }

    cache.mark_accrued();
