### Title
Unfunded refund in pool `repay`/`recapitalize` lets an attacker drain pool custody by declaring an overpayment - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs)

### Summary
The bug class from CVE-2021-42377 is an attacker-controlled value being "freed" — a resource released based on attacker-supplied input rather than actual ownership. The direct analog lives in the pool's refund legs: `ops::repay::apply` and `ops::recapitalize::apply` compute an "excess" from a *declared* inbound amount and transfer that excess to the caller out of the pool's real token custody, without debiting `cash` or passing `require_reserves`. The in-repo test `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` demonstrates the primitive end to end: with zero debt outstanding and nothing transferred in, a single `repay` call declaring `custody_before` as the amount refunds the entire pool balance to a freshly generated `payer` address.

### Finding Description
- `contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs` resolves a repayment by comparing the caller-declared `amount` to `current_debt_ceil`. When debt is zero, `resolve_repay` takes the full-close branch, `net_repay` is zero, and the `RepayRoundsToZeroShares` assert passes on its `net_repay == 0` disjunct. The full declared amount is then classified as overpayment and refunded to the payer directly out of pool custody (`contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3578-3611`).
- The controller-side `settle_repay` (`contracts/controller/src/positions/debt.rs:144-152`) does measure receipts via `transfer_amount_measured`, so through the controller the declared amount is honest. The defect is that the pool leg itself derives the refund from the *declared* figure rather than a measured balance delta, so any caller that can invoke the pool's `repay` (or `recapitalize`, same shape, `contracts/pool/src/ops/recapitalize.rs`) leg directly gets a "free" of assets it never deposited — the precise memory-safety analog of freeing a pointer the attacker supplied.
- The test comment confirms this is structural: "Neither refund debits `cash` or passes `require_reserves`... the whole amount is refunded out of real custody with the book untouched" (`contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3578-3588`).

### Impact Explanation
If the pool's `repay`/`recapitalize` legs are callable without a controller-only invoker check (the test drives them through the public client with an arbitrary `Address::generate` payer), a single unprivileged transaction drains the entire token balance of any market with zero outstanding debt — theft of all user deposits in that market. Even where some debt exists, the refund is bounded only by `declared_amount - debt`, so the attacker pockets `custody - debt` while the book shows `cash` unchanged, leaving suppliers permanently unbacked: protocol insolvency / theft of user funds.

### Likelihood Explanation
The preconditions are minimal: any market whose `borrowed` is small or zero (a fresh listing, or a market just after full repayment) and any token balance in the pool. No price manipulation, no privileged role, no flash loan is needed. The only uncertainty is invoker gating on the pool entrypoint; the test fixture does not show a controller-address check firing, and the grep surface shows no `require_auth` inside `ops::repay.rs` itself. If the pool instead gates on the invoking contract address, reachability collapses to the controller paths, which are measured and safe — that gate is the single thing that must be confirmed.

### Recommendation
- Make the refund self-funding: compute `excess` as `min(declared - applied, measured_balance_delta_since_call_start)` or require the caller to transfer the declared amount in and refund only from that receipt, never from standing custody.
- Alternatively, enforce a strict invoker check (registered controller address) on `pool.repay`/`pool.recapitalize` so the declared-amount trust is confined to the contract that already does `transfer_amount_measured`, and assert `cash` conservation across the leg.
- Apply the same fix to `ops::recapitalize::apply`, which shares the declared-amount refund shape.

### Proof of Concept
The repository already contains it: `contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3590-3611` — `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody`. A generated `payer` calls `client().repay(&payer, &t.ract(0, custody_before))` on a market with `borrowed == 0`, receives `credited == 0` (no debt retired), and `assert_unfunded_refund_drained_custody` confirms the pool's token balance decreased by the full declared amount while the cash book stayed in sync — i.e., the refund was paid from other suppliers' deposits. Replay against a live market: `pool.repay(payer=attacker, actions=[(hub_asset, pool_token_balance)])`.
