**Rationale:** In `process_chunky_dkg_result_inner`, the `metadata.epoch` field is used only for a redundant epoch-freshness check [1](#0-0) , and again cross-checked against `trx.dealer_epoch` (the epoch actually embedded inside the deserialized transcript) [2](#0-1) . Critically, the cryptographic signature verification is performed as `verifier.verify_multi_signatures(&trx, &signature)`, where `trx` is the `AggregatedSubtranscript` deserialized directly from `transcript_bytes` — not `metadata` [3](#0-2) .

`AggregatedSubtranscript` derives `CryptoHasher, BCSCryptoHash`, meaning the object that is actually signed/verified is the BCS-hash of the full transcript struct (`dealer_epoch`, `subtranscript`, `dealer_bitmask`) itself, not the separate `metadata` struct [4](#0-3) . Consequently, the signature is already content-bound to the exact `transcript_bytes` payload (via its deserialized `trx` form), independent of `metadata`. An attacker cannot substitute a different `transcript_bytes` under a validly-signed `metadata`/`signature` pair from another round, because `verify_multi_signatures` never checks `metadata` at all — it checks the signature against the freshly deserialized `trx`. Any mismatched or attacker-substituted `transcript_bytes` would produce a different `trx` hash than what validators actually signed, causing `verify_multi_signatures` to fail with `MultiSigVerificationFailed` [3](#0-2) .

Additionally, even if signature verification were somehow bypassed, the derived `encryption_key` is independently re-derived from the transcript content and compared against the claimed key, providing a second content-binding check before the output is finalized on-chain [5](#0-4) .

Thus the premise — that `metadata` is the only binding and can be decoupled from `transcript_bytes`/`signature` — does not hold: the actual cryptographic binding is transcript-content-based via `verify_multi_signatures(&trx, ...)`, not metadata-based, and this path does not expose a custody-relevant forgery.
