### Title

Bad-debt index floor leaves wiped suppliers a claim that drains recapitalized liquidity - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary

`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps a fully wiped market's `supply_index` upward to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead of writing down or deleting the underlying supply shares. The old shares therefore retain a positive floored withdrawal value even when the loss equals or exceeds total supplied value. After permissionless `clean_bad_debt` creates this state, ordinary `supply` is blocked by `require_backed_market`, but `recapitalize` can credit cash for the residual shortfall. The holder of the supposedly wiped shares can then withdraw that recapitalized cash.

### Finding Description

During bad-debt seizure, the pool converts the debt position to a RAY value, passes it to `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, and burns the debt shares. [1](#0-0) 

`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` caps the write-down at the total supplied value, computes a remaining-value factor, and then enforces `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. [2](#0-1) 

When `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, `remaining` is zero, so the mathematically reduced index is zero. The final `max(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)` raises it back to `RAY / 1000`, leaving every old supply share with a nonzero claim. This behavior is explicitly demonstrated by the project's regression-style test: a wipeout produces a positive "stranded" claim, fresh cash is added, and the old position withdraws exactly that cash. [3](#0-2) 

The controller exposes the setup through permissionless `clean_bad_debt`, which requires caller authentication and invokes the dust-capped insolvency gate. [4](#0-3)  The gate accepts insolvent accounts whose remaining collateral is below the dust threshold and calls `execute_bad_debt_cleanup`. [5](#0-4) 

After the wipeout:

- `supply` rejects entry while `backing_shortfall > 0`. [6](#0-5) 
- `backing_shortfall` counts the floored value of all supply shares against cash plus outstanding debt. [7](#0-6) 
- `recapitalize` credits cash up to that shortfall. [8](#0-7) 
- `withdraw` resolves the old shares at the floored supply index, checks cash reserves, burns the shares, and debits cash. [9](#0-8) 

Thus, residual shares that should have zero economic value remain withdrawal claims against future recapitalization.

### Impact Explanation

This is protocol insolvency amplification and theft of recapitalized funds. A permissionless bad-debt cleanup can leave a market with zero real backing but positive supply claims. Any later `recapitalize` call intended to restore the market creates withdrawable cash for holders of the wiped shares, up to their floored residual values. The payer receives no shares or repayment right for the credited amount; `recapitalize` only credits cash and refunds the excess above the shortfall. [10](#0-9) 

Until recapitalization, normal supply is unusable because `require_backed_market` rejects the market. [6](#0-5)  After recapitalization, the residual holders can drain the injected funds, recreating the shortfall and leaving honest recapitalizers or future suppliers unpaid.

### Likelihood Explanation

The trigger path is reachable by an unprivileged address once a qualifying insolvent account exists:

1. An account becomes insolvent with collateral at or below the configured dust threshold.
2. Any caller signs `clean_bad_debt(caller, account_id)`.
3. The controller calls `seize_positions` for the account's supply and debt legs.
4. A sufficiently large borrow leg drives `supply_index` below the floor, where it is raised to `RAY / 1000`.
5. Any holder of the wiped supply shares can later withdraw cash credited by `recapitalize`.

No privileged action, oracle failure, trusted counterparty, or contract upgrade is required for the cleanup step. The exploit magnitude depends on the amount of residual scaled supply and subsequent recapitalization.

### Recommendation

Do not preserve supply claims through a nonzero index floor after a complete write-down. Suitable fixes include:

- remove the `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` clamp for bad-debt write-downs and allow the reduced index to reach zero;
- explicitly burn or zero all supply shares when `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`;
- or track wiped markets with a separate tombstone/reset mechanism that prevents residual shares from resolving to withdrawals.

Add an invariant that after `bad_debt >= supplied * supply_index`, every preexisting supply position has zero withdrawable value, and test that `recapitalize` cannot be withdrawn by positions that existed before the wipeout.

### Proof of Concept

The repository's own cache-level scenario demonstrates the accounting result in `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs`. A market with `1_000 * RAY` supply shares and `1_000 * RAY` debt shares applies bad debt, clamps the index to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, leaves a positive stranded claim, adds fresh cash, and lets the old position withdraw exactly that fresh cash. [11](#0-10) 

The production path is:

```text
clean_bad_debt(attacker, victim_account)
  -> execute_bad_debt_cleanup
  -> pool.seize_positions
  -> apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index
  -> supply_index = max(0, RAY / 1000)
```

Then:

```text
recapitalize(payer, wiped_market, amount)
  -> cash += min(amount, backing_shortfall)

withdraw(old_supplier, old_supplier_account, [(wiped_market, i128::MAX)], to)
  -> gross = unscale_supply_floor(old_scaled_amount)
  -> pool transfers gross to attacker-controlled `to`
```

The stranded holder receives recapitalized tokens even though its shares' economic value had already been fully consumed by bad debt.

```rust
pub(crate) fn apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache: &mut Cache, bad_debt: Ray) {
    let total_supplied_value = cache.supplied().mul(cache.env(), cache.supply_index());

    if total_supplied_value == Ray::ZERO {
        return;
    }

    let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
    let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);

    let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
    let new_supply_index = cache
        .supply_index()
        .mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);

    cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
```
