### Title
Stale dual-source price legs let borrowers overvalue collateral through midpoint blending - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs)

### Summary
When two configured price feeds disagree, the price aggregator accepts both observations as long as neither leg exceeds its own staleness limit and their prices remain inside the configured tolerance band. It then values the asset at the arithmetic midpoint rather than the lower, fresher market price. [1](#0-0)  This permits an ordinary borrower to supply a collateral token during an anchor-feed outage and borrow more against its inflated midpoint value than its current market value supports. [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
`blend` marks a two-leg result stale only when either individual leg is stale, or when both are `FeedNature::Market` and their timestamps are more than `MAX_LEG_AGE_SPREAD_SECONDS` apart. [3](#0-2)  A stale-priced `Fundamental` anchor therefore bypasses the market-to-market timestamp-spread check when it remains within its own much longer `max_stale_seconds`. [4](#0-3)  If the stale anchor and fresh primary differ by less than the configured tolerance, `blend` publishes their midpoint as the strict lending price. [5](#0-4)  The controller's risk path consequently uses the midpoint collateral value rather than the current market-side value. [6](#0-5) 

### Impact Explanation
An attacker can obtain borrow proceeds backed by less true collateral than the protocol believes is present, leaving lender assets exposed to bad debt after the borrower defaults or the inflated price disappears. [7](#0-6)  In the repository's executable scenario, an 82,800-second-old $1.00 anchor is blended with a fresh $0.91 XLM observation under a 10% tolerance, inflating Alice's collateral valuation by more than 4%. [8](#0-7)  The same account can then make a 70,000 USDC borrow that is rejected when the anchor is fresh, directly demonstrating excess credit issuance. [9](#0-8) 

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack is reachable by an unprivileged account owner through the normal `supply` and `borrow` entrypoints; no privileged role, code upgrade, leaked key, or oracle compromise is required. [7](#0-6)  It depends on an anchor feed being old enough to contain a materially different price while still passing its configured freshness and tolerance checks. [10](#0-9)  The checked-in audit fixture shows the required state is admitted when the anchor is 23 hours old under an 86,400-second freshness window and 1,000-basis-point tolerance. [11](#0-10) 

### Recommendation
For risk-sensitive lending reads, do not average a materially older anchor with a fresh market leg. [12](#0-11)  Apply a timestamp-spread limit whenever both legs contribute to the blended price, not only when both are labeled `Market`, or use the more conservative leg for collateral valuation. [13](#0-12)  At minimum, mark the outcome invalid when the non-market anchor is older than the primary by the market freshness bound, so an unavailable anchor fails closed instead of pulling the strict price toward stale data. [14](#0-13) 

### Proof of Concept
1. Configure XLM with a fresh market primary and a RedStone anchor whose `max_stale_seconds` is 86,400 and whose tolerance admits roughly 10% divergence. [15](#0-14) 
2. Let the primary move to $0.91 while the anchor remains at $1.00 and is 82,800 seconds old. [8](#0-7) 
3. Supply 100,000 XLM to the attacker's account. [16](#0-15) 
4. Call `borrow` for 70,000 USDC. [17](#0-16) 
5. The stale-anchor run succeeds, while the fresh-anchor control returns `InsufficientCollateral`, proving that the midpoint of the stale and fresh legs created the excess borrowing capacity. [9](#0-8)
