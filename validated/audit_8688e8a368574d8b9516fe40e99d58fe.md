The `DIAWhitelistedStaking.stakeForAddress` bug is rooted in a "stake on behalf of a beneficiary with a caller-chosen reward share" design. NEAR core-contracts has no equivalent function. In `staking-pool/src/lib.rs`, the staking pool operations (`deposit`, `deposit_and_stake`, `stake`, `withdraw`, `unstake`) all act exclusively on `env::predecessor_account_id()` — there is no way for a caller to designate an arbitrary beneficiary account, nor any reward-share parameter that could be manipulated in favor of a "principal" over a beneficiary [1](#0-0) . Reward distribution in this contract is computed purely from the epoch's staking rewards and a single, owner-set `reward_fee_fraction`, split between the pool owner and delegators pro-rata by "stake" shares — there is no per-call reward-share assignment mechanism at all [2](#0-1) .

The `whitelist/src/lib.rs` contract (the actual "whitelist" component in this codebase) only tracks approved staking-pool and factory account IDs; `add_staking_pool` is gated by `factory_whitelist` membership or foundation privilege, with no reward-share or beneficiary-assignment logic that an unprivileged caller could hijack [3](#0-2) . Similarly, `staking-pool-factory/src/lib.rs`'s `create_staking_pool` lets any user create and self-whitelist a new pool, but the owner is fixed to the caller-provided `owner_id` and doesn't create any second-party reward-share ambiguity analogous to the reported bug [4](#0-3) .

Since core-contracts has no `stakeForAddress`-style entrypoint or any function permitting an unprivileged caller to set reward-share allocation on behalf of another account, there is no reachable root cause analogous to the reported issue.
