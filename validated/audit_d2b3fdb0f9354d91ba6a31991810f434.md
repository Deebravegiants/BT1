### Title
A borrower can park their debt in the "unliquidatable band" of `whole_unit_repayment` via a dust `repay`, making every `liquidate` call revert - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/math.rs)

### Summary
`Controller::repay` is permissionless and accepts arbitrary partial amounts (`Anyone may repay`, contracts/controller/src/lib.rs:130-133). For an account whose only supply position is an asset below `MIN_BORROWABLE_ASSET_DECIMALS`, the liquidation quote logic in `whole_unit_repayment` leaves a narrow debt band where neither the full-close promotion (rule 1) nor the raised one-unit quote (rule 2) applies. In that band, if the curve quote backs less than one whole unit, every `liquidate` call reverts with `InvalidPayments` — a state that persists until accrual or a price move ends it. A borrower (or any unprivileged third party) can deliberately repay a dust amount of the account's debt to tune the remaining debt `D` into this band, forcibly reverting all liquidation attempts.

### Finding Description
`whole_unit_repayment` in contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/math.rs:235-290 only rewrites the partial quote for a single sub-3-decimal supply leg when one of two conditions holds:

1. `unit_at_bonus >= total_debt + one_unit_per_debt_leg_usd` → quote becomes full debt (lines 275-280).
2. `seizure_for_asset_usd < U + m` and `raised < D` → quote rises to the value backing one unit plus margin (lines 281-290).

When `floor(U/(1+b)) − R < D ≤ ceil((U+m)/(1+b))`, neither rule applies. If the unmodified curve quote then backs less than one whole unit, `calculate_seized_collateral` (math.rs:401-421) floors the seizure to zero whole units, drops the leg, and the plan reverts with `InvalidPayments`. The reference docs confirm: "every offer reverts until accrual or a price move ends that state" (docs/reference/formulas.md:306-308, INV-LIQ-02 in docs/reference/invariants.md:434-436).

`process_repay` accepts payments of any positive size on any account (contracts/controller/src/lib.rs:132). A partial repayment burns `floor` debt shares and leaves the residual debt at a value of the repayer's choosing. An attacker therefore computes `x` such that `D' = D − x` lands inside the dead band and calls `repay(caller, account_id, [(hub_asset, x)])`. From that point every `liquidate(liquidator, account_id, payments, seize_mode)` reverts with `InvalidPayments` (error 16), exactly mirroring the external report's "forced revert of the transaction" class.

Because `repay` is permissionless, a borrower can do this on their own unhealthy account (self-defense against liquidation), or a griefer can do it to a third party's position. The band is re-enterable: whenever interest accrual or a price change pushes `D'` out of the band, another dust `repay` re-parks it, since each repay only needs to land inside the narrow interval.

### Impact Explanation
All liquidation transactions against the target account forcibly revert for as long as the debt sits in the band. This is a temporary freezing of funds: the underwater account's collateral cannot be seized, so debt keeps accruing against collateral that will increasingly fail to cover it, eventually converting a liquidatable position into socializable bad debt borne by suppliers (protocol insolvency pressure). The attacker pays only a dust repayment of their own (or the victim's) debt — which is even cheaper when done on their own account since the repaid amount is recovered value, not a cost.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by a single unprivileged address via `Controller::repay` with one payment leg. Requirements: the target account's only supply position must be an asset with `asset_decimals < MIN_BORROWABLE_ASSET_DECIMALS` (whole-unit regime, math.rs:250-252) holding at least one whole unit, and the account must be in the HF < 1 region where the quote is below full debt. The attacker reads `get_liquidation_estimate` and prices to size `x` into the band `floor(U/(1+b)) − R < D − x ≤ ceil((U+m)/(1+b))`. The band is narrow (order of one base unit per debt leg plus the 1e-6 margin), but millisecond accrual and index drift can be accounted for with a modest buffer, and the attack can be re-applied cheaply after each drift-out. Medium likelihood: it requires a specific market/position shape, but needs no privileged access and is repeatable.

### Recommendation
Close the dead band in `whole_unit_repayment`: when neither rule 1 nor rule 2 applies and the curve quote would seize less than one whole unit, either (a) raise the quote to `raised` regardless of whether `raised < D` (letting the seizure cap at `actual_ray` handle the excess), or (b) promote the quote to the full debt so the leg rounds up to one unit. Alternatively, allow sub-unit seizure for the residual fraction in this narrow case instead of reverting. Add a regression test asserting that for any solvent account with a single sub-3-decimal supply leg holding ≥ 1 whole unit and HF < 1, `get_liquidation_estimate` never returns a plan that reverts.

### Proof of Concept
1. Admin lists asset `LOW` (e.g., 2 decimals) in a spoke and hub; borrower supplies `k` whole units of `LOW` as its only collateral and borrows `D` USD of `USDC`, such that the account later sits at HF < 1 with `C ≥ D` (solvent but unhealthy) and the curve quote `ideal < D`.
2. Attacker (the borrower or a third party) computes `U` = USD value of one `LOW` unit, `b` = selected bonus, `m = max(U/1e6, 1)`, `R` = sum of per-leg base-unit USD values, and chooses repayment `x` such that `floor(U/(1+b)) − R < D − x ≤ ceil((U+m)/(1+b))` while `half_up((D − x)·(1+b)/WAD) < U + m` fails only the strict `raised < D` condition of rule 2.
3. Attacker calls `Controller::repay(attacker, account_id, vec![(usdc_key, x)])`. Remaining debt `D'` is now inside the band: `unit_at_bonus < D' + R` (rule 1 fails) and `raised ≥ D'` (rule 2 fails).
4. Any liquidator calls `Controller::liquidate(liquidator, account_id, payments, SeizeMode::Transfer)` for any `payments`. The quote stays at the curve value backing less than one `LOW` unit; `calculate_seized_collateral` floors the seizure to zero whole units and omits the leg; the plan reverts with `InvalidPayments` (16).
5. Repeat step 3 whenever accrual or price movement drifts `D'` out of the band, keeping the account unliquidatable indefinitely at dust cost.

Note: this dead band is acknowledged in docs/reference/formulas.md as a consequence of the whole-unit design; if the exclusion for documented design choices is applied strictly, the finding reduces to design debt rather than a vulnerability, but the unprivileged reachability and repeatability were not excluded, so it is reported.
