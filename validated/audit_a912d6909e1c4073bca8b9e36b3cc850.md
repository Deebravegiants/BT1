### Title
No lower-bound protection on `_seedRebalancing`'s post-rebalance `router.addLiquidity` deposit leg — LP can be seeded at a manipulated ratio, permanently diverting curve-raised LT/token value - ([File: packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol])

### Summary
The external report flags a StopLoss executor that closes a position against whatever the live price happens to be, with no floor to prevent the trigger from firing far past the point the trader intended, causing unnecessary loss. The closest reachable analog in alt.fun is `Bonding._seedRebalancing`'s deposit leg, which calls the HyperSwap V2 router's `addLiquidity` with `amountMin = 1` for both sides [1](#0-0) . Like the StopLoss order that has no lower bound on the price it will execute at, this deposit has no lower bound on the ratio/amounts it will accept — it will deposit at whatever ratio the pool sits at after the (attacker-boundable) rebalance swap, without ever reverting on an unfavorable outcome.

### Finding Description
Graduation's phase 2 (`finalizeGraduation` → `_seedUniswapV2Direct`) has three regimes; the vulnerable one is regime 3, reached when an attacker pre-mints LP on the HyperSwap V2 TOKEN/LT pair before `finalizeGraduation` runs (a permissionless two-phase graduation window that anyone can front-run, per the scope rules) [2](#0-1) . In that regime, `_seedRebalancing` computes a swap direction and budget to push the pool toward the cached curve-close ratio (`tokensForLP`/`ltFromPair`), then hands the *unswapped remainder* to `_pairRebalance`/`_routerDepositAndDispose`, which deposits via the router's `addLiquidity` [3](#0-2) .

The rebalance swap itself is capped at a fixed `maxSwap` budget (`_swapBudget`), and the natspec states explicitly that the captured LP share for the attacker is only "bounded" — not eliminated — by `max(reserveToken/tokensForLP, reserveLT/ltFromPair)`, i.e. the attacker's donation size relative to the graduation's own inventory determines how much value bleeds to their pre-existing LP position [4](#0-3) . Because the final deposit uses `addLiquidity(min=1, min=1)`, there is no lower bound requiring the resulting price/ratio to be within any tolerance of the true curve-close price — exactly analogous to the StopLoss order executing at any price at or below the trigger with no floor. The deposit will always succeed and "donate" value into the attacker's LP share rather than reverting when the post-swap ratio is still meaningfully off from `tokensForLP/ltFromPair`.

### Impact Explanation
This is a permanent, protocol-funded loss: curve-raised LT and the 250M creator/curve-reserved tokens are drained from `Bonding` and deposited into a pool an attacker pre-seeded and partially controls, with no floor rejecting a bad outcome. The value that "leaks" to the attacker's LP position is real LT/token value that belonged to the curve (i.e., ultimately to sellers/holders exiting post-graduation and to the protocol's LP), a class of loss the validation rules explicitly accept ("an LP seeded away from the curve close price"). Because `addLiquidity`'s `amountMin` is hardcoded to `1`, the deposit can never protect against this — same root defect as a StopLoss with no lower price bound: the executor faithfully does its job but at a price/ratio nobody who owns the funds would have accepted, with no way to abort.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reaching regime 3 requires only that an unprivileged address mint LP on the not-yet-existing/empty HyperSwap V2 TOKEN/LT pair before `finalizeGraduation` runs — explicitly named as reachable in the scope ("pre-creating or pre-seeding the HyperSwap V2 TOKEN/LT pair before graduation"). `finalizeGraduation` is permissionless and callable by anyone once `TokenGraduating` fires, giving an attacker a window (the doc notes a keeper drives it "within ~60s," but any address can call it earlier or race the keeper) to pre-seed the pair with a self-funded, skewed ratio before the phase-2 call lands. No special role or timing precision beyond "front-run the keeper" is needed.

### Recommendation
Replace the `amountMin = 1` calls in the `_seedRebalancing` → deposit path with a bound derived from the cached `(tokensForLP, ltFromPair)` ratio and the rebalance's own `maxSwap`/budget accounting — i.e., require the router deposit to actually land within an acceptable tolerance of the curve-close price, and fall back to `_seedDirectMint` (burn-and-retry) rather than accepting an under-protected donation when that tolerance can't be met, mirroring how a real StopLoss needs a floor beyond which it refuses to execute.

### Proof of Concept
1. Token graduates via `Bonding._enterGraduating`, caching `tokensForLP`/`ltFromPair` at the last curve price [5](#0-4) .
2. Before the keeper (or anyone) calls `finalizeGraduation`, an attacker calls HyperSwap V2 `Factory.createPair(TOKEN, LT)` (or it doesn't exist yet), transfers a self-funded skewed `(TOKEN, LT)` amount into the pair, and calls `pair.mint(attacker)` to obtain LP tokens at a hostile ratio.
3. `finalizeGraduation(tokenAddress)` is called (permissionless) → `_seedUniswapV2Direct` sees `totalSupply() != 0` → routes to `_seedRebalancing` [6](#0-5) .
4. The rebalance swap, capped at `maxSwap`, only partially corrects the ratio if the attacker's pre-seed is large enough; `_routerDepositAndDispose` then deposits the remainder via `addLiquidity(amountMin=1, amountMin=1)`, which unconditionally succeeds and mints LP at the still-skewed ratio, handing a portion of the curve's LT/token value to the attacker's pre-existing LP position.
5. Result: `LPLock` receives less value than the curve close price implies, breaking Invariant #1 ("Zero price gap") documented in `docs/contracts-scope.md` for a large-enough pre-seed, with no revert path available since the deposit has no effective minimum-output floor.
