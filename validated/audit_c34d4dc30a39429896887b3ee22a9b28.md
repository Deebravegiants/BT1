### Title
Predictable TWAP lag in the price aggregator lets a borrower front-run repricing and leave the pool undercollateralized - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/reflector.rs)

### Summary
The Bancor bug class is "oracle repricing is predictable, so an attacker trades on the stale rate before the update and profits after it." XOXNO Lending has the same shape in its own pricing pipeline: every production mainnet market configures its primary Reflector leg with `read_mode: {Twap: 3}`, and `read_twap` returns an arithmetic mean of the last `records` observations stamped with the *oldest* observation's timestamp. Because the blended price necessarily trails spot by up to `(records - 1) * resolution` seconds, a single unprivileged address can observe a market move, know with certainty which direction the aggregated price will take, and call `controller::borrow` (or `multiply` / `flash_position`) at the stale, too-high collateral valuation before the TWAP catches down — extracting a loan that becomes bad debt once the price re-anchors.

### Finding Description
`read_twap` averages the last N Reflector records and deliberately uses `oldest_ts` as the observation timestamp (`contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/reflector.rs:196-202`). `attest` only requires that the TWAP span fit inside `max_stale` (`contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/reflector.rs:51-57`), so a `Twap(3)` feed at typical Reflector resolution (~300s) is admitted with a built-in lag of up to ~600s and accepted as fresh for up to `max_stale_seconds` (3600 in production configs, e.g. the XLM oracle at `configs/ops/mainnet/11fc3ae3...json`).

Two properties make this exploitable rather than merely slow:

1. **Deterministic repricing.** The mean and its timestamp are pure functions of already-published Reflector rounds. An attacker who reads the same feed (open view, `read_price_data`/`prices` on the Reflector contract) knows exactly what price the aggregator will report for the next several hundred seconds — the precise analog of seeing the Chainlink update in the mempool.
2. **Timestamp backdating masks the lag.** Reporting `timestamp: oldest_ts` means the engine's staleness and leg-age-spread checks treat the blended price as old-but-valid, so the stale-high price remains usable by `borrow`'s health-factor / `min_borrow_collateral_usd` checks for the entire `max_stale` window instead of failing fast.

The dual-leg tolerance band (`RequireDisjoint`, ~±5–10% in the production configs) only fails the price when the two legs disagree by more than the band. It does not protect this path in the two cases that matter: (a) the second leg (RedStone `Fundamental` nature, `max_stale_seconds` up to 57600) also lags the move, so both legs sit within the band at a stale-high price; and (b) moves smaller than the band pass through fully, giving the attacker a repeatable, bounded-but-guaranteed overvaluation each time the market drifts.

### Impact Explanation
Protocol insolvency via bad debt. The attacker supplies collateral, calls `borrow` (or uses `multiply`/`flash_position` for leverage) at the predictably-too-high TWAP valuation, and lets the position default when the aggregated price re-anchors downward. Recovery flows through `liquidate`/`clean_bad_debt`, which writes the shortfall down against the supply index — i.e., the loss is socialized onto suppliers. Each exploit cycle is bounded by the tolerance band (~5–10% of collateral), but it is repeatable on every market move, matching the "depletes reserves over time" character of the original finding.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium-to-high: it requires only an unprivileged account, own funds for collateral, and a public price feed read — no mempool ordering games are even needed because the lag is structural in `read_twap`'s averaging, not dependent on transaction ordering. The limiting factor is that extraction per cycle is bounded by the tolerance band and requires the second leg to also lag (true for `Fundamental`-nature RedStone feeds with 46800–57600s staleness windows in production configs) or a sub-band move, so realized profit per iteration is modest but recurring.

### Recommendation
- Use the *newest* observation timestamp for TWAP observations, or apply a tighter effective staleness bound (`max_stale - twap_span`) so a lagging blend expires sooner.
- Add a directional asymmetry: for collateral valuation prefer `min(spot_leg, twap_leg)` instead of blending, so the price used for borrowing cannot sit above the freshest available reading.
- For the second leg, prefer `Market`-nature feeds with short `max_stale_seconds` so the tolerance band actually fails on lag divergence rather than passing on two equally stale legs.
- Optionally reject `borrow` when the per-leg timestamps differ by more than a small bound (the engine already tracks per-source timestamps), so stale-high blends can't be collateralized at all.

### Proof of Concept
1. Market: XLM collateral, USDC debt. Oracle leg A = Reflector `read_mode: Twap(3)`, `resolution` 300s, `max_stale_seconds` 3600; leg B = RedStone `Fundamental`, `max_stale_seconds` 46800 (`configs/ops/mainnet/11fc3ae3...json`).
2. XLM spot drops 8% on external venues. Reflector's next round publishes at the lower price, but `read_twap` still returns the mean of two old high rounds + one new round, timestamped `oldest_ts` — a ~5% overvaluation, inside the 11000/9091 bps tolerance band against the equally lagged RedStone leg.
3. Attacker supplies XLM and calls `controller.borrow` for the maximum USDC permitted at the blended (stale-high) price — a loan that is already undercollateralized at true market value.
4. Two more Reflector rounds later the mean converges to the real price; HF < 1, the position is liquidated, and the residual shortfall is absorbed by `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` as a supply-index write-down — loss borne by suppliers. Repeat on the next volatility episode.

Uncertainty note: I did not fully trace the `borrow` health-factor path in `contracts/controller` to confirm there is no additional per-leg freshness or directional guard beyond the aggregator's staleness/tolerance checks; if the controller independently clamps collateral value to the cheaper leg or a spot read, this specific vector would be mitigated.
