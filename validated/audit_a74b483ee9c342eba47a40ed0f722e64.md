### Title
LP-collateral liquidation DoS: an unprivileged user can push an Aquarius LP fair-value price outside its sanity band, reverting every `liquidate` - ([File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs])

### Summary
XOXNO Lending prices Aquarius LP collateral with a **sole-source** `AquariusLp`/`AquariusStableLp` oracle whose value is derived from the pool's **live reserves**. The resolved price is enforced against a stored `[min_sanity_price_wad, max_sanity_price_wad]` band and any out-of-band result raises `SanityBoundViolated` (223), which propagates through `prices()` and reverts the controller call. Because LP oracles are restricted to exactly one source (`SourceCountOutOfRange` if mixed) and are exempt from tolerance checks ("the band is the only backstop"), there is no second leg to fall back to. A borrower holding LP-token collateral can imbalance the underlying Aquarius pool — e.g., a direct token transfer into the pool address or a one-sided swap — to inflate `sqrt(value_a * value_b)` (or the stable invariant `D`) beyond the band, making every attempt to price that asset revert and therefore every `liquidate`/`clean_bad_debt` on their account revert.

This mirrors the report's bug class exactly: a single-pool price read trusted for liquidation, manipulable by an unprivileged address into a revert path, yielding liquidation avoidance.

### Finding Description
- `common/src/oracle/lp.rs::fair_lp_price_wad` computes LP share price as `2 * sqrt(value_a * value_b) * WAD / share_supply_wad` where `value_*` come from live pool `reserve` reads. Reserve imbalance directly moves the product.
- `contracts/price-aggregator/src/admin.rs::validate_asset_oracle` enforces that an Aquarius LP oracle has exactly one source (`SourceCountOutOfRange` otherwise) and skips tolerance validation for it — the sanity band is the only guard.
- The resolution engine enforces the stored band; per `docs/reference/errors.md`, code 223 `SanityBoundViolated` fires when "the resolved price falls outside the oracle's stored min/max sanity band," and `prices`/`price_spread` "fail on invalid outcomes."
- `contracts/controller/src/external/price_aggregator.rs::fetch_prices` calls `prices(...)` and panics on missing entries — any health-factor computation (required by `liquidate`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `multiply`, etc.) reverts when the LP price resolution fails.
- Deployed configs confirm sole-source LP oracles with finite bands, e.g., `configs/mainnet/markets.json` `XLMUSDC_LP` band [0.45, 2.5] (~5.5x), `AQUAUSDC_LP` band ~10x, testnet `XLMUSDC_LP` [0.4, 3.0] with `min_pool_value_wad` of only ~$2.5k.

`min_pool_value_wad` only guards against the pool being too small; it does not cap upward reserve inflation. Donating the cheaper leg to the pool inflates its reserve unboundedly, and since donation does not mint shares, an attacker holding most of the pool's shares can recover nearly all of it via `remove_liquidity` afterward — the net cost is only the share leaked to other LPs.

### Impact Explanation
While the band is breached:
- `liquidate` on any account whose collateral or debt set includes the LP asset reverts in `fetch_prices`, so a borrower whose HF drops below 1 cannot be liquidated — same impact as the report (avoid liquidation, eventual bad debt / protocol insolvency).
- `clean_bad_debt` and `recapitalize` paths that price the account also revert.
- Any user supplying or owing the LP asset cannot `withdraw`, `borrow`, `repay` with collateral-swap, or run strategies — temporary freezing of funds until governance narrows/rebases the band or the pool rebalances.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires moving `sqrt(va·vb)` (or stable `D`) by the band ratio — roughly √(band_multiple) reserve inflation of one leg, e.g., ~5–10x reserve imbalance for a 10x band. For thin pools (testnet floor ~$2.5k, and any shallow mainnet LP like XLMAQUA/AQUAUSDC) this is cheap and largely recoverable. A motivated underwater borrower only needs to sustain the imbalance around liquidation attempts; even intermittent reverts block liquidation. Reachable by an unprivileged address via `pool.swap` or plain `token.transfer(pool, ...)`.

### Recommendation
- Quote the LP composition against a manipulation-resistant reserve source: use time-weighted/TWAP reserves from Aquarius or value the share by redeeming constituents at oracle prices rather than spot reserves, i.e., the analog of "query the correct pool directly" is "don't derive collateral value from spot pool balances."
- Treat an out-of-band LP reading as *last-known-good* or degrade to `PriceStatus` that blocks new borrows but still permits liquidation with a conservative (band-min) collateral valuation, rather than reverting.
- Alternatively allow a second independent source leg for LP oracles so a single manipulated pool cannot force a revert.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker supplies `XLMUSDC_LP` collateral and borrows near max LTV in hub 3.
2. Borrowed/own funds are used to `transfer` a large amount of USDC directly to the Aquarius pool `CA6PUJL…` (or swap one direction), inflating `reserve_b`.
3. `fair_lp_price_wad` resolves `2*sqrt(va*vb)/shares` above `max_sanity_price_wad = 2.5e18`.
4. Any `controller.liquidate(attacker_account, ...)` → `fetch_prices` → `prices(Token(LP))` → `SanityBoundViolated` (223) → revert.
5. Attacker maintains the imbalance (or repeats it cheaply around liquidation attempts), avoiding liquidation until their debt becomes bad debt; they later `remove_liquidity` to recover most of the donated reserve.

Caveat: I could not fully confirm within the iteration limit the exact revert site inside `engine.rs` (band check during `resolve`) versus a status-flag-then-fail path in `lib.rs::prices`, but `docs/reference/errors.md` and `fetch_prices` semantics establish that an out-of-band LP resolution makes the controller's price fetch fail, which is sufficient for the DoS path described.
