### Title
Bad-debt write-down clamps the supply index at a nonzero floor, leaving wiped-out suppliers a phantom claim that drains fresh deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
When socialized bad debt meets or exceeds the total supplied value, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps the new supply index at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW = RAY/1000` instead of zeroing supplier shares. Total `supplied` shares are left untouched, so every pre-wipeout share retains a positive floor-valued claim with zero cash behind it. The deficit is only masked by the per-withdrawal `require_reserves` cash check; the first fresh deposit into the market can be fully withdrawn by a wiped-out holder. The unprivileged `liquidate` (borrow-side seize) and `clean_bad_debt` entrypoints both reach this code path.

### Finding Description
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs` computes `remaining = total_supplied_value - min(bad_debt, total_supplied_value)` and derives `new_supply_index = supply_index * remaining / total_supplied_value`. When `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, `remaining` is zero, but the result is floored at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY / 1_000`, `common/src/constants/pool.rs:9`):

```rust
// contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89
let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);
let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
let new_supply_index = cache.supply_index().mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);
cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
```

Neither `supplied` shares nor per-account `scaled_amount` positions are burned or reset — `seize::apply` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:24-28`) only calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` plus `burn_debt`. The result is analogous to the CVE's out-of-bounds read: a claim value read "past the end" of actual backing — `unscale_supply_floor(scaled)` returns a positive token amount while pool `cash` is zero.

The project's own unit tests demonstrate the exploit mechanics directly in `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-495` (`test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` and `test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard`): after the clamp, `stranded > 0`, a fresh deposit is credited, and `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, old_scaled)` pays out exactly the fresh deposit, leaving `cash < fresh_claim` — the new depositor's funds are gone.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds. After a full wipeout socialization, the pool's books show `supplied * FLOOR_INDEX > 0` of stranded claims against zero cash. Any subsequent depositor's tokens can be withdrawn by a pre-wipeout supplier (who may be the attacker themselves via a second account holding dust supply). The withdrawal is legitimate per `resolve_withdrawal` — it burns all shares and pays the floor-valued balance (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:105-121`) — and `require_reserves` passes because the fresh deposit just increased cash. Each fresh deposit is drained up to the stranded claim size, and the new supplier is left with an unbacked claim. This repeats, permanently impairing the market.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by a single unprivileged address:

1. `liquidate` → borrow-side `PoolSeizeEntry` → `ops::seize::apply` → `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:24-28`). A liquidation that seizes debt worth more than the whole market's supply value triggers the clamp. No admin role is required.
2. `clean_bad_debt` → `socialize_bad_debt` with `BadDebtGate::DustCapped` → same seize path (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs:196-238`). The permissionless gate only requires insolvency and dust-level collateral — satisfiable by a self-created dust-insolvent account or any naturally underwater account.

A market with bad debt exceeding total supply value is realistic after a sharp price crash on a collateral asset or a low-liquidity spoke market. Once clamped, every share retains 0.1% of its pre-wipeout face value indefinitely; the attacker needs only a residual supply position (dust is enough to participate pro-rata) plus patience for the next depositor. The drain is bounded per deposit by the stranded claim but repeats across deposits.

### Recommendation
When `capped == total_supplied_value` (full wipeout), reset supplier claims to zero rather than clamping the index: either set `supplied = 0` and `revenue` correspondingly (or write the index down to a true zero and accept the index reset on next deposit), so no share retains a claim on future cash. More generally, gate withdrawals and supply claims on the market not being in a wiped state: if `supply_index == SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` as a result of a wipeout, treat surviving `scaled_amount` as zero claim until `recapitalize` restores backing. Alternatively, remove the floor clamp and document the share-reset behavior on re-seeding the market.

### Proof of Concept
Mirrored by the in-repo unit test `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:373-428` (`test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard`):

```text
1. Market state: supplied = 1000 RAY shares, supply_index = RAY, cash = 0.
2. Trigger wipeout: a liquidate/clean_bad_debt seize entry whose borrow-side
   bad_debt = unscale_borrow_ceil_ray(debt) >= total_supplied_value (e.g. 5000 RAY).
   → apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index clamps supply_index to RAY/1000 instead of 0.
3. Stranded claim: unscale_supply_floor(1000 RAY shares) > 0 with cash = 0;
   require_reserves blocks withdrawal only because there is nothing to steal yet.
4. Victim supplies `stranded` tokens → cash = stranded, their shares minted at the
   floored index.
5. Attacker (wiped holder) calls withdraw with amount = 0 (WITHDRAW_ALL_SENTINEL):
   resolve_withdrawal returns gross = stranded > 0, require_reserves passes,
   gross = victim's entire deposit is paid out.
6. Post-state: cash < victim's claim — protocol is insolvent toward the fresh
   depositor; funds were stolen.
```

Reachability: step 2 is permissionless via `liquidate` (any account whose debt-side seizure writes down more than the market's supply) or `clean_bad_debt` on a dust-insolvent account (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs:196-238`); steps 4-5 are ordinary `supply`/`withdraw` calls.

Note: I verified the seize/interest code path and the floor clamp directly, and the two existing unit tests assert the same drain semantics. I could not fully verify whether an additional guard elsewhere (e.g., a market-halt flag on index-floor) blocks `supply`/`withdraw` after a wipeout — no such guard appears in `ops::seize::apply`, `interest.rs`, or `withdraw.rs`'s reserve check, but a complete enumeration of every entrypoint guard was not possible within the available searches.
