### Title
Emporium EIP-712 signature does not bind the CircomData/output-destination context, allowing a signed EmporiumStack to be front-run and captured by an unrelated attacker - (File: contracts/external-actions/emporium/upgradeable/EmporiumUpgradeable.sol)

### Summary
`verifyWallet` in `EmporiumUpgradeable.sol` only recovers a signature over `EMPORIUM_SIGNATURE_TYPEHASH(message, opsHash, maxFee, deadline)`, leaving `circomData.feeStructure`, `circomData.relay`, `circomData.erc20TokenAddresses`, `deltaAmountChanges`, and — critically — the output `stealthAddressStructure`/beneficiary entirely unbound by the signer's EIP-712 signature. `usedMessages[emporiumMessage]` is a one-shot, first-come-first-served guard, so any unprivileged party who observes a signer's not-yet-submitted `EmporiumStack` (with a far-future `deadline`) can wrap it inside their own freshly-generated proof/`CircomData`, execute the signed `ops` against the signer's `HinkalWallet`, and redirect any resulting token balance change into their own shielded output instead of the signer's.

### Finding Description
The claimed invariant is: `(assets leaving the wallet, their destination) == (ops, maxFee)` the owner signed.

`verifyWallet` (contracts/external-actions/emporium/upgradeable/EmporiumUpgradeable.sol:302-349) computes: [1](#0-0) 
which recovers the signer over `(message, opsHash, maxFee, deadline)` only. Nothing here — nor anywhere else in the contract — commits to `circomData.stealthAddressStructure` (the recipient of the minted output UTXO), `circomData.erc20TokenAddresses` (which tokens are swept), `circomData.relay`, or the eventual `msg.sender` of `Hinkal.transact`.

`usedMessages` is consumed unconditionally at the very top of `verifyWallet`, before the signature is even checked, and is keyed purely by `circomData.emporiumMessage` — a single global namespace, not scoped to signer or a specific submitter/proof: [2](#0-1) 

Meanwhile, `getHashedCalldata`/`performHinkalChecks` do bind the *entire* `CircomData` (including `feeStructure`, `relay`, `externalActionData` and hence the raw signed `EmporiumStack` bytes) into `calldataHash`, and that `calldataHash` feeds `getSignedMessageHash`, which is a public input the SNARK constrains against the prover's own `spendingPublicKey`/`eddsaSignature`: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

This binding prevents literally *reusing the identical proof* with mutated `calldata` — but it does **not** prevent a different unprivileged party from generating their **own valid proof over their own UTXOs/deposits** and simply embedding the victim's already-signed `EmporiumStack` bytes (`v, r, s, signerAddress, ops, maxFee, deadline`) as `externalActionData.externalActionMetadata`, as long as they use the same `emporiumMessage` value the victim signed (required for the signature to recover) and submit it before the victim's own transaction lands.

In `runAction` (contracts/external-actions/emporium/upgradeable/EmporiumUpgradeable.sol:76-160), the ops execute exactly as the victim authorized (`IHinkalWallet(stack.signerAddress).callHinkalWallet(op.endpoint, op.callData, op.value)`), pulling from the victim's `HinkalWallet` balance/allowance. But the resulting balance delta on the Emporium contract for `circomData.erc20TokenAddresses` — attacker-chosen — is swept into a UTXO output: [5](#0-4) 
which is minted to whichever `stealthAddressStructure`/spending key the *caller of `Hinkal.transact`* (the attacker, using their own proof) supplied. Nothing ties the destination of this output back to the signer of the `EmporiumStack`.

Existing guards that were considered and found insufficient:
- `performHinkalChecks`'s `calldataHash` check and the circuit's `signedMessageHash` constraint bind the CircomData to the *submitter's own proof*, not to the *EmporiumStack signer's intent* — they only stop verbatim proof reuse, not front-running with a fresh proof.
- `usedMessages` only stops the *same* `emporiumMessage` from being used twice; it does not restrict who can be the first to use it nor what surrounding `CircomData`/destination accompanies it.
- `feeStructure.flatFee > stack.maxFee` bounds the fee only, not the destination of swept balance changes.
- `onlyAllowedRecipient` restricts callers of `runAction` to Hinkal itself, but Hinkal is callable by any unprivileged address via `transact`.

### Impact Explanation
An attacker can capture token value that results from executing another wallet owner's authorized `EmporiumOperation`s (e.g. proceeds of a swap/withdraw executed on the victim's `HinkalWallet`) into their own shielded balance, by racing to submit the victim's signed-but-unused `EmporiumStack` wrapped in the attacker's own `CircomData`/proof/output-destination. This is "executing calls or moving assets a wallet owner or prover never authorised" — the owner authorized *what* gets called, not *who* receives the resulting funds or under what surrounding fee/relay/token context. This matches the High severity category. It is repeatable against every signed `EmporiumStack` with a still-unused `emporiumMessage` and a not-yet-elapsed `deadline`, and a far-future deadline widens the exploitation window significantly.

### Likelihood Explanation
Preconditions: the attacker must observe a valid, not-yet-consumed `EmporiumStack` signature+`emporiumMessage` (e.g., via mempool observation of the victim's or a relay's pending transaction, a leaked signed payload, or any other means an unprivileged observer has of seeing calldata before it lands), and the victim's `ops` must, when executed against the victim's `HinkalWallet`, produce a net positive balance change in some ERC-20/ETH held by the Emporium contract. Attacker cost is limited to generating one additional proof over their own deposit/UTXOs (something any unprivileged user can do) and front-running with sufficient gas. This is realistic wherever signed Emporium payloads are broadcast or relayed before being mined, which is the normal operational flow (a relay submits `Hinkal.transact` with a previously off-chain-signed `EmporiumStack`).

### Recommendation
Extend `EMPORIUM_SIGNATURE_TYPEHASH` to bind the full execution context the signer is authorizing: at minimum include the recipient/destination (`circomData.stealthAddressStructure` or an explicit recipient commitment), `circomData.erc20TokenAddresses`, `circomData.feeStructure`, and `circomData.relay` (or a hash of the full `calldataHash`) in the signed payload, so that a valid signature can only be used together with the exact `CircomData` context the signer approved. Additionally, consider binding `usedMessages` to `(signerAddress, emporiumMessage)` and requiring `msg.sender`/relay to match an allow-listed submitter chosen by the signer, closing the front-running window entirely.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry test plan:
1. Deploy `EmporiumUpgradeable`, a `HinkalWallet` for a victim EOA, fund the wallet with ERC-20 tokens.
2. Victim signs an `EmporiumStack` (`v,r,s`, `ops` = a call that swaps/transfers wallet tokens producing an on-Emporium balance increase, `maxFee`, `deadline` = far future) for a chosen `emporiumMessage`.
3. Before the victim's intended relay submits it, have "attacker" (a different EOA, with its own funded shielded UTXO and own valid ZK proof) call `Hinkal.transact` with a `CircomData` whose `externalActionData.externalActionMetadata` = the victim's signed `EmporiumStack` bytes and the same `emporiumMessage`, but attacker's own `erc20TokenAddresses`, `feeStructure`, `relay`, and `stealthAddressStructure`.
4. Assert: `usedMessages[emporiumMessage]` transitions to used on the attacker's transaction (blocking the victim's later submission with `UsedMessage`), and the token balance increase produced by executing the victim's ops is credited as a UTXO belonging to the attacker's shielded identity, not the victim's — i.e., assert `(assets leaving the victim wallet, their destination)` diverges from `(ops, maxFee)` the victim signed, since destination == attacker, not victim.
