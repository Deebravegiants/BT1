### Title
Stale non-market anchor leg is blended into collateral valuation without an age-spread bound, allowing over-borrowing and bad debt - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs)

### Summary
Tapioca M-17 maps directly onto XOXNO Lending's dual-source price path. In `blend` (`contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs:419-441`), the cross-leg age-spread staleness gate (`MAX_LEG_AGE_SPREAD_SECONDS = 3_600s`, `common/src/oracle/observation.rs:34`) is only applied when *both* legs are `FeedNature::Market`. When one leg is a non-market "anchor" feed (e.g., a NAV/anchor provider), its age is only bounded by its own `max_stale_seconds`/`max_price_stale_seconds` window — which may be as large as 86,400–93,600s. A stale anchor price that differs from the fresh market price by less than the configured tolerance band is then averaged into the midpoint price (`engine.rs:430`) that the controller uses for `borrow`, `withdraw`, `multiply`, and other risk-increasing entrypoints. This is exactly the "stale but within validity window" rate acceptance that enabled atomic bad-debt creation in Tapioca.

### Finding Description
- `read_feed` (`engine.rs:606-619`) marks a leg stale only when `now - ts > feed.max_stale_seconds`.
- `read_source` (`engine.rs:542-567`) ORs in staleness only versus `oracle.max_price_stale_seconds` (up to 26h).
- In `blend`, for `Legs::Two { primary, anchor }`, `spread_bounded = primary.nature == Market && anchor.nature == Market` (`engine.rs:420-421`), so a Reflector/Xoxno-anchor leg of non-Market nature can lag the primary leg by its entire declared staleness window without tripping `PriceFeedStale`.
- `deviation` only requires the two legs to sit inside the oracle tolerance band (up to 2,500 BPS per `BadLastTolerance` bounds), and the accepted `price_wad` is the midpoint (`engine.rs:427-430`).
- The existing audit test `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_borrow_withdraw_liquidate_stale_anchor_blend.rs` demonstrates the end-to-end path: an anchor frozen at $1.00 with a fresh market price of $0.91 (9% divergence, inside a 1,000 BPS tolerance) produces a midpoint that inflates ALICE's collateral >4% and lets a `borrow` succeed that fails with `INSUFFICIENT_COLLATERAL` when the anchor is fresh.

### Impact Explanation
An unprivileged attacker can supply collateral, let (or wait for) the anchor leg to go stale at a price above the true market price, then call `borrow`/`withdraw`/`multiply` against the midpoint-inflated valuation and extract the difference as real debt from the pool. When the market price remains depressed the position is undercollateralized at open — bad debt is created atomically with no prior position and no capital beyond the supplied collateral, which is itself overvalued. Loss is borne by lenders via the bad-debt write-down path (`clean_bad_debt`/supply-index write-down). Impact: High (protocol insolvency).

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a real price move that stays within the configured tolerance band yet is economically meaningful relative to LTV (e.g., ~5–10%), coinciding with anchor feed staleness within its allowed window — plausible for slow NAV/anchor feeds whose `max_stale_seconds` is legitimately set near a day, and for dual-source configs pairing a fast market leg with a slow anchor. No privileged access, bad parameters, or oracle dishonesty is needed — the feed is merely old within its own declared bounds. Likelihood: Low–Medium → Medium severity overall.

### Recommendation
Apply `MAX_LEG_AGE_SPREAD_SECONDS` (or a tighter, per-source bound) to *all* leg pairs, not only `Market`/`Market` pairs — i.e., drop the `spread_bounded` nature check or treat a non-Market leg's age as stale when it exceeds the primary leg's age by more than the spread. Alternatively, bound the anchor leg's effective staleness by `min(feed.max_stale_seconds, MAX_LEG_AGE_SPREAD_SECONDS)` relative to the freshest sibling leg when a two-source blend is produced. Consider also weighting the blend toward the fresher leg or failing closed for risk-increasing operations when a leg's timestamp lags the sibling beyond the spread, mirroring Tapioca's fix of requiring a current oracle reading for solvency-sensitive paths.

### Proof of Concept
Reproduced by the in-repo scenario `audit_borrow_withdraw_liquidate_stale_anchor_blends_5pct_skew_into_ltv` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_borrow_withdraw_liquidate_stale_anchor_blend.rs`):
1. Configure XLM oracle as dual-source: fresh Reflector primary at $0.91 plus a RedStone anchor leg frozen at $1.00 with timestamp `now - 82,800s` (within its 86,400s `max_stale_seconds`; tolerance 1,000 BPS).
2. `supply(BOB, USDC, 500_000)` to fund the pool; `supply(ALICE, XLM, 100_000)`.
3. `borrow(ALICE, USDC, 70_000)` succeeds because valuation uses midpoint $0.955 (collateral inflated >4% vs the fresh-anchor control), while the identical borrow with a fresh anchor reverts with `INSUFFICIENT_COLLATERAL`.
4. ALICE keeps the USDC; once the anchor updates to the true price the account is undercollateralized, and liquidation/bad-debt cleanup socializes the loss to suppliers.
