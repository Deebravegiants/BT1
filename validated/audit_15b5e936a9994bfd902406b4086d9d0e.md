### Title
Aquarius LP collateral price is manipulable by a flash-funded single-leg reserve donation, enabling over-borrowing - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs)

### Summary
The CUPID incident was a flash-loan-funded price manipulation against a valuation mechanism that trusted manipulable on-chain quantities. The XOXNO analog is the `AquariusLp` price source: `read()` prices an LP share from the pool's **live reserves** (`aquarius_pool_reserves_call`) combined with external leg prices via the geometric-mean formula `2 * sqrt(value_a * value_b) / total_shares` in `common/src/oracle/lp.rs:57-87`. That formula is invariant to swaps (which preserve the product of values) but is **not** invariant to a direct-token-transfer donation of one leg: donating `D` units of `token_a` raises `reserve_a` and inflates the LP price by `sqrt(1 + D/reserve_a)`, with no share minted to the donor. An unprivileged address can perform this donation inside the same transaction as a `borrow`, since the controller's Context fetches the price at borrow time and direct transfers to an AMM pool are unrestricted.

### Finding Description
`aquarius::read` at `contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs:90-114` reads `reserve_a`, `reserve_b`, and `total_shares` live from the Aquarius pool and feeds them into `fair_lp_price_wad` (`common/src/oracle/lp.rs:57`). Because `fair_lp_price_wad` uses `2 * sqrt(value_a * value_b)`, a swap inside the pool leaves the price unchanged, but a raw token transfer to the pool address raises exactly one leg's reserve, so the reported price rises as the square root of the donation ratio. The only bounds are `min_pool_value_wad` (a floor, not a cap) and the market's `min/max_sanity_price_wad` band — e.g. the mainnet `XLMUSDC_LP` market admits prices in `[0.45, 2.5] WAD` (`configs/mainnet/markets.json:1199-1230`), a ~5.5x window that comfortably contains a manipulated price. The inflated price flows into `Context`-cached strict pricing used for collateral valuation and `min_borrow_collateral_usd`, so `controller.borrow` accepts more debt than the collateral fairly supports. The donation is also recoverable: an attacker who mints LP shares (attacker-controlled share of supply) retrieves the donated reserve pro-rata via a normal `remove_liquidity`, so the manipulation cost approaches zero — the flash loan only needs to cover the mint collateral and fees.

### Impact Explanation
The attacker supplies LP shares as collateral, inflates their reported value within the sanity band, borrows above the true collateral value, then unwinds (remove liquidity to reclaim the donation, repay flash loan). The residual undercollateralized debt is left to the protocol: liquidation of the manipulated collateral cannot recover the shortfall once the price reverts, producing protocol insolvency / bad debt that is later socialized via the supply-index write-down in `clean_bad_debt`. Severity Medium: magnitude is capped by the sanity band and requires the attacker to dominate the LP pool's supply, but it needs only unprivileged actions (own trades on Aquarius, direct token transfer to the pool, controller `supply`/`borrow`) and no oracle dishonesty.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a market whose collateral is an Aquarius LP token priced by `AquariusLp`/`AquariusStableLp` with borrowing enabled, and an Aquarius pool shallow enough that the attacker can acquire a dominant share of `total_shares` — for stable pools the analogous single-leg donation shifts `D` less, but the constant-product case is directly exploitable. The flash loan removes the capital constraint; every step is a single-transaction, unprivileged call reachable per the scope rules (own trades on Aquarius, direct token transfer to the pool, `controller.supply`/`borrow`).

### Recommendation
Make the LP price donation-resistant: validate reserves against a manipulation bound (e.g., read reserves, additionally fetch `total_shares` twice or compare against a stored/TWAP reserve snapshot), or price the LP share by `min`-leg redemption (`min(value_a, value_b) * 2 / supply`-style conservative bounds) rather than the geometric mean alone, or tighten `min_sanity_price_wad`/`max_sanity_price_wad` per LP market so the donation gain ceiling is below the LTV-proportional profit. Alternatively, reject `AquariusLp` sources for borrowable-collateral markets where the underlying pool's shares are mintable to arbitrary minters within one transaction.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker takes a cash `flash_loan` (or `flash_position`) of USDC.
2. Calls Aquarius `deposit` to mint a dominant share of the target LP pool (e.g., XLM/USDC), receiving LP token `S`.
3. `controller.supply(hub, spoke, S)` — collateral enabled.
4. Direct SAC `transfer` of `D` units of `token_a` to the Aquarius pool address, raising `reserve_a` without minting shares.
5. `controller.borrow(hub, spoke, borrow_asset, amount)` — the Context resolves the LP key via `aquarius::read` (`contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs:90-114`), which reads the inflated `reserve_a`; `fair_lp_price_wad` (`common/src/oracle/lp.rs:72-86`) returns a price inflated by `sqrt(1 + D/reserve_a)`, still inside `[min_sanity_price_wad, max_sanity_price_wad]`.
6. Aquarius `remove_liquidity` burns `S` and returns the pro-rata reserves, recovering the donation.
7. Repay the flash loan (exact-balance settlement passes — the loan was fully serviced). The account retains debt exceeding fair collateral value; when the LP price reverts on the next block, the position is liquidatable but underwater, forcing `clean_bad_debt` to write the shortfall down against the supply index.

### Caveats / unexplored
I did not have remaining iterations to verify (a) whether the borrow path re-reads the oracle per transaction versus using a keeper-pushed cached price that would blunt intra-transaction manipulation (the Context is described as caching "strict prices" per operation, which supports the intra-tx read assumption, but the exact cache lifetime is unverified), and (b) whether `flash_position`'s flash guard or strategy sequencing prevents calling `supply`/`borrow` inside the same transaction as the Aquarius interactions — the guard blocks *controller* re-entry during callbacks, but the deposit/donation/borrow/remove-liquidity sequence can be issued as a plain transaction without any flash loan if the attacker has capital, so the bug does not depend on the flash path.
