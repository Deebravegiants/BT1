### Title
Push-pattern revenue claim permanently reverts when the accumulator or pool owner is blocked by the asset's issuer - (File: contracts/controller/src/markets.rs)

### Summary
XOXNO Lending mirrors the reported bug class: protocol revenue is collected by *pushing* tokens to externally-controlled recipient addresses inside `claim_revenue`, with no pull/claim pattern and no fault isolation. The pool pays its `Ownable` owner (the controller), and the controller immediately forwards the measured receipt to a single configured `accumulator` address. If either hop's recipient is denied by the token contract — e.g. an RWA share token with an issuer allowlist (LIQVID-style) or an SAC with clawback/blacklist semantics — the token transfer reverts, the whole `claim_revenue` call reverts, and accrued protocol revenue for that asset (and every asset batched with it) becomes unclaimable. The accrued revenue shares themselves are burned/debited in pool state during `accounting` before the transfer attempt within the same atomic invocation, so the failure repeats on every retry with no escape hatch inside the claim path.

### Finding Description
Two chained push transfers exist:

1. In the pool, `revenue::apply` burns claimable revenue shares, debits cash, then pushes `actual_amount` to `ownable::get_owner(env)` — an address the caller cannot choose (fixed at pool construction, and in deployment it is the controller): `contracts/pool/src/ops/revenue.rs` lines 22–34 [1](#0-0) .

2. In the controller, `claim_revenue_for_asset` reads a single storage-configured `accumulator`, measures the controller's balance delta from the pool payout, then pushes the full measured receipt to the accumulator via `payments::transfer_amount_measured`: `contracts/controller/src/markets.rs` lines 168–208 [2](#0-1) .

`claim_revenue` iterates a caller-supplied `Vec<HubAssetKey>` and calls `claim_revenue_for_asset` per asset inside one atomic transaction — a single failing asset reverts the entire batch [3](#0-2) .

The test suite already proves the failure mode is real and reachable: `lqv_revenue_claim_needs_the_controller_and_accumulator_allowlisted` demonstrates that with a LIQVID-style allowlisted RWA asset, `claim_revenue` reverts with `ACCOUNT_NOT_ALLOWED` (i) when the controller is not allowlisted (pool→controller hop fails), (ii) when the accumulator is not allowlisted (controller→accumulator hop fails), and (iii) that one gated market reverts the whole multi-asset batch [4](#0-3) .

`claim_revenue` is reachable by any signer — `require_authorized_caller` only requires the caller's own auth, and `test_permissionless_revenue_endpoints` shows a non-admin caller (BOB) can drive the sweep [5](#0-4) . The payout destinations, however, are push-fixed: the pool owner is set at construction, and the accumulator is only changeable through a governance `SetAccumulator` operation — so while the accumulator is blocked, **no** `claim_revenue` call including that asset can succeed, and revenue share burning/payment can never complete for it.

### Impact Explanation
Temporary freezing of unclaimed yield / freezing of protocol revenue. While the accumulator (or the controller/owner hop) is blocked by the token issuer, all accrued revenue for the affected market is locked: interest keeps minting revenue shares, but every `claim_revenue` attempt reverts. For a USDC/USDT-class asset with a blacklist, or an RWA asset whose issuer revokes admission, the freeze persists until governance executes a new `SetAccumulator` — and for the pool-owner hop, until ownership is transferred. There is no per-asset fault isolation: batching a failing asset with healthy assets DoSes the healthy claims too. Funds are not stolen, matching a Medium-severity temporary-freeze analog of the reported "automation DoS via reverting fee recipient."

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. The trigger does not require a malicious or privileged actor — it requires a token issuer action (blacklist/allowlist revocation) against the accumulator or controller address, which is exactly the class of external failure the original report describes for USDT/USDC recipients. The codebase's own RWA-collateral test path proves the denied-transfer scenario occurs in practice, and the claim entrypoint is permissionless, so the failure is immediately observable and unbypassable by any user.

### Recommendation
Adopt the pull pattern recommended in the report: instead of pushing to a storage-configured `accumulator`, credit an internal claimable balance per asset that the accumulator withdraws via its own authorized call. Alternatively, isolate failures per asset (skip-and-report rather than revert the batch) so one blocked market cannot freeze the others. For allowlisted RWA assets, gate or preflight the recipient admission check off the hot path so a revoked admission freezes only that market's claim.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy controller/pool; list an allowlist-gated asset (LIQVID-style share token) and a normal asset; supply and borrow so revenue accrues (the test does this via forced bad-debt socialization, leaving seized shares as pool revenue).
2. Governance sets `accumulator = A` via `AdminOperation::SetAccumulator`.
3. The token issuer leaves the controller and `A` off the allowlist (or blacklists `A` on a USDC-class SAC).
4. Any user calls `controller.claim_revenue(caller, [gated_asset])`:
   - pool `revenue::apply` transfers to owner (controller) — reverts with `ACCOUNT_NOT_ALLOWED` if the controller isn't admitted; or
   - controller `claim_revenue_for_asset` forwards to `A` — reverts if `A` isn't admitted.
5. `controller.claim_revenue(caller, [healthy_asset, gated_asset])` also reverts — one gated market takes the whole batch down.
6. Every retry reverts; revenue for that market is unclaimable until governance replaces the accumulator (or ownership moves) — matching the demonstrated test assertions at `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/liqvid_rwa_collateral.rs` lines 800–816 [4](#0-3) .

    let controller = env.current_contract_address();

    let _ = pool_claim_revenue_call(env, &pool_addr, hub_asset);

            asset: asset.clone(),
            caller: caller.clone(),
            accumulator,
