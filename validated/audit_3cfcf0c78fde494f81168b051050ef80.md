### Title
Silent `i128` saturation in `protocol_fee_shares` mints an unbounded revenue-share claim that can drain a market - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
`protocol_fee_shares` converts accrued protocol fees into supply-index shares using `mul_div_floor_saturating`, which silently clamps to `i128::MAX` when `fee * RAY / supply_index` overflows `i128`. The resulting share count is only bounded by `i128::MAX - supplied`, so an extreme-but-reachable accrual mints a near-`i128::MAX` revenue share position instead of reverting, corrupting the market's `supplied` book and backing a massive claim against pool cash.

### Finding Description
`common/src/math/fp_core.rs::mul_div_floor_saturating` returns `i128::MAX` on positive quotient overflow rather than panicking (fp_core.rs:180-201). `protocol_fee_shares` (index.rs:94-99) feeds `fee * RAY / supply_index` through it, then caps only at `headroom = i128::MAX - supplied`. The fee comes from `calculate_supplier_rewards` (index.rs:73-89): `accrued = borrowed*new_index - borrowed*old_index` in RAY units. With `borrow_index` capped at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY = 10^36` and `borrowed` admitted up to ~`i128::MAX / 10^(27-d)` scaled units, `borrowed * new_index / RAY` can approach `i128::MAX` while still fitting (the `Ray::mul` widened path handles it exactly). The accrued fee itself can therefore exceed `i128::MAX / RAY ≈ 1.7e11` raw — at which point `fee * RAY` overflows and saturates.

Unlike every other conversion in the codebase, which panics with `MathOverflow` on unrepresentable results (`mul_div_half_up`, `mul_div_floor`, position conversion "rejects overflow" per docs/reference/formulas.md:417), this site fails open to the maximum representable share count. `add_protocol_revenue` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:59-66` then adds those shares to both `revenue` and `supplied` via `cache.accrue_revenue`, so total supply shares are inflated to near `i128::MAX` without any corresponding cash.

### Impact Explanation
Once `supplied` is inflated to ~`i128::MAX`, the market's supply claim `supplied * supply_index` overflows subsequent `i128` products, and the revenue shares — which "participate in the supply index until claimed" (interest.rs:57) — represent a claim vastly exceeding real cash. `claim_revenue` and `recapitalize` operate on the claim-vs-cash shortfall; a permanently corrupted claim either lets the protocol revenue claim absorb the entire pool balance through `claim_revenue` (theft/freezing of supplier funds, since withdrawals then fail the backing gate), or bricks the market's arithmetic permanently. Either way the market is insolvent or inoperable.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachability requires a single market whose accrued interest fee crosses the `~1.7e11` raw RAY threshold in one accrual — i.e. very large `borrowed` at high index. The docs admit caps of ~170 billion whole tokens and a borrow-index ceiling of `10^36`, so the domain is not excluded by construction; it requires a large market and/or long accrual at the 200% APR cap. No privileged action is needed: `update_indexes` is permissionless, so any unprivileged caller triggers the saturating conversion once the fee crosses the threshold. Likelihood is gated by market size, so Medium.

### Recommendation
Replace `mul_div_floor_saturating` in `protocol_fee_shares` with `mul_div_floor` (panicking `MathOverflow`), matching the fail-closed behavior of every other share conversion, or explicitly cap `fee` at a value whose `* RAY` product provably fits `i128` before conversion. Keep the headroom cap as a secondary bound only.

### Proof of Concept
1. Populate a market with `borrowed` scaled shares and let `borrow_index` accrue toward `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (e.g. large borrow at high APR over chunked year-long accrual windows via `update_indexes`).
2. On the accrual step where `accrued_interest * reserve_factor / BPS > i128::MAX / RAY`, `accrue_chunk` calls `add_protocol_revenue`, which calls `protocol_fee_shares`.
3. `mul_div_floor_saturating(fee, RAY, supply_index)` overflows its `I256`-to-`i128` result and returns `i128::MAX`; `raw.min(headroom)` still yields `~i128::MAX - supplied` revenue shares.
4. `cache.accrue_revenue` adds them to `revenue` and `supplied`. Subsequent `claim_revenue` (or any withdrawal valuing `supplied * supply_index`) operates on the corrupted book — revenue claims absorb pool cash or all valuation overflows, freezing supplier funds.

Relevant code: `common/src/rates/index.rs:94-99` (saturating conversion + headroom cap), `common/src/math/fp_core.rs:180-201` (silent `i128::MAX` clamp), `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:59-66` (revenue mint path).
