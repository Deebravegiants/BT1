### Title
Whale-sized high-decimal market permanently freezes: borrow-index accrual overflows `i128` before `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap engages — (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
Analogous to CVE-2016-9535 — where unusual image dimensions drive arithmetic past representable bounds and abort/overflow — the pool's index accrual multiplies `scaled * index` RAY products in `i128`. For a very large, high-decimals token book at sustained high utilization, the product `scaled_borrowed * borrow_index` overflows `i128` while `borrow_index` is still far below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. Because the multiplication in `scaled_to_original`/`Ray::mul` panics (`GenericError::MathOverflow`) *before* the cap check, the cap is dead code on this path and every operation that accrues first — `supply`, `withdraw`, `borrow`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `update_indexes` — aborts forever. The market is permanently bricked with all supplier funds locked.

### Finding Description
`update_borrow_index` applies `old_index.mul(env, interest_factor)` and only caps *after* the multiplication succeeds (`common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19`). The value ceiling is hit earlier inside `calculate_supplier_rewards`, which computes `borrowed.mul(env, new_borrow_index)` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:80-81`), and inside `scaled_to_original` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`), all of which use panicking `Ray::mul`. `global_sync` runs `accrue_chunk` unconditionally at the head of every mutating entrypoint in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-53`, so once the product exceeds `i128::MAX` the panic fires on every call and `last_timestamp` can never be advanced past the failing chunk — the freeze is permanent, not temporary. An unprivileged attacker sets this up with only `supply` + `borrow` of a whitelisted high-decimals token (e.g., 18 decimals, ~10^27 native units) at ~98% utilization, then just waits: accrual is driven by elapsed wall-clock time, and the harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360`) demonstrates `try_update_indexes_for`, `try_withdraw_raw`, and `try_repay` all reverting with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of all supplier and collateral funds in the affected (hub, token) book: no withdrawal, repayment, or liquidation can ever execute because accrual panics first. This satisfies the "permanent freezing of funds" acceptance criterion and effectively bricks the market.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a large-notional, high-decimals market pushed to high utilization and left to compound; economically expensive but fully reachable by a single unprivileged address via `supply`/`borrow`, with no privileged call, oracle manipulation, or timing race — only the passage of time.

### Recommendation
Apply the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap *before* (or via) saturating arithmetic: use `mul_div_floor_saturating` for `borrowed * borrow_index` products in `calculate_supplier_rewards` and `scaled_to_original`, or short-circuit `update_borrow_index` to return the cap when `old_index` is already near the saturation boundary, and validate at listing time that `max_cap_for_decimals(d) * MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cannot exceed `i128::MAX`.

### Proof of Concept
See the repository's own harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360`: it supplies `BILLION * 10^18` of an 18-decimal token, borrows ~98% of it, advances time in yearly chunks until `try_update_indexes_for` fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and then asserts that `withdraw` and `repay` also revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while the stored `borrow_index` remains below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, proving the cap never engages.
