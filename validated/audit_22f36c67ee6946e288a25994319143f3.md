### Title
Bridge delivery fee factor can be inflated by unrelated (non-bridge) traffic to the sibling BridgeHub, since congestion is measured at the shared XCMP-channel level, not at the bridge-lane level - (File: bridges/modules/xcm-bridge-hub-router/src/lib.rs)

### Summary
`Pallet::on_message_sent_to_bridge` and `on_initialize` gate the exponential fee-factor increase/decrease purely on `T::LocalXcmChannelManager::is_congested(&SiblingBridgeHubLocation)`. On the deployed asset-hub configs, this resolves to `InAndOutXcmpChannelStatusProvider`/`OutXcmpChannelStatusProvider`, which reports congestion based on the generic HRMP/XCMP outbound (and inbound) channel state to the *whole* sibling BridgeHub parachain (suspended state or `queued_pages > 4`), not on actual bridge-lane/bridge-message throughput. Any XCM traffic to that same parachain destination — not just bridged messages — can push `queued_pages` past the threshold and flip `is_congested` to `true`, letting an attacker who never uses the bridge itself trigger fee-factor increases for real bridge users and slow their recovery.

### Finding Description
`on_message_sent_to_bridge` (`bridges/modules/xcm-bridge-hub-router/src/lib.rs:205-239`) increases `Bridge::delivery_fee_factor` via `FeeTracker::do_increase_fee_factor` whenever `T::LocalXcmChannelManager::is_congested(&T::SiblingBridgeHubLocation::get())` is true, and `on_initialize` (`lib.rs:126-157`) refuses to decrease the factor under the same condition.

In the real runtimes (`asset-hub-rococo`/`asset-hub-westend`, `lib.rs:1032-1033` / `1355-1356`), `LocalXcmChannelManager` is set to `cumulus_pallet_xcmp_queue::bridging::InAndOutXcmpChannelStatusProvider<Runtime>`. Its `is_congested` (`cumulus/pallets/xcmp-queue/src/bridging.rs:24-96`) treats `with` purely as a `Parachain(para_id)` location — the BridgeHub parachain as a whole — and returns `true` if:
- the inbound channel with that para is suspended, or
- the outbound channel is suspended, or
- `queued_pages > MAX_QUEUED_PAGES_BEFORE_DEACTIVATION (4)`.

`OutboundXcmpStatus`/queued pages for a given destination parachain are shared by **all** XCM traffic routed to that parachain over HRMP, regardless of whether the message is a bridged export or an unrelated message. Any pallet/extrinsic that queues XCM to the BridgeHub parachain (e.g. permissionless `pallet_xcm::send` to an arbitrary destination, subject only to normal transaction fees/weight, not to the router's exponential fee) contributes to `queued_pages` for that channel. Because congestion is evaluated per-destination-parachain rather than per-bridge-lane, an attacker can inflate `queued_pages` with cheap, non-bridge messages and cause `is_congested` to read `true` — with no relation to actual bridged-message volume.

Once `is_congested()==true`:
- Every subsequent bridge user's `on_message_sent_to_bridge` call multiplies `delivery_fee_factor` by `EXPONENTIAL_FEE_BASE` (1.05) plus a message-size term (`do_increase_fee_factor`, `polkadot/runtime/parachains/src/lib.rs:80-85`), even though these payers did not cause the congestion.
- `on_initialize`'s divide-by-1.05-per-block recovery is blocked entirely while `is_congested()` remains true, so the attacker only needs to maintain queued_pages > 4 intermittently to both ratchet the fee up (on real bridge sends) and freeze its decay.
- The attacker's own queuing cost is ordinary transaction fees for non-bridge messages, decoupled from the exponential/ByteFee-scaled bridge delivery fee that legitimate bridge users pay via `ExporterFor::exporter_for`.

This is a known, previously-flagged trade-off in the codebase: `prdoc/stable2412/pr_6781.prdoc` documents that "the congestion mechanism based on monitoring XCMP queues... could cause issues, such as suspending the entire XCMP queue instead of isolating the affected bridge," and reverted the analogous bridge-hub-side mechanism (`pallet_xcm_bridge_hub::LocalXcmChannelManager`, i.e. `CongestionManager`) back to explicit `report_bridge_status` signaling from the bridge-hub. However, the asset-hub-side `pallet_xcm_bridge_hub_router` configuration in this codebase still uses `InAndOutXcmpChannelStatusProvider`, i.e., the same generic-queue-based congestion signal that was identified as problematic, without an equivalent bridge-specific isolation.

### Impact Explanation
Any signed account able to queue XCM messages to the sibling BridgeHub parachain (a normal, permissionless capability via `pallet_xcm::send` or other XCM-emitting extrinsics) can indirectly and cheaply flip `is_congested` for that parachain destination. This lets the attacker force `delivery_fee_factor` up for all bridge users (via `on_message_sent_to_bridge`) and stall its decay (via `on_initialize`), imposing an economic griefing cost on legitimate bridge senders who pay `fee_factor * (base_fee + byte_fee*size)` per `ExporterFor::exporter_for` (`lib.rs:302-332`), without the attacker needing to actually use the bridge or incur the exponential fee itself. This matches the scoped impact: griefing bridge users with inflated fees at low cost.

### Likelihood Explanation
Feasibility requires the attacker to be able to enqueue enough XCM traffic toward the BridgeHub parachain to keep `queued_pages > 4` (or force channel suspension), faster than the channel can be drained. This is more difficult than trivially "sending one cheap message," since page-draining happens each relay block and pushing pages past the threshold requires sustained throughput; it is bounded by weight/fee costs of the extrinsics used to emit XCM and by any barriers/filters on `pallet_xcm::send` in the specific runtime. The exact throughput needed and whether current relay-chain HRMP channel capacities make this economically cheap versus the gains from griefing was not directly measurable from the indexed code (channel capacity limits, `SendXcmOrigin`/filter configuration values were not fully retrievable in this pass). The design flaw itself (coupling generic queue congestion with bridge-specific fee accounting) is confirmed and is acknowledged in the codebase's own prdoc discussing a very similar migration for the bridge-hub side.

### Recommendation
Decouple the bridge fee-factor congestion signal from generic per-destination-parachain XCMP queue state. Either:
1. Complete the same migration used for `pallet_xcm_bridge_hub::LocalXcmChannelManager` (per `pr_6781.prdoc`) for the asset-hub-side router configuration — i.e., have BridgeHub explicitly report actual bridge/lane congestion via `report_bridge_status` (already supported by `Pallet::report_bridge_status`), and stop relying solely on `InAndOutXcmpChannelStatusProvider`'s generic HRMP-queue heuristic, or
2. Track queued bytes/pages specifically attributable to bridge-exported messages (a bridge-specific sub-queue or lane-level counter) rather than the parachain-wide `OutboundXcmpStatus`.

### Proof of Concept
Extend `bridges/modules/xcm-bridge-hub-router/src/lib.rs` tests (mirroring `fee_factor_is_decreased_from_on_initialize_when_xcm_channel_is_uncongested`) with a mock `LocalXcmChannelManager` that becomes congested purely due to unrelated queued messages (simulate `TestLocalXcmChannelManager::make_congested` being toggled by a call unrelated to `on_message_sent_to_bridge`, e.g., a helper representing "non-bridge XCM enqueued to same destination"):
1. Set up `uncongested_bridge(MIN_FEE_FACTOR)`.
2. Simulate non-bridge congestion: call `TestLocalXcmChannelManager::make_congested(&SiblingBridgeHubLocation::get())` without any bridge message being sent.
3. Call `send_xcm::<XcmBridgeHubRouter>` once with a small bridge message and assert `DeliveryFeeFactorIncreased` fires and `delivery_fee_factor` increases, even though the bridge/lane itself never carried real load (only asserted congestion is from an unrelated source).
4. Call `on_initialize` repeatedly while congestion persists and assert the fee factor never decreases.
5. Assertion of the bug: fee factor increase/no-decrease is driven entirely by `is_congested()==true` regardless of whether any bridge-lane traffic occurred, demonstrating the fee factor is not bound to actual bridge-path usage. A companion `xcm-emulator` test on asset-hub-rococo/westend could enqueue several unrelated `pallet_xcm::send` messages to the sibling BridgeHub para to push `queued_pages > 4`, then assert `pallet_xcm_bridge_hub_router::Bridge::get().delivery_fee_factor` increases for an actual bridged message sent afterward.
