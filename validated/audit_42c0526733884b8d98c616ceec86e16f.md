### Title
Borrower can make its own account unliquidatable by draining an Aquarius LP pool below `min_pool_value_wad`, forcing a fail-closed price error on a dust collateral leg - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs)

### Summary
Analogous to the indy-node DoS (cheap, permissionless exhaustion of a shared resource that blocks the protocol's function), an unprivileged borrower can disable liquidation and bad-debt cleanup of its own account at the cost of a dust deposit plus an ordinary Aquarius withdrawal. Every valuation-dependent controller operation prices **every** collateral leg of the target account through strict (fail-closed) oracle reads. The Aquarius LP price provider returns `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` whenever the pool's total value falls below the configured `lp.min_pool_value_wad`. A borrower who holds even a one-unit supply position of a listed Aquarius LP share token can, as an LP of the underlying pool, withdraw liquidity until the pool value drops below that floor — permanently erroring the price read for that asset and reverting `liquidate`/`clean_bad_debt` for the account.

### Finding Description
- `liquidate` builds a `Context` and calls `build_liquidation_plan`, which values the account's risk via `calculate_account_risk_totals`. That function iterates **all** entries of `account.supply_positions` and calls `cache.cached_price(&hub_asset.asset)` for each — a missing/invalid price panics before any seizure leg can be selected or skipped (strict reads, INV-ORACLE-01 / ADR-0005: one healthy leg is not a fallback; pro-rata seizure requires every leg's price anyway). [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 
- In the Aquarius provider, after computing the LP fair price, the read compares pool value to a configured floor and errors when it is below it: [3](#0-2) 
- `read` also fails closed (`NoLastPrice`) if pool-kind, share binding, decimals, or the reserves/shares calls fail, so even shrinking reserves to zero is not required — crossing `min_pool_value_wad` suffices. [4](#0-3) 
- Adding the shield leg is permissionless: the borrower calls `supply` with a dust amount of a listed Aquarius-LP-priced collateral on its own account. Supply does not require a healthy final risk gate involving unrelated legs, and the threat model itself confirms this exact shield: "an indebted borrower can add a dust leg of any listed collateral and choose which feed outage shields the account. For an Aquarius LP leg, liquidity providers can cause that outage by withdrawing pool value below `min_pool_value_wad`. The same leg blocks bad-debt cleanup and force-socialization." [5](#0-4) 
- A test pins the ordering: a failing price read reverts `liquidate` with `NO_LAST_PRICE` **before** the health-factor check, so the account cannot even be probed, let alone liquidated. [6](#0-5) 

### Impact Explanation
The borrower's insolvent account becomes unliquidatable while the LP pool stays below the floor. `clean_bad_debt` and governance's `force_socialize_bad_debt` are also blocked because they require pricing every position ("Missing or invalid required prices prevent cleanup"). [7](#0-6) 
Result: the protocol cannot realize collateral against the debt, cannot socialize it, and accrues bad debt — a path to protocol insolvency, which is an accepted impact class (temporary freezing of the protocol's recovery path / insolvency). Unlike a pure fail-closed oracle outage (excluded as a third-party-honesty issue), the attacker here *causes* the outage with its own unprivileged on-chain action (a normal Aquarius withdrawal of its own liquidity), so this is a reachable single-actor DoS, not an external failure assumption.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requirements: (1) a listed collateral whose price source is an Aquarius LP feed with `min_pool_value_wad > 0` (the field exists on `AquariusLpSource` and is enforced unconditionally at read time), (2) the borrower holds or acquires enough LP share of that pool to push pool value under the floor — cheap for thin pools, and the attacker is itself an LP so it recovers most value by re-depositing after the danger passes, (3) a dust supply deposit of the LP token on its own account. No privileged role, no oracle signer compromise, no flash loan needed. Cost is bounded by pool depth; the attack is repeatable and reversible. Given the threat model documents the pattern as a residual risk rather than a mitigated one, likelihood is moderate-to-high wherever shallow Aquarius LP collateral is listed.

### Recommendation
- Do not let a single failing collateral price abort the whole liquidation when that leg's seizure value is negligible: skip or zero-value legs below a dust threshold in `calculate_seized_collateral`/`build_liquidation_plan`, or require a minimum per-leg value so a dust LP leg cannot veto the operation.
- Allow `liquidate`/`clean_bad_debt` to accept a caller-supplied subset of collateral legs to price, sized to cover the repayment, so an unpriceable dust leg is excluded from the plan instead of aborting it.
- For cleanup paths, permit `clean_bad_debt`/`force_socialize_bad_debt` to proceed with a bound on the unpriceable leg's value (e.g., treat as zero for collateral, since cleanup forfeits it anyway).
- At listing time, require `min_pool_value_wad` to be a small fraction of realistic pool TVL so an ordinary LP cannot cross it alone; governance should also cap LTV for LP-token collateral.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance has listed Aquarius LP share token `LP-AB` as collateral, priced by `AquariusLpSource { pool: P, min_pool_value_wad: F, .. }`.
2. Attacker (unprivileged): is an LP of pool `P`. It supplies $50k of normal collateral, borrows $40k, then supplies 1 unit of `LP-AB` on the same account via `controller::supply` — a dust collateral leg.
3. Attacker withdraws its liquidity from `P` so `pool_value_wad < F`.
4. Collateral price drops; HF < 1. Any liquidator calling `liquidate(liquidator, account_id, debt_payments, seize_mode)` reaches `calculate_account_risk_totals`, which iterates `supply_positions`, hits the `LP-AB` leg, calls the aggregator, gets `Err(InsufficientAquariusLiquidity)` from `aquarius::read` (aquarius.rs:120-122), and the strict read panics — whole transaction reverts, same as `NO_LAST_PRICE` in `liquidation_coverage.rs:81-84`.
5. `clean_bad_debt` and scheduled `force_socialize_bad_debt` revert identically (both price all positions). The account remains unliquidatable until the attacker re-deposits, letting debt accrue into bad debt the protocol must socialize.

Uncertainty: I verified the fail-closed ordering and the `min_pool_value_wad` gate in production code and the documented threat-model acknowledgment; I did not read `load_markets`/`cached_price` internals line-by-line, but the test at `liquidation_coverage.rs:67-85` empirically pins that a price failure aborts `liquidate` before eligibility checks, which is the load-bearing behavior.
