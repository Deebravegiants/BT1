### Title
Bad-debt floor leaves stale supply shares redeemable against future deposits - ([File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs](contracts/pool/src/interest.rs))

### Summary
The supply-index floor used after a full bad-debt write-down preserves a residual value for existing scaled supply shares instead of eliminating them. Because `clean_bad_debt` is permissionless and only removes the insolvent account, unrelated suppliers retain controller positions that can later withdraw this residual claim from newly deposited liquidity. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes the remaining supplier value after absorbing bad debt and multiplies the current `supply_index` by that factor. If the bad debt consumes all supplier value, `new_supply_index` is zero, but the function clamps it upward to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, i.e. `RAY/1000`. [3](#0-2) 

The bad-debt seizure path invokes this function and burns the insolvent account’s debt shares, but it does not burn unrelated suppliers’ shares or reduce `supplied` to zero. [4](#0-3)  Controller bad-debt cleanup removes only the insolvent account after sending all of its deposit and debt positions to `pool_seize_positions_call`. [5](#0-4) 

Withdrawal resolution still unscaled a retained position using the now-floored index. A full withdrawal therefore returns `pos_scaled` and pays its floor-rounded residual amount. [6](#0-5)  The pool then checks only that cash covers the transfer, skips no ordinary-withdrawal gate other than utilization, burns the shares, and debits cash. [7](#0-6) 

This creates stale claims after socialized insolvency: a market whose suppliers were economically wiped out still lets those shares recover `0.1%` of their former value from later deposits.

### Impact Explanation
A wiped-out supplier can wait for a fresh depositor and withdraw the residual floor claim, directly receiving the new depositor’s funds. Once the residual withdrawal drains the fresh deposit, the new supplier’s shares are backed by insufficient pool cash, causing theft of user funds and market insolvency. [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8) 

The impact scales with the wiped supplier’s original scaled position. Although only the floor residual remains, a sufficiently large wiped position can leave a substantial absolute claim that is payable entirely out of subsequent deposits.

### Likelihood Explanation
An unprivileged caller can invoke `clean_bad_debt` once an account’s remaining collateral satisfies the protocol’s socializable-bad-debt condition. Interest accrual can turn an under-collateralized position into socializable bad debt, and liquidation cleanup can also reach the same path. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10) 

A coordinated attacker can keep a wiped supply position on a different account from the account whose debt is cleaned. No privileged call, oracle manipulation, callback reentry, or token semantics are required after the insolvency exists. The prerequisite is a severe enough write-down to drive the computed index to the floor.

### Recommendation
When capped bad debt consumes the entire supplier value, clear the residual supply claims rather than clamping the index upward. Options include:

- Set `supply_index` to zero only after setting `supplied` and `revenue` to zero, and ensure zero-index unscaling cannot revive old shares.
- Introduce an explicit market wipeout flag that blocks withdrawals of pre-wipeout scaled positions.
- Track a write-down epoch/generation so positions created before a full wipeout cannot be unscales under the floored index.

Any fix must also preserve the `revenue <= supplied` invariant and prevent fresh deposits from being redeemable by wiped-out share balances. [12](#0-11) [13](#0-12) 

### Proof of Concept
1. Supplier account A supplies `X` of token `T`, receiving scaled shares `S`.
2. Borrower account B borrows `T` and later becomes socializable bad debt. A keeper calls `clean_bad_debt(B)`.
3. `execute_bad_debt_cleanup` sends B’s borrow position to `pool_seize_positions_call`; `seize::apply` converts it to a bad-debt amount, calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, and burns B’s debt. [14](#0-13) [4](#0-3) 
4. If the bad debt equals or exceeds total supplied value, `remaining` is zero and `new_supply_index` is zero, but line 88 stores `RAY/1000` instead. [3](#0-2) 
5. A’s account and scaled shares remain. The market has no cash backing for them.
6. Fresh depositor C supplies amount `D`, equal to or greater than A’s residual `floor(S * RAY/1000)`.
7. A calls `withdraw` for the full `T` position. `resolve_withdrawal` returns all `S` shares and the residual floor amount. [15](#0-14) 
8. `require_reserves` sees C’s deposited cash, `burn_supply` removes A’s shares, and `debit_cash` pays A. C’s claim now exceeds remaining cash. [16](#0-15)

pub(crate) fn apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache: &mut Cache, bad_debt: Ray) {
    let total_supplied_value = cache.supplied().mul(cache.env(), cache.supply_index());

    if total_supplied_value == Ray::ZERO {
        return;
    }

    let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
    let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);

    let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
    let new_supply_index = cache
        .supply_index()
        .mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);

    cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
}

/// Enforces reserve, utilization, and solvency guards, then debits cash for
