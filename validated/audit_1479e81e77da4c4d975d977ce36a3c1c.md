### Title
Permanent market freeze via RAY-value overflow in accrual before the index cap engages - (File: common/src/rates/scaling.rs / contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
Analogous to CVE-2016-9632 (crafted input → global buffer overflow → process crash/DoS), an unprivileged actor can drive a (hub, token) market into a state where every subsequent call that accrues interest panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` inside `scaled_to_original`. The borrow index grows with debt utilization, but the RAY-domain debt value `borrowed × borrow_index` overflows `i128` well before `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` is reached. Because every verb (withdraw, repay, liquidate, borrow, update_indexes) runs `interest::global_sync` first, the market freezes permanently and all supplied funds in it become unrecoverable.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` (called by `global_sync` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:39-53` and simulated by `simulate_update_indexes_body` in `common/src/rates/simulate.rs:158-175`) computes `borrowed_original = scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` each chunk. `update_borrow_index` caps the index at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (10³⁶), but no cap exists on the product `borrowed × borrow_index`. When scaled `borrowed` shares are large (reachable via `supply`/`borrow` with large amounts on an 18-decimal market after caps are lifted by governance configuration, or via the natural i128 domain of ~1.7e38 RAY), a few years of accrual at steep utilization pushes the product past `i128::MAX` and `mul_div` panics with `MathOverflow`.

The repo's own test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361`) demonstrates the end state: `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay` all revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, i.e., the ceiling never engages to stop accrual.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds: once the RAY-value product overflows, no accrue-first operation on that market can ever succeed again. Suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot liquidate, and `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` paths that touch the market's sync also abort. All tokens custodied in the pool for that market book are bricked.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a whale-scale position (large `borrowed` shares) and sustained high utilization over a long horizon, so likelihood is moderate; but it needs no privilege — `supply`, `borrow`, and `update_indexes` are all permissionless, and the attacker can hold the debt itself on a fully collateralized account, paying only interest while waiting for the overflow cliff. Once crossed, the state is irreversible: `last_timestamp` only advances on a successful `accrue_step`, so no later call can skip the panicking multiplication.

### Recommendation
Clamp or saturate the market debt value instead of panicking: in `accrue_step`/`scaled_to_original`, when `borrowed × borrow_index` would exceed `i128::MAX`, treat the market as saturated — cap `borrow_index` at the largest representable value (or a dedicated debt-value ceiling below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`) and mark the market's debt as fully matured so accrual becomes a no-op while exits remain functional. Alternatively, gate `update_borrow_index` on the representable product so the index cap engages before the value cliff.

### Proof of Concept
Reproduced by `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs::a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap`:
1. On an 18-decimal market with the steep `xlm_curve`, supply `1e9 × 1e18` and borrow ~98% of it as an unprivileged user.
2. Advance ledger time year by year calling the permissionless `update_indexes`.
3. After a finite number of years, `try_update_indexes_for` returns `MathOverflow` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
4. Subsequent `withdraw` (even amount 1) and `repay` revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW` — the market is permanently frozen.
