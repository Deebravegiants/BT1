### Title
Unfunded repay drains pool custody: declared inbound amount refunded without verifying a transfer - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs)

### Summary
`ops::repay::apply` resolves the declared `action.amount` into burned debt shares plus an `overpayment`, then unconditionally calls `cache.transfer_out(payer, overpayment)` — refunding tokens out of the pool's real token custody without checking that `amount` was ever transferred in, and without debiting the cash book. The controller always prefunds the pool via `transfer_amount_measured` before calling repay (`settle_repay`, `apply_liquidation_repayments`), so the pool trusts the declared amount the way a proxy trusts an injected header — a declared-vs-measured desynchronization. A direct, unprivileged `pool.repay` call with a zero-debt position and `amount` equal to the pool's entire balance makes the full amount "overpayment" and pays it out of custody with the books untouched.

### Finding Description
In `contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs`, `accounting()` computes `(burned, overpayment) = cache.resolve_repay(amount, position)` where `amount` is the caller-declared `action.amount`. When the position's outstanding debt is zero, `current_debt_ceil` is zero, `resolve_repay` takes the full-close branch, `net_repay` is zero, and the `RepayRoundsToZeroShares` assert passes on its `net_repay == 0` disjunct. `apply()` then executes `outcome.cache.transfer_out(payer, outcome.overpayment)`, a real token transfer to the attacker. The pinned regression test `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` (`contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3590-3610`) demonstrates exactly this: calling `pool.repay(&payer, &ract(0, custody_before))` with nothing transferred in pays `custody_before` to `payer`, leaves `cash` untouched, and returns `actual_amount == 0`. The same gap exists in `ops::recapitalize::apply` (excess over shortfall is refunded from custody without a cash debit), reachable through the controller's permissionless `recapitalize` path — although controller `recapitalize` does measure its inbound transfer first, so the pool-side trust assumption is only exploitable where the caller controls the declared amount against a pool entrypoint that accepts it directly.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds: the entire token balance of a pool can be transferred to an arbitrary `payer` while the market's cash book is never debited, leaving suppliers/borrowers' positions intact on paper but the physical custody empty — subsequent withdraw/borrow/flash calls fail for lack of funds (permanent freezing / insolvency of the market).

### Likelihood Explanation
A single unprivileged address reaches `pool.repay` directly (the test calls `client().repay(&payer, ...)` with a freshly generated address and no privileged auth). The attack requires only choosing a market and passing `amount` up to the pool's token balance. No price manipulation, flash loan, or privileged role is needed. Caveat: I could not fully confirm whether the deployed pool additionally gates `repay` on a registered controller address; the harness test's direct call with an arbitrary `payer` indicates no such gate, but if an external caller-check exists this vector narrows to the declared-amount trust boundary between controller prefunding and pool settlement.

### Recommendation
Verify inbound settlement inside the pool leg: snapshot the pool's token balance before accounting, or have `repay`/`recapitalize` compute the refundable excess from the measured pool balance delta rather than the declared `action.amount`. Alternatively gate `repay`, `recapitalize`, and other settlement legs to the configured controller address so the declared-amount trust contract is enforced at the boundary.

### Proof of Concept
From `contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3590`:
```rust
// Nothing transferred in, no debt to retire: the whole amount is "excess".
let credited = t
    .client()
    .repay(&payer, &t.ract(0, custody_before))
    .get_unchecked(0)
    .actual_amount;
assert_eq!(credited, 0);
assert_unfunded_refund_drained_custody(&t, &payer, custody_before, &before);
```
An attacker repeats this with `amount` equal to the pool's full token balance on any market where the referenced position carries no debt, and `transfer_out` delivers the entire custody to `payer`.
