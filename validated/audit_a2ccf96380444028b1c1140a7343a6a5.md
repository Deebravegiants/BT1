### Title
i128 overflow panic in scaled-value accrual permanently freezes every verb on a large, high-utilization market - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs, common/src/rates/scaling.rs)

### Summary
The Fletcher32 report is a numeric-processing overflow that corrupts state and causes denial of service. The analog in XOXNO Lending is an `i128` overflow panic inside the accrual path: once the RAY-scaled value of `borrowed * borrow_index` (or `supplied * supply_index`) exceeds `i128::MAX`, `global_sync` panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow`. Because `global_sync` runs at the head of every state-changing pool/controller verb, the market is frozen permanently — no supply, withdraw, repay, borrow, liquidation, or `clean_bad_debt` can ever execute again for that book.

### Finding Description
Interest accrual is computed over scaled `Ray` share counts multiplied by RAY indexes:

- `common/src/rates/index.rs:80-83` computes `borrowed.mul(old_borrow_index)` and `borrowed.mul(new_borrow_index)` inside `calculate_supplier_rewards`.
- `common/src/rates/index.rs:34` computes `supplied.mul(old_index)` inside `update_supply_index`.
- `common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16` `scaled_to_original` uses the same `Ray::mul` → `mul_div_half_up`, which panics on overflow (`fp_core.rs:108-118`); only the widened `I256` intermediate is safe, the *result* must still fit `i128`.

`accrue_chunk` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:39-53`) runs this for every elapsed-time chunk inside `global_sync` (`interest.rs:20-33`), which every entrypoint calls before touching state. The borrow index is clamped at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (`index.rs:13-19`), but the *product* `scaled_shares * index` overflows first: the harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-361`) demonstrates that a 1-billion-token 18-decimal market at ~98% utilization on the XLM rate curve hits `MathOverflow` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and confirms `withdraw` and `repay` revert with the same panic. The panic is a host trap with no catch — the transaction aborts, and since time only moves forward, every subsequent call hits the identical overflow. There is no unwind path: `clean_bad_debt`, `recapitalize`, and liquidation all accrue first.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds. Once the cliff is crossed, all supplier principal and accrued yield in that market, and all collateral and positions routed through the frozen book's accrual, are locked forever — liquidation cannot rescue undercollateralized lenders and `repay` cannot unlock borrower collateral. The stored index stays below its cap, so no governance parameter change retroactively avoids the multiply; the overflow lives in `scaled * index` evaluated at call time.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by unprivileged addresses but requires a whale-scale position: the attacker must supply and borrow enough that `scaled_debt * index` reaches ~1.7e38. On an 18-decimal token that is ~170 billion whole tokens of debt value — feasible only for low-priced/high-supply assets, and utilization must stay high while the index compounds (~170× at the steep XLM curve segment over several years). No privilege, leaked key, or parameter change is needed; a single funded actor calling `supply`, `borrow`, and later `update_indexes` (permissionless) triggers it, and ordinary users trigger it accidentally. Market caps raise the bar but `lift_caps`-equivalent configurations and low-unit-price tokens make the cliff real. High-severity impact, moderate likelihood → High.

### Recommendation
- Compute `total_debt`/`total_supplied_value` through `I256` end-to-end and saturate or clamp the stored *scaled share counts* (or the effective index) instead of panicking, so accrual degrades gracefully at the value ceiling.
- Alternatively enforce a hard domain bound at borrow/supply time: reject positions where `scaled * MAX_*_INDEX_RAY` could exceed `i128::MAX`, i.e., cap scaled shares at `i128::MAX / MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` in `calculate_scaled_supply`/`calculate_scaled_borrow`, keeping the panic unreachable.
- Emit a separate `accrue` entrypoint that can still run at the boundary so the market is not bricked silently at the cliff.

### Proof of Concept
Mirrors the existing harness test (`large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-361`), all calls unprivileged:

1. `supply(BOB, BIG18, 1e9 * 10^18)` after caps are lifted to `max_cap_for_decimals(18)`.
2. `supply(ALICE, COL, large collateral)`; `borrow(ALICE, BIG18, 0.98 * principal)` → ~98% utilization on the XLM curve (max rate 1.75 RAY).
3. Advance ledger time; call the permissionless `update_indexes(BIG18)` once per year. Within a few decades the index is ~170× and `borrowed * borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
4. `update_indexes` aborts with `MathOverflow`. Every subsequent `withdraw`, `repay`, `borrow`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `flash_loan`, `multiply`, `swap_*`, `claim_revenue`, and `recapitalize` on the market aborts identically — permanent freeze, demonstrated by `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` both returning `MATH_OVERFLOW` in the test.

Uncertainty note: `accrue_step` in `common/src/rates/simulate.rs` was not fully read in this pass; the panic site is inferred from `calculate_supplier_rewards`/`update_supply_index` multiplies plus the test's assertion that the failure is `MATH_OVERFLOW` inside `scaled_to_original`-equivalent math.
