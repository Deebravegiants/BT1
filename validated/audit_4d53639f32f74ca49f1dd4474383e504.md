### Title
Accrual `scaled_to_original` i128 overflow permanently freezes a market (no repay/withdraw/liquidate) - (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary
Every mutating pool operation runs `global_sync` first, which calls `accrue_step` per elapsed chunk. `accrue_step` computes `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` — a product of RAY-scaled debt shares and the borrow index that must fit in i128. In a large, high-utilization market the compounding borrow index drives this product past `i128::MAX` well before the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY = 10^36` cap engages, so accrual panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow`. Once reached, every verb that accrues (supply, borrow, withdraw, repay, seize/liquidation, recapitalize) reverts forever: the market is permanently bricked with all supplier and collateral funds inside.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` at `common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-61` calls `scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)` and `scaled_to_original(env, supplied, supply_index)`. `global_sync` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33` invokes `accrue_chunk` → `accrue_step` for every elapsed chunk, and every pool op accrues first. `update_borrow_index` clamps the index only *after* the multiplication, so nothing prevents the RAY-value product `shares × index` from overflowing i128 — the docs acknowledge this at `docs/reference/invariants.md` (INV-IDX-01: "Debt-value overflow can still revert accrual before that ceiling is reached").

The repository's own test proves the freeze end-to-end: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-356` supplies 10^27 raw units of an 18-decimal token, borrows at 98% utilization on the XLM rate curve, then advances time until `update_indexes` returns `MATH_OVERFLOW` with `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. Afterwards `withdraw` and `repay` both revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW` — including a 1-unit withdrawal and a 1-unit repayment.

Scaled shares are `amount × 10^(27−decimals)`, so the product `shares × index` scales as `value × 10^(27−decimals) × index`. For high-decimal tokens (18 decimals) or simply very large balances, the index needs only a few hundred × of growth (reachable in a handful of year-long chunks at the steep end of the curve, max 200% APR) before the product exceeds i128.

This is an exact analog of the CVE bug class (availability DoS via crash/hang): a state reached through normal unprivileged operations makes all subsequent operations on that market abort.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds, per the acceptance criteria. Once the overflow threshold is crossed:
- Suppliers cannot withdraw any amount (withdraw accrues first → panic).
- Borrowers cannot repay, and liquidations cannot execute (seize accrues first → panic), so bad debt cannot be cleaned and collateral is trapped.
- `recapitalize` and `clean_bad_debt` also accrue, so no recovery path exists short of a contract upgrade.

The trapped funds are all supplier deposits plus all collateral backing borrows in that market — potentially the entire market TVL.

### Likelihood Explanation
Triggerable entirely by unprivileged addresses (`supply`, `borrow`, `update_indexes` are all in-scope entrypoints), but requires:
- A large market: scaled share supply on the order of `~10^35` RAY-units — e.g., ~10^9 whole tokens of an 18-decimal asset, or proportionally more for lower-decimal assets. Whale-scale capital or organic growth is needed; it cannot be conjured cheaply.
- Sustained near-max utilization (the test uses 98%) for enough year-chunks for the index to multiply by the needed factor (single-digit years at the curve's steep segment; tens of years at moderate rates).
- Governance must have set caps/utilization limits high enough to allow the large position (`lift_caps` in the test), which is plausible for mature markets.

Time-locked and capital-heavy, hence Medium rather than High — matching the source CVE's Medium rating.

### Recommendation
Make accrual saturate instead of panicking when the RAY-value product overflows:
- In `accrue_step` (`common/src/rates/simulate.rs`), clamp `borrow_index` to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` *before* computing `scaled_to_original`, or use `I256`/`mul_div` widening for the utilization numerator so the check is against the capped index rather than an overflowing intermediate.
- Alternatively, cap `borrowed`/`supplied` scaled-share growth inside `scaled_to_original` (return a saturating utilization of RAY::ONE when the product overflows), since utilization is already capped at 1 RAY in `calculate_borrow_rate` — an overflowing numerator implies 100%+ utilization anyway, so saturating is economically equivalent while keeping the market operable.
- Add a regression test mirroring `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` asserting that `update_indexes`, `repay`, and `withdraw` succeed after the index cap engages.

### Proof of Concept
Concretely demonstrated by `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360`:
1. Create market `BIG18` (18 decimals, XLM rate curve) and collateral market `COL`; lift supply/borrow caps.
2. `BOB.supply(BIG18, 10^9 × 10^18)`; `ALICE.supply(COL, ...)` and `ALICE.borrow(BIG18, 98% of principal)` — all unprivileged entrypoints.
3. `controller.update_indexes` once per simulated year; after the index grows ~170× (still below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`), `update_indexes` reverts with `MATH_OVERFLOW` from `scaled_to_original` inside `accrue_step`.
4. From then on: `pool.withdraw(BOB, BIG18, 1)` → `MATH_OVERFLOW`; `pool.repay(ALICE, BIG18, 1)` → `MATH_OVERFLOW`. Liquidation and `recapitalize` revert identically since all paths call `global_sync` first. The market — and all funds in it — is frozen permanently.
