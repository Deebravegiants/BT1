### Title
Unfunded repay leg refunds a purely declared amount out of live pool custody - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs)

### Summary
The pool's `repay` op treats the caller-declared `action.amount` as authoritative and refunds any amount exceeding the position's debt back to `payer` via `Cache::transfer_out`, without verifying that the declared amount was ever transferred in. On a market with zero outstanding debt the entire declared amount becomes "overpayment", letting any caller drain the market's real token custody up to its balance — the direct analog of ComfyUI's unvalidated `annotated` filename: an attacker-controlled input that is supposed to denote a prefunded quantity is joined straight to a payout with no containment check.

### Finding Description
`ops::repay::apply` in `contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs:25-34` resolves the repay into burned debt shares plus `overpayment` and then unconditionally pays `transfer_out(payer, outcome.overpayment)`. The doc comment states the design assumption — "The controller transfers the repay amount into the pool before this call" — but nothing in `accounting` measures the inbound balance delta; `resolve_repay` (`repay.rs:44`) compares `action.amount` against the book's `current_debt_ceil`, so for a market with no debt the full-close branch marks the whole declared `amount` as `overpayment`, `net_repay` is 0, and the `RepayRoundsToZeroShares` assert passes on its `net_repay == 0` disjunct (`repay.rs:48-52`). The refund neither debits `cash` nor passes `require_reserves`, so it is paid straight out of shared token custody.

The in-repo test `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` (`contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3589-3611`) pins exactly this: with a fresh `payer`, nothing transferred in, and zero debt, `pool.repay(&payer, [action(_, custody_before)])` succeeds and `assert_unfunded_refund_drained_custody` confirms the payout came out of real custody while the book was untouched. The sibling test `test_unfunded_recapitalize_is_bounded_by_custody_not_by_the_declared_amount` (`flows.rs:3488-3511`) shows the same unfunded-refund shape for `recapitalize`, bounded only by live balance — i.e., a caller can claim up to the entire token balance the pool holds, which also sweeps donations and other hubs' cash for a shared token.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency: on any market whose `borrowed` book is zero (or whose declared amount exceeds the position debt), a caller can name `amount = pool_balance(token)` in a single `repay` leg and receive the whole overpayment refund out of custody — draining suppliers' principal for that token, including cash booked to other hubs that share the physical balance (per the hub/spoke shared-custody model). The pool's liquidity and solvency guards do not fire because the refund path never debits `cash`; the only bound is the SAC balance itself.

### Likelihood Explanation
A single unprivileged address supplies only a `payer` address and a declared `amount`; no collateral, debt position, oracle state, or timing is needed. The repo's own flows tests invoke `repay`/`recapitalize` directly on the pool with a generated `payer`, demonstrating the entrypoint is externally callable. Exploitation requires only choosing a market with no outstanding debt (any newly created or fully repaid market) — a common, persistent state.

### Recommendation
Measure the inbound transfer instead of trusting `action.amount`: snapshot the pool's token balance before resolving the leg (or require the caller to pass the measured received amount) and cap `overpayment`/`net_repay` at that measured receipt, as `Cache::transfer_out` refunds and controller paths already do via `transfer_amount_measured`/`balance_delta_since`. Equivalently, assert `amount <= measured_inbound` at the top of `ops::repay::apply` and `ops::recapitalize::apply`, and/or authenticate the repaying flow so a bare declared amount can never exceed real receipts.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy pool; create market `key = HubAssetKey{hub_id:0, asset:T}`; supply funds so `token::Client(T).balance(pool) = C` and `borrowed = 0`.
2. Attacker `E` (fresh address, zero balance) calls `pool.repay(E, [PoolRepayEntry{action: PoolAction{hub_asset: key, position: _, amount: C}}])` without transferring any tokens.
3. `resolve_repay` sees `current_debt_ceil = 0`, takes the full-close branch: `burned = 0`, `overpayment = C`, `net_repay = 0`; the `net_repay == 0` disjunct bypasses `RepayRoundsToZeroShares`.
4. `cache.transfer_out(E, C)` sends the entire custody to `E`; books are unchanged, so remaining suppliers' withdraws later revert inside the SAC with balance error 10 — supplier funds are permanently gone.

This is exactly what `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` (`contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3589-3611`) already executes, minus the attacker framing.

Caveat: the tests exercise the pool entrypoint directly; if a deployment-level guard restricts `pool.repay` to the controller, reachability narrows — but nothing in `contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs` or the test harness shows such a guard, and `recapitalize` exhibits the same unfunded-refund shape on a path the rules list as unprivileged-reachable.
