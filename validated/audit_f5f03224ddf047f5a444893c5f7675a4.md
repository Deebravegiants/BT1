### Title
RAY-scaled debt multiplication overflows during accrual and freezes all market operations - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
**Medium severity.** A sufficiently large market can reach a borrow index at which `borrowed * borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX`, even though that index remains below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1)  The resulting `MathOverflow` panic occurs inside index accrual before the index cap can protect the market. [3](#0-2) 

### Finding Description
`global_sync` invokes `accrue_step` with the market’s total scaled debt, total scaled supply, and current indexes. [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4)  Accrual converts RAY-scaled balances back to RAY asset values through multiplication by the live indexes. [6](#0-5)  `calculate_supplier_rewards` separately calculates both `borrowed * old_borrow_index` and `borrowed * new_borrow_index`. [7](#0-6) 

The configured borrow-index ceiling is `1e36`, but the maximum representable RAY asset value is `i128::MAX`, approximately `1.7e38`. [2](#0-1)  Therefore, a normalized scaled-debt balance near `1e36` overflows once the index exceeds roughly `170x`, far below the configured index ceiling. [8](#0-7) 

The repository’s directed test demonstrates that the value ceiling is reached before `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, after which `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay` all revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW`. [9](#0-8)  Supply, borrow, withdrawal, repayment, liquidation, and strategy operations all route through market loading/accrual paths before their state mutations. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10) [12](#0-11) [13](#0-12) 

### Impact Explanation
Once the threshold is crossed, suppliers cannot withdraw and borrowers cannot repay through the public `Controller::withdraw` and `Controller::repay` entrypoints because both execute the overflowing accrual first. [14](#0-13) [15](#0-14)  The same condition prevents `Controller::update_indexes` from committing a new timestamp, so subsequent calls encounter the same overflowing state rather than recovering. [16](#0-15) [17](#0-16)  Absent a contract upgrade or administrative rescue path, the affected market’s supplied funds are permanently frozen and its debt cannot be settled.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation requires an extremely large normalized market and sustained high utilization so that interest compounds the index beyond the representable-value threshold. [18](#0-17)  An unprivileged user can establish the required books through `Controller::supply` and `Controller::borrow` when the deployed market’s configured caps and collateralization permit them. [19](#0-18)  The capital requirement makes exploitation impractical in ordinary markets, but the failure is deterministic once such a market reaches the relevant index and cannot be unwound by user transactions. [20](#0-19) 

### Recommendation
Enforce a market-size-aware arithmetic ceiling below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, such as rejecting growth when `borrowed * candidate_index` or `supplied * candidate_index` would exceed the maximum representable RAY value. [21](#0-20) [1](#0-0)  Alternatively, lower the global index cap, cap normalized total supply/debt to a value that remains representable at the maximum index, and add regression coverage proving that withdrawal, repayment, liquidation, and bad-debt settlement remain executable at the boundary. [2](#0-1) [22](#0-21) 

### Proof of Concept
1. On an 18-decimal market, call `Controller::supply(attacker, 0, spoke_id, [(BIG18, 1_000_000_000 * 10^18)])` to create approximately `1e36` normalized RAY supply shares. [23](#0-22) [24](#0-23) 
2. Supply enough independent collateral and call `Controller::borrow(attacker, account_id, [(BIG18, 98_000_000 * 10^18)], None)` to place the market at approximately 98% utilization. [25](#0-24) [26](#0-25) 
3. Repeatedly call `Controller::update_indexes(attacker, [BIG18])` as ledger time advances. [16](#0-15) 
4. When the borrow index exceeds approximately `170x`, the debt-value multiplication overflows while it is still below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 
5. Subsequent `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay` calls revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, leaving supplied funds inaccessible and the debt unsettleable. [27](#0-26)

        .with_market(col())
