### Title
Borrower shields an underwater account from liquidation and bad-debt cleanup by planting an Aquarius LP collateral leg, then draining that pool below `min_pool_value_wad` - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs)

### Summary
XOXNO Lending prices every collateral leg with strict (valid) prices when computing health factor for `liquidate` and collateral value for `clean_bad_debt`. A borrower can supply a dust amount of an Aquarius LP token as a collateral leg — `supply` does not require a price for the supplied asset — and then, as an LP of that Aquarius pool, withdraw their own liquidity so the pool's computed value falls below the oracle's configured `min_pool_value_wad`. Every subsequent price resolution for that collateral returns `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity`, so the account can never be liquidated or cleaned up while the feed stays below the floor.

### Finding Description
- `AquariusLpSource` carries a `min_pool_value_wad` floor: "A pool whose computed value falls below `min_pool_value_wad` is treated as unpriceable" [1](#0-0) 
- At read time the provider computes `pool_value_wad = price_wad * total_shares / share_unit` and returns `OracleError::InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` when it is below the floor — a hard, repeatable failure rather than a degraded price [2](#0-1) 
- The threat model itself records this reachability: "Supply needs no price, so an indebted borrower can add a dust leg of any listed collateral and choose which feed outage shields the account. For an Aquarius LP leg, liquidity providers can cause that outage by withdrawing pool value below `min_pool_value_wad`. The same leg blocks bad-debt cleanup and force-socialization." [3](#0-2) 
- The analogous stale-feed shield is already demonstrated for regular feeds: planting a dust leg whose feed errors makes `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and even the borrower's own `withdraw` revert (`PRICE_FEED_STALE`) [4](#0-3) 

The Aquarius variant is stronger than the stale-feed variant: the outage is not time-limited by `max_stale_seconds` and does not depend on a third-party signer going silent — the attacker controls it directly and can toggle it (re-add liquidity to unblock themselves, withdraw again to re-shield), while listed mainnet LP collaterals carry concrete floors, e.g. `AQUAUSDC_LP` with `min_pool_value_wad = 200_000 USD` [5](#0-4) 

### Impact Explanation
While the shield is up, `liquidate` (any offer, any `SeizeMode`) and `clean_bad_debt` for the account revert, so the position's interest keeps accruing and neither partial liquidation nor socialization can stop the loss. The debt grows past the value of the remaining collateral with no available remedy — governance `force-socialize` is also blocked per the threat model — producing protocol insolvency borne by the debt market's suppliers, whose claims are written down or left unpaid backing. This is attacker-induced and selectively reversible, not ambient oracle downtime.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires the borrower to hold enough of the pool's LP supply to pull its value under `min_pool_value_wad`. For thin Aquarius pools whose LP tokens are listed as collateral, an attacker can deliberately become the dominant LP before borrowing, making the cost bounded and the trigger atomic. A single unprivileged address performs the whole sequence (`supply` dust LP → `borrow` → wait for HF < 1 → Aquarius `withdraw_liquidity`). Medium likelihood, medium impact → Medium.

### Recommendation
Do not let one unpriceable collateral leg poison liquidation/cleanup for the whole account: value collateral legs with the best available (non-strict) price or skip legs whose price resolution fails while treating them as zero collateral, so HF computation and `clean_bad_debt` proceed on the priced legs. Alternatively, allow `liquidate`/`clean_bad_debt` to drop collateral legs that fail with `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity`, or require supply of LP-collateral markets to verify a strict price at entry so a dead leg can be removed or never planted.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker adds liquidity to Aquarius pool `P` (e.g. the pool behind `AQUAUSDC_LP`), receiving LP shares, then `supply(controller, AQUAUSDC_LP, dust)` — succeeds because supply does not price the leg.
2. Attacker supplies ordinary collateral and `borrow`s up to near the limit.
3. Price moves (or the attacker self-liquidation-defers) until `get_health_factor(attacker_id) < WAD`.
4. Attacker calls the Aquarius pool's `withdraw_liquidity` so `price_wad * total_shares / share_unit < min_pool_value_wad` (feasible when the attacker holds the dominant share).
5. Any liquidator's `liquidate(liquidator, attacker_id, offers, mode)` reverts with `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity`; `clean_bad_debt(attacker_id)` reverts the same way, and per the threat model the governance force-socialize path fails too. The account is permanently shielded until the attacker chooses to restore liquidity; debt accrues into bad debt that suppliers absorb.
