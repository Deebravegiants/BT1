### Title
Missing deadline parameter in `Zap.buy` / `Zap.sell` (and permit variants) - ([File: packages/contracts/src/Zap.sol])

### Summary
`Zap.sol` is alt.fun's user-facing entry point for the bonding-curve trades that an unprivileged trader submits (`Zap.buy`, `Zap.sell`, and their `*WithPermit` variants). Like the `LiquidationRow._performLiquidation()` bug referenced in the external report, these functions implement slippage protection (a `minTokensOut` / `minAssetOut`-style bound that reverts with `SlippageExceeded`) but never check a transaction `deadline`. The only `deadline` field anywhere in `Zap.sol` belongs to `PermitData` [1](#0-0)  and is consumed solely by the EIP-2612 `permit()` call in `_tryPermit` [2](#0-1) ; it bounds the validity of the *signature*, not of the trade itself. `Zap.buy`/`Zap.sell` carry no analogous execution deadline.

### Finding Description
`Zap`'s trade functions rely exclusively on a slippage floor to protect the trader (e.g. `SlippageExceeded` error declared at `packages/contracts/src/Zap.sol:114`, enforced against the curve/AMM output computed via `Router.buy`/`Router.sell` through `_buyOnCurve`/`_sellOnCurve` [3](#0-2) ). There is no `deadline` argument threaded through `buy`/`sell` (or their permit variants) that would bound how long a signed/submitted transaction remains valid before inclusion. A signed trade transaction sitting in the mempool (or held by a builder/validator) can be included at any arbitrary future block. Because the bonding-curve price (`Pair` reserves via `Router._computeBuy`/`_computeSell`) and the external LT `exchangeRate()` both move over time, a trader's minOut bound computed off a stale quote can be satisfied at execution time by a materially worse price than the trader intended, or the trade can be timed by whoever controls inclusion (searcher/validator) to land at the most extractive moment for them (e.g., immediately after a sandwich or after LT rate manipulation), while still nominally clearing the trader's slippage floor.

### Impact Explanation
A trader who signs a `Zap.buy`/`Zap.sell` (or permit-variant) transaction with slippage bounds based on the quote at signing time has no on-chain guarantee that the transaction executes promptly. Absent a deadline, the transaction can be held and replayed by a block builder/validator at a time that maximizes extractable value against the trader (worse effective price within the trader's slippage tolerance, or coordinated with LT `exchangeRate()` drift), resulting in a worse-than-expected execution for the trader — a value-extraction/theft-adjacent impact on trader funds, consistent with the "Medium" severity of the original TOKE-7 finding.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any unprivileged trader's signed `Zap.buy`/`Zap.sell` transaction is directly exposed to mempool visibility and inclusion-timing manipulation by anyone controlling block production/relay — no special privilege is required to exploit the missing deadline, only ordinary MEV/searcher capability, which is common on live chains. This makes the exploit path realistically and continuously reachable, not merely theoretical.

### Recommendation
Add an explicit `deadline` parameter to `Zap.buy`, `Zap.sell`, and their `*WithPermit` variants, and revert (e.g., with a new `Expired`/`DeadlineExceeded` error) if `block.timestamp > deadline`, mirroring the fix already applied to the referenced `LiquidationRow`/`IAsyncSwapper` pattern. This bounds the execution window independently of the slippage check, closing the gap between "acceptable price" and "acceptable time."

### Proof of Concept
1. Trader calls `Zap.buy(tokenAddr, usdcAmount, minTokensOut, referrer)` (or `buyWithPermit`) with `minTokensOut` computed against the current quote from `Router.previewBuy`/`getAmountOut`.
2. The transaction is broadcast but not immediately included (e.g., held by a builder, delayed by low gas price, or intentionally withheld by a searcher who can see it in the mempool).
3. Time passes; the bonding-curve reserves in `Pair` move (other trades) and/or the external LT `exchangeRate()` drifts.
4. The transaction is eventually included at a moment chosen to be maximally unfavorable to the trader while still nominally clearing `minTokensOut` (i.e., `SlippageExceeded` in `Zap.sol` is not tripped because the bound was set loosely relative to the original quote, or the attacker sandwiches around the eventual inclusion).
5. Because `Zap.buy`/`Zap.sell` have no `deadline` check, the trade executes successfully despite being stale, at a price/timing the trader did not intend to accept.
