### Title
Bad-debt write-down leaves floor-valued supply claims that drain later deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
`clean_bad_debt` is permissionless and routes account cleanup into the pool’s supply-index write-down path. [1](#0-0)  When bad debt exceeds the supplied value, the supply index is clamped to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead of reducing every existing supply claim to zero. [2](#0-1)  Those old shares retain a positive floor-valued claim; a later depositor’s cash can then be withdrawn by the old supplier even though the later supplier’s own claim remains unpaid. [3](#0-2) 

### Finding Description
Supply positions are fixed RAY-scaled share balances, while their token claim is derived by unscaling those shares through the mutable supply index. [4](#0-3)  A full withdrawal burns all shares but pays only the floor-valued claim and checks only available cash before debiting it. [5](#0-4) 

The pool’s own test constructs a wipeout where `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` receives twice the supplied scaled value and clamps the index at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`; the old position still resolves to a positive `stranded` claim. [6](#0-5)  A second supplier deposits exactly that claim amount, after which the stranded position withdraws it completely, leaving pool cash below the second supplier’s claim. [7](#0-6) 

### Impact Explanation
This is theft of later suppliers’ funds: once a market is wiped down to the supply-index floor, any positive residual value of pre-existing shares can consume cash deposited after the write-down. [8](#0-7)  The attacker does not need privileged access because account cleanup is permissionless and withdrawal is available to the owner or delegate of the residual supply position. [9](#0-8) [1](#0-0) 

### Likelihood Explanation
An attacker can pre-position a large supply balance in the vulnerable market and separately create a debt account that becomes insolvent through collateral loss or debt accrual. [10](#0-9)  Once that debt qualifies for cleanup, any unprivileged caller can invoke `clean_bad_debt`; afterward, normal user deposits replenish cash that the attacker’s residual claim can withdraw. [1](#0-0)  The economic trigger requires genuine bad debt, but the exploit does not depend on oracle manipulation, leaked authorization, malformed token behavior, or a privileged operation. [1](#0-0) 

### Recommendation
When bad debt exceeds the market’s total supplied value, burn or explicitly invalidate the market’s existing supply shares instead of clamping their common supply index to a positive floor. [11](#0-10)  Alternatively, introduce an insolvency epoch/generation so pre-write-down shares cannot claim cash deposited after the wipeout. [7](#0-6) 

### Proof of Concept
`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs::test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` already demonstrates the sequence exactly: apply bad debt equal to twice the supplied shares, observe the index clamp to the floor, deposit a fresh claim amount, and withdraw the stranded claim until the fresh supplier’s cash is gone. [12](#0-11) 

A reachable sequence is:

```text
1. Attacker supplies X to market M, obtaining scaled supply shares.
2. A separate account borrows M against other collateral.
3. Collateral value falls or debt accrues until cleanup eligibility.
4. Attacker calls Controller::clean_bad_debt(caller, account_id).
5. The pool writes down M but clamps supply_index to SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW.
6. Victim supplies X to M, adding cash.
7. Attacker calls Controller::withdraw for the old M supply leg.
8. The withdrawal pays the floor-valued residual claim from the victim's cash.
```

#[test]
fn test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard() {
    let t = TestSetup::new();
    t.as_contract(|| {
        let scaled_a_raw = 1_000_000 * RAY;
        let mut cache = t.fresh_cache(PoolStateRaw {
            supplied: scaled_a_raw,
            borrowed: 0,
            revenue: 0,
            borrow_index: RAY,
            supply_index: RAY,
            last_timestamp: 0,
            cash: 0,
        });
        let scaled_a = Ray::from(scaled_a_raw);

        apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(&mut cache, Ray::from(2_000_000 * RAY));
        assert_eq!(
            cache.supply_index().raw(),
            SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW,
            "wipeout must clamp supply index UP to the floor, not reset the base"
        );

        let stranded = cache.unscale_supply_floor(scaled_a);
        assert!(stranded > 0, "floor clamp leaves userA a phantom claim");
        assert_eq!(cache.cash(), 0, "empty market: no cash to extract yet");

        let c = stranded;
        let scaled_b = cache.calculate_scaled_supply(c);
        cache.mint_supply(scaled_b);
        cache.credit_cash(c);

        let b_claim = cache.unscale_supply_floor(scaled_b);
        assert_eq!(b_claim, c, "userB's honest claim equals their deposit");

        let (burn, gross) = cache.resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, scaled_a);
        cache.require_reserves(gross);
        cache.burn_supply(burn);
        cache.debit_cash(gross);

        assert!(gross > 0, "stranded position pays out non-zero");
        assert_eq!(
            gross, c,
            "userA drains exactly userB's fresh deposit out of the pool"
        );

        assert!(
            cache.cash() < b_claim,
            "pool cash ({}) can no longer cover userB's claim ({}): honest supplier lost funds",
            cache.cash(),
            b_claim
        );
        assert_eq!(cache.cash(), 0, "userA drained the pool to empty");
    });
