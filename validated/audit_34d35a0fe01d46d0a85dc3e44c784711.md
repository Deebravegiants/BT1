### Title
Third-party collateral top-up keeps an insolvent account above the bad-debt dust gate, blocking permissionless `clean_bad_debt` - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs)

### Summary
The Ree6 bug let one principal configure a target inside another guild's context without that context's consent, bypassing its protections. The analog here is the permissionless third-party `supply` path: any address may push collateral into a foreign account's existing supply leg, and the account owner cannot refuse it. When that foreign account is insolvent and sitting at or below the $5 bad-debt dust threshold, a single donation of just over $5 of an already-held collateral asset pushes `total_collateral` above the dust cap, permanently disabling the permissionless `clean_bad_debt` gate. The bad debt then cannot be socialized by keepers and requires the owner-gated, timelocked `force_socialize_bad_debt` governance path instead.

### Finding Description
`process_supply` admits a third-party top-up whenever every listed hub asset already exists in the target account's `supply_positions` — no owner consent is required [1](#0-0) . This is documented as INV-AUTH-03, but its interaction with the bad-debt gate is not guarded.

Permissionless `clean_bad_debt` admits socialization only when `is_socializable_bad_debt` holds: debt strictly exceeds collateral **and** `total_collateral <= BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD` ($5 WAD) [2](#0-1) . The gate reads the account's aggregate collateral [3](#0-2) , so collateral the owner never asked for counts the same as the owner's own.

An insolvent account in the dust band necessarily holds some collateral (the gate needs `total_collateral` between 0 and $5), so it always has at least one existing supply leg eligible for a foreign top-up. The attacker donates e.g. $5.01 of that asset; `total_collateral` rises above the cap and `clean_bad_debt` reverts with `CannotCleanBadDebt` from then on. The only remaining cleanup path is `process_force_socialize_bad_debt`, which is controller-owner-only and reaches the Sensitive timelock tier via governance [4](#0-3) , [5](#0-4) . There is no counterpart to Ree6's missing check — nothing distinguishes "owner's collateral" from "forced donation" at the gate, and no entrypoint lets the owner shed the unsolicited position without first restoring solvency.

### Impact Explanation
Bad debt that should be socialized by any keeper instead sits unresolved: it keeps accruing borrow interest while suppliers of the debt market carry an unbooked shortfall, and the position NFT/account persist. Resolution is pushed onto a timelocked governance operation that normally would not be needed at all, so this is a temporary freezing of insolvency resolution (and of supplier liquidity behind the unpaid backing) reachable by a single unprivileged address for a donation on the order of $5. If the collateral asset's price drifts upward, the attacker's one donation keeps the account above the dust cap indefinitely, extending the freeze until governance acts.

### Likelihood Explanation
The preconditions are common: any liquidated-but-insolvent account that lands in the dust band is eligible. The attacker needs only one existing supply leg on the target (guaranteed by the dust-band shape), ~$5 of that token, and one `supply` call. The main natural counter-pressure is that liquidators may seize the donated collateral on subsequent liquidation rounds, so the attacker may need to repeat the donation after each liquidation — each repetition is still cheap and each donates value to liquidators, which bounds the griefing duration. Against accounts whose collateral legs are `no_seize`-flagged or priced such that no liquidator acts, the block is effectively durable.

### Recommendation
Exclude third-party supply from accounts whose risk totals satisfy the insolvency condition (`total_debt > total_collateral`), or make `is_socializable_bad_debt` tolerate small recent top-ups — e.g., track owner-initiated collateral vs. third-party additions per position and evaluate the dust cap on the owner's own contributions, or cap third-party top-up size so a single donation cannot lift collateral above `BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD`. Alternatively, allow permissionless `clean_bad_debt` to first seize-then-evaluate, ignoring collateral credited within the same ledger window.

### Proof of Concept
1. ALICE supplies $8 USDC and borrows ~$22 of ETH (dust-disabled fixture). `set_price(USDC, $0.05)` makes the account insolvent with `total_collateral ≈ $3 ≤ $5` — the shape used by `test_keeper_clean_bad_debt_decreases_supply_index` [6](#0-5) .
2. Before any keeper calls `clean_bad_debt`, attacker BOB calls `supply(BOB, alice_account_id, spoke, [(USDC hub key, 5.01)])`. `require_third_party_existing_supply` passes because USDC is already an ALICE supply position; Bob's $5.01 is credited to ALICE's account and pulls `total_collateral` to ≈$8.
3. Any subsequent `clean_bad_debt(account_id)` computes `is_socializable_bad_debt` as false and reverts `CannotCleanBadDebt`; the ETH bad debt stays on the book accruing interest.
4. The only remaining resolution is the owner-gated, timelocked `force_socialize_bad_debt` runbook, confirming the permissionless cleanup path was defeated by an unsolicited foreign deposit the victim could not refuse — the same trust-shape violation as the Ree6 cross-guild channel target.
