## Analysis

The reported Pyth issue is a classic "overpaid native ETH not refunded" bug class: a contract accepts `msg.value` intended to cover a fee that may be less than what was sent, but never returns the surplus to the caller, letting ETH accumulate and later be exploited or permanently stranded.

Hyperbridge's `EvmHost` contains an analogous pattern in its dispatch/fee-funding entry points.### Title
Excess native ETH sent to `EvmHost.dispatch()`/`fundRequest()` is not refunded and becomes permanently stuck in the contract - (File: `evm/src/core/EvmHost.sol`)

### Summary
`EvmHost.dispatch(DispatchPost)`, `EvmHost.dispatch(DispatchGet)`, and `EvmHost.fundRequest()` all accept `msg.value` and forward it to `IUniswapV2Router02.swapETHForExactTokens{value: msg.value}(fee, path, address(this), block.timestamp)`. The canonical UniswapV2Router02 implementation only spends the exact ETH-equivalent amount needed to buy `fee` units of the fee token and refunds `msg.value - amounts[0]` back to `msg.sender` of the swap call — which is `EvmHost` itself, not the original transaction sender. `EvmHost` never forwards this refunded surplus back to `_msgSender()`/`post.payer`, so any excess ETH sent above the exact fee-token cost is permanently trapped in the contract.

All three functions call `swapETHForExactTokens{value: msg.value}(exactFeeAmount, path, address(this), block.timestamp)`. Standard UniswapV2Router02 semantics compute `amounts[0]` (the exact ETH needed) via `getAmountsIn`, wrap only that amount into WETH, execute the swap, and then refund `msg.value - amounts[0]` to the caller (`msg.sender` of the router call, i.e. `EvmHost`) via `TransferHelper.safeTransferETH`. Because callers rarely know the exact live Uniswap price at submission time, they will commonly send `msg.value` with a safety margin above the exact required amount (this is explicitly acknowledged elsewhere in the same codebase, e.g. the LZ endpoint adapter's `quote()` function which applies "a generous 2x buffer to absorb ... Excess native is refunded by the uniswap router").

Unlike `IntentGatewayV2`/`ExtrinsicIntents`, which explicitly track `msgValue` and refund unspent native tokens to the caller after any Uniswap swap (see `_sendValue(msg.sender, msgValue)` patterns), `EvmHost.dispatch()` and `fundRequest()` do no such accounting. The refunded ETH received by `EvmHost` from the router is simply absorbed into the contract's balance with no bookkeeping and no forwarding mechanism to the original sender.

### Impact Explanation
Every overpayment (which will occur routinely, since exact swap output amounts cannot be known precisely ahead of time due to price movement between quote and execution) accumulates as native ETH permanently locked in `EvmHost`. There is no user-facing mechanism to reclaim it. This satisfies the "permanent freezing of funds" criterion: legitimate depositors' surplus ETH is irrecoverably lost, and over time this can accumulate to a meaningful amount across the many `dispatch`/`fundRequest` calls that constitute normal Hyperbridge usage from any unprivileged relayer, token bridge app, or intents contract (`HyperFungibleToken.send`, `WrappedHyperFungibleToken.send`, `HyperbridgeLzEndpoint.send`, `IntentGatewayV2`/`ExtrinsicIntents` cross-chain dispatch paths all route into `EvmHost.dispatch`).

### Likelihood Explanation
High likelihood — this triggers on essentially any legitimate call where the caller sends `msg.value` with even a small safety margin above the live-quoted Uniswap price for the fee, which is standard/recommended practice (as seen in the SDK's own `quote()` buffering logic). No malicious actor is required; ordinary usage patterns cause loss.

### Recommendation
After the `swapETHForExactTokens` call in `dispatch(DispatchPost)`, `dispatch(DispatchGet)`, and `fundRequest()`, capture the returned `amounts` array and refund `msg.value - amounts[0]` to `_msgSender()` (or `post.payer`), mirroring the refund pattern already implemented in `IntentGatewayV2.sol` (`_sendValue(msg.sender, msgValue)` after the fee swap).

### Proof of Concept
1. Caller invokes `EvmHost.dispatch(DispatchPost)` with `post.fee = 100` (fee-token units) and sends `msg.value = 1 ether` (a safety margin over the current quoted price, since the exact live Uniswap price is unknown at submission time).
2. `EvmHost` calls `swapETHForExactTokens{value: 1 ether}(100, [WETH, feeToken], address(this), block.timestamp)`.
3. The router computes `amounts[0] = 0.4 ether` (exact ETH needed for 100 fee tokens), performs the swap, and refunds `0.6 ether` to `msg.sender` — i.e., to `EvmHost`.
4. `EvmHost.dispatch()` completes without any refund logic; the `0.6 ether` remains in `EvmHost`'s balance.
5. The original caller has no way to reclaim the `0.6 ether`; it is permanently stuck in the contract, repeating on every over-funded dispatch/fundRequest call.

        if (msg.value > 0) {
            address[] memory path = new address[](2);

        }
