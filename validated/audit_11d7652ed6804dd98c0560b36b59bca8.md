### Title
Bad-debt supply-index write-down silently clamps at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, leaving unbacked supply claims that drain fresh deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
The permissionless `clean_bad_debt` path socializes residual bad debt by writing the supply index down via `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`. Analogous to CVE-2022-23037 — where a PV frontend assumes revoking a grant always succeeds while the backend retains access — the pool assumes the write-down fully removes supplier claims. The index is clamped at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`), so a write-down larger than 99.9% of supplied value silently under-executes: every supplier's scaled shares keep a floor-valued claim that exceeds actual backing. Those phantom claims are then payable out of future cash (new supplies, `recapitalize`), i.e., access to the page was never actually revoked.

### Finding Description
`clean_bad_debt(caller, account_id)` is callable by any authenticated address once an account's ceil debt exceeds its half-up collateral and collateral is at or below the ~$5 dust threshold (INV-LIQ-04). It reclassifies remaining collateral shares as revenue and writes off all remaining debt against each debt market's supply index through `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs`, exercised in `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs`).

The write-down lowers `supply_index` so that `supplied × supply_index` shrinks to cover the shortfall. But the function clamps the result at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead of zeroing or proportionally burning shares. The unit test `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-427`) demonstrates the consequence directly: after a write-down that would require an index below the floor, `S_old` scaled shares still unscale to a positive claim; once any fresh cash enters the market, `resolve_withdrawal` + `require_reserves` pay the wiped claim real tokens, leaving the fresh supplier's claim undercollateralized ("pool cash can no longer cover fresh supplier claim").

INV-LIQ-04 acknowledges that "the index floor can leave a shortfall" and `recapitalize` fills it, but the documented residual is described only as a shortfall; the exploitable mechanics — that surviving floor-clamped claims have first claim on any subsequently injected cash — is the same check-then-assume-revocation-succeeds flaw as the Xen grant-table race.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent loss/freezing of user funds. After an extreme bad-debt event, suppliers' stored scaled positions retain claims up to 1000× above true backing. The first suppliers to `withdraw` (or a liquidation/`recapitalize` that injects cash) are paid out of tokens that economically belong to later depositors or to the recapitalizer. Late withdrawers find `require_reserves` failing — their funds are effectively frozen or stolen by earlier withdrawals of claims that should have been destroyed. This is theft of user funds / protocol insolvency, reachable entirely through permissionless entrypoints.

### Likelihood Explanation
- Entrypoints: `controller::clean_bad_debt` (permissionless, not pause-gated), followed by ordinary `withdraw` / `supply` / `recapitalize` — all unprivileged.
- Conditions needed: a market where socialized bad debt exceeds 99.9% of the floor-valued supply. Because `clean_bad_debt` only requires remaining *collateral* ≤ $5 while debt is uncapped, a collateral collapse near zero on an account with large debt (e.g., a rapidly devaluing listed asset, or a long-unliquidated position in a thin market) produces exactly this: a massive debt write-off against a modest supply base.
- The clamp only triggers in catastrophic scenarios (>99.9% write-down), so likelihood is low-to-moderate; impact when triggered is protocol-level insolvency for that market. Rated Medium.

### Recommendation
Do not rely on index flooring to bound bad-debt write-downs. Options:

- Track the residual explicitly: when `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` hits `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, record the unsocialized remainder as a distinct unbacked-claim liability that `recapitalize` must satisfy before new supply mints or withdrawals release cash against floor-valued claims.
- Alternatively, proportionally burn/scale down `supplied` shares (rebase) when the write-down would exceed the floor, so claims are actually revoked rather than assumed revoked.
- At minimum, gate withdrawals in a market whose realized write-down was clamped, until backing is restored — turning the silent under-revocation into an explicit fail-closed state.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual flow (mirroring the existing unit test):

1. Market M has modest supply base `S` at `supply_index = RAY`. An account accumulates large debt `D` in M while its collateral crashes below the $5 dust threshold, so `clean_bad_debt` becomes permissionless-callable.
2. Any caller invokes `controller::clean_bad_debt(account_id)`. The pool executes `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache, D_scaled)`; the required index falls below `RAY/1000`, so it is clamped at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. Suppliers' `scaled_amount` entries are untouched.
3. Each supplier still holds claims worth `floor(scaled × RAY/1000)` — collectively `S × 1e-3` tokens of claim against near-zero real backing.
4. A fresh supplier calls `controller::supply` (or a payer calls `recapitalize`), crediting cash. `require_reserves` sees cash ≥ the stranded claim, so an old supplier's `withdraw` succeeds and is paid from the fresh deposit.
5. Fresh suppliers' claims can no longer be covered — funds are lost/frozen. Verified shape in `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard`: after clamp, `cache.resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, old_scaled)` pays `gross > 0` equal to the fresh deposit, and `cache.cash() < fresh_claim`.

Supporting code:
- `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs` — `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` and the `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` clamp.
- `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-427` — explicit demonstration that the clamp strands a claim that later drains fresh cash.
- `docs/reference/invariants.md` (INV-LIQ-04) — confirms the floor can leave a shortfall and that cleanup applies no post-backing assertion.
- `contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:111-118` — `require_reserves`/`debit_cash` pay stranded claims from current cash without distinguishing backed vs. clamp-residual claims.
- `common/src/rates/scaling.rs:105-121` — `resolve_withdrawal` pays the floor-valued balance for full closes, giving stranded claims their maximum payout.

Uncertainty: the exact severity of the residual depends on market size vs. written-down debt; if the codebase intentionally accepts this as a bounded tail risk (the "recapitalize fills the shortfall" note), it is a design assumption with an exploitable ordering hole — recapitalized cash is paid to the stale claims rather than restoring genuine backing.
