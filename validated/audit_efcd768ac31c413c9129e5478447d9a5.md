### Title
Stale secondary oracle observations inflate dual-source collateral prices - ([File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs](contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs))

### Summary
A dual-source oracle can combine a fresh market price with an old secondary “anchor” price while both observations remain inside their independently configured staleness windows. `blend` averages the two prices without bounding how old a non-market anchor may be relative to the current market observation, so a lagging anchor can skew the accepted price by up to roughly half the configured tolerance band. An unprivileged borrower can wait for this natural feed lag and call `borrow` to obtain more debt than the current market value of the collateral supports.

### Finding Description
`read_source` marks a source stale only when its provider-specific check or the oracle-wide `max_price_stale_seconds` rejects its timestamp. [1](#0-0) 

For two readings, `blend` marks the result stale if either leg exceeds its own bound or if two `Market` legs differ in age by more than `MAX_LEG_AGE_SPREAD_SECONDS`. [2](#0-1) 

When the secondary leg is configured as `Fundamental`, that inter-leg age-spread check does not apply, and the final price is simply the midpoint of the fresh primary and the old anchor. [3](#0-2) 

The tolerance check only compares the two price values, not whether the older observation still represents current market conditions. [4](#0-3) 

A stale-but-accepted anchor therefore passes all three hard gates—staleness, deviation, and sanity—and `force` returns the blended price for valuation. [5](#0-4) 

`Controller::borrow` delegates to `process_borrow`, which pays out the requested debt before the post-pool solvency gate evaluates the account. [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) 

The solvency check compares LTV-weighted collateral and health factor calculated from the accepted oracle price, so the inflated midpoint increases the account’s measured borrowing capacity. [8](#0-7) 

### Impact Explanation
An attacker can supply collateral whose secondary oracle is lagging upward relative to its fresh market price, then borrow against the inflated midpoint valuation.

The existing audit scenario uses a fresh XLM price of `$0.91` and a 23-hour-old `$1.00` RedStone anchor. With a 10% tolerance, the observations remain inside the band, but the midpoint is `$0.955`, inflating recognized collateral by more than 4% relative to the honest fresh-anchor result. [9](#0-8) [10](#0-9) 

That inflation lets the attacker withdraw borrowed assets that should have been rejected as undercollateralized; if the collateral does not recover, the pool is left with bad debt borne by suppliers. [11](#0-10) 

### Likelihood Explanation
The exploit does not require oracle compromise or privileged access: the attacker needs a listed asset whose secondary anchor is allowed to remain valid long enough for the fresh market leg to diverge within the configured tolerance band.

The demonstrated lag is 82,800 seconds against an 86,400-second source limit, meaning the old observation is still accepted by design rather than being rejected as stale. [12](#0-11) [13](#0-12) 

The attack is opportunistic rather than continuously available because it requires provider lag or an outage plus a sufficiently large price move that still remains inside the tolerance band. Since borrowing is permissionless for the account owner, any user can execute it when those market conditions occur.

### Recommendation
Do not blend observations whose usable freshness windows are materially different without an explicit cross-leg age bound.

For two-source prices, reject or downgrade a `Fundamental` leg when its timestamp is older than the primary market leg by a conservative market-data interval, rather than limiting `MAX_LEG_AGE_SPREAD_SECONDS` to pairs where both legs are labeled `Market`.

Alternatively, when the secondary leg is materially older than the primary, use the fresher leg only if policy explicitly permits single-source fallback; otherwise fail closed with `PriceFeedStale`.

A regression test should keep the existing stale-anchor borrow scenario and assert `PriceFeedStale` or `UnsafePriceNotAllowed` instead of allowing the inflated borrow.

### Proof of Concept
The repository already contains an executable audit PoC at `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_borrow_withdraw_liquidate_stale_anchor_blend.rs`.

It configures XLM with a fresh `$0.91` primary observation and an 82,800-second-old `$1.00` secondary anchor, supplies 100,000 XLM, and requests a 70,000 USDC borrow. [14](#0-13) 

The test asserts that collateral value is inflated by more than 4%, that the exploit borrow succeeds, and that the otherwise identical fresh-anchor control fails with `INSUFFICIENT_COLLATERAL`. [15](#0-14)

/// Evaluates `source` into a `Reading`, if it produces one. Combines the
/// source's own component-level staleness with staleness computed against
/// `oracle.max_price_stale_seconds`.
fn read_source(
    session: &mut Session,
    key: &PriceKey,
    oracle: &AssetOracle,
    source: &PriceSource,
    depth: u32,
) -> Result<Option<Reading>, OracleError> {
    let Some((observation, component_stale)) =
        evaluate_source(session, key, source, oracle.asset_decimals, depth)?
    else {
        return Ok(None);
    };
    let timestamp = observation.timestamp;
    let stale = component_stale
        || is_stale(
            session.now_secs(),
            timestamp,
            oracle.max_price_stale_seconds,
        );

        return false;
