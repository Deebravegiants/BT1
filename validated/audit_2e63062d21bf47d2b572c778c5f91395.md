### Title
Permissionless `claim_revenue` can drain all pool cash and temporarily freeze withdrawals - (File: contracts/pool/src/cache/shares.rs)

### Summary
Any authenticated address can invoke `controller::claim_revenue`, which sweeps protocol revenue through the controller to the configured accumulator. The pool caps the claim at `min(cash, floor(revenue_value))`, so when accrued revenue is at least the cash balance, the claim transfers the entire cash reserve and leaves `cash = 0`. Subsequent withdrawals revert in `Cache::require_reserves` even though suppliers retain valid supply positions. This creates a front-runnable, temporary freezing-of-funds condition until cash is replenished by repayments, new supplies, or recapitalization.

### Finding Description
`controller::claim_revenue(caller, assets)` requires only `caller.require_auth`; it does not restrict the caller to governance or a keeper role. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

For each market, the controller calls `pool_claim_revenue_call`, measures its token balance delta, and forwards the positive receipt to the configured accumulator. [3](#0-2) 

In the pool, `burn_claimable_revenue` computes `treasury_actual = floor(revenue * supply_index)` and chooses `amount = min(cash, treasury_actual)`. [4](#0-3)  When `treasury_actual >= cash`, it burns all revenue shares and returns the full cash balance. [5](#0-4) 

The revenue path then debits that amount from cash and transfers it to the pool owner, which is the controller. [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) 

Withdrawals require `cash >= net_transfer` before debiting cash. [8](#0-7)  `require_reserves` panics with `CollateralError::InsufficientLiquidity` when the requested payout exceeds cash. [9](#0-8) 

### Impact Explanation
A supplier's withdrawal can be front-run by an unprivileged `claim_revenue` call that removes the pool's full cash balance. The victim's `withdraw` transaction then reverts with `InsufficientLiquidity`, even for a small withdrawal, because no cash remains.

The freeze is temporary rather than permanent: user supply shares remain recorded, and withdrawals become possible after borrowers repay, new suppliers deposit, or `recapitalize` restores backing. Nevertheless, an attacker can repeatedly monitor revenue accrual and claim newly claimable cash, extending the period during which exits are unavailable. This is strongest classified as Medium severity: temporary freezing of user funds with no direct theft by the caller.

### Likelihood Explanation
The condition requires accrued, claimable revenue to be at least the market's current cash. This is reachable after substantial borrowing followed by interest accrual, or after seizure/booked revenue grows relative to idle cash. The triggering transaction is explicitly permissionless: the caller only needs to sign its own authorization and cannot redirect the proceeds. [10](#0-9) 

No malformed parameter, privileged role, external oracle manipulation, or contract upgrade is required. The attacker simply submits `claim_revenue` before a withdrawal when `get_revenue`-equivalent claimable value is at least current reserves.

### Recommendation
Prevent revenue claims from consuming cash needed for withdrawals by introducing a liquidity floor analogous to the liquidation buffer. For example, change the claimable amount to:

```rust
let reserved = minimum_cash_floor(&cache);
let amount = cache.cash.saturating_sub(reserved).min(treasury_actual);
```

Alternatively, cap each claim to a fraction of available cash or enforce a post-claim liquidity invariant in `claim_revenue`. If full-cash claims are intentionally allowed, consider making the keeper operation rate-limited or documenting that exits can be blocked by revenue sweeps. The accounting fix should be in `contracts/pool/src/cache/shares.rs` or `contracts/pool/src/ops/revenue.rs`, not merely in the controller, because the pool owns the authoritative cash invariant.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice supplies `1_000 USDC` to a hub market.
2. Bob borrows `900 USDC`, leaving pool `cash = 100 USDC`.
3. Time advances until accrued protocol revenue satisfies `floor(revenue * supply_index) >= 100 USDC`.
4. An arbitrary attacker calls:
   ```rust
   controller.claim_revenue(attacker, vec![HubAssetKey { hub_id, asset: usdc }])
   ```
   `burn_claimable_revenue` selects `min(cash, treasury_actual) = 100`, burns the corresponding revenue shares, debits the full `100`, and transfers it through the controller to the accumulator. [11](#0-10) 
5. Alice calls `withdraw` for any positive USDC amount.
6. The pool reaches `require_reserves(net_transfer)` with `cash = 0`, reverts with `InsufficientLiquidity`, and Alice's supply remains locked until new cash enters the market. [12](#0-11) [13](#0-12)

    /// Partial claims burn a pro-rata ceiling of revenue shares. Returns `0` when
    /// nothing is available.
    pub(crate) fn burn_claimable_revenue(&mut self) -> i128 {
        let treasury_actual = self.unscale_supply_floor(self.revenue);
        let amount = self.cash.min(treasury_actual);
        if amount <= 0 {
            return 0;
        }

            self.revenue

            self.revenue
