### Title
Permanent DoS of `finalizeGraduation` via oversized HyperSwap pre-seed causing V2's `INSUFFICIENT_LIQUIDITY_MINTED` revert - ([File: packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol])

### Summary
CVE-2022-0583 is a crash triggered by a crafted/injected input that a dissector cannot handle, causing denial of service. The closest reachable analog in alt.fun is a crafted, attacker-funded HyperSwap V2 pre-seed of the graduating `TOKEN/LT` pair that forces UniswapV2's integer-division `mint()` formula to round the deposit's minted liquidity to zero, causing `finalizeGraduation` to revert with `INSUFFICIENT_LIQUIDITY_MINTED` deterministically and permanently, bricking graduation and freezing the curve-raised LT and the 250M `LP_RESERVE` tokens parked on `Bonding`.

### Finding Description
`Bonding._prepareGraduationLiquidity` caches a fixed, small `(tokensForLP, ltFromPair)` pair at the end of phase 1 [1](#0-0) . `tokensForLP` is capped at `LP_RESERVE = 250_000_000 ether` [2](#0-1) , and `ltFromPair` is bounded by the curve's actual raise, which can be made arbitrarily small in absolute LT units by graduating at a moment when the LT's `exchangeRate()` is high (the USD trigger only requires `realLtRaised × exchangeRate ≥ $9K`) [3](#0-2) .

Between phase 1 (`_enterGraduating`) and phase 2 (`finalizeGraduation`) the HyperSwap pair is permissionlessly creatable and mintable by anyone, per the documented "mint pre-seed" attack surface [4](#0-3) . `_seedRebalancing` defends against a *skewed-ratio* pre-seed by rebalancing via `_pairRebalance` and reserving a 1% swap budget so the subsequent deposit always has non-zero `remToken`/`remLT` [5](#0-4) . This guarantees the *deposit amounts* are non-zero, but it does **not** bound the *absolute scale* of the attacker's pre-existing reserves. If an attacker pre-seeds the pair with reserves at (approximately) the correct curve-close ratio but many orders of magnitude larger than `(tokensForLP, ltFromPair)`, the swap barely needs to fire (the pool is already near-target), and `_routerDepositAndDispose` calls `router.addLiquidity` [6](#0-5) , which drives V2's `mint()`:

```
liquidity0 = amount0 * totalSupply / reserve0
liquidity1 = amount1 * totalSupply / reserve1
liquidity = min(liquidity0, liquidity1)
require(liquidity > 0, "INSUFFICIENT_LIQUIDITY_MINTED")
```
(mirrored exactly by the test mock) [7](#0-6) .

Because `amount0`/`amount1` (Bonding's fixed, capped deposit) are constants while `reserve0`, `reserve1`, and `totalSupply` scale with the attacker's self-funded pre-seed, an attacker can grow the pre-seed until `amount0 * totalSupply < reserve0` (equivalently `amount1 * totalSupply < reserve1`), causing Solidity integer truncation to `0`. `require(liquidity > 0)` inside `pair.mint()` then reverts the entire `finalizeGraduation` call. Because `pendingGraduation[token]` and the pair's now-hostile reserves are unchanged across retries, **every subsequent call to `finalizeGraduation` reverts identically** — the token is permanently stuck in `Lifecycle.Graduating`.

The developers' own comment on `_swapBudget` acknowledges the near-miss version of this bug (an *extreme* pre-seed driving `remToken`/`remLT` to exactly zero) and patches only that specific failure mode [8](#0-7) , but the 1%-reservation fix only guarantees non-zero *deposit amounts*, not non-zero *minted liquidity* — the actual arithmetic that decides whether `pair.mint` succeeds or reverts.

### Impact Explanation
Once bricked, `Lifecycle.Graduating` freezes:
- The full curve-raised LT (`ltFromPair`, already drained into `Bonding` by `Router.graduate` during phase 1 and irretrievable — there is no owner rescue path for it outside `finalizeGraduation`).
- The `tokensForLP` allotment of the 250M `LP_RESERVE` tokens, also stuck on `Bonding`.
- All trading: both `Bonding.buy` and `Bonding.sell` revert with `TokenIsGraduating`/`TokenNotTrading` once `lifecycle != Lifecycle.Curve`, so holders cannot exit even by selling back on the curve.

This satisfies the "permanent freezing of trader, creator, or LP funds" bar in the validation rubric — every future holder of that token loses the ability to trade or realize the graduated LP position.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires real capital: the attacker must accumulate a large TOKEN balance (via ordinary curve buys, bounded by up to 750M tokens) and mint/acquire a correspondingly large LT balance, then permissionlessly `createPair` + `transfer` + `mint` before `finalizeGraduation` lands — all of which are unprivileged, reachable actions explicitly in scope (pre-seeding the HyperSwap V2 pair before graduation). Because the attacker's LP position dominates the resulting pool and is not economically destroyed (they still hold ~100% of a large, if illiquid, pool), the capital is not fundamentally lost, only parked — lowering the practical cost of the griefing relative to a pure burn. This makes it a plausible, if capital-intensive, denial-of-service vector, particularly attractive against a competitor's or disliked token's graduation.

### Recommendation
- After computing `remToken`/`remLT` in `_routerDepositAndDispose`, pre-compute the expected minted liquidity against the pair's live `reserve0`/`reserve1`/`totalSupply` and, if it would round to zero, fall back to a defensive path (e.g., skip the router deposit and route the residual TOKEN/LT through `_sweepLTToOwner`/burn instead of calling `addLiquidity`) rather than letting the call revert.
- Alternatively, bound the acceptable pre-existing pool size (via `reserve0`/`reserve1` vs. `tokensForLP`/`ltFromPair`) and, once it exceeds a safety multiple, skip the router deposit entirely and disburse Bonding's inventory as a direct sweep/burn so `finalizeGraduation` can never revert regardless of scale — consistent with the documented "brick resistance" invariant that already governs the ratio-skew case.

### Proof of Concept
1. Launch a token normally; let it accumulate real curve raise until `canGraduate` trips (`_enterGraduating` caches a small `tokensForLP`/`ltFromPair`, e.g. proportional to a `$9K` USD raise) [9](#0-8) .
2. Before anyone calls `finalizeGraduation`, the attacker (who accumulated a large TOKEN balance via prior curve buys and minted a large LT balance) calls `factory.createPair(token, lt)`, transfers TOKEN and LT to the pair at approximately the curve-close ratio but scaled up by, e.g., 10^6×, and calls `pair.mint(attacker)`.
3. Call `Bonding.finalizeGraduation(token)`. `_seedRebalancing`'s rebalance leaves the pool near the target ratio; `_routerDepositAndDispose` calls `router.addLiquidity` with Bonding's fixed small `remToken`/`remLT`, which underlying `pair.mint()` computes as `amount * totalSupply / reserve`, truncating to `0` given the attacker's inflated `reserve`/`totalSupply`, and reverts with `INSUFFICIENT_LIQUIDITY_MINTED`.
4. Every retry of `finalizeGraduation` recomputes the same cached `pendingGraduation` amounts against the same (now static) hostile reserves and reverts identically — the token is permanently stuck in `Lifecycle.Graduating`.

    function _prepareGraduationLiquidity(
        address tokenAddress
    ) internal returns (uint256 tokensForLP, uint256 ltFromPair, uint256 lpBurned, uint256 unsoldBurned) {
        address pairAddr = _s().tokenInfo[tokenAddress].pair;
        (uint256 tokenReserve, uint256 assetReserve) = IPair(pairAddr).getReserves();

        unsoldBurned = IPair(pairAddr).tokenBalance();
        if (unsoldBurned > 0) {
            Token(tokenAddress).burn(pairAddr, unsoldBurned);
        }

        ltFromPair = assetReserve - _launchTimeVirtualLtReserve(tokenAddress, pairAddr);
        if (ltFromPair > 0) {
            _s().router.graduate(tokenAddress, ltFromPair);
        }

        tokensForLP = assetReserve == 0 ? 0 : (ltFromPair * tokenReserve) / assetReserve;
        if (tokensForLP > LP_RESERVE) tokensForLP = LP_RESERVE;

        lpBurned = LP_RESERVE - tokensForLP;
        if (lpBurned > 0) {
            Token(tokenAddress).burn(address(this), lpBurned);
        }
    }

        address tokenAddress,
        address lt,
        uint256 protectedLT
    ) internal returns (uint256 liquidity) {
        BondingStorage storage $ = _s();
        address routerAddr = $.uniswapV2Router;
        address lpLock_ = $.lpLock;
        uint256 remToken = IERC20(tokenAddress).balanceOf(address(this));
        // Subtract `protectedLT` (LT that doesn't belong to this graduation
        // — concurrent escrows or stray dust, snapshotted at the top of
        // `finalizeGraduation`) so the deposit allowance can never pull
        // another graduation's earmark or accidentally absorb dust into a
        // locked LP.
        uint256 ltBal = IERC20(lt).balanceOf(address(this));
        uint256 remLT = ltBal > protectedLT ? ltBal - protectedLT : 0;

        if (remToken > 0 && remLT > 0) {
            IERC20(tokenAddress).forceApprove(routerAddr, remToken);
            IERC20(lt).forceApprove(routerAddr, remLT);
            (,, liquidity) = IUniswapV2Router02(routerAddr)

            IERC20(tokenAddress).forceApprove(routerAddr, 0);
            IERC20(lt).forceApprove(routerAddr, 0);

        // Hostile pre-seeds reduce circulating supply by the attacker's
        // wasted-side share, net positive for honest holders.

        // this contract until `finalizeGraduation`'s post-bookend sweeps
        // it to the owner. Honest graduations never reach this code path,
        // so the residue is zero outside attack scenarios.

```markdown
### The exploit

A vanilla UniswapV2 pair is deployable by anyone: `factory.createPair(token, lt)` is permissionless, and after creation anyone can call `pair.mint(to)` against pre-transferred tokens. So between phase 1 (`_enterGraduating` flips lifecycle to `Graduating` and caches `tokensForLP / ltFromPair`) and phase 2 (`finalizeGraduation` mints LP via `pair.mint(lpLock)`), an attacker can:

1. Front-run by calling `factory.createPair(token, lt)` themselves
2. `transfer(pair, smallToken)` and `transfer(pair, smallLT)` at any ratio they choose
3. Call `pair.mint(attacker)` — they now own LP at a hostile reserve ratio

When our `pair.mint(lpLock)` runs in phase 2 against this non-empty pair, V2's mint formula picks up the existing reserves:

```
liquidity = min(amount0 · totalSupply / reserve0, amount1 · totalSupply / reserve1)
```

The `min(...)` arm whose denominator is bigger relative to its numerator wins, and the OTHER arm's "excess" deposit is donated pro-rata to existing LP holders — i.e. to the attacker. Two harms:

- **Wrong opening price.** Post-mint reserves are `(R_attacker + T_a, R_attacker + T_b)`, so the LP opens at `(R_a + T_a) / (R_b + T_b)`, NOT at the curve close `T_a / T_b`. A `$15` LT pre-seed at 50% off curve close opens the pool ~454 bps off.
- **LP capture.** The wasted-side excess goes to the attacker's LP claim. A `1 wei + 1 LT` pre-seed (~`$1` attack budget) captures ~34 bps of LP.

A cheaper variant skips step 3 entirely: `transfer(pair, dust) + pair.sync()` forces the stored reserves to the dust ratio without minting any LP, leaving the pair at `reserves > 0 && totalSupply == 0`. Regime 1 below covers both shapes by keying on supply rather than reserves.

This is the same exploit class as the four.meme Feb 2025 incident (~$183K loss).
```
