### Title

Interest accrual can overflow `scaled_to_original` and permanently freeze a market - (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary

A sufficiently large market operating at sustained high utilization can reach a state where the next accrual panics while converting scaled debt or supply into its `Ray` value. Because `Controller::update_indexes` is permissionless and every pool mutation synchronizes interest before accounting, this arithmetic edge permanently blocks repayment, withdrawal, liquidation, and other actions for that market.

### Finding Description

`accrue_step` computes utilization by materializing `borrowed * borrow_index` and `supplied * supply_index` through `scaled_to_original` before it updates the borrow index. [1](#0-0)  `scaled_to_original` delegates to `Ray::mul`, whose underlying fixed-point multiplication panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` when the result exceeds `i128`. [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

The borrow-index cap is only applied after `update_borrow_index` computes a new index, so it cannot prevent the earlier valuation product from exceeding `i128`. [4](#0-3)  Once `borrowed * borrow_index` or `supplied * supply_index` exceeds the `i128` domain, `global_sync` cannot complete any accrual chunk or advance `last_timestamp`. [5](#0-4) 

All pool mutation legs load a synced market, meaning they run `global_sync` before performing supply, borrow, withdrawal, repayment, liquidation, or other accounting. [6](#0-5)  For example, `repay::accounting` calls `ops::load_leg` before resolving the repayment, and `withdraw::accounting` does the same before resolving the close amount. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7) 

The permissionless controller entrypoint `update_indexes(caller, assets)` forwards the caller-selected `HubAssetKey` list to the pool. [9](#0-8) [10](#0-9)  After the crossing point, an unprivileged caller can invoke that entrypoint and receive `MATH_OVERFLOW`; subsequent `repay`, `withdraw`, `liquidate`, `supply`, and `borrow` calls touching the market hit the same accrual panic before their state changes. [11](#0-10) [6](#0-5) 

### Impact Explanation

This is a permanent freezing-of-funds condition for every supplier and borrower in the affected market: debt cannot be repaid, collateral cannot be withdrawn or liquidated through that market, and interest cannot be accrued past the failing timestamp. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5)  The repository’s regression test demonstrates that both withdrawal and repayment revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW` after the cliff is reached. [12](#0-11) 

### Likelihood Explanation

The trigger requires an exceptionally deep market—such as the tested one-billion-token, 18-decimal market—combined with sustained high utilization and enough elapsed time for the index multiplier to make the `Ray` valuation exceed `i128`. [13](#0-12)  Those conditions are economically demanding but do not require privileged execution at trigger time: users reach them through `supply`, collateralized `borrow`, the passage of ledger time, and finally permissionless `update_indexes`. [14](#0-13) [9](#0-8) 

### Recommendation

Bound or saturate the `scaled * index` valuation before calling the panicking multiplication, or perform utilization and accrual calculations in a wider/saturating representation that preserves ordering without materializing an overflowing `i128` value. The borrow-index ceiling should be enforced before an intermediate valuation can poison the next accrual, and accrual should retain a recovery path that can clamp indexes rather than leaving `last_timestamp` permanently behind. [15](#0-14) [4](#0-3) 

### Proof of Concept

1. Configure an 18-decimal market using the XLM rate curve and a collateral market, then supply `1_000_000_000 * 10^18` raw units of the market asset. [16](#0-15) 
2. Supply sufficient collateral to a second account and borrow 98% of the market’s debt capacity. [17](#0-16) 
3. Advance ledger time in yearly intervals while calling the permissionless `Controller::update_indexes` path until the next accrual returns `MATH_OVERFLOW`. [18](#0-17) 
4. Observe that the stored borrow index remains below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, proving that the overflow occurred before the index cap could engage. [19](#0-18) 
5. Attempt a one-unit supplier withdrawal and borrower repayment; both fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW` because each operation accrues the poisoned market first. [20](#0-19)
