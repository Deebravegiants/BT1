### Title
Self-custody face frame is not bound to a specific eye objective, allowing capture of a face frame from a different physical moment than the iris frames it is packaged with - (File: src/plans/biometric_capture/mod.rs)

### Summary
`Plan::handle_face_identifier` selects `self_custody_candidate_rgb` purely by the highest `IsValidImage` score seen so far, with no reset or windowing tied to the currently active objective. Because `set_next_objective` only resets `max_sharpness` and never `self_custody_candidate_rgb`, a face frame captured during the left-eye objective can remain the winning candidate throughout the right-eye objective (or vice versa), so the final `Capture` couples iris frames and the self-custody face frame from different points in the signup session.

### Finding Description
`Plan::new` initializes `self_custody_candidate_rgb: None` once for the whole capture session [1](#0-0) . The objective queue alternates between left-eye and right-eye targets, and `set_next_objective` is called on every objective transition, but it only resets `self.max_sharpness = 0.0` and updates `target_left_eye`/IR settings — it never clears `self_custody_candidate_rgb` [2](#0-1) .

`handle_face_identifier` compares the new `IsValidImage` score only against the previous highest score stored in `self.self_custody_candidate_rgb`, with no check on which objective/eye was active when either frame was captured: `let highest = self.self_custody_candidate_rgb.as_ref().map_or(0.0, |p| p.estimate.score.unwrap_or_default()); if output.score.is_some_and(|s| s > highest) { ... self.self_custody_candidate_rgb = Some(...) }` [3](#0-2) . `poll_extra` only checks that both iris and RGB for the *current* eye are present, and that `self_custody_candidate_rgb.is_some()` in the last objective — it never checks that the winning face frame's timestamp falls within the last (or current) objective's window [4](#0-3) . `FrameInfo::timestamp` is recorded (`Instant::now()`) but is explicitly marked `#[allow(dead_code)]` and never used for validation [5](#0-4) . Finally, `into_capture()` builds the `Capture` from `self.left_ir`, `self.right_ir`, and `self.self_custody_candidate_rgb` independently, with no cross-check that all three were captured within the same objective/time window [6](#0-5) .

An unprivileged attacker presenting to the Orb can therefore: (1) present their face with a high-scoring pose during the left-eye objective so it wins as `self_custody_candidate_rgb`, then (2) proceed to complete the right-eye objective (e.g. by changing pose/lighting/expression) without ever presenting a face frame that scores higher. The final `self_custody_candidate_rgb` remains the frame from the left-eye objective, while `eye_left`/`eye_right` IR/RGB frames come from their respective (later) objectives.

### Impact Explanation
This decouples the self-custody face image bound into the signup package from the physical presentation used to capture one or both iris scans, weakening the intra-signup identity binding invariant (all frames should originate from the same continuous physical presentation). The practical impact is limited: this does not enable a different *person's* face to be substituted (the RGB frames are still valid images from the same physical presence, just from a different sub-window of one signup), it does not defeat the iris-based OP-1.4/identity verification, and there's no cross-signup bleed. The severity is best characterized as a low/informational binding-weakness rather than an unauthorized signup or wrong-identity binding since the face and iris still belong to the same physical person in the same session.

### Likelihood Explanation
Highly feasible and repeatable: nothing more than varying facial pose/lighting during one's own signup is required, and no operator or hardware access is needed. However, the actual exploitable value is minimal — the attacker cannot force a *different identity's* face into the package, only an earlier-vs-later frame of themselves, which is unlikely to bypass any downstream fraud or liveness checks that operate on the eye/iris images themselves.

### Recommendation
Reset `self_custody_candidate_rgb` (and its associated score) at the start of each new eye objective in `set_next_objective`, or otherwise scope candidate selection to the objective/time window during which the currently-active eye is being captured, and validate in `into_capture()` that the self-custody candidate timestamp falls within the same overall capture session bounds as the final iris captures.

### Proof of Concept
Unit test plan in `src/plans/biometric_capture/mod.rs` test module:
1. Construct `Plan` with a 2-objective queue (left then right eye).
2. Simulate `handle_face_identifier` with a high-score `IsValidImage` output while `target_left_eye == true`.
3. Call `set_next_objective` to switch to right-eye objective (as `run_check`/`poll_extra` would trigger).
4. Simulate `handle_ir_net`/`handle_rgb_net` for right eye with valid frames but do NOT send any higher-scoring `IsValidImage`.
5. Call `into_capture()` and assert that `self_custody_candidate_rgb`'s underlying `FrameInfo::timestamp` predates the right-eye objective start — expected (current, buggy) behavior: assertion passes, showing the retained frame is from a stale objective window, confirming lack of any rejecting/flagging logic.
