### Title
Relayer fee balance is zeroed on Hyperbridge before the destination-chain payout is confirmed, causing permanent loss of relayer fees on transfer failure — ([File: modules/pallets/relayer/src/withdrawal.rs])

### Summary
`Pallet::withdraw` in `modules/pallets/relayer/src/withdrawal.rs` reads the relayer's full accrued balance from `Fees<T>`, dispatches a cross-chain `WithdrawRelayerFees` POST request instructing the destination chain to pay it out, and only *after* the dispatch succeeds, unconditionally zeroes the `Fees<T>` entry [1](#0-0) . The zeroing happens purely on the strength of a *successful dispatch* of the outbound message, not on confirmed delivery/settlement at the destination. This mirrors the `BalancedVault.claim` bug class: an internal accounting entry is deleted before the actual pro-rata/availability of the underlying funds at settlement time is guaranteed.

### Finding Description
On the destination chain, the corresponding settlement is handled by `HyperbridgeWithdrawalModule::on_accept` in `modules/pallets/ismp/src/dispatcher.rs`, which performs a single all-or-nothing `T::Currency::transfer` from the shared `RELAYER_FEE_ACCOUNT` to the relayer's beneficiary account for the full `amount` [2](#0-1) . `RELAYER_FEE_ACCOUNT` is a shared pool that is credited/debited by many independent per-request fee escrows and payouts (`fee.payer -> RELAYER_FEE_ACCOUNT` at dispatch time, and RELAYER_FEE_ACCOUNT -> account at withdrawal time) across the whole system, not a per-relayer-earmarked balance. If the pool's actual on-chain balance at the moment `on_accept` runs is lower than `amount` (e.g. because other relayers/apps have drawn down the same shared account, due to timing/ordering of concurrent withdrawals, refunds, or fee accounting drift), the `transfer` call fails and `on_accept` returns an error.

Because `Fees<T>[dest_chain][address]` was already reset to `U256::zero()` on Hyperbridge at dispatch time — before the destination ever attempted the transfer — there is no pro-rata fallback and no way to reclaim the un-paid remainder. The `on_timeout` handler for this module also does not restore anything (`Err(IsmpError::CannotHandleMessage)` and, separately, `FeeMetadata { payer: [0u8;32].into(), fee: Default::default() }` means the generic per-request timeout-refund logic can't help since there was no fee/payer attached to this outbound dispatch) [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) . The relayer's earned reward is permanently lost with no recourse, exactly the "claim resets tracked balance before confirming actual availability of funds" pattern flagged in the external report.

### Impact Explanation
This directly causes permanent loss of relayer fee rewards — real economic value earned by unprivileged relayers for delivering messages. Since `RELAYER_FEE_ACCOUNT` is a shared pool funded from many unrelated fee payers/apps across the protocol, its balance is not guaranteed to always cover any single relayer's full accrued claim at settlement time, especially under concurrent withdrawal activity. Once the withdraw extrinsic dispatches successfully, the relayer's internal `Fees` ledger entry is destroyed unconditionally, regardless of whether the destination-side transfer later succeeds.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is Medium: it requires the shared `RELAYER_FEE_ACCOUNT` balance to be insufficient at the moment of destination-side settlement, which can occur from the natural, permissionless interleaving of many parties dispatching fee-bearing requests and other relayers/withdrawals draining the pool, or from any transient accounting discrepancy between accrued relayer credits and the pool's real balance. No privileged action is required to trigger it — it is an unprivileged relayer calling `Pallet::withdraw` (or the sequence of relayers/apps interacting with the shared fee account) that can reach this state.

### Recommendation
Do not zero `Fees<T>` optimistically at dispatch time. Either:
- Only clear/decrement the `Fees<T>` entry after the destination confirms successful settlement (e.g., via an ack/response message back to Hyperbridge), or
- On `on_accept` transfer failure, emit a distinguishable failure so the withdrawal can be retried/re-credited (e.g., have the destination emit a compensating message, or track a pending-withdrawal state on Hyperbridge that is only finalized once the destination-side receipt is proven), so a shortfall does not silently and permanently zero out relayer earnings.

### Proof of Concept
1. Multiple apps dispatch fee-bearing requests into `RELAYER_FEE_ACCOUNT` on a destination chain; relayers deliver them and their earned amounts accumulate in Hyperbridge's `Fees<T>[dest_chain][relayer]` via `accumulate_fees`.
2. Relayer A calls `withdraw()` for `dest_chain`. `Pallet::withdraw` reads `available_amount = Fees::<T>::get(dest_chain, A)`, dispatches the `WithdrawRelayerFees` POST, then immediately sets `Fees::<T>::insert(dest_chain, A, U256::zero())` [5](#0-4) .
3. Before the message lands on `dest_chain`, other relayers' withdrawals (or refunds) drain `RELAYER_FEE_ACCOUNT` below `available_amount`.
4. When the message is delivered, `HyperbridgeWithdrawalModule::on_accept` attempts `T::Currency::transfer(RELAYER_FEE_ACCOUNT, A, amount, Expendable)`, which fails due to insufficient balance and returns an error [6](#0-5) .
5. Relayer A's `Fees` entry on Hyperbridge is already zero; A never receives the funds and has no ledger entry left to retry the withdrawal — the accrued reward is permanently lost.
