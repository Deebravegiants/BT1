### Title
Governance-Set Outbound Delivery Reward Lacks Treasury Solvency Check, Causing Failed Claims for Legitimate Relayers - (File: modules/pallets/relayer/src/outbound_consensus.rs)

### Summary
`set_outbound_consensus_delivery_reward` and `set_outbound_request_delivery_reward` (via `dispatch_set_tiers`-style governance calls in `pallet-relayer`) let governance configure a per-destination or per-module reward amount that is paid from a single shared `TreasuryPalletId`-derived account whenever any relayer successfully proves delivery of a mandatory consensus rotation or a hyperbridge-originated request. Neither the setter nor the claim path checks that the treasury balance is sufficient to cover the (unbounded) number of future claims that reward rate can generate. A late, otherwise-valid claimant's transaction fails at the `transfer` call once earlier claimants have drawn the treasury down, exactly mirroring the reported Olympus Treasury bug class: an "approval"-like commitment (the configured reward) is created without validating it against the actual backing balance, so downstream legitimate withdrawers get reverted transactions.

### Finding Description
`set_outbound_consensus_delivery_reward` writes `OutboundConsensusDeliveryReward[destination] = amount` under `RelayerOrigin` with no balance check against the treasury account: [1](#0-0) 

The same absence of a solvency check applies to the per-module `OutboundRequestDeliveryReward` storage, which is written by the analogous outbound-request governance call (`docs/outbound-request-incentivization.md` documents the same shape, "structurally identical to the existing `claim_outbound_consensus_delivery_reward`").

Every relayer that proves delivery of a distinct `(destination, set_id)` rotation, or a distinct request `commitment`, is independently entitled to the full configured reward — there is no cap on how many such distinct deliveries can occur, and thus no cap on aggregate exposure against the treasury. The actual payout only checks the idempotency map (`OutboundConsensusRotationsClaimed` / `OutboundRequestsClaimed`) to prevent double-claiming the *same* rotation/request, not whether the treasury can afford *all* rotations/requests at the configured rate: [3](#0-2) [2](#0-1) 

The transfer at claim time is the only place a balance is checked, and it is a hard revert rather than a graceful degradation: [4](#0-3) 

This is the direct analog of `OlympusTreasury.increaseWithdrawApproval` never checking `token_.balanceOf(address(this))`: here, `set_outbound_consensus_delivery_reward`/the request-reward setter never checks the treasury's `Currency` balance before enabling an unbounded stream of future entitlements at that rate, and `withdrawReserves`'s later failure mode is mirrored by the `OutboundRewardTransferFailed` / `OutboundRequestRewardTransferFailed` revert in the claim path.

### Impact Explanation
If governance sets (or leaves) a reward rate that is not resupplied fast enough relative to the frequency of authority-set rotations landing on many EVM destinations (or of reward-eligible module requests), the treasury account can be drawn down below the configured reward. Any relayer that then submits a fully valid, correctly-proven claim — one that has done real, costly work (delivering a consensus rotation or hyperbridge-originated request to a destination chain) — has their claim transaction revert with `OutboundRewardTransferFailed`/`OutboundRequestRewardTransferFailed`, wasting the submission cost and denying the earned incentive despite doing everything correctly. This degrades the relayer incentive mechanism the outbound-delivery reward system exists to guarantee (per `docs/outbound-request-incentivization.md`, this reward is what makes relayers deliver hyperbridge-originated system messages at all — without it "the only thing that keeps them flowing today is altruism"). Because the claim is unsigned (`ensure_none(origin)`) and permissionless once proof conditions are met, exhaustion is not attacker-privileged: any set of relayers racing to prove genuine deliveries can exhaust the pool for others. This is a Medium-severity availability/fund-flow issue: no funds are stolen, but the incentive/payout mechanism becomes unreliable, matching the report's "inefficiency causing failed transactions for legitimate withdrawers".

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate: it requires (a) governance to configure per-destination/per-module rewards without provisioning matching treasury balance headroom, and (b) enough distinct rotations/requests to be claimed in a period to draw the treasury below the configured reward. Given that authority-set rotations happen periodically and are mandatory (propagated to every connected destination per `docs/content/developers/explore/relayers.mdx`), and that outbound-request rewards are meant to be claimed by any relayer proving delivery of increasingly many system messages (host-executive, intents-coprocessor, token-governor, bandwidth, etc., per the incentivization doc), the number of eligible claims scales with the number of connected chains and message volume — a scenario that is realistic during normal operation, not just an edge case.

### Recommendation
Before or during setting `OutboundConsensusDeliveryReward` / `OutboundRequestDeliveryReward`, validate against the treasury's current balance and/or track outstanding unclaimed exposure (e.g., an accrual counter incremented whenever a slot/commitment becomes claimable, checked against treasury balance), rejecting or capping reward configuration that would let aggregate outstanding entitlements exceed the treasury's funds. Alternatively, emit an on-chain low-treasury warning event and/or allow governance to pause reward accrual automatically when the treasury balance drops below a safety threshold, so genuine relayers are not left with reverted claims after having already delivered.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance calls `set_outbound_consensus_delivery_reward(dest_chain_A, 1000)` and `set_outbound_consensus_delivery_reward(dest_chain_B, 1000)` (and/or several `set_outbound_request_delivery_reward`-equivalent calls for different modules), with the treasury funded only for e.g. 1500 total. [1](#0-0) 
2. Relayer 1 delivers the mandatory consensus rotation to `dest_chain_A`, and submits `claim_outbound_consensus_delivery_reward` with a valid state proof and signature; `process_outbound_consensus_delivery_claim` succeeds, transferring 1000 from the treasury. [2](#0-1) 
3. Relayer 2, having independently and correctly delivered the mandatory rotation to `dest_chain_B` (equally valid work), submits its own claim. The treasury now holds only 500, below the configured 1000 reward.
4. `<<T as pallet_ismp::Config>::Currency as Mutate<T::AccountId>>::transfer(...)` fails `Preservation::Preserve`'s balance requirement, and the extrinsic reverts with `Error::OutboundRewardTransferFailed`, even though Relayer 2 performed the required delivery and proof correctly and has no way to know the treasury was insufficient before submitting.
