### Title
Unpriced dust collateral lets an Aquarius LP position owner shield debt from liquidation and cleanup - ([File: contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/plan.rs])

### Summary

A borrower can add an economically insignificant Aquarius LP collateral leg, then withdraw enough of their own liquidity from that pool to push its value below `min_pool_value_wad`. The oracle refuses the LP price, while liquidation and bad-debt cleanup must value every stored collateral leg before proceeding. This allows the borrower to temporarily prevent liquidation and cleanup without relying on an oracle malfunction or privileged action. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description

`process_supply` accepts listed collateral and does not require a successful price read before crediting the position; it checks entry gates, transfers the measured deposit to the pool, and records the returned supply shares. [3](#0-2) 

For liquidation, `build_liquidation_plan` unconditionally calls `calculate_account_risk_totals` over all supply and borrow positions before checking whether the account is unhealthy. [4](#0-3) 

`calculate_account_risk_totals` loads every position market and calls `cached_price` for every supply leg; a missing or rejected price therefore aborts valuation instead of skipping the economically irrelevant leg. [5](#0-4) 

The price aggregator panics when strict resolution fails, and the Aquarius provider returns `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` whenever the LP's computed pool value is below `min_pool_value_wad`. [6](#0-5) [1](#0-0) 

Standalone `clean_bad_debt` uses the same all-position risk totals to decide eligibility, so the poisoned leg also blocks permissionless cleanup. [7](#0-6) 

### Impact Explanation

The borrower can keep an unhealthy account unliquidatable for as long as they can keep the referenced Aquarius pool below the configured value floor. During that interval, liquidators cannot reduce the debt through `liquidate`, and a caller cannot clear eligible bad debt through `clean_bad_debt`; suppliers consequently remain exposed to growing unpaid debt and cannot recover the frozen claim through these exits. [4](#0-3) [8](#0-7) 

The collateral leg can be dust because liquidation planning iterates positions rather than ignoring low-value legs. [9](#0-8) 

The condition is temporary because restoring pool liquidity above `min_pool_value_wad` makes the LP price usable again; nevertheless, the borrower controls the outage duration while holding enough LP liquidity or influencing an already-thin pool. [10](#0-9) 

### Likelihood Explanation

This is reachable by one unprivileged address that already has borrowable collateral and sufficient Aquarius LP liquidity to move the referenced pool across the configured floor. The borrower adds a small LP position through `supply`, waits until the account is unhealthy through ordinary price movement or accrued debt, and invokes the venue's normal liquidity-withdrawal path; no leaked key, privileged role, malformed market parameter, or invalid oracle submission is required. [11](#0-10) [1](#0-0) 

The repository's regression test demonstrates the equivalent control-flow property: supplying a dust leg with an unusable price succeeds, after which liquidation, cleanup, and even withdrawing that leg revert until the feed becomes usable. [12](#0-11) [13](#0-12) 

### Recommendation

Risk valuation and liquidation should not let an economically negligible collateral position veto the operation. [14](#0-13) 

A suitable change is to evaluate collateral legs with unusable prices as zero for liquidation eligibility and pro-rata seizure planning, while continuing to require valid debt prices and treating that collateral as unavailable rather than seizable. The same fallback should be applied consistently to `clean_bad_debt` eligibility so an unpriceable dust leg cannot prevent socializing debt whose value exceeds all priceable collateral. [15](#0-14) [8](#0-7) 

Alternatively, reject new supply of a collateral asset whose strict price cannot be resolved, including an Aquarius pool below its liquidity floor; this must be implemented before the token transfer and share mint. [16](#0-15) 

### Proof of Concept

The following attacker flow uses only the permitted supply, borrow, and venue actions. [11](#0-10) 

```text
1. Attacker supplies ordinary collateral C through controller.supply.
2. Attacker borrows D through controller.borrow.
3. Attacker obtains a listed Aquarius LP share and supplies a dust amount
   through controller.supply(account_id, spoke_id, [(hub, lp_asset, dust)]).
4. After market movement or accrued interest makes the account's HF < 1,
   the attacker withdraws their own liquidity from the referenced Aquarius
   pool until its LP pool value is below min_pool_value_wad.
5. Liquidator calls controller.liquidate(liquidator, account_id,
   [(hub, debt_asset, amount)], SeizeMode::Transfer).
6. The transaction aborts during the all-collateral valuation because the
   LP source returns InsufficientAquariusLiquidity.
7. A caller invokes controller.clean_bad_debt(account_id); it aborts on the
   same required LP valuation.
8. The attacker later restores LP liquidity above the floor and repeats the
   sequence if the account becomes liquidatable again.
```

`process_liquidation` reaches `build_liquidation_plan` before any debt repayment or seizure occurs, and `socialize_bad_debt` reaches the same risk-total calculation before cleanup eligibility is checked. [17](#0-16) [7](#0-6)

fn socialize_bad_debt(env: &Env, account_id: u64, gate: BadDebtGate) {
    let mut cache = Context::new(env);
    let account = storage::get_account(env, account_id);

    assert_with_error!(

        !account.borrow_positions.is_empty(),

        env,

        &account.supply_positions,
        &account.borrow_positions,

    };
