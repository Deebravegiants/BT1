### Title
Accrual `i128` overflow freezes a whale-sized market permanently — index cap engages too late - (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary
The analog of CVE-2019-13486's "expansion past a fixed-size buffer" in this codebase is expansion of a `Ray` product past the fixed `i128` container during interest accrual. `accrue_step` computes `borrowed_original = scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` — i.e. `borrowed_shares * borrow_index / RAY` — *before* `update_borrow_index` can clamp the index at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. For a sufficiently large `borrowed` position, `borrowed * borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX` while `borrow_index` is still far below its cap, so `Ray::mul` panics with `MathOverflow`. Every state-changing entrypoint on the pool runs `global_sync` first, so the panic permanently bricks the market: no withdraw, repay, borrow, or liquidation can ever execute on it again.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` at `common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-66` unscales debt and supply each step before clamping:

```rust
let borrowed_original = scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index);
let supplied_original = scaled_to_original(env, supplied, supply_index);
let util = utilization(env, borrowed_original, supplied_original);
...
let new_borrow_index = update_borrow_index(env, borrow_index, interest_factor);
```

`scaled_to_original` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14`) is `scaled.mul(env, index)`, a `half_up(x * y / RAY)` that panics via `mul_div_half_up` when the product doesn't fit `i128` (`common/src/math/fp_core.rs:108-118`). The index cap only exists *after* this multiplication, in `update_borrow_index` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19`), which clamps the *index*, not the `borrowed * index` product. `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY = 1e36` gives ~170× headroom over `RAY`, but an 18-decimal market can hold a book value up to ~`i128::MAX / RAY` (~1.7e11 whole tokens); the product `borrowed_shares × index` overflows `i128` once the index grows past `i128::MAX × RAY / (borrowed_ray)` — reachable well before the index cap when the scaled debt is near the top of the representable range.

The panic propagates through `accrue_chunk` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-53` (`global_sync`, called first by every mutation), through permissionless `update_indexes`, and through every controller verb (supply/borrow/withdraw/repay/liquidate), since they all sync the market before operating. The repo's own harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-356` demonstrates exactly this: `MATH_OVERFLOW` on `update_indexes`, `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (cap never engaged), and subsequent `withdraw`/`repay` attempts both reverting with `MATH_OVERFLOW` — a permanent freeze, not a transient one, because accrued time only moves forward.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of user funds on the affected market. Once `borrowed × borrow_index` crosses the `i128` ceiling, no accrual can ever succeed again on that `(hub, token)` book: suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot liquidate, and `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` also route through accrual. All supplied principal and accrued yield on that market are locked forever.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by a single unprivileged address with whale capital, no privileges needed: `supply` a book near the asset-domain cap on a high-decimals market, `borrow` to sustained high utilization against the attacker's own collateral position, then let wall-clock time compound the index (anyone can call the permissionless `update_indexes` to keep accrual running — although the attacker's own index would keep rising). Reaching the cliff requires a very large book at high utilization on a steep rate curve over many years of ledger time, which caps this at Medium: it demands capital and patience but no governance action, no leaked key, and no third-party cooperation.

### Recommendation
Guard the accrual before unscaling. Either:
- Cap `borrow_index`/`supply_index` at `min(MAX_*_INDEX_RAY, i128::MAX * RAY / max(borrowed, supplied))` — i.e. an *effective* index ceiling derived from the live book size — clamped inside `accrue_step` before `scaled_to_original` is called; or
- Use a saturating `scaled_to_original` for the utilization/rate inputs (the utilization ratio is scale-invariant, so saturating both numerator terms preserves the rate decision) while keeping panicking unscaling for settlement paths, where the amounts are still within range once the index is clamped.

A targeted fix is to compute utilization as `borrowed × borrow_index / (supplied × supply_index)` with an `I256` intermediate, so accrual never depends on fitting either product into `i128`.

### Proof of Concept
The repository already contains the executable witness — `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361`:

1. Create an 18-decimal market (`big("BIG18", 18, xlm_curve())`) plus a collateral market; lift caps (`lift_caps`).
2. `supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", BILLION * 10^18)`, `supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", ...)`, `borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", 98% of the book)` — all ordinary unprivileged calls.
3. Advance ledger time year-by-year calling `try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"])`.
4. Observed: `update_indexes` reverts with `errors::MATH_OVERFLOW` while `book.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
5. Observed: `try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1)` and `try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0)` both revert `MATH_OVERFLOW` — the freeze is permanent because `last_timestamp` only moves forward and the accrual can never complete.

The root cause lines: `scaled_to_original` panicking multiply (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14`, `common/src/math/fp_core.rs:131-136`), called ahead of the index clamp in `accrue_step` (`common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-66`), reached via `global_sync` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33`.
