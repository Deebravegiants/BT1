### Title
Tokens sent to the controller outside declared collateral/refund lists are permanently stranded — no rescue or sweep path exists - (File: contracts/controller/src/strategies/flash_position.rs)

### Summary
The controller credits only declared `collaterals` deltas and refunds only `refund_assets` deltas in `flash_position`, and no other controller entrypoint credits or returns arbitrary inbound tokens. Any token balance pushed to the controller that is not one of those measured deltas — an undeclared callback asset in `flash_position`, or a direct `token.transfer` to the controller/pool address — is neither credited to any account nor refundable, and there is no sweep/rescue endpoint. The funds are permanently frozen, mirroring the FEYTraderJoeProduct stuck-deposit class.

### Finding Description
In `process_flash_position`, the controller snapshots balances of (a) the declared collateral assets and (b) the caller-listed `refund_assets` immediately before invoking the receiver's `execute_flash_position` callback (`contracts/controller/src/strategies/flash_position.rs:120-143`). After the callback:

- `collect_collateral_deposits` measures each declared collateral's delta and supplies it (`flash_position.rs:325-352`).
- `refund_listed_assets` calls `refund_controller_balance_delta` for each listed refund asset, returning only the positive delta above its pre-callback baseline (`flash_position.rs:372-384`, `contracts/controller/src/payments.rs:41-52`).

Any asset pushed by the receiver (or by anyone else) that appears in neither list keeps its full delta at the controller: it is not collateral-credited, not refunded, and not repaid toward the minted debt. The same applies to a token sent directly to the controller or pool outside any call — `supply`, `repay`, and `recapitalize` credit only amounts they themselves measure/declare, and pool custody is a shared physical balance across hub books that donations do not rewrite. The refund baseline logic actively protects (i.e., strands) pre-existing balances: `refund_controller_balance_delta` returns only `balance_after - balance_before`, so a prior stuck balance is never swept. The docs confirm the gap: "Undeclared callback assets receive neither credit nor refunds. There is no controller sweep endpoint" (`docs/reference/endpoints.md:86`) and "the adapter has no recovery path for arbitrary stranded assets" (`docs/explanation/threat-model.md:179`).

Contrast with the correctly handled legs: `swap_tokens` refunds router underspend to `refund_to` (`contracts/controller/src/strategies/swap.rs:49-52`), and `repay`/`recapitalize` refund overpayment. The missing case is any token arriving outside an enumerated measurement list.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of user funds. A user whose `flash_position` receiver pushes an asset they forgot to declare in `collaterals`/`refund_assets`, or who transfers tokens directly to the controller/pool (e.g., intending a deposit or repayment), loses those tokens forever — no entrypoint credits, refunds, or sweeps them, and governance cannot recover them either since there is no rescue function.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by any unprivileged address via `flash_position` (NFT owner, flash-flagged debt market, caller-chosen receiver) or by a plain SAC `transfer` to the controller/pool address. It requires user error (undeclared asset or a mistaken direct transfer) rather than an attacker, matching the original report's self-inflicted medium-severity pattern; the shared-custody design makes such mistakes plausible since the controller address legitimately receives tokens in many flows.

### Recommendation
Add a recovery mechanism mirroring the report's recommendation: either (a) a permissionless `sweep(asset, to)` endpoint that returns controller token balances exceeding booked obligations (sum of per-hub `cash` claims for that token plus reserved refund deltas), or (b) make `refund_assets` optional-to-exhaustive by refunding the post-callback delta of every token the controller holds, not only listed ones. At minimum, document and surface the stranded-funds risk, or allow `refund_assets` entries that are also collateral assets so over-delivery can be recovered.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice owns an account NFT and calls `flash_position` with `debt = USDC` market (flash enabled), `collaterals = [(XLM, min 1)]`, `refund_assets = [USDC]`, receiver = her Wasm contract.
2. Inside `execute_flash_position`, the receiver transfers `XLM` collateral and also pushes `1_000_000` units of `EURC` (an asset listed in the hub but not declared in either list) to the controller.
3. The call succeeds: XLM delta is collateral-credited, USDC delta is refunded. The EURC delta remains at the controller.
4. No subsequent entrypoint — `supply`, `repay`, `recapitalize`, `claim_revenue`, `withdraw` — can credit or return that EURC to Alice; `claim_revenue` pays only the configured accumulator's booked revenue, and `recapitalize` measures only its own inbound leg. The EURC is permanently locked.
