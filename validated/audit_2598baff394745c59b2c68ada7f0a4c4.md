### Title
Accrued protocol revenue becomes unclaimable whenever utilization is at or above `max_utilization` — the revenue burn raises utilization and the all-or-nothing claim then self-reverts — (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/revenue.rs)

### Summary
Analogous to the Dahlia report (fee shares are credited but the recipient has no viable withdrawal path), XOXNO Lending mints protocol fees as `revenue` supply shares, but the only exit — `Pool::claim_revenue` — can revert permanently under ordinary market conditions. `accounting()` burns `min(cash, revenue_value)` shares from `supplied` (which *raises* utilization), then unconditionally enforces `require_utilization_below_max`. Since utilization drifts upward over time (borrow index grows faster than supply index), any market at/above `max_utilization` — or near it with a large revenue share — makes every claim revert, and the API offers no partial-claim parameter to route around it.

### Finding Description
- Fees from interest, flash loans, strategy openings and liquidations are minted as revenue via `accrue_revenue`, which increments both `revenue` and `supplied` (`contracts/pool/src/cache/shares.rs:36-39`, `contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:141-142`).
- `claim_revenue` calls `accounting`, which calls `burn_claimable_revenue()` — it always attempts the full `min(cash, floor(revenue_value))` and subtracts the burned shares from `supplied` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/revenue.rs:39-46`, `contracts/pool/src/cache/shares.rs:54-74`).
- After the burn, `guards::require_utilization_below_max` computes `ceil(borrowed·borrow_index) / floor(supplied·supply_index)` and reverts with `UtilizationAboveMax` if it exceeds `params.max_utilization` (`contracts/pool/src/guards.rs:19-34`).
- Burning revenue lowers `supplied` while `borrowed` is unchanged, so the act of claiming strictly raises measured utilization. Two lock-up modes result:
  1. Utilization already above `max_utilization` (reachable passively: `borrow_index` grows faster than `supply_index` since suppliers earn less than borrowers pay, and exits don't restore the buffer) — every claim reverts until debt is repaid or seized.
  2. Utilization below but near the cap — the full claim itself pushes the ratio over `max_utilization` and reverts; with no `amount` parameter on `claim_revenue`, there is no smaller claim to try.
- The controller path inherits this: `claim_revenue` → `claim_revenue_for_asset` → `pool_claim_revenue_call` propagates the revert, so revenue cannot be forwarded to the accumulator either (`contracts/controller/src/markets.rs:129-138`, `contracts/controller/src/markets.rs:184`).

### Impact Explanation
Permanent-to-indefinite freezing of unclaimed yield. Revenue minted into the market cannot be paid out while utilization stays at/above the cap, which can persist arbitrarily long in a heavily borrowed market. Unlike a normal supplier, the protocol cannot "withdraw less" — the claim is all-or-nothing — so there is no self-help path analogous to a partial withdrawal. Suppliers can still exit (withdraw skips nothing but is likewise gated), yet the protocol's yield is frozen by a state no single fee-recipient action can fix.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. No attacker action is required: interest accrual alone drives utilization monotonically upward in any market where `borrow_index` outpaces `supply_index` (always true at positive reserve factor). Once the ratio crosses `max_utilization` — or once revenue is a large enough share of `supplied` that its burn crosses the cap — the claim path reverts 100% of the time. Any unprivileged caller can confirm this by calling `controller::claim_revenue` / `pool::claim_revenue` and observing `UtilizationAboveMax`.

### Recommendation
Apply the utilization guard to the state *before* the revenue burn, or exempt `claim_revenue` from `require_utilization_below_max` (claiming revenue removes protocol-owned shares, not user backing, and reduces cash that the liquidation buffer — not the utilization gate — is meant to protect). Alternatively, accept an `amount` parameter on `claim_revenue` so a partial, utilization-safe claim is always reachable.

### Proof of Concept
On a pool market with `max_utilization < RAY::ONE`:

```rust
// setup
client.supply(&t.sup(0, 100_000_000_000i128));           // supplier deposits
client.borrow(&borrower, &t.bor(0, 90_000_000_000i128)); // borrow to ~90% util
t.advance_time(31_556_926);                              // 1 year
client.update_indexes(&vec![&t.env, hub(&t.asset)]);     // borrow_index compounds
                                                        // faster than supply_index
// utilization is now > max_utilization purely from accrual
let res = client.try_claim_revenue(&hub(&t.asset));
assert!(res.is_err()); // UtilizationAboveMax — revenue frozen
// Even below the cap: with revenue a large fraction of `supplied`,
// burn_claimable_revenue reduces `supplied` enough that the same guard reverts,
// and claim_revenue exposes no partial-amount argument to avoid it.
```
