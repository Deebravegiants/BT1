### Title
Emporium EIP712 signature authorizes only `(message, ops, maxFee, deadline)` but not the output recipient/amountChanges, letting an attacker front-run and steal the shielded value produced by a signer's wallet-call ops - ([File: contracts/external-actions/emporium/upgradeable/EmporiumUpgradeable.sol])

### Summary
`EmporiumUpgradeable.verifyWallet` authenticates an `EmporiumStack` via EIP712/ECDSA over only `circomData.emporiumMessage`, the hash of `stack.ops`, `stack.maxFee` and `stack.deadline` — never over `circomData.amountChanges`, `erc20TokenAddresses`, `stealthAddressStructure` or nullifiers. Any unprivileged actor who observes a signer's valid `(message, ops, v, r, s, maxFee, deadline)` (e.g., in a pending/public transaction) can resubmit the identical ops with their own `CircomData`/Groth16 proof, causing the signer's authorized wallet-call to execute while the resulting shielded output (and its value) is credited to the attacker's own `stealthAddressStructure` instead of the signer's.

### Finding Description
The broken equality: **"authorized-op-executor == authorized-value-recipient"** should hold, but does not. `EMPORIUM_SIGNATURE_TYPEHASH` binds signer `S`'s signature to `circomData.emporiumMessage`, `_hashEmporiumOps(stack.ops)`, `stack.maxFee`, `stack.deadline` only [1](#0-0) . Nothing in that signature or in `usedMessages[circomData.emporiumMessage]` ties it to a specific `stealthAddressStructure`, `amountChanges`, or `erc20TokenAddresses` [2](#0-1) .

`runAction` executes `stack.ops[i]` (identical regardless of who submits, since they're fixed by the signature) against `IHinkalWallet(stack.signerAddress)` for `CASE 1: op.invokeWallet` [3](#0-2) . Whatever balance increase those ops cause on the Emporium contract is then converted into an output `UTXO` addressed to `circomData.stealthAddressStructure` — a field fully controlled by whoever submits the transaction, not by signer `S`: [4](#0-3) .

`Hinkal.transact`'s balance-consistency check (`balanceDif == amountChanges[i] + utxoAmount`) only verifies that the submitter's *claimed* `amountChanges`/UTXO matches the *actual* on-chain balance change — it never checks that the party who caused the balance change (signer `S`, via their pre-signed ops) is the one receiving the shielded output [5](#0-4) . `performHinkalChecks`/`getSignedMessageHash`/`formBasicInput` likewise only ensure the *submitter's own* proof is internally self-consistent with their own `emporiumMessage`, `stealthAddressStructure`, nullifiers, etc. — none of that reconciles against `S`'s ECDSA-authorized `ops` [6](#0-5) .

Attacker's exact call: observe `S`'s pending/public `(message M, ops, v, r, s, maxFee, deadline)` (e.g., a not-yet-mined transaction, or one shared with a relay), build their own `CircomData` using their own Groth16 proof over their own UTXOs/nullifiers/`stealthAddressStructure`, set `circomData.emporiumMessage = M` and reuse the identical `stack.ops`/`maxFee`/`deadline` bytes so `verifyWallet`'s `ECDSA.tryRecover` still succeeds, and set `amountChanges` to match the balance increase the ops will actually cause. Submit with higher priority so it lands before `S`'s own transaction. `verifyWallet` consumes `usedMessages[M]`, executes `S`'s wallet-call ops (moving `S`'s own funds out of `S`'s `IHinkalWallet` into Emporium), and the resulting value is minted as a shielded UTXO under the attacker's `stealthAddressStructure`. `S`'s own subsequent submission then reverts with `UsedMessage`, having lost the funds the ops moved with no compensating shielded credit.

None of the existing guards (`performHinkalChecks`, `dimensionsCheck`, `checkOnchainCreation`, `rootHashExists`, `verifyProof`, the balance/slippage requires, `insertNullifiers`) prevent this because they all validate the *submitter's own* proof/circomData self-consistency — they have no mechanism to bind the ECDSA-authorized ops' execution to the ECDSA signer's own output ownership.

### Impact Explanation
Critical: direct theft of shielded value. A victim signer `S`'s pre-authorized wallet action (moving funds from `S`'s `IHinkalWallet` into the Emporium contract) can be hijacked by any unprivileged attacker holding an unrelated valid shielded-UTXO proof, redirecting the resulting shielded output to the attacker's own stealth address instead of `S`'s. This is repeatable for every signed `EmporiumStack` an attacker can observe before it lands on-chain, and requires no privileged role — only front-running/observing a pending signed payload.

### Likelihood Explanation
Preconditions: a signer `S` must have produced a valid `EmporiumStack` signature whose ops cause a net positive balance change on the Emporium contract (a common pattern for "withdraw yield/claim into wallet" style ops), and that signed payload/tx must be observable by the attacker before inclusion (standard front-running assumption, not a privileged relay compromise — any public mempool observation or a naive off-chain relay/API that broadcasts the signed payload before submission suffices). Attacker cost is one Groth16 proof over their own existing UTXOs plus gas to front-run. This is feasible and repeatable per occurrence of such signed emporium ops.

### Recommendation
Include the fields that determine value custody in the EIP712 signature scope, e.g. bind `EMPORIUM_SIGNATURE_TYPEHASH` (or a companion hash checked in `verifyWallet`) to `circomData.stealthAddressStructure`, `circomData.amountChanges`, and `circomData.erc20TokenAddresses` (or at minimum a commitment/hash of the full `circomData` context), so a valid signature can only be redeemed by the exact transaction context `S` intended, not replayed with attacker-substituted output recipients/amounts.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry fork test:
1. Deploy `Hinkal`, `EmporiumUpgradeable`, and a mock `IHinkalWallet` for signer `S` that, when called via `callHinkalWallet`, transfers `X` tokens to the Emporium contract (simulating `S`'s legitimate withdraw-to-Emporium action).
2. Have `S` sign a valid `EmporiumStack` (`message = M`, `ops = [opWithdrawX]`, `maxFee`, `deadline`) per `EMPORIUM_SIGNATURE_TYPEHASH`, intended to be submitted with `circomData_S` (S's own `stealthAddressStructure` and matching `amountChanges`).
3. Before `S`'s transaction is mined, have an unrelated attacker generate their own Groth16 proof over their own UTXOs/nullifiers, build `circomData_attacker` with `emporiumMessage = M`, identical `stack.ops`/`maxFee`/`deadline` bytes, but `stealthAddressStructure = attackerAddress` and `amountChanges` matching the predicted `X` balance increase, and call `Hinkal.transact` first.
4. Assert: `verifyWallet` passes (ECDSA recovers to `S`), `X` tokens leave `S`'s wallet into Emporium, and the resulting shielded UTXO commitment is inserted under the attacker's `stealthAddressStructure` (not `S`'s).
5. Assert `S`'s subsequent `transact` call with `circomData_S` reverts with `UsedMessage`, confirming `S` lost the funds moved by their own signed ops with no compensating shielded credit.
