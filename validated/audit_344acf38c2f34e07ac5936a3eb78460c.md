### Title
Emporium relay-fee token/recipient not bound by the wallet signer's EIP-712 signature, allowing unauthorised fee extraction from the Hinkal Wallet - (File: contracts/external-actions/emporium/upgradeable/EmporiumUpgradeable.sol)

### Summary
`EmporiumUpgradeable.verifyWallet` builds and checks an EIP-712 signature that only binds `emporiumMessage`, the hash of `stack.ops`, `stack.maxFee` and `stack.deadline`. It does not bind `circomData.relay` or `circomData.feeStructure.feeToken`, yet both fields are later used by `payRelayFees` → `sendToRelayFromWallet` → `IHinkalWallet(signerAddress).doSendToRelay(...)` to pull an arbitrary ERC-20 token out of the signer's `HinkalWallet` and send it to an arbitrary `relay` address.

### Finding Description
`verifyWallet` recovers the signer from: [1](#0-0) 

Note that `EMPORIUM_SIGNATURE_TYPEHASH` only covers `message`, `ops`, `maxFee`, `deadline`: [2](#0-1) 

The only constraint tying `feeStructure` to the signature is a raw numeric comparison, with no token binding: [3](#0-2) 

Later, `payRelayFees` uses `circomData.feeStructure.feeToken` and `circomData.relay` — fields that are part of `circomData` (crafted by whoever calls `runAction`/`transact`, i.e. the prover/relay submitting the transaction, not necessarily the wallet owner) — to call the wallet directly: [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

`HinkalWallet.doSendToRelay` performs no additional check besides `onlyEmporium`, so it fully trusts whatever `(relay, actualAmount, erc20TokenAddress)` Emporium passes in: [6](#0-5) 

Because `feeToken` and `relay` are outside the `signedMessageHash`, the wallet signer's authorization ("pay a flat fee of at most `maxFee`") is not tied to *which* ERC-20 asset is drained or *who* receives it. `maxFee` is a bare `uint256`, with no decimals/units context — the signer may have intended `maxFee` to denominate a stablecoin (e.g. 6-decimal USDC), but the entity submitting `circomData` (the prover/relay, who is untrusted with respect to the wallet's assets — that is exactly why the separate wallet signature exists) can set `feeStructure.feeToken` to any other ERC-20 the wallet happens to hold (e.g. a high-decimals/high-value token left over from a prior stateful op), still satisfying `flatFee <= maxFee` numerically while extracting a materially larger real value, and redirect it to an arbitrary `circomData.relay` address of their choosing.

This breaks the intended equality "wallet asset moved ⇒ authorised by signer's `signedMessageHash`": the value and destination actually moved from the wallet are not covered by what the signer cryptographically approved.

### Impact Explanation
This is theft of protocol/relay-fee-adjacent funds and, more importantly, of the wallet owner's own held ERC-20 balance beyond what they authorised — an unauthorised asset movement out of a user's `HinkalWallet` triggered by a party (the transaction submitter/prover) who never had the signer's key. Per the rules this maps to "High – executing calls or moving assets a wallet owner or prover never authorised," since the exact token/amount/recipient of the drained asset is decided by the un-trusted circomData author, not the signer.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitability requires: (1) a `HinkalWallet` holding more than one ERC-20 balance (realistic after any prior stateful `Emporium` operation deposits/receives tokens into the wallet), and (2) a caller of `runAction`/`transact` who is not the wallet owner but who is allowed to submit circomData/proof for a `stack.signerAddress` they don't control key-wise — which is the entire premise of the separate EIP-712 wallet-authorization design (the prover and the wallet signer are meant to be distinguishable parties). No special privilege is needed beyond being able to submit a valid transaction with a previously obtained valid signer signature over an ops/maxFee/deadline tuple; the attacker only needs freedom to choose `feeStructure.feeToken` and `circomData.relay` in the surrounding `circomData`, which is not constrained by that signature.

### Recommendation
Include `feeStructure.feeToken`, `feeStructure.flatFee` (already partly checked, but should be bound to token), and `relay` inside the `EMPORIUM_SIGNATURE_TYPEHASH` payload that the wallet signer signs, so the signature explicitly authorises the exact token and recipient the flat fee will be paid in/to, not just a bare numeric cap.

### Proof of Concept
1. Wallet `W` (owned by signer `S`) executes a prior Emporium op that leaves a balance of a valuable token `T2` in `W` (in addition to the intended fee token `T1`, e.g. USDC).
2. `S` signs an `EmporiumSignature` authorizing `ops`, `maxFee = 100`, `deadline`, expecting the fee to be paid in `T1` (a low-decimal/low-value stablecoin) since that's what was communicated off-chain, but `feeToken` is not part of the signed struct.
3. An unrelated party (the prover/relay submitting the `transact` call) crafts `circomData.feeStructure = {feeToken: T2, flatFee: 100, variableRate: ...}` and `circomData.relay = attackerRelay`, along with a matching SNARK proof for the shielded leg of the transaction (independent of the wallet's token choice, which is not covered by the circuit's constraints tying it to `S`'s intent).
4. `runAction` calls `verifyWallet`, which only checks `100 <= maxFee` — passes.
5. `payRelayFees` sees `erc20TokenAddress == feeStructure.feeToken (T2)`, sets `flatFee = 100`, and calls `payRelay` → `sendToRelayFromWallet` → `IHinkalWallet(S).doSendToRelay(attackerRelay, 100, T2)`.
6. `HinkalWallet.doSendToRelay` (only checks `onlyEmporium`) transfers 100 units of `T2` from `W` to `attackerRelay` — a token and amount the signer never explicitly bound in their signature, resulting in value extraction beyond the intended, lower-value fee token.
