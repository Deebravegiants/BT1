### Title
Flash-style oversized supply suppresses utilization and reprices the whole accrual interval, letting a borrower underpay interest and stripping supplier/protocol yield - (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary
XOXNO Lending has no P2P queue, but the same bug class — an unprivileged user forcing a worse rate for everyone else with a transient oversized supply — maps directly onto its rate engine. `accrue_step` recomputes `utilization` and the borrow rate from the **current** `supplied`/`borrowed` at sync time and applies that single rate retroactively over the entire elapsed interval since `last_timestamp`. An attacker who parks a huge supply across an accrual boundary forces the borrow rate toward `base_rate` for the whole gap, then withdraws. If the attacker holds a large debt, they pay a near-minimum rate on it while real suppliers and the protocol (`reserve_factor` fee) lose the interest differential.

### Finding Description
Every pool mutation and `update_indexes` starts with `synced_market` → `interest::global_sync`, which accrues from `last_timestamp` to now using the cache's live `supplied`/`borrowed` (contracts/pool/src/ops/mod.rs L30-33, contracts/pool/src/interest.rs L20-33). Inside `accrue_step`, utilization is computed from the *current* balances and the resulting `borrow_rate` is applied to the full `delta_ms` chunk (common/src/rates/simulate.rs L60-64).

`supply::apply` accrues first, then mints (contracts/pool/src/ops/supply.rs L23-36), and `withdraw::accounting` likewise accrues via `load_leg` before burning and debiting cash (contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs L63-79). Therefore the sequence:

1. Attacker `borrow`s (or already holds) a dominant share of market debt.
2. Attacker `supply`s an amount several times larger than total supply — minted at floor and fully collateralized by their own tokens. This accrues the old rate up to `t1`.
3. On the next ledger(s), the attacker calls `withdraw` (or anyone calls `update_indexes`). `global_sync` accrues `t1 → t2` at the utilization implied by the inflated supply — i.e., `borrowed_original / supplied_original ≈ 0` → `calculate_borrow_rate` returns roughly `base_rate`.
4. The withdraw then burns the attacker's shares and pays out principal plus the (tiny) accrued supply interest.

Interest for the gap is `borrowed × (borrow_index' − borrow_index)` (common/src/rates/simulate.rs L66-69, docs math L236-237), so both the supplier rewards and the `reserve_factor` protocol fee for the entire interval are computed at the suppressed rate. The loss is socialized across all suppliers and the revenue account exactly like the Morpho `p2pBorrowRate`/`shareOfDelta` socialization.

The only friction is `require_utilization_below_max` on non-liquidation withdrawals (contracts/pool/src/guards.rs L19-34): pulling the huge supply back out can push post-state utilization above `max_utilization`. The attacker can (a) partially withdraw down to the cap and repeat, or (b) target a market where `max_utilization >= RAY`, which disables the check entirely (README notes setting it to `RAY` turns the cap off). `require_reserves` is satisfied because the cash withdrawn is the attacker's own deposit.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of unclaimed yield: for every ledger interval the oversized supply is parked, the market's borrow index grows at ~`base_rate` instead of the curve rate, so all suppliers and the protocol's revenue shares lose `(curve_rate − base_rate) × borrowed × interval` of accrual. Symmetrically, a borrower who holds most of the debt underpays interest by the same amount — a direct profit at suppliers' and the protocol's expense. Repeating the park across ledgers compounds the loss. There is no recovered cost or fee on the attacker's supply that compensates for this repricing.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by any unprivileged address via `supply` and `withdraw` (through the controller). Requirements: capital large relative to market supply (to push utilization near zero) and a debt position large enough that the interest saving exceeds the (near-zero at low utilization) opportunity cost of the parked supply plus gas. The attack is most damaging on stale markets and on markets configured with `max_utilization == RAY` (no exit cap). It cannot be done atomically — at least one ledger boundary must elapse for accrual to tick — which raises the capital cost but does not prevent the rate suppression. Medium severity: real yield theft, but bounded per interval and capital-gated.

### Recommendation
Decouple rate computation from instantaneous balances, e.g.:
- Accrue in bounded chunks (e.g. per-ledger or per-minute `MAX_COMPOUND_DELTA_MS`-style sub-chunks) and/or compute utilization on a TWAP/time-weighted supply rather than the point-in-time `supplied` value in `accrue_step`.
- Alternatively, weight utilization by time-at-balance (track a running `supplied × time` integral) so a supply parked for one interval only suppresses the rate for that interval, not retroactively — this is already partially true, so the stronger fix is to charge borrows a rate floor during intervals where utilization moved materially within the accrual window.
- At minimum, document that `base_rate` is the effective floor an attacker can force and size `max_utilization` and reserve parameters with this manipulation in mind; avoid `max_utilization >= RAY` on markets with material debt.

### Proof of Concept
1. Market M: `supplied = S`, `borrowed = B` with `B/S` near optimal; attacker holds debt `≈ B` (borrowed earlier or via `borrow` against their own collateral in another asset).
2. Attacker calls controller `supply` with `amount ≈ k·S` (k large). `supply::apply` accrues at the old utilization, mints `k·S` shares to the attacker, credits cash.
3. Wait one ledger. Anyone's next interaction (or attacker's own `withdraw`) runs `global_sync`: `accrue_step` computes `utilization = B / (S + k·S) ≈ 0`, so `calculate_borrow_rate` ≈ `base_rate`, and the whole inter-ledger `delta_ms` accrues at that floor.
4. Attacker calls `withdraw(amount = i128::MAX)` (or partial down to `max_utilization`). Interest owed on their debt for the gap is priced at `base_rate`; supplier rewards and `revenue` mint are correspondingly reduced by `(rate_optimal − base_rate) × B × delta_ms`.
5. Repeat 2–4 each ledger to keep the market repriced at the floor while the debt remains open.
