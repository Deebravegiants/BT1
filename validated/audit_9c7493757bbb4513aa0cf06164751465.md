### Title
Interest accrual overflows `i128` before the borrow-index cap engages, permanently freezing the market - (File: contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs)

### Summary
Like CVE-2017-7227, where a malformed input drives the linker into an unrecoverable crash, the pool's accrual path evaluates a RAY-scaled multiply that can exceed `i128` before the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` ceiling is applied. An unprivileged user can steer a listed market into that overflow domain; since every pool verb accrues first, the market is permanently bricked — no supply, borrow, repay, withdraw, or liquidation ever succeeds again.

### Finding Description
The pool compounds the borrow index in fixed point at millisecond granularity inside the market cache (`contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs`, `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs`; helpers in `common/src/math/fp_core.rs`). The accrual computes the new index as a RAY-scaled product of the current index and the rate factor, and the unscaling path (`scaled_to_original`-style `x * y / d`) evaluates `borrow_index * scaled_amount` in `i128`. The cap `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` is only enforced on the resulting index, but the multiply that produces/uses it overflows `i128` first and panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow`. Because `Context`/cache accrual runs at the top of every state-changing path (`supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `update_indexes`), once the stored `borrow_index` and scaled balances are large enough that the product exceeds `i128::MAX`, every subsequent call reverts — the panic is deterministic and state-derived, so no transaction can escape it.

The committed regression test demonstrates the exact reachable shape: a whale supplies a billion-unit 18-decimal market, an account borrows at ~98% utilization on a steep rate curve, and after sustained accrual `try_update_indexes` returns `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` — i.e., the overflow fires before the index cap can clamp it — and both `withdraw` and `repay` then fail with the same error (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-360`).

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds and protocol insolvency vector. Once the market crosses the `i128` accrual ceiling: suppliers cannot withdraw (`try_withdraw_raw` → `MathOverflow`), borrowers cannot repay (`try_repay` → `MathOverflow`), liquidations cannot execute, and `update_indexes` itself reverts, so nothing can ever roll the index back or fix the state. All supplied collateral and debt in that market is stuck forever, and the controller paths that touch the market are unusable. Unlike a bounded revert, this is a persistent, unrecoverable trap baked into stored state.

### Likelihood Explanation
The trigger needs only unprivileged entrypoints: `supply` (to inflate the scaled book), `borrow` at high utilization, and time/accrual ticks via `update_indexes`. The honest requirements are a large token holding (mitigated by supply caps, but caps are per-listing config and can be lifted or set high, as the test does via `lift_caps`) and sustained high utilization on a steep rate segment. No privileged access, oracle manipulation, or external dependency is required. The main mitigations are the size of the required position and the accrual horizon, so this is High-impact / Medium-likelihood rather than a free exploit.

### Recommendation
Order the index cap before the overflowing multiplication: clamp `borrow_index` to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` inside the accrual math itself (saturating rather than panicking), and perform `scaled_to_original`/accrual products through the `I256`-widened path or a saturating check before multiplying index by scaled amounts. Also consider capping effective utilization (`max_utilization`) so the compounding term cannot run away, and make `update_indexes` able to clamp-and-recover rather than panic so a market near the ceiling remains live.

### Proof of Concept
The committed harness test is the PoC (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360`): build an 18-decimal market on the XLM curve with caps lifted, `supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", BILLION * 10^18)`, `borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", 98%)`, then advance ledger time in yearly steps. `try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"])` eventually fails with `errors::MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and afterwards `try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1)` and `try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0)` both revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW` — the market is frozen.
