### Title
Attacker plants a dust collateral leg backed by a stale/unreliable price feed to make their own account unliquidatable and uncleanable — (`contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs`)

### Summary
XOXNO Lending's `supply` path admits a new collateral leg under permissive (non-strict) price handling, while every risk-critical operation — `liquidate`, `withdraw`, `borrow`, and `clean_bad_debt` — requires a strict, fresh price for **every** supply position on the account via `Context::cached_price`. A single unprivileged borrower can therefore add a dust-sized leg of an asset whose oracle feed is stale or prone to staleness, and thereafter any attempt to liquidate or clean that account reverts with `PriceFeedStale`. The account's debt keeps accruing while it cannot be liquidated, shifting losses onto suppliers.

### Finding Description
`process_supply` → `process_deposit` runs `validate_position_entry_gates` and then `get_or_create_supply_position` for each leg (`contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs:100-135`). Supply-side entry does not demand a strict fresh price: the harness test `test_missing_twap_history_allows_permissive_supply_fallback` confirms supply tolerates missing/weak price history, and `audit_supply_setup_blocks_liquidation_via_stale_dust_leg` shows a `supply` of a dust amount of `WBTC` succeeds even while that feed is already stale (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_supply_stale_shield.rs:26-31`).

In contrast, `calculate_account_risk_totals_body` iterates **all** `supply_positions` and calls `cache.cached_price(&hub_asset.asset)` (`contracts/controller/src/risk/totals.rs:171-186`), and `Context`-cached prices are strict/fail-closed per INV-ORACLE-03 (`docs/reference/invariants.md:320-328`). So one unpriceable dust leg poisons the entire account's risk computation:

- `liquidate` → `PriceFeedStale` (`audit_supply_stale_shield.rs:51-52`)
- `clean_bad_debt` → `PriceFeedStale` (`audit_supply_stale_shield.rs:54-55`); INV-LIQ-04 confirms cleanup also requires valid prices for all positions (`docs/reference/invariants.md:463-469`)
- `withdraw` (even of the poison leg itself is blocked while debt exists, since withdrawal re-proves the account) → `PriceFeedStale` (`audit_supply_stale_shield.rs:57-58`)

The owner cannot remove the leg either: `withdraw` on the dust leg runs the same strict risk path, and there is no force-remove. Third parties cannot help — `repay` is permissionless but liquidation and cleanup stay blocked. The only exits are the feed recovering or the timelocked owner path `force_socialize_bad_debt`, which also requires prices for every position (`docs/reference/runbooks/force-socialize-bad-debt.md:34-35`), so even governance cleanup is bricked while the feed is stale.

Attack path (single unprivileged address):
1. `supply(caller, 0, spoke_id, [(USDC, big), (FRAGILE, dust)])` — creates an account with a dust leg in a listed market whose feed is stale or intermittently stale.
2. `borrow(caller, account_id, [(ETH, max)])` — borrow up to LTV.
3. Collateral price drops; HF < 1. While `FRAGILE`'s feed is stale, every `liquidate` and `clean_bad_debt` reverts, so the debt is unenforcable.

A borrower who already holds a healthy account can add the poison leg defensively at any time before going underwater.

### Impact Explanation
While the feed is stale the account is completely unliquidatable and cannot be cleaned or force-socialized (all strict-price paths revert). The attacker retains borrowed assets; if collateral value falls below debt during the window, the resulting bad debt is borne by the market's suppliers via the supply-index write-down — theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. At minimum it is a temporary freezing of the liquidation mechanism for that account with no permissionless workaround, since the poison leg cannot be removed by anyone while debt exists.

### Likelihood Explanation
The cost is one dust supply in any listed asset — essentially free. The trigger (a listed feed going stale) is outside the attacker's control, but listed assets can have thin, single-source, or rarely-updated feeds, and the attacker chooses the weakest listing. The bug is reachable through normal entrypoints (`supply`, `borrow`) with no privileged action, and once planted there is no permissionless recovery path — only feed recovery unblocks liquidation. Severity: High (conditional insolvency vector; rated Medium if feeds are assumed reliable).

### Recommendation
- Enforce a strict, fresh price at `supply` time for any new supply slot (reject supply of an asset whose feed is stale/invalid), so a leg that can poison the account's risk path cannot be created.
- Alternatively, value an unpriceable supply leg at 0 in `calculate_account_risk_totals_body` instead of aborting the whole computation, and treat its seizure leg as zero (dropped) in liquidation planning.
- Provide an owner path to withdraw/forfeit an unpriceable leg, or let `clean_bad_debt`/`force_socialize_bad_debt` skip legs whose price is unavailable rather than reverting.

### Proof of Concept
Pinned by the existing harness tests:

- `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_supply_stale_shield.rs:26-58`: `supply(ALICE, "WBTC", 0.001)` succeeds on a stale feed; afterwards `try_liquidate`, `try_clean_bad_debt_by_id`, and `try_withdraw` all revert `PRICE_FEED_STALE`, while a twin account without the leg liquidates normally.
- `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_liquidate_and_clean_stale_leg.rs:36-47`: same revert shape; identical liquidation succeeds once the feed refreshes, proving the leg — not the account's risk — is the blocker.
- `contracts/controller/src/risk/totals.rs:171-186`: every supply position goes through `cache.cached_price`, and a missing/invalid price fails closed, aborting the whole totals computation.
