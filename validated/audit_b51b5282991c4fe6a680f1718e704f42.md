### Title
Scaled-debt value overflows `i128` before the borrow-index cap engages, permanently freezing a market - (File: `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs`)

### Summary
`global_sync` accrues compound interest in chunked steps via `accrue_step` on every pool interaction. On a whale-scale market at sustained high utilization, the RAY-scaled borrowed value (`scaled_amount * index`) exceeds `i128::MAX` inside `scaled_to_original` before the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` index cap can halt growth. Every accrual then panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow`, and because every verb accrues first, the market is permanently frozen: no `repay`, no `withdraw`, no `liquidate`, no `clean_bad_debt`.

### Finding Description
`global_sync` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs` loops `accrue_chunk` over elapsed time, writing back `step.borrow_index`/`step.supply_index` unconditionally. The fixed-point engine in `common/src/math/fp_core.rs` widens products to `I256` for mul/div, but `scaled_to_original`-style unscaling converts the result back to `i128` and panics (`MathOverflow`) when the represented value no longer fits — i.e. when `borrowed_scaled * borrow_index` crosses the RAY value ceiling. The index cap (`MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`) exists but only bounds the index, not the scaled-value product, so a large enough `borrowed_scaled` (whale principal at ~98% utilization on a steep rate curve) overflows the product while the index is still below the cap. The repo's own harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs`) demonstrates the cliff, notes the documented bound in `docs/reference/formulas.md` is wrong, and confirms `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` both revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW` afterward.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds. Once the scaled-debt product crosses the `i128` ceiling, accrual panics on every subsequent call, and since `global_sync` runs at the top of every state-changing path, suppliers can never withdraw, borrowers can never repay, and liquidators can never liquidate that market. All supplied liquidity and collateral tied to the market is bricked, and bad debt cannot be cleaned.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by unprivileged actors: an attacker (or coordinated whales) supplies a large principal and borrows ~98% of it via `controller::supply`/`controller::borrow`, then leaves the position to accrue. The trigger requires a whale-scale market and sustained high utilization, so it is conditional rather than instant, but it needs no privilege, no oracle manipulation, and no cooperation — just capital and time. There is no recovery path since the panic is deterministic in stored state.

### Recommendation
Cap accrual safely before the scaled value can overflow: clamp `borrow_index`/`supply_index` growth in `accrue_step` such that `max_scaled * index` stays below `i128::MAX`, or clamp/saturate `scaled_to_original` instead of panicking when the represented value exceeds the ceiling. Alternatively, enforce a per-market `borrowed_scaled_ray` ceiling (in `SpokeUsageContext::apply_entry`-style cap checks) chosen so that `cap * MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` always fits `i128`, making the overflow state unreachable regardless of utilization history.

### Proof of Concept
1. Register an 18-decimal market with a steep interest curve and lift supply/borrow caps.
2. `supply(principal = 1e9 * 1e18)` from one user; `supply` collateral and `borrow(0.98 * principal)` from another.
3. Advance ledger time in yearly steps and call `update_indexes`.
4. Within the bound demonstrated by `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs`, `accrue_step` recomputes the scaled debt and the product `borrowed_scaled * borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`; `update_indexes` reverts with `Error(Contract, #33)` (`MathOverflow`).
5. `withdraw(1)`, `repay`, and `liquidate` on that market all revert identically forever — the panic precedes any state update.
