The test file points to a strong candidate: an unprivileged-triggered permanent market freeze via overflow in `scaled_to_original` during accrual. Let me verify the root cause in pool code.### Title
Unprivileged permanent market freeze via i128 overflow in `scaled_to_original` during accrual — borrow index cap never engages - (File: contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs)

### Summary
A lending market whose scaled debt is large and whose utilization stays high permanently freezes: once the RAY value `borrowed * borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX`, every operation that accrues interest (`supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `update_indexes`, `claim_revenue`) panics with `MathOverflow` inside `scaled_to_original`. The `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap that was intended to bound index growth is checked too late — the value ceiling is hit first. The analog to CVE-2023-37015 is a remotely-triggered assertion that bricks the component; here the trigger is attacker-shaped state (large supply + max-size borrow + letting accrual run) rather than a malformed packet.

### Finding Description
`Cache::calculate_utilization` and every unscale path in `contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:23-24` call `scaled_to_original(&self.env, self.borrowed, self.borrow_index)`, which multiplies the RAY-scaled share total by the RAY index and panics (`GenericError::MathOverflow`) when the product exceeds `i128::MAX`. This multiplication runs inside `accrue_step` / `global_sync` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33`), which is invoked at the top of every mutating pool verb before any state change. A whale book is enormously sensitive here: 1 billion 18-decimal tokens is `1e36` raw RAY shares, and `i128::MAX` is only ~170x that, so the panic lands while `borrow_index` is still far below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. The harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-360`) demonstrates the end state explicitly: after the index reaches ~170x, `update_indexes`, `withdraw` and `repay` all revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and `last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` proves the cap never fires.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds for the entire market: suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay (so their collateral elsewhere is locked too), liquidators cannot close underwater positions, and protocol revenue is unclaimable. There is no privileged rescue path — the panic occurs in shared accrual, so even governance-driven operations on the same market book fail. This matches the "permanent freezing of funds" and "contract unable to operate" acceptance categories.

### Likelihood Explanation
No oracle manipulation, timing race, or privilege is needed — only time and size. An attacker seeds a market with a very large deposit (cheap if done via a hub/spoke book on a low-value or attacker's-own listed asset, or in any market with ≥18 decimals and loose caps), borrows near the cap to pin utilization on the steep part of the curve, and simply waits while `update_indexes` is periodically callable by anyone. The freeze is self-reinforcing: once utilization is stuck high, the index compounds at near-max rate. The test shows the cliff arrives in well under 40 simulated years; at 98% utilization on the XLM curve it is reached in a handful of years, and for an attacker who can over-fund the book (caps permitting) the required index multiple shrinks proportionally, since the ceiling is on the product, not the index alone. Severity: High.

### Recommendation
Check the index against `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` *before* the value multiplication, or perform the `borrowed × borrow_index` product with saturating/checked arithmetic inside `accrue_step` and clamp the index at the cap instead of overflowing. Additionally, reorder `accrue_chunk` so the cap is applied to the projected index (e.g., compute `step.borrow_index.min(MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY)` before any downstream `scaled_to_original`), and add a regression test asserting markets remain operable at the cap. Optionally bound `scaled × index` totals via a tighter per-market `supply_cap` expressed in RAY-value terms rather than raw units, since the cliff depends on decimals.

### Proof of Concept
Reproduced by the existing harness test at `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361`:

1. List market `BIG18` (18 decimals, XLM steep curve, `max_utilization` disabled) and collateral `COL`; lift caps via `edit_asset_in_spoke_caps`.
2. `BOB.supply("BIG18", 1_000_000_000 * 10^18)` — `1e36` raw RAY shares.
3. `ALICE.supply("COL", ...)` then `ALICE.borrow("BIG18", 0.98 * principal)` — pins utilization at 98%.
4. Loop `env.advance_time(YEAR_SECS)` and call `update_indexes("BIG18")`. Once `borrowed * borrow_index > i128::MAX` (index ≈ 170x, a few years on this curve), the call reverts with `MathOverflow` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
5. Thereafter `withdraw(BOB, "BIG18", 1)` and `repay(ALICE, "BIG18", ...)` both revert with `MathOverflow` — the market is permanently frozen for all participants.
