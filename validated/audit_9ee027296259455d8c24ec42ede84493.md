### Title
Unprivileged dust top-up force-restamps cached LTV/LT without the health-factor floor, letting an attacker push a victim under water and liquidate them - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs)

### Summary
Any unprivileged address may call `controller::supply` to top up an existing supply position on a foreign account. Every supply leg passes through `merge_supply_leg`, which unconditionally calls `refresh_supply_risk_params` with `RiskRefreshScope::FullTuple` — overwriting the position's cached loan-to-value and liquidation threshold with the *current* listing values. The dedicated restamp entrypoint `update_account_threshold` (with `has_risks`) is guarded by a post-update health-factor floor (reverts unless the account clears 1.05); the supply path has no such gate. After governance tightens a listing's risk parameters, an attacker can supply dust to a victim's existing collateral leg, force the stricter parameters onto the victim's account, and immediately liquidate them for the bonus.

### Finding Description
`process_supply` in `contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs:40-74` allows third-party callers as long as every paid asset already exists as a supply position on the target account — enforced by `require_third_party_existing_supply` at `supply.rs:78-97`. Note the check only requires the *position key* to exist; the amount may be dust.

`process_deposit` → `merge_supply_leg` (`supply.rs:275-324`) then calls `refresh_supply_risk_params` with `RiskRefreshScope::FullTuple` for each leg before writing the position back via `update_or_remove_supply_position`. There is no subsequent solvency/health-factor check on the supply path (`finalize_position_flow` is invoked with `PositionSides::Supply` at `supply.rs:65-72`).

By contrast, the documented behavior of `update_account_threshold` is that a full-risk refresh "reverts unless the account clears the update health-factor floor, so it cannot be used to push an account into liquidation" (scripts/permissionless_entrypoints.txt, controller::update_account_threshold row). The supply top-up bypasses exactly this protection.

This is not hypothetical: `contracts/controller/tests/spoke.rs` and `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/security_audit.rs:507-532` (`poc_third_party_top_up_force_restamps_ltv`) demonstrate that a third-party top-up of an existing leg restamps the cached LTV to the currently listed value and immediately reduces the borrowable headroom of the foreign account.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds via forced liquidation. Cached per-position LTV/threshold drive the account's health factor, which is computed from `Context`-cached strict prices and indexes. If governance ever lowers a collateral's `loan_to_value`/`liquidation_threshold` (a routine risk tightening), every account holding that collateral keeps the *old, looser* cached parameters until a keeper restamps them. An attacker can pick the timing: supply 1 wei to the victim's existing leg, stamp the stricter threshold, and — if the victim's HF drops below 1 — call `liquidate` in the same or next transaction, seizing collateral with the HF-based bonus. The victim loses collateral value equal to the liquidation bonus even though their position was healthy under the parameters they borrowed against.

### Likelihood Explanation
High feasibility, conditional trigger. Requirements: (1) a listed collateral whose risk parameters are tightened by governance — a normal operational event; (2) a victim whose account is healthy under the old parameters but unhealthy under the new ones; (3) an existing supply position on that account in the asset, which `require_third_party_existing_supply` requires — satisfied whenever the victim supplied that collateral. Cost to the attacker is one dust deposit plus a liquidation call; both `supply` and `liquidate` are permissionless (`caller-auth`). No price manipulation, privileged role, or leaked key is needed. The window exists for every account between a governance parameter tightening and its keeper restamp — the attacker simply front-runs the benign restamp and converts it into a liquidation trigger that `update_account_threshold`'s HF floor was designed to prevent.

### Recommendation
Apply the same post-condition as `update_account_threshold` to third-party supply top-ups: in `merge_supply_leg`/`process_supply`, when the caller is not the account owner or an active delegate, either skip `refresh_supply_risk_params` (or restrict to `RiskRefreshScope` LTV-only-equivalent restamping that cannot change the liquidation path), or enforce the update health-factor floor (HF ≥ 1.05) after the restamp, so a foreign top-up cannot push an account into liquidation eligibility. Alternatively, gate `require_third_party_existing_supply` top-ups to a `RiskRefreshScope` that cannot raise the victim's risk parameters.

### Proof of Concept
Sequence (mirrors `poc_third_party_top_up_force_restamps_ltv` in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/security_audit.rs`):

```text
1. Victim supplies 10,000 USDC (listed with LT = 5500 bps) and borrows to
   HF ≈ 1.02 under the cached parameters.
2. Governance executes a listing update lowering USDC liquidation_threshold
   to 5000 bps and loan_to_value to 5000 bps.
3. Attacker calls controller::supply(caller=attacker, account_id=victim_id,
   spoke_id, assets=[(USDC_hub_key, 1)]).
   - require_third_party_existing_supply passes: victim already holds a USDC
     supply position (supply.rs:86-96).
   - merge_supply_leg → refresh_supply_risk_params(FullTuple) restamps the
     victim's cached LT to 5000 bps (supply.rs:288-296).
   - No health-factor floor is evaluated on the supply path.
4. Victim's recomputed HF < 1 with unchanged debt and prices.
5. Attacker calls controller::liquidate(attacker, victim_id, debt_asset, x)
   and seizes collateral with the liquidation bonus.
```

Confirm with the existing harness: extend `poc_third_party_top_up_force_restamps_ltv` by asserting that after `t.edit_asset_config("USDC", |c| { c.loan_to_value = 5_000; c.liquidation_threshold = 5_000; })`, `t.try_supply_to_account(BOB, ALICE, "USDC", 1.0)` succeeds and `t.assert_liquidatable(ALICE)` then holds for a position that was healthy at HF < the new threshold but ≥ the old one — while the equivalent `update_account_threshold` call with `has_risks` reverts.
