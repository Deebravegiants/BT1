### Title
Relayer fee rewards become permanently unclaimable when the delivering address is a smart contract - (File: `modules/pallets/relayer/src/withdrawal.rs`)

### Summary
`pallet-ismp-relayer` accumulates delivery fees keyed by the raw address recorded as the delivering relayer on the destination chain's receipt trie (`RequestReceipts`/`ResponseReceipts`). Message delivery through `HandlerV2` is permissionless — "these proofs can be generated by anyone" [1](#0-0)  — so the address attributed as "relayer" can be any caller, including a smart contract with no private key. `withdraw_fees` requires a cryptographic signature (EVM secp256k1 recovery, Sr25519, or Ed25519) that must resolve to exactly that same address before the accumulated fee balance can be paid out. A contract address can never produce such a signature, so fees accrued to it are permanently stuck in the pallet, structurally identical to BalancedVault being unable to claim Incentivizer rewards because it never implements a claim path.

### Finding Description
Fee accumulation (`Pallet::accumulate` / `accumulate_fee_and_deposit_event`) credits `Fees::<T>` under the `delivery_address` decoded from the destination chain's receipt storage via `decode_receipt_relayer`, which for EVM sources RLP-decodes whatever 20-byte address is stored in the `RequestReceipts` slot [2](#0-1) , and for substrate sources decodes raw signer bytes [3](#0-2) . This address is not validated as an EOA at accumulation time — the pallet simply trusts whatever address the destination chain recorded as having delivered the message [4](#0-3) .

Withdrawal, in `Pallet::withdraw`, then strictly requires the caller to sign a `(nonce, dest_chain, beneficiary?)` message and recover/verify that signature against the very same `address` that fees were accumulated under [5](#0-4) . There is no admin override, no beneficiary-redirect path usable without first producing a valid signature from the delivery address itself (the `beneficiary_details` redirect in `accumulate` also requires a signature matching `delivery_address` [6](#0-5) ), and no alternate claim interface at all.

Message delivery to `EvmHost`/`HandlerV2` is unpermissioned by design — anyone (an EOA or a contract) can submit the proof and become the attributed relayer, and the protocol explicitly documents this as open to any party [1](#0-0) . If a smart contract (e.g. an automation/keeper bot, a relayer-as-a-service smart wallet, a multisig executor, or simply a misconfigured integration) ends up as the caller recorded in the destination receipt, the fee that hyperbridge subsequently accumulates for that address is permanently unwithdrawable: secp256k1/Sr25519/Ed25519 signature verification can never produce a valid signature "from" a contract address with no private key, so `withdraw_fees` will always fail with `Error::InvalidPublicKey`/`InvalidSignature`.

This mirrors the reported bug class exactly: a party (here, a smart-contract-based relayer) legitimately and automatically accrues rewards through the protocol's normal incentive-accumulation flow, but the protocol provides no way for that party to actually claim what it has accrued, permanently trapping the funds in escrow.

### Impact Explanation
Funds are the protocol's own escrowed relayer-fee revenue (fees paid by users/apps for message delivery, later disbursed from the destination `HostManager`/EvmHost balance or via `WithdrawRelayerFees` on substrate). Once fees accumulate under a non-EOA delivery address, they are permanently frozen with no recovery mechanism — this is a permanent freezing-of-funds condition satisfying the "concrete... permanent freezing of funds" acceptance criterion. As more automated/smart-contract-based relaying becomes common (which the protocol explicitly permits since delivery is open to anyone), the amount of stuck relayer revenue grows monotonically and irreversibly.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate-to-high: nothing in `HandlerV2`/`EvmHost` restricts message delivery to EOAs, and relaying is explicitly documented as open to any submitter. Contract-mediated relaying (bots, smart-contract wallets, automation frameworks, MEV/keeper infrastructure) is a normal and expected pattern in this ecosystem, so a smart contract becoming the attributed "relayer" for at least some deliveries is a realistic, unprivileged, single-transaction occurrence rather than a contrived edge case.

### Recommendation
Add a claim/redirect mechanism that does not require the delivery address itself to produce a signature — e.g., allow the destination-chain contract that was recorded as relayer to itself call an authenticated interface (an `IApp`-style `onAccept`/callback that hyperbridge can dispatch to, similar to how `HostManager.withdraw` is invoked), or require delivery attribution to register a beneficiary EOA at delivery time, or restrict/validate that the attributed relayer address must be capable of producing a signature (e.g., reject/flag contract-code addresses during accumulation) before crediting `Fees`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy a minimal smart contract wrapper (no owner-controlled signing capability) that calls `HandlerV2.handlePostRequests` (or `handleGetResponses`) to deliver a fee-bearing ISMP message to a destination `EvmHost`. The destination chain's `RequestReceipts`/`ResponseReceipts` trie records this contract's address as the delivering relayer.
2. A relayer (anyone) submits `accumulate_fees` with state proofs of the source request and the destination receipt as in `modules/pallets/relayer/src/accumulate.rs`; `Fees::<T>::get(state_machine, contract_address)` is credited with the message's fee, per `accumulate_fee_and_deposit_event` [7](#0-6) .
3. Attempt `withdraw_fees` with any `WithdrawalInputData` targeting `contract_address`: no valid `Signature::Evm`/`Sr25519`/`Ed25519` can ever recover to `contract_address` because it has no private key, so `Pallet::withdraw` unconditionally returns `Error::InvalidSignature`/`InvalidPublicKey` at the verification step [8](#0-7) .
4. The credited `Fees::<T>` entry for `contract_address` remains non-zero indefinitely; there is no other pallet call that can zero it out or redirect it, confirming permanent freezing of the accumulated fee.

	pub fn decode_receipt_relayer(state_id: StateMachine, raw: &[u8]) -> Result<Vec<u8>, Error<T>> {

				Ok(Address::decode(&mut &*raw)
					.map_err(|_| Error::<T>::ProofValidationError)?
					.0
					.to_vec())
