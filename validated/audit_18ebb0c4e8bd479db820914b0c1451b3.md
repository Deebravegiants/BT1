Based on my investigation, I found the key vulnerability: `pallet-intents-coprocessor`'s `place_bid`/`retract_bid` flow lets a filler unilaterally reclaim (unreserve) the deposit that is supposed to back a live bid, while that bid can still be selected and win the order — an exact analog of "position owner withdraws collateral without closing the position."

### Title
Filler can retract a bid's deposit while the bid remains selectable and can still win the order, decoupling the escrowed collateral from the live obligation - (File: `modules/pallets/intents-coprocessor/src/lib.rs`)

### Summary
`pallet-intents-coprocessor::place_bid` reserves a `StorageDepositFee` from a filler as the economic backing for a bid recorded in `Bids<T>` (commitment, filler) → deposit, and `retract_bid` immediately unreserves and deletes that record on the filler's own signed call, with no check that the bid has been "closed" (i.e., that it can no longer be selected/won). Because bid selection on the EVM side (`IntentGatewayV2.select`/`fillOrder`, `SolverAccount`) operates off a `UserOperation` signed by the filler and stored off-chain/in the coprocessor independently of `Bids<T>`'s liveness, a filler can retract (withdraw) its on-chain deposit at any time — including in the same block a user is about to select it, or after being selected but before the fill lands — and its bid remains fulfillable exactly as reported to the user/coprocessor.

### Finding Description
`place_bid` reserves `StorageDepositFee` and inserts `Bids::<T>::insert(&commitment, &filler, deposit)` [1](#0-0) . `retract_bid` unconditionally unreserves the deposit and removes the bid the moment the filler calls it — the only check is that a bid entry exists for `(commitment, filler)`; there is no check against order status (filled/expired/selected) or against a "cannot retract once selected" state: [2](#0-1) 

This deposit is documented as the thing that "backs" the bid/quote presented to users and coprocessor consumers ("A successful bid locks a deposit only reclaimable by retraction") [3](#0-2) . However, nothing in the fill path (`IntentGatewayV2.fillOrder`, `SolverAccount`/`select`) or in `retract_bid` re-checks `Bids<T>` liveness before honoring a `UserOperation` signature — the `UserOperation` itself, once signed and posted, is valid independent of whether its underlying `Bids<T>` deposit still exists [4](#0-3) . The `select`/`fillOrder` authorization check only verifies the signer/session match, not that a live deposit still exists on Hyperbridge: [5](#0-4) 

So a filler can: (1) `place_bid` to get counted/displayed as a backed quote, (2) get selected by the user/session key, (3) call `retract_bid` to reclaim its deposit before the fill transaction lands, all while the previously-signed `UserOperation` remains valid and fillable — exactly mirroring the Perennial bug where a position owner withdraws backing collateral while the risk-bearing position (here, the selected bid) stays open.

### Impact Explanation
The `StorageDepositFee` is the only economic bond discouraging spam/bad-faith bids and (per the docs) the thing users/coprocessor rely on as "locked" for a live bid. If a filler can withdraw it at will regardless of selection state, the deposit provides no assurance at the exact moment it matters (post-selection, pre-fill), turning what is advertised as a collateralized, accountable bid into a risk-free/unbacked one. This is a moderate-severity bookkeeping/incentive-integrity break in the solver-selection auction rather than a direct fund-theft path, since actual settlement funds move through the separate `IntentGatewayV2` escrow, but it defeats the deposit-backed guarantee the auction economics depend on and could be leveraged to grief users (bidding without real skin in the game) or to bypass any future slashing tied to the deposit.

### Likelihood Explanation
High likelihood of occurrence given normal operation: `retract_bid` is a permissionless, unconditional signed extrinsic available to any filler at any time, and the existing SDK/simplex filler code already calls it opportunistically (e.g., batched retract+re-bid) with no on-chain barrier tied to selection state [6](#0-5) .

### Recommendation
Add a liveness/selection check to `retract_bid` (or a separate lock mechanism) so a bid cannot be retracted once it has been selected/authorized for fill (e.g., track a "selected" flag set when `select()`/an equivalent Hyperbridge-side selection event fires, and block `retract_bid` for that commitment/filler until the order settles or the selection window expires). Alternatively, require the deposit to be re-verified as live (via a Hyperbridge state proof of `Bids<T>`) before `IntentGatewayV2.fillOrder` honors a `UserOperation`, so retraction terminates fillability rather than being purely orthogonal to it.

When `solverSelection` is enabled in the intent gateway parameters, orders are protected from unauthorized fills. At order placement, the user specifies a `session` key — a temporary keypair generated for this order. Only a solver explicitly authorized by the session key can fill the order.

### `SolverAccount`
