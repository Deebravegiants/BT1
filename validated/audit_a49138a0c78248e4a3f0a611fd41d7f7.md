### Title
Unbounded on-chain Uniswap V2 spot-price swaps in `EvmHost.dispatch`/`fundRequest` and `IntentGatewayV2.placeOrder` expose users to sandwich/flash-loan price manipulation, causing direct value loss - ([File: evm/src/core/EvmHost.sol])

### Summary
`EvmHost.dispatch(DispatchPost)`, `EvmHost.dispatch(DispatchGet)`, `EvmHost.fundRequest`, and `IntentGatewayV2.placeOrder` all convert a user-supplied native-token payment into the protocol fee token by calling `IUniswapV2Router02.swapETHForExactTokens` directly against the live AMM pool, with the swap's implicit max input capped only by `msg.value` and no independent price sanity check (no TWAP, no oracle bound, no explicit slippage parameter). This is functionally identical to the price-manipulation/flash-loan bug class in the referenced FLOKI incident: an unprivileged actor can manipulate the pool's instantaneous reserves in the same block as a victim's dispatch to extract value from the swap.

In every case, the swap consults `IUniswapV2Router02(uniswapV2)`'s live spot reserves at execution time, with `msg.value` acting as the implicit `amountInMax`. Any address can call `dispatch`, `fundRequest`, or `placeOrder` — this is the exact "unprivileged message dispatcher" surface named in scope. An attacker can, within the same block (or via a flash loan), swap a large amount of the fee token or WETH into/out of the pool immediately before a victim's dispatch/placeOrder transaction to shift the pool's exchange rate, then reverse the trade after the victim's transaction executes (sandwich), or exploit stale reserves generally. Because there is no minimum-output/maximum-slippage parameter distinct from the attacker-influenced spot price, and no oracle cross-check, the victim's native-token payment is converted at an artificially unfavorable rate, and the difference is captured by the attacker as arbitrage profit — the same mechanic as flash-loan price manipulation of an AMM used for a critical on-chain calculation, as in the FLOKI report.

The project's own documentation acknowledges this exact AMM-sandwich risk for the *off-chain* `quote()` helper (`HyperApp.quote`), explicitly warning developers never to call it inside a transaction: [5](#0-4) 

However, the *actual state-changing* swaps in `EvmHost.dispatch`/`fundRequest` and `IntentGatewayV2.placeOrder` execute this same unprotected `getAmountsIn`-priced swap on-chain, in the exact context the documentation warns against, with no additional guard.

### Impact Explanation
Any user paying protocol/relayer/solver fees in native token through `dispatch`, `fundRequest`, or `placeOrder` can have their payment executed at a manipulated exchange rate, resulting in direct loss of funds (overpayment extracted by the manipulating attacker as MEV/arbitrage profit). Because these are core, frequently-used entry points (every native-fee-paying POST/GET dispatch and every native-fee intent order), the attack surface is broad and repeatable, not a one-off. This matches a Medium severity, concrete-theft class of impact rather than a purely informational or governance-only issue.

### Likelihood Explanation
The functions are callable by anyone, require no privileged role, and the underlying Uniswap V2 pools (especially on less liquid EVM deployments) are realistically manipulable with a flash loan in a single transaction/block, exactly as in the analog FLOKI incident. Any relayer, bot, or MEV searcher can trivially detect pending `dispatch`/`placeOrder` transactions with native `msg.value` and sandwich them.

### Recommendation
Do not perform live AMM swaps with only `msg.value` as slippage bound. Either (a) require an explicit caller-supplied `amountInMax`/minimum-output parameter validated against a TWAP or a governance-configured price band, or (b) route this conversion through a manipulation-resistant oracle (e.g., a TWAP over the same Uniswap pool, or a Chainlink-style price feed) before executing the swap, reverting if the spot price deviates beyond an acceptable bound.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker takes a flash loan and swaps a large amount of WETH into the `feeToken`/WETH Uniswap V2 pool used by `EvmHost`, sharply raising the WETH price of `feeToken` (or lowering it, depending on the desired direction).
2. Victim calls `IDispatcher(host).dispatch{value: nativeCost}(post)` (or `IntentGatewayV2.placeOrder` with a native-paid `order.fees`) in the same block; `swapETHForExactTokens{value: msg.value}(post.fee, path, address(this), block.timestamp)` executes against the manipulated reserves — see [1](#0-0)  — consuming an unfavorable amount of the victim's native payment (or reverting/wasting more gas than intended).
3. Attacker reverses the flash-loan swap in the same transaction/block, restoring the pool and pocketing the value extracted from the victim's swap as arbitrage profit, mirroring the flash-loan-driven price manipulation described in the FLOKI incident report.
