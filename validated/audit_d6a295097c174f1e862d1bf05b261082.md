### Title
Unprivileged supplier/borrower can permanently freeze a market by pushing accrued debt value past the `i128` RAY ceiling before the index cap engages - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
CVE-2021-2203 is an availability-only DoS: a crash that hangs the server. The analog in XOXNO Lending is a permanent arithmetic freeze of a market: `global_sync` accrues first on every pool verb, and accrual computes `borrowed * borrow_index` in RAY, which panics with `MathOverflow` once the accrued debt value exceeds `i128::MAX` — a ceiling reachable long before `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` is hit. Once crossed, every subsequent accrual panics, so `supply`/`borrow`/`withdraw`/`repay`/`liquidate`/`clean_bad_debt`/`flash_loan`/`update_indexes` all revert for that market forever.

### Finding Description
`global_sync` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20`) runs `accrue_chunk` over elapsed time, calling `accrue_step`, which multiplies `borrowed` (RAY-scaled shares) by the new `borrow_index` via `Ray::mul`. The borrow index is capped at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` = 10^36 in `update_borrow_index` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19`), but that cap clamps the index only — it does not prevent the intermediate `scaled * index` product from overflowing `i128`. The RAY domain admits at most ~170 billion whole tokens of *value* (`i128::MAX / RAY`, per `docs/reference/formulas.md#numeric-limits`), so a market holding e.g. ~10^18-token-scale debt at ~98% utilization on a steep curve (175% max APR) crosses the value ceiling after a few years of compounding, while `borrow_index` is still far below 10^36 — confirmed by `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-360`, which shows `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay` all reverting with `MATH_OVERFLOW` and the index cap never engaging.

Because the panic is inside accrual, which every verb performs first, the state is unrecoverable: `repay` cannot run to shrink `borrowed`, and there is no path that reduces debt without accruing. The debt value can only grow over time, so the freeze is permanent, not transient.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds. All suppliers' deposits and borrowers' collateral tied to the frozen market become unwithdrawable; the debt can never be repaid or liquidated on-chain. Unlike a pause, no governance action can repair it — any call that touches the market's `Cache` accrues first and traps. This matches the accepted impact "permanent freezing of funds."

### Likelihood Explanation
A single unprivileged address can seed the condition: call `supply` then `borrow` near the market's admitted cap (caps are the only bound; `calculate_scaled_cap` saturates and fails open at low supply index), hold utilization high on a high-rate curve, and wait for compounding — or accelerate it on an 18-decimal market where the RAY cliff is only ~170× the index growth. No privileged action, oracle manipulation, or third party is needed; the only requirement is capital and time. Medium likelihood due to the capital/time cost, consistent with the report's Medium severity.

### Recommendation
Bound accrual inputs rather than only the index: before computing `borrowed * new_borrow_index`, saturate or clamp the resulting debt *value* (e.g. clamp `borrowed` shares or the index so the product stays under `MAX_RAY_VALUE`), and/or add a permissionless "cap index at value-safe bound" path so `repay`/`liquidate` remain callable once the ceiling is near. At minimum, enforce per-market supply/borrow caps low enough that `cap * MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cannot overflow `i128` for the asset's decimals.

### Proof of Concept
Reproduced by the existing harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321`): supply `BILLION * 10^18` of an 18-decimal asset, borrow 98% of it on the steep XLM curve, advance time; `try_update_indexes_for(["BIG18"])` fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and subsequent `try_withdraw_raw`/`try_repay` revert identically — the market is frozen with no recovery path.
