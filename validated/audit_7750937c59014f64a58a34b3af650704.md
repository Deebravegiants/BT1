### Title
Webhook shop/topic/id headers are trusted for tenant dispatch but excluded from HMAC verification, enabling cross-tenant webhook spoofing - (`lib/shopify_api/webhooks/request.rb`, `lib/shopify_api/webhooks/registry.rb`)

### Summary
`ShopifyAPI::Webhooks::Registry.process` authenticates an incoming webhook solely by validating an HMAC computed over the raw request body, then unconditionally trusts the `shop-domain`, `topic`, and `webhook-id` HTTP headers — none of which are covered by that HMAC — to build the `WebhookMetadata` handed to the app's handler. An attacker who possesses one legitimately-signed webhook body (e.g. from their own shop) can replay the identical body+HMAC pair while substituting the `shop-domain` header for a victim shop, and the gem will report it as an authenticated webhook for the victim tenant.

### Finding Description
`Webhooks::Request#to_signable_string` returns only `@raw_body`: [1](#0-0) . `Utils::HmacValidator.validate` computes/compares the HMAC exclusively over that signable string: [2](#0-1) .

`Registry.process` gates on this HMAC check and, once it passes, builds `WebhookMetadata` directly from the unauthenticated headers `request.topic`, `request.shop`, and `request.webhook_id`: [3](#0-2) . Those accessors read straight from the raw HTTP headers with no cross-check against the signed body: [4](#0-3) .

The binding that should hold is:
`bytes_covered_by_hmac == bytes_used_to_identify_the_tenant`

but in this code path:
`bytes_covered_by_hmac (raw_body only) != tenant_identity_used_by_handler (shop-domain header, topic header, webhook_id header)`

Because the signature never binds the shop identity to the payload, an unprivileged party who can obtain even one legitimately HMAC-signed webhook body (trivially available to anyone who owns/installs the app on their own shop, since Shopify signs webhooks per-app, not per-shop-pair) can resend that exact `(body, hmac)` pair to the app's webhook endpoint while forging the `x-shopify-shop-domain` (and/or `topic`, `webhook-id`) header to name a different, victim shop. `HmacValidator.validate` will accept it because the HMAC is still valid for the (unmodified) body, and `Registry.process` will hand the handler a `WebhookMetadata` claiming the payload originated from the victim shop.

### Impact Explanation
This breaks the tenant/authentication boundary that `Registry.process` is documented to provide: the "authenticated" webhook's shop identity is not actually authenticated. Any downstream logic that keys off `WebhookMetadata#shop` (e.g. looking up that shop's session/access token, writing shop-scoped data, or triggering shop-scoped side effects) can be tricked into acting as if the data came from an arbitrary shop, i.e. cross-tenant access — one of the explicitly in-scope Critical impacts.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is meaningful but bounded: the attacker needs at least one valid `(body, hmac)` pair signed with the app's `api_secret_key`, which they can obtain by installing/using the app on their own store (many apps are installable by anyone) and capturing a webhook delivery Shopify sends them. No access token, `client_secret`, or leaked credential is required — only ordinary use of the app as an unprivileged merchant/user, then a header-substitution replay against the shared webhook endpoint.

### Recommendation
Include the tenant-identifying headers (at minimum `shop-domain`, and ideally `topic`/`webhook-id`) in the HMAC-signed material used for verification, or otherwise cryptographically bind the shop domain to the payload before trusting it in `WebhookMetadata`. At minimum, `Registry.process` should independently verify that `request.shop` corresponds to a shop with an active, known session/installation before dispatching, rather than trusting it purely from the header.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker installs the target app on their own shop `attacker.myshopify.com` and captures a legitimate webhook delivery: body `B`, header `x-shopify-hmac-sha256: H` (valid HMAC of `B` under the app's secret), `x-shopify-shop-domain: attacker.myshopify.com`.
2. Attacker sends a new HTTP request to the app's webhook endpoint with the same body `B` and same header `x-shopify-hmac-sha256: H`, but sets `x-shopify-shop-domain: victim.myshopify.com` (and optionally forges `topic`/`webhook-id`).
3. `Utils::HmacValidator.validate` recomputes the HMAC over `B` only — it matches `H`, so validation succeeds (`lib/shopify_api/webhooks/request.rb` lines 35-38; `lib/shopify_api/utils/hmac_validator.rb` lines 12-31).
4. `Registry.process` builds `WebhookMetadata.new(topic: ..., shop: "victim.myshopify.com", body: JSON.parse(B), ...)` and invokes the app's handler as though this payload were authentically delivered for `victim.myshopify.com` (`lib/shopify_api/webhooks/registry.rb` lines 188-200), despite the body/HMAC never having been produced for that shop.
