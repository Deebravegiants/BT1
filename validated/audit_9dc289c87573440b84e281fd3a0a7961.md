### Title

Stale fundamental oracle leg inflates collateral through dual-source midpoint blending - (`contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs`)

### Summary

A dual-source oracle can blend a current market price with a much older `Fundamental` feed observation, provided that observation remains inside its own configured staleness window and within the tolerance band. Because cross-source age checking applies only when both legs are `Market`, an outdated fundamental anchor can pull the accepted midpoint away from the current market price and allow borrowing against inflated collateral. [1](#0-0) 

### Finding Description

Each source receives a stale flag based on its own `max_stale_seconds` and the oracle-level `max_price_stale_seconds`. [2](#0-1)  For two readable legs, `blend` marks the result stale if either leg is stale, but the timestamp-spread check is skipped unless both legs have `FeedNature::Market`. [3](#0-2)  The code then accepts the midpoint whenever the two prices remain within the configured tolerance. [4](#0-3) 

Production XLM configuration pairs a one-hour Reflector TWAP leg with a RedStone `Fundamental` leg that may be 46,800 seconds old, under a 10% tolerance band and a 57,600-second oracle-level staleness ceiling. [5](#0-4)  Consequently, a nearly 13-hour-old fundamental price is not considered stale and its age difference from the fresh Reflector observation does not trigger the one-hour `MAX_LEG_AGE_SPREAD_SECONDS` bound. [6](#0-5) 

The controller’s permissionless `borrow` entrypoint settles the debt before enforcing the post-borrow collateral and health-factor gates. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7)  Those gates value collateral using the price returned by the aggregator. [9](#0-8) [10](#0-9) 

### Impact Explanation

An attacker who owns or delegates an account can supply collateral whose fast market leg has fallen while its slow fundamental leg remains near an older higher value. If the leg ratio remains below tolerance, the midpoint raises the reported collateral value and lets `borrow` pass with debt that would be rejected under the fresh market valuation. [11](#0-10) 

The repository’s regression test demonstrates the issue concretely: a stale anchor at `$1.00`, a fresh market price of `$0.91`, and a 10% tolerance inflate collateral by more than 4% and allow a borrow that fails with `INSUFFICIENT_COLLATERAL` when the anchor is fresh. [12](#0-11) [13](#0-12)  If the market does not recover, liquidation cannot restore the borrowed value, leaving lender losses or protocol bad debt. [14](#0-13) 

### Likelihood Explanation

No privileged call, oracle-key compromise, malformed parameter, or malformed price is required; the attacker waits for a slow fundamental update to lag a legitimate market move and then calls `supply` and `borrow` for its own account. [15](#0-14)  Mainnet markets permit fundamental observations up to 13 hours old while allowing a 10% divergence between the two legs, so an ordinary delayed update plus a sub-10% market move is sufficient. [16](#0-15) 

The attacker’s extractable amount is bounded by tolerance and available liquidity: at a 10% leg divergence, midpoint blending can shift the accepted collateral price by nearly 5%. [17](#0-16)  This is enough to cross the solvency boundary demonstrated by the test, so the issue is a Medium severity protocol-insolvency risk. [18](#0-17) 

### Recommendation

Apply a maximum timestamp spread to every two-source blend, including `Market`/`Fundamental` pairs, and reject or ignore a fundamental leg whose age materially exceeds the market leg’s freshness requirements. [1](#0-0)  For mixed-nature oracles, use the fresher market leg rather than averaging it with an old anchor, or reduce the fundamental leg’s configured `max_stale_seconds` to a value consistent with the tolerated price deviation. [19](#0-18) 

### Proof of Concept

1. Configure XLM with the production-style Reflector TWAP primary and RedStone `Fundamental` anchor, where the anchor accepts observations up to 46,800 seconds old and tolerance allows a 10% ratio. [5](#0-4) 
2. Let RedStone retain an old XLM price of `$1.00` while Reflector reports the fresh market price `$0.91`; neither leg is stale under its configured window, and the timestamp-spread gate does not run because RedStone is `Fundamental`. [3](#0-2) 
3. The aggregator accepts the midpoint `$0.955`, inflating collateral by roughly 4.95% versus the fresh valuation. [17](#0-16) 
4. The attacker calls `supply` with 100,000 XLM and then `borrow` for 70,000 USDC; the test shows the stale-anchor borrow succeeds while the fresh-anchor control reverts with `INSUFFICIENT_COLLATERAL`. [20](#0-19) [21](#0-20)

/// Returns whether the ratio `larger * BPS / smaller` of `anchor` and
/// `primary`, rounded half up, is at most `tolerance.upper_ratio_bps`
/// (`10_500` allows a 5% gap). Returns `false` when the smaller value is not
/// positive or the ratio overflows `i128`.
pub(crate) fn within_tolerance_band(
    env: &Env,
    anchor: i128,
    primary: i128,
    tolerance: &OracleTolerance,
) -> bool {
    let high = anchor.max(primary);
    let low = anchor.min(primary);
    let Some(upper_ratio_bps) = fp_core::try_mul_div_half_up(env, high, BPS, low) else {
        return false;
    };

    upper_ratio_bps <= i128::from(tolerance.upper_ratio_bps)
}

/// Returns the average of `anchor_price` and `primary_price`, truncated
/// toward zero. Returns `0` if the sum overflows `i128`.
pub(crate) fn midpoint_price_or_zero(anchor_price: i128, primary_price: i128) -> i128 {
    anchor_price
        .checked_add(primary_price)
        .map(|sum| sum / 2)
        .unwrap_or(0)
}
