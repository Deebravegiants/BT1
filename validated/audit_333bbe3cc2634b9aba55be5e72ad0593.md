### Title
Large scaled balances overflow RAY valuation and permanently freeze a market - (File: `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs`)

### Summary
Interest accrual can hit an `i128` overflow when total scaled supply or debt is large enough that `scaled_shares * index` exceeds the RAY value ceiling before the configured borrow-index cap is reached. Because every pool mutation synchronizes interest before applying its own state transition, one failed accrual blocks subsequent withdrawals, repayments, liquidations, and index updates for that market. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
`Controller::update_indexes` is permissionless and forwards caller-authorized requests to `markets::update_indexes`. [3](#0-2)  The pool implementation loads each market and calls `interest::global_sync`, which processes elapsed time through `accrue_step`. [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4)  Utilization and position valuation unscale RAY shares by multiplying them with the market index. [6](#0-5) 

The checked test creates an 18-decimal market containing one billion whole tokens, then maintains approximately 98% utilization on the XLM curve until `scaled_to_original` returns `MathOverflow`. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7)  The recorded borrow index remains below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, so the intended cap does not save the market. [9](#0-8) 

### Impact Explanation
Once this state is reached, the market is permanently frozen: both withdrawal and repayment fail with `MathOverflow` because they must first complete the same accrual. [10](#0-9)  `supply`, `borrow`, `repay`, and `withdraw` all route through batch legs that load a synced market, while `update_indexes` runs the same global synchronization directly. [11](#0-10) [12](#0-11)  Suppliers cannot exit, borrowers cannot repay, and liquidators cannot operate on that market, permanently freezing the value already booked there. [2](#0-1) 

### Likelihood Explanation
An unprivileged caller needs enough capital to establish a very large market and maintain utilization near the steep part of its configured rate curve, but does not need privileged access to trigger the eventual failure. [13](#0-12) [3](#0-2)  The crash requires substantial liquidity and sustained accrual rather than a single inexpensive transaction, so the availability impact is Medium rather than Critical or High. [14](#0-13) 

### Recommendation
Perform utilization and accrual arithmetic on scaled values without first materializing `scaled_shares * index` in `i128`, or use a wider intermediate representation with an explicit checked reduction. The accrual step should clamp or reject index growth before the product can exceed the representable RAY-value range, commit a safe capped state, and ensure `last_timestamp` advances so all future operations do not deterministically repeat the overflowing calculation. Market caps should also be validated against token decimals, maximum plausible index growth, and the `i128` RAY-value ceiling rather than only against the configured index cap. [6](#0-5) [15](#0-14) 

### Proof of Concept
1. Create or use a listed 18-decimal debt market configured with the steep XLM-style rate curve and a separate collateral market.
2. As one unprivileged caller, call `supply` to deposit approximately `1_000_000_000e18` units of the debt asset, then supply sufficient collateral and call `borrow` for approximately 98% of that liquidity.
3. Let ledger time advance while utilization remains near the steep curve segment; periodically call `update_indexes(caller, [debt_asset])`.
4. At an index around the point where the scaled balance times the index reaches `i128::MAX`, `update_indexes` fails with `MathOverflow` even though the stored borrow index is still below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
5. Subsequent `withdraw`, `repay`, liquidation, and `update_indexes` calls on that market fail at the same synchronization step, leaving the market permanently unusable. [16](#0-15)

/// Applies one compound step of `delta_ms` to indexes and protocol revenue.
///
/// The arithmetic lives in [`accrue_step`], shared with the read-only
/// `simulate_update_indexes` so the view and the mutator cannot drift.
fn accrue_chunk(env: &Env, cache: &mut Cache, delta_ms: u64) {
    let step = accrue_step(
        env,
        cache.params(),
        cache.borrowed(),
        cache.supplied(),
        cache.borrow_index(),
        cache.supply_index(),
        delta_ms,
    );

    cache.set_borrow_index(step.borrow_index);
    cache.set_supply_index(step.supply_index);
    cache.accrue_revenue(step.revenue_shares);
}
