### Title
Signed EmporiumStack ops are not bound to `circomData.stealthAddressStructure`, letting anyone who obtains a signed stack reroute the resulting shielded UTXO to their own key - (File: contracts/external-actions/emporium/upgradeable/EmporiumUpgradeable.sol)

### Summary
`EmporiumUpgradeable.verifyWallet` only checks the victim's EIP-712 signature over `ops`, `maxFee`, `deadline`, and `emporiumMessage`; it never binds the signature to any specific `stealthAddressStructure` or to the caller of `transact`. Since `handleOut` mints the resulting UTXO using whatever `circomData.stealthAddressStructure` the transaction submitter supplies, anyone who obtains a validly-signed `EmporiumStack` for a victim's `HinkalWallet` can resubmit it in their own `Hinkal.transact` call with their own `stealthAddressStructure`, stealing the shielded UTXO that results from moving the victim's funds through the Emporium.

### Finding Description
The broken equality: the funds the victim's wallet moves ("assets leaving wallet, their destination") must equal what the victim actually authorized when they signed `EMPORIUM_SIGNATURE_TYPEHASH`. But that typehash covers only `message, ops, maxFee, deadline`: [1](#0-0) 

`verifyWallet` recovers the signer purely from this hash and never references `circomData.stealthAddressStructure`: [2](#0-1) 

`runAction` then executes the victim-signed ops (including `op.invokeWallet` calls into `stack.signerAddress`'s `HinkalWallet`) and, in `handleOut`, mints the resulting balance change as a `UTXO` keyed to `circomData.stealthAddressStructure` - a field the current transaction's submitter (not the victim) controls: [3](#0-2) 

The Groth16 proof does not close this gap: `formBasicInput`/`getSignedMessageHash` bind the ZK signature (EdDSA over `spendingPublicKey`/`nullifyingPrivateKey`) to `circomData.stealthAddressStructure`, but that signature is generated by the *submitter of `transact`* with their *own* keys - it proves nothing about the victim's intent, it only proves the submitter owns the destination stealth address they chose: [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

Finally, `Hinkal.performHinkalChecks` does not force the caller to be a whitelisted relay when `circomData.relay == address(0)` - it only requires `circomData.originalSender == msg.sender`, a self-referential field the attacker fully controls: [6](#0-5) 

**Exploit flow:**
1. Victim (or their intended relay) constructs and signs an `EmporiumStack` (`v,r,s`, `ops`, `maxFee`, `deadline`, `signerAddress = victim's HinkalWallet`) where `ops` moves victim funds from their `HinkalWallet` into the Emporium (e.g. `invokeWallet=true` call transferring/approving tokens to the Emporium contract), intending the resulting shielded balance to be theirs.
2. The signed stack (bytes for `externalActionMetadata`) becomes visible to an attacker - e.g. it is broadcast to the public mempool as calldata of `Hinkal.transact`, or handed to a relay queue - before it is mined.
3. Attacker crafts their own `CircomData` with `relay = address(0)`, `originalSender = attacker`, their own trivially-generated Groth16 proof for their own `nullifyingPrivateKey`/`spendingPublicKey`, and sets `circomData.stealthAddressStructure` to their own stealth address while reusing the *exact* harvested `externalActionMetadata` (same `emporiumMessage`, `ops`, signature).
4. Attacker submits this to `Hinkal.transact` first (front-running the legitimate submission). `performHinkalChecks` passes (no relay whitelist required), `verifyProof` passes (attacker's own proof over their own keys), `verifyWallet` passes (victim's signature is unrelated to `stealthAddressStructure` and still recovers correctly), `runAction` executes the victim's ops moving the victim's funds into the Emporium, and `handleOut` mints the resulting UTXO under the attacker's `stealthAddressStructure`.
5. `usedMessages[circomData.emporiumMessage]` is now set, so the victim's/relay's legitimate transaction reverts with `UsedMessage` - the attacker has permanently captured the shielded output.

No existing guard closes this: `dimensionsCheck`, `checkOnchainCreation`, the balance/slippage `require`s in `Hinkal.transact`, `insertNullifiers`, and the circuit's `inTotal + amountChanges === outTotal`/`OverflowPreventer`/`ForceEqualIfEnabled` constraints all validate internal amount consistency, not who is entitled to receive the newly-minted UTXO.

### Impact Explanation
Critical: direct theft of shielded funds. The victim's wallet funds are moved into the Emporium exactly as they authorized, but the resulting shielded UTXO commitment is inserted into the Merkle tree spendable only by the attacker's `nullifyingPrivateKey` (`Hinkal.transact`'s `createOnchainCommitment`/`insertCommitments` on `utxoSet` built from `handleOut`). This is repeatable for every signed `EmporiumStack` an attacker can observe or intercept, and it is a full loss of the moved funds for the victim, not merely fee or gas griefing.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attacker needs no privileged role: they only need (a) their own funded EOA to submit a `transact` call, (b) the ability to generate a Groth16 proof for their own shielded keys (trivial, self-service), and (c) visibility of a genuinely victim-signed `EmporiumStack` before it is consumed on-chain (e.g., via public mempool observation and a higher-gas front-run, or via any leak from a relay/queue). None of `owner`, `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE`, whitelisted-relay status, or victim key material is required, satisfying the unprivileged-attacker constraint. The cost is one transaction plus gas to win the race.

### Recommendation
Bind the resulting UTXO destination to what the wallet owner actually authorized: include `stealthAddressStructure` (or a commitment to it) inside `EMPORIUM_SIGNATURE_TYPEHASH` so the victim explicitly signs the intended beneficiary, and/or require `msg.sender`/`circomData.originalSender` in `verifyWallet` to match a value the signer committed to (e.g., bind `emporiumMessage` cryptographically to the destination stealth address at signing time and enforce that binding in `handleOut`/`verifyWallet`).

### Proof of Concept
Foundry test plan:
1. Deploy `Hinkal`, `EmporiumUpgradeable`, `HinkalWallet` (victim's wallet) as in existing setup fixtures.
2. Victim generates `nullifyingPrivateKey_V`/`spendingPublicKey_V` and signs an `EmporiumStack` (`ops` = one `invokeWallet=true` op transferring ERC20 from `HinkalWallet_V` to the Emporium) with `maxFee`, `deadline`, `emporiumMessage = M`.
3. Attacker builds `CircomData` with `stealthAddressStructure` derived from attacker's own `nullifyingPrivateKey_A`, `relay = address(0)`, `originalSender = attacker`, `externalActionData.externalActionMetadata = abi.encode(victimStack)`, generates a valid Groth16 proof locally for their own keys, and calls `Hinkal.transact(...)` from `attacker`.
4. Assert: (a) the call succeeds and victim's wallet balance decreases by the op amount; (b) the emitted/inserted commitment corresponds to a `UTXO` whose `stealthAddressStructure` equals attacker's, not victim's; (c) attacker can later produce a valid nullifier/spend proof for that commitment using `nullifyingPrivateKey_A`; (d) a subsequent legitimate resubmission of the same `emporiumMessage` (by victim/relay) reverts with `UsedMessage`.
5. Equality assertions: before - `(victim signed ops, maxFee, deadline)` implies no destination; after - `outUtxo.stealthAddressStructure == attacker's structure != victim's structure`, proving the divergence.

    function handleOut(
        int256 balanceChange,
        CircomData calldata circomData,
        uint256 i
    ) internal returns (UTXO memory outUtxo) {
        // total change can be less than zero if there was some balance before the call -> that's why we have <=
        if (balanceChange <= 0) {
            return outUtxo;
        }

        transferERC20TokenOrETH(
            circomData.erc20TokenAddresses[i],
            msg.sender,
            uint256(balanceChange)
        );

        outUtxo = UTXO(
            uint256(balanceChange),
            circomData.erc20TokenAddresses[i],
            circomData.stealthAddressStructure,
            circomData.timeStamp
        );
    }

    function verifyWallet(
        EmporiumStack memory stack,
        CircomData calldata circomData
    ) internal {
        EmporiumStorageVars storage $ = _getEmporiumStorage();

        if ($.usedMessages[circomData.emporiumMessage]) {
            revert UsedMessage();
        }

        $.usedMessages[circomData.emporiumMessage] = true;

        if (stack.signerAddress == address(0)) {
            return;
        }

        bytes32 hashedMessage = _hashTypedDataV4(
            keccak256(
                abi.encode(
                    EMPORIUM_SIGNATURE_TYPEHASH,
                    circomData.emporiumMessage,
                    _hashEmporiumOps(stack.ops),
                    stack.maxFee,
                    stack.deadline
                )
            )
        );

        (address recoveredAddress, ECDSA.RecoverError err) = ECDSA.tryRecover(
            hashedMessage,
            stack.v,
            stack.r,
            stack.s
        );
        bool verified = err == ECDSA.RecoverError.NoError &&
            recoveredAddress == stack.signerAddress;
        if (!verified) {
            revert InvalidSignature();
        }

        address verifyingContract,
        CircomData calldata circomData,
        uint256 emporiumMessage
    ) internal pure returns (uint256) {
        // split into two encode calls to avoid "stack too deep"
        uint256 hash1 = uint256(
            keccak256(
                abi.encode(
                    chainId,
                    verifyingContract,
                    circomData.rootHashHinkal,
                    _encodeTokenAddresses(circomData.erc20TokenAddresses),
                    _encodeAmountChanges(circomData.amountChanges),
                    circomData.timeStamp,
                    _flatUint256Matrix(circomData.inputNullifiers),
                    _flatUint256Matrix(circomData.outCommitments),
                    circomData.calldataHash,
                    emporiumMessage
                )
            )
        );
        uint256 hash2 = uint256(
            keccak256(
                abi.encode(
                    circomData.stealthAddressStructure.H1x,
                    circomData.stealthAddressStructure.H1y,
                    circomData.stealthAddressStructure.H0x,
                    circomData.stealthAddressStructure.H0y
                )
            )
        );
        return
            uint256(keccak256(abi.encode(hash1, hash2))) % CIRCOM_P;
    }
