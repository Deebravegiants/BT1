### Title
Supply-index floor clamp resurrects wiped-out supplier claims after full bad-debt socialization, letting phantom shares drain fresh deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
When a borrow-side seize socializes bad debt that meets or exceeds the market's entire supplied value, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps the supply index *up* to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`) instead of zeroing supplier claims. Every existing supply share retains a positive claim (`shares × 10^24`), so wiped-out suppliers — whose backing was fully written off — can still withdraw real tokens deposited later by new suppliers, i.e. a stale/dangling claim is dereferenced after its backing was freed. This is the direct analog of the CVE-2018-8178 class: a freed/invalidated object (the written-off supply claim) is accessed as if still valid.

### Finding Description
In `contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs` the `Borrow` side computes `bad_debt = unscale_borrow_ceil_ray(position)`, calls `interest::apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, then burns the debt shares. In `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89`, `bad_debt` is capped at `total_supplied_value`, the index is multiplied by `remaining / total_supplied_value`, and finally clamped: `new_supply_index.max(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)`. When `capped == total_supplied_value`, `remaining == 0`, so `new_supply_index == 0` and the clamp lifts it to `10^24`. All `supplied` shares are untouched — `burn_supply` is never called — so `unscale_supply_floor(shares)` still returns a positive amount. `resolve_withdrawal`/`require_reserves` then let a wiped supplier withdraw against any cash that later enters the market (fresh `supply`, measured receipts), exactly the "freed object still reachable" shape. The pool's own unit test `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` in `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-428` pins this: after the clamp, a stranded position withdraws and "drains exactly the fresh deposit", leaving cash below the fresh supplier's claim.

Reachability: `controller::liquidate` and `controller::clean_bad_debt` are permissionless (`scripts/permissionless_entrypoints.txt`, INV-AUTH-03). `clean_bad_debt` is gated only by the account's remaining collateral being at/below the dust threshold — the *debt* is unbounded. An account that borrowed near the `max_utilization` cap and whose collateral crashed can accumulate debt exceeding the market's entire supplied value via borrow-index accrual (borrow index grows faster than supply index because of the reserve factor; nothing re-balances the two). A single `clean_bad_debt`/`liquidate` then writes down `bad_debt ≥ total_supplied_value`, triggers the floor clamp, and leaves every pre-existing supplier share as a live claim against future cash.

### Impact Explanation
Protocol insolvency / theft of user funds: after a full write-down the market should owe nothing, yet all wiped suppliers hold phantom claims worth `shares × 10^24` base units. Any subsequent depositor (or `recapitalize` payer, or inflowing repayment cash) funds withdrawals for accounts whose claims were already fully socialized away — new money is stolen by holders of dead claims. First-come-first-served withdrawal ordering makes the theft immediate once fresh liquidity arrives.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a full-wipeout socialize: bad debt ≥ total supplied value on one (hub, token) book. Utilization is capped below 100% at borrow time (`require_utilization_below_max`), so this needs interest-driven debt growth to outrun supply value on a book whose collateral evaporated — a tail event requiring sustained accrual on an abandoned insolvent book, but reachable entirely by permissionless calls (`liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`) once the market state exists. Medium likelihood, critical severity.

### Recommendation
Do not floor the supply index at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` when the write-down consumes the entire supplied value. In `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, detect `remaining == 0` (or `capped >= total_supplied_value`) and zero out `supplied`/`revenue` share totals (or tombstone the market) so no share retains a claim. Alternatively, when the index would clamp up, proportionally rescale/burn all supply shares so `shares × floor_index` equals the actual remaining backing (zero). A wipeout must retire claims, not preserve them at a synthetic floor.

### Proof of Concept
1. Supplier BOB supplies the whole book of asset T (`supplied = S`).
2. Attacker/victim account borrows near the utilization cap; collateral price crashes so the account is insolvent and collateral ≤ dust threshold.
3. Wait for interest (permissionless `update_indexes` accrues it) until `debt_value ≥ S × supply_index` — i.e., `bad_debt ≥ total_supplied_value`.
4. Anyone calls `controller::clean_bad_debt(caller, account_id)` (or a full `liquidate`): `seize.rs` writes `bad_debt` down; `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps `supply_index` to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`; BOB's `S` shares still unscale to `S × 10^24` units of claim.
5. DAVE supplies `X` of T (market still "backed" per `test_socialization_leaves_the_market_backed_and_open` shape since `borrowed` was burned).
6. BOB calls `withdraw` for the full position: `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, S)` pays `floor(S × 10^24 / 10^(27-d))` real tokens, draining DAVE's deposit — while DAVE's own claim is now under-backed.

The mechanism is reproduced verbatim by `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs::test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard`, which asserts `gross == fresh_cash` — the stranded position drains exactly the fresh deposit, and `cash < fresh_claim` afterward.
