### Title
Hostile pre-seed can force the graduation LP to open materially off curve-close price when the rebalance swap budget is insufficient - ([File: packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol])

### Summary
Like the English-auction reserve price that is never enforced once bidding starts, alt.fun's graduation LP-seeding mechanism relies on a *best-effort* rebalance swap (`_pairRebalance`) to correct a hostile pre-seeded HyperSwap pair back to the curve-close ratio before depositing. That swap is capped at `_swapBudget` = 99% of the available inventory side [1](#0-0)  and there is no floor/guarantee that the post-swap ratio actually reaches the cached `(tokensForLP, ltFromPair)` target. When a pre-seed is lopsided enough (e.g. an LT-rich mint pre-seed with a required swap larger than the entire budget), the correction only partially closes the gap and `_routerDepositAndDispose` deposits at whatever skewed ratio remains, using `addLiquidity(min0=1, min1=1)` [2](#0-1)  — no reserve/floor price is enforced on this one-shot, permissionless deposit.

### Finding Description
`finalizeGraduation` is a permissionless, one-shot action, analogous to the one-time English auction in the report: once it runs, the LP is minted and locked forever (`LPLock` has no withdraw path in v1) [3](#0-2) . The protocol's stated invariant is that the LP should open at exactly the last curve price ("zero price gap") [4](#0-3) .

To defend against a front-runner who pre-mints a hostile-ratio LP on the HyperSwap pair between phase 1 (`_enterGraduating`) and phase 2 (`finalizeGraduation`), `_seedRebalancing`/`_pairRebalance` compute a no-fee closed-form swap size and execute it directly against the pair [5](#0-4) . That swap is explicitly capped at 99% of the available inventory (`_swapBudget`) purely to guarantee `finalizeGraduation` never reverts (brick-resistance) [1](#0-0)  — there is no mechanism that enforces the swap actually reaches the target ratio; brick-resistance is prioritized over price accuracy by design, as the code's own comments state: "Brick-resistance ... ranks above the LP-capture defense" [6](#0-5) .

The remaining, still-skewed inventory is then deposited via `router.addLiquidity(..., 1, 1, lpLock, ...)` with no minimum-ratio/reserve-price guard [7](#0-6) . The protocol's own regression test (`test_hostilePreSeed_budgetCappedSwap_isNotProfitable`) proves this residual concretely: an extreme LT-rich pre-seed (TOKEN side at 1% of target, LT side at 200×) forces the pool to open "materially off curve-close" — more than 20% away from the fair price — and this is explicitly accepted as a known "M-02" residual [8](#0-7) .

### Impact Explanation
Just as the auction's un-enforced reserve price meant LPs could lose funds whenever bids never reach the outstanding debt, here the rebalance swap's un-enforced target means the graduation LP — locked permanently with no withdraw path — can open at a materially distorted price whenever the budget cap binds. Once open, arbitrageurs will trade the pool back toward the true market price; because the LP position is locked and cannot be withdrawn or rebalanced afterward, that arbitrage permanently transfers value out of the locked LP (owned in aggregate by the protocol/creator ecosystem) to the arbitrageurs, exactly matching the "LP seeded away from the curve close price" loss category. The pre-seeder's own capital is confiscated/burned (so the attacker is not directly enriched), but the *protocol/creator-owned locked LP* still opens and settles at an incorrect, arbitrage-exploitable price — a real, quantifiable value loss with no path to recovery given `LPLock`'s no-withdraw design.

### Likelihood Explanation
The precondition — front-running `finalizeGraduation` with a large, self-funded `pair.mint` pre-seed skewed heavily toward one side (larger than the graduation's own `tokensForLP`/`ltFromPair` budget allows the swap to correct) — is fully reachable by any unprivileged address: pair creation is permissionless (`factory.createPair`), and minting against a pre-transferred hostile ratio requires only owning tokens/LT and calling `pair.mint(attacker)`, as the codebase's own threat model documents in detail [9](#0-8) . The fuzz test `testFuzz_hostilePreSeed_neverProfitable_neverBricks` confirms this residual is reachable across a range of seed multiples, not just a contrived edge case [10](#0-9) .

### Recommendation
Consider raising the per-side rebalance budget (or funding it from a small protocol reserve) so the swap can reach the cached ratio across a wider pre-seed range, and/or add a bounded retry/second-swap pass before falling back to the skewed deposit, similar to how the original report recommends starting from (rather than merely aiming for) the fair/reserve price. At minimum, document and monitor the economic exposure of the accepted M-02 residual, since it represents value permanently extracted from a locked, unrecoverable LP position rather than merely denying attacker profit.

### Proof of Concept
The protocol's own test `test_hostilePreSeed_budgetCappedSwap_isNotProfitable` is a working PoC: pre-seed the HyperSwap pair via `pair.mint(griefer)` with `reserveToken = tokensForLP/100` and `reserveLt = ltFromPair*200`, then call permissionless `finalizeGraduation(tokenAddr)`. The resulting pool price is asserted to be more than 20% off the curve-close price, confirming the LP is seeded away from fair value [11](#0-10) .

## HyperSwap Pre-Seed Defense (Read This Before Touching `_seedUniswapV2Direct`)

The whole sub-system inside `_seedUniswapV2Direct` exists to defuse one specific attack class. It's the most subtle code in the package. Read this before touching any of the helpers (`_seedRebalancing`, `_pairRebalance`, `_routerDepositAndDispose`, `_noFeeSwapInput`).

### The exploit

A vanilla UniswapV2 pair is deployable by anyone: `factory.createPair(token, lt)` is permissionless, and after creation anyone can call `pair.mint(to)` against pre-transferred tokens. So between phase 1 (`_enterGraduating` flips lifecycle to `Graduating` and caches `tokensForLP / ltFromPair`) and phase 2 (`finalizeGraduation` mints LP via `pair.mint(lpLock)`), an attacker can:

1. Front-run by calling `factory.createPair(token, lt)` themselves
2. `transfer(pair, smallToken)` and `transfer(pair, smallLT)` at any ratio they choose
3. Call `pair.mint(attacker)` — they now own LP at a hostile reserve ratio

When our `pair.mint(lpLock)` runs in phase 2 against this non-empty pair, V2's mint formula picks up the existing reserves:

```
liquidity = min(amount0 · totalSupply / reserve0, amount1 · totalSupply / reserve1)
```

The `min(...)` arm whose denominator is bigger relative to its numerator wins, and the OTHER arm's "excess" deposit is donated pro-rata to existing LP holders — i.e. to the attacker. Two harms:

- **Wrong opening price.** Post-mint reserves are `(R_attacker + T_a, R_attacker + T_b)`, so the LP opens at `(R_a + T_a) / (R_b + T_b)`, NOT at the curve close `T_a / T_b`. A `$15` LT pre-seed at 50% off curve close opens the pool ~454 bps off.
- **LP capture.** The wasted-side excess goes to the attacker's LP claim. A `1 wei + 1 LT` pre-seed (~`$1` attack budget) captures ~34 bps of LP.

A cheaper variant skips step 3 entirely: `transfer(pair, dust) + pair.sync()` forces the stored reserves to the dust ratio without minting any LP, leaving the pair at `reserves > 0 && totalSupply == 0`. Regime 1 below covers both shapes by keying on supply rather than reserves.

This is the same exploit class as the four.meme Feb 2025 incident (~$183K loss).

### Brick-resistance contract

`_seedUniswapV2Direct` MUST never revert under any pre-seed shape. The brick-resistance contract is the load-bearing security property — it ranks above the LP-capture defense, because a brick locks every holder in `Graduating` forever. The pre-seed defense is layered to honour this:

- **Regime 1/2 don't touch the router.** Even if the V2 router is misbehaving, the empty + donation paths run on direct pair calls.
- **`_pairRebalance` falls back to a direct mint when no swap can run.** `_noFeeSwapInput` may return `s == 0`, or the pair's fee-charging `getAmountOut(s)` may round to zero, against a pre-seed whose swap-output side is dust — `pair.swap` would otherwise revert with `INSUFFICIENT_OUTPUT_AMOUNT`. In either case `_pairRebalance` returns `false`, and `_seedRebalancing` overpowers the dust with a direct `transfer + pair.mint` at the cached `tokensForLP / ltFromPair` ratio (`_seedDirectMint`), opening the pool on-ratio.
