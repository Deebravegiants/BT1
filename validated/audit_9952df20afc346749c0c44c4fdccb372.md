### Title
Whale market RAY-value overflow permanently freezes all market operations - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary

The accrual engine multiplies the market's total scaled supply/debt by its live index inside `i128` arithmetic (`Ray::mul`). When `supplied * index` or `borrowed * index` exceeds `i128::MAX`, the multiplication panics with `MathOverflow` instead of saturating — and every pool verb accrues first, so once the product crosses the representable ceiling the market is bricked forever: no withdraw, repay, borrow, liquidation, or index update can execute. An unprivileged attacker can reach this state by supplying and borrowing a large book at sustained high utilization and letting interest compound (permissionless `update_indexes` advances accrual), permanently freezing all suppliers' and borrowers' funds in that market.

### Finding Description

`update_supply_index` computes `total_supplied_value = supplied.mul(env, old_index)` where `Ray::mul` is checked `i128` multiply-divide; an unrepresentable result raises `MathOverflow` (common/src/rates/index.rs:34; docs/reference/formulas.md:20-24). `update_borrow_index` computes `old_index.mul(env, interest_factor)` before clamping to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and the per-step interest calc uses `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` with the same overflow surface (common/src/rates/index.rs:13-18).

`global_sync` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33` runs `accrue_step` on every mutation before any user logic. So the overflow is not confined to `update_indexes`: `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `supply`, and `borrow` on that `(hub_id, asset)` market all hit the same panic. The index caps (`MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY = 10^36`, `MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY`) do not protect this path because the RAY asset *value* `scaled × index` overflows `i128` long before the index itself reaches its cap — the protocol's own regression test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-360) demonstrates exactly this: a ~1B-token market at 98% utilization on the 175% stress curve hits `MATH_OVERFLOW`, after which both `withdraw` and `repay` revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW` and "the market is frozen: no repay, no withdraw, no liquidation. The index cap never engages."

Unlike the MySQL CVE (privileged local attacker crashing the server), here the trigger is fully unprivileged: `supply`, `borrow`, and `update_indexes` are open entrypoints (docs/reference/endpoints.md:24-40). The only requirements are a large deposit (caps are bounded by `max_cap_for_decimals`, which the harness shows can be lifted to admit ~10^27 raw units for an 18-decimal asset) and elapsed time — no oracle manipulation, flag, or admin action needed.

### Impact Explanation

Permanent freezing of funds for every supplier and borrower in the affected market. Supplier shares become unwithdrawable, debt unpayable, collateral unseizable (liquidation accrues the market first), and bad-debt cleanup unreachable for positions on that market — since `clean_bad_debt` also runs `global_sync`. The freeze is not recoverable by governance or `recapitalize`: any code path that touches the market recomputes the same overflowing product. Severity is bounded to one market per attack, but per-market total loss of the book qualifies as High.

### Likelihood Explanation

Reachability requires a whale-scale deposit (billions of whole tokens on a high-decimal asset, or proportionally less at higher index growth) plus years of accrual at high utilization — or faster on a steep kinked rate curve past optimal utilization, where `max_borrow_rate` up to 175% compounds at ~e^1.75 per year. The `i128` ceiling is ~170× the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` domain boundary per the test's measurement; an attacker who both supplies and borrows can also donate/position to maximize `borrowed` so the debt-value overflow arrives sooner. Supply caps mitigate but are per-market governance-settable and the harness lifts them via `max_cap_for_decimals`. No privileged action is required at any point; the attacker can also be the sole victim-and-beneficiary concern aside, the freeze harms all other suppliers in that market.

### Recommendation

Make accrual fail-operational at the representable bound rather than panic:

- In `update_supply_index` and `update_borrow_index`, use `mul_div` with an `I256` intermediate (the codebase already has exact `I256` for multiply-divide per formulas.md) or detect overflow and clamp to `MAX_*_INDEX_RAY` before the panic.
- In `accrue_step`/`scaled_to_original`, saturate `borrowed × borrow_index` at `i128::MAX` and treat the market as fully capped so exits remain callable.
- Alternatively, add an explicit "accrue-only" escape: cap supply/debt value at the ceiling and skip further index growth, letting `withdraw`/`repay` proceed on the clamped values.
- Enforce tighter `max_cap_for_decimals` supply/borrow caps so `scaled × MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` cannot exceed `i128` for any listed decimal.

### Proof of Concept

```rust
// tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-360
// 18-decimal market, 1B tokens supplied, 98% utilization, 175% max borrow rate.
t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", BILLION * 10i128.pow(18));
t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt); // 98% utilization

loop {
    t.advance_time(YEAR_SECS);
    if let Err(e) = t.try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"]) { break e; } // -> MATH_OVERFLOW
}
// After overflow, every verb panics:
assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
assert_contract_error(t.try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
// borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY: the cap never engaged; the i128
// RAY-value product `borrowed * index` overflowed first.
```

Root cause: `supplied.mul(env, old_index)` at `common/src/rates/index.rs:34` and `scaled_to_original` in the interest calculation use checked `i128` multiply on the (scaled × index) product with no saturation or pre-clamp, while `global_sync` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33`) forces this computation ahead of every user-facing operation on the market.
