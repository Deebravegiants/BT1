### Title
Supply-index floor clamp resurrects wiped suppliers' claims after bad-debt wipeout — ([File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs])

### Summary
The Dovecot CVE is an unauthenticated use-after-free: a resource is logically destroyed but stale references to it can still be exercised. The XOXNO Lending analog lives in bad-debt socialization. `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` caps the write-down at total supplied value and then clamps the resulting supply index UP to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`) instead of letting supplier claims go to zero. The scaled supply shares are never burned, so wiped suppliers retain a "freed-but-still-live" claim of `scaled * floor / RAY`. That claim is redeemable for real cash as soon as the market holds any tokens — in particular after `recapitalize` tops up cash without minting shares — draining funds that belong to fresh/honest suppliers.

### Finding Description
In `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89`, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes the post-write-down index as `supply_index * (total_supplied_value - capped) / total_supplied_value` and then applies `.max(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)`. When `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value` (full wipeout), `remaining = 0` and the index is clamped up to the floor rather than to zero, leaving every supplier's scaled shares with a positive floor claim `unscale_supply_floor(scaled) > 0` while backing is zero.

The in-tree unit tests already demonstrate the consequence on the raw cache: `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-428` and `:430-495` show a wiped position whose stranded claim withdraws exactly a fresh depositor's cash, leaving the honest supplier unbacked. The production supply path is guarded by `require_backed_market` (`contracts/pool/src/guards.rs:52-58`), which blocks *new supply* into a shortfall market — but it does not neutralize the stranded claim:

- Exits (`withdraw`) check only `require_reserves` (cash availability), not full backing — `guards.rs:36-47` explicitly notes "Exits do not."
- `recapitalize` (reachable via controller `recapitalize`) credits cash to repair the measured shortfall *without minting shares* and does not restore the index or delete the stranded claims (documented in `docs/reference/invariants.md:477-479` and `docs/explanation/threat-model.md:301-308`). Once cash is credited, the stranded floor claim of wiped shares becomes withdrawable again, paid from the recapitalized funds.

Reachable path for an unprivileged address: (1) drive or find an account insolvent with collateral ≤ the $5 dust threshold; (2) call permissionless `controller.clean_bad_debt(caller, account_id)` → `execute_bad_debt_cleanup` (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/bad_debt.rs:14-60`) → `pool.seize_positions` → `ops/seize.rs:24-28` → `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`; (3) wait for (or arrange) any cash in the market — e.g., recapitalization or residual revenue — then have a pre-wipeout supplier call `withdraw`, redeeming scaled shares at the floored index against cash that no longer backs them.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent loss of funds / protocol insolvency at Medium severity. After a wipeout-level socialization, wiped suppliers hold an unbacked residual claim that is still honored by `resolve_withdrawal`/`require_reserves`. Any recapitalization or residual cash is paid out first-come-first-served to holders of already-written-off shares, so recapitalized funds (or the claims of suppliers whose value should have been zeroed) are consumed by phantom claims. The docs acknowledge the floor "can preserve an unbacked residual claim" (`docs/reference/invariants.md:246-247`), but the concrete redeemable-for-cash drain after recapitalization is the exploitable consequence.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. Triggering requires a full-supply-value wipeout: debt written off ≥ total supplied value in that market, i.e., a market where bad debt roughly equals total deposits — plausible in small/spoke markets or after a large price collapse combined with `no_seize`-blocked liquidation (the runbook explicitly contemplates cleanup when `no_seize` blocks liquidation). The cleanup path is permissionless, and the attacker only needs to hold pre-existing supply shares and wait for cash to reappear; no privileged role is needed. The limiting factor is that the stranded claim only pays out if cash is present, and `require_backed_market` blocks new supply into the shortfall — so realization depends on recapitalization or residual market cash.

### Recommendation
When `capped == total_supplied_value` (full wipeout), burn or zero the supply side instead of clamping the index to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`: e.g., set `supplied`/`revenue` scaled totals to zero and reset the index to `RAY`, so no residual shares survive. Alternatively, record wiped scaled supply as permanently non-redeemable (a separate "written-off" bucket excluded from `resolve_withdrawal`), or gate `withdraw` on `backing_shortfall == 0` for markets whose index sits at the floor. At minimum, make `recapitalize` burn the floor-clamped stranded shares or refuse to recapitalize a market where floored claims exceed measured backing.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual trace (mirrors `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:430-495` at the contract boundary):

1. Alice supplies 1000 units of X; Carol (or Alice via a second account) borrows ≈ all of it. Price collapse makes the debt unrecoverable and liquidation insufficient; residual account is insolvent with collateral ≤ $5 dust.
2. Any caller invokes `controller.clean_bad_debt(account_id)`. `seize.rs` unscales the debt and calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache, bad_debt)` with `bad_debt >= supplied * index`; index clamps to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`; Alice's scaled shares remain, `cash ≈ 0`.
3. `controller.recapitalize` (or residual revenue / a stray direct token transfer to the pool address, which is unauthenticated) restores `cash > 0` against the shortfall.
4. Alice calls `controller.withdraw(X, i128::MAX)`: `resolve_withdrawal` unscales her shares at the floored index → `gross > 0`; `require_reserves` passes against the new cash; `burn_supply`/`debit_cash` pay her tokens from the recapitalized funds — value that was meant to restore backing for honest claims.
