### Title
First-come-first-served loss realization: suppliers can withdraw at the pre-write-down index before bad debt is socialized - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs)

### Summary
Bad debt in a market is only written into the `supply_index` when a liquidation or bad-debt cleanup runs `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`. Between the on-chain observable event that makes an account insolvent (a price update) and that write-down call, `withdraw` pays out at the unwritten-down index and checks only raw `cash` via `require_reserves` — it does not check `require_backed_market`. An early withdrawer therefore exits at par and pushes the entire loss onto suppliers who remain, exactly the Bancor "losses are not distributed evenly" bug class.

### Finding Description
Withdraw accounting resolves the payout against the current `supply_index`, burns shares, and gates only on cash:

- `accounting` → `gate_and_debit` calls `cache.require_reserves(net_transfer)`, `require_utilization_below_max`, and `require_supply_for_debt`, but never `require_backed_market` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:111-119`). `require_backed_market` exists in `contracts/pool/src/guards.rs:52-58` but is not applied to withdrawals; `backing_shortfall` (`guards.rs:61-66`) values outstanding debt at face, so a market with an insolvent borrower still looks "backed" until someone calls `liquidate` or `clean_bad_debt`.
- The write-down only happens inside `seize::apply` for `AccountPositionType::Borrow` via `interest::apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:24-28`), i.e. only when a liquidation or cleanup entrypoint is submitted.
- The price crash that creates the bad debt is public state before any write-down runs, so any supplier can race to `controller::withdraw` (amount `0` → withdraw-all) and exit at the pre-write-down index.

The repo's own harness proves the asymmetry: `supplier_can_exit_ahead_of_bad_debt_writedown` shows Bob withdrawing 75% of the ETH supply whole after Alice's position becomes insolvent but before liquidation, amplifying Carol's loss ~4x (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/bad_debt_index.rs:402-462`).

### Impact Explanation
Theft/loss of user funds: the loss that should be pro-rata across all suppliers is instead borne entirely by the last suppliers to exit. In the tested scenario, the first mover recovers ≥100% of their deposit while the remaining supplier's loss is amplified ~4x (proportional to the dodger's share). In a full bank run the earliest withdrawers drain `cash` completely and later suppliers' claims become unpayable, matching the reported "first to withdraw get all tokens; last get nothing" outcome.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires only a market that accrues bad debt (unavoidable in lending) plus an insolvency-creating price update, which is public on-chain before any cleanup executes. Any unprivileged supplier can submit `withdraw(hub_asset, 0)` immediately; no privileged role, timing luck beyond a single transaction ordering, or external dependency is needed. Natural mempool races and automated keeper withdrawal bots make the FCFS window reliably exploitable whenever a large bad-debt event occurs.

### Recommendation
Distribute losses pro-rata at withdrawal time rather than at cleanup time: gate or haircut withdrawals by the live `backing_shortfall` (e.g., pay `min(claim, claim * backing / supplied_claim)` or call `require_backed_market` in `gate_and_debit`), so an insolvent debt position reduces every supplier's claim symmetrically the moment it becomes unrecoverable, instead of letting the first mover socialize the loss onto everyone else.

### Proof of Concept
1. Bob supplies 75 ETH, Carol supplies 25 ETH into the same market; Alice supplies USDC collateral and borrows ETH.
2. USDC price crashes (public oracle update) making Alice's ETH debt bad — no write-down has been applied; `supply_index` still values Bob's shares at par.
3. Bob calls `controller::withdraw` (amount `0`, withdraw-all). `gate_and_debit` passes `require_reserves` because `cash` covers the payout; Bob exits whole.
4. Liquidator calls `liquidate`/`clean_bad_debt`; `seize::apply` → `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` writes the entire loss down against the remaining supply — Carol alone.
5. `carol_loss_b / carol_loss_a > 3x` as asserted in `bad_debt_index.rs:447-462`.
