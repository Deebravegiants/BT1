### Title
Flash deposit + self-swap + withdraw lets an attacker reclaim their own swap fee (and dilute LP fee revenue) with no time-lock between Deposit and Withdraw - (File: `program/src/processor.rs`)

### Summary
Raydium's `Deposit` and `Withdraw` instructions compute LP mint/redemption amounts strictly from the AMM's *current* vault balances, with no cooldown, minimum holding period, or same-transaction/same-slot restriction between the two. Because swap fees are settled directly into the coin/pc vaults (increasing the pool's underlying token totals used for both LP pricing and the constant-product swap math), a single-transaction sequence of `Deposit` → `Swap` → `Withdraw` lets an attacker temporarily inflate their own LP-token ownership share, swap against the pool, and immediately withdraw — reclaiming the bulk of the fee they just paid and, correspondingly, diluting the fee revenue that would otherwise accrue entirely to the pre-existing liquidity providers.

### Finding Description
`process_deposit` mints LP tokens proportionally to the ratio of deposited tokens to `total_coin_without_take_pnl` / `total_pc_without_take_pnl`, both of which are derived directly from live vault balances via `Calculator::calc_total_without_take_pnl_no_orderbook`: [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

`process_swap_base_in` transfers the full `swap.amount_in` (which includes the fee) into the AMM's vault, and the fee amount is never routed elsewhere — it simply raises the vault balance, hence the effective invariant value used for both future swaps and future LP share calculations: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

`process_withdraw` then redeems LP tokens for a proportional share of the (now fee-inflated) vault balances, again computed live at withdrawal time with no restriction tied to when the LP tokens were minted: [5](#0-4) 

There is no check anywhere in `process_deposit`, `process_swap_base_in`, `process_swap_base_out`, or `process_withdraw` that forbids depositing and withdrawing from the same pool within the same transaction, the same slot, or within any minimum elapsed time. The only state touched at the end of these handlers is `amm.recent_epoch = Clock::get()?.epoch;`, which is not used to gate deposit/withdraw eligibility. This mirrors the reported bug class: a value-accruing event (swap fee accrual into pool value, analogous to the tranche's `liquidationPenalty`) is delivered atomically to whoever holds LP shares at that instant, and nothing prevents a single actor from flash-minting a large LP position immediately before triggering that event and burning it immediately after, all inside one attacker-controlled transaction.

### Impact Explanation
An attacker who deposits a large amount `D` relative to existing pool reserves `X` obtains LP share `M/(L+M) ≈ D/(D+X)` of the pool. When they execute a swap of size `S` in the same transaction, the swap fee stays in the vault and inflates pool value; the attacker's share of that fee inflation is reclaimed on withdrawal in the same transaction. As `D` grows relative to `X`, the attacker recoups a share of their own paid swap fee approaching 100%, meaning the effective realized fee paid to the pre-existing liquidity providers approaches zero for that trade, instead of the full swap fee they would otherwise have collected. This is a direct diversion of fee revenue away from genuine LPs to whoever is willing to flash-deposit, at essentially no cost or risk to the attacker (deposit/withdraw round-trips are risk-free in a single atomic transaction beyond negligible rounding). This constitutes an unauthorized transfer of value (fee yield) that should accrue to LPs, satisfying the "concrete theft ... of LP funds" bar.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. It requires the attacker to have enough capital to temporarily dominate the LP pool for the duration of a single transaction (a flash loan of the pool's base assets is generally obtainable on Solana or via the attacker's own capital), and it is more profitable on pools with thin existing liquidity relative to available flash capital. No frontrunning, privileged keys, or off-chain components are needed — it is fully executable by an ordinary permissionless actor issuing one transaction containing `Deposit`, one of the four swap instructions, and `Withdraw` against the same `AmmInfo`/vault accounts.

### Recommendation
Introduce a minimum holding period or a same-transaction/same-slot restriction preventing LP tokens minted via `Deposit` from being redeemed via `Withdraw` in the same transaction or slot (e.g., record the deposit slot in `AmmInfo`/a per-user account and reject withdrawals before `N` slots have elapsed, or disallow `Deposit` and `Withdraw` instructions from co-occurring in one transaction). This closes the specific atomic round-trip abuse while leaving normal deposit/withdraw flows unaffected.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker observes an `AmmInfo` pool with reserves `X` coin / `Y` pc and low outstanding `lp_amount`.
2. In one transaction, attacker issues:
   - `Deposit` with `max_coin_amount`/`max_pc_amount` sized to `D >> X` (funded via flash loan or attacker capital), minting LP amount `M ≈ D/X * L` per `process_deposit`'s `InvariantPool::exchange_token_to_pool` at `program/src/processor.rs:1243-1250`.
   - `SwapBaseIn`/`SwapBaseOut` for the attacker's real desired trade of size `S`, whose fee (`amm.fees.swap_fee_numerator`) is added into the vault balance per `program/src/processor.rs:1970-1982, 2000-2023`.
   - `Withdraw` for the full `M` LP tokens, redeeming a proportional share of `total_coin_without_take_pnl`/`total_pc_without_take_pnl` (now inflated by the just-paid fee) per `program/src/processor.rs:1719-1761`, returning to the attacker their original `D` deposit plus `≈ M/(L+M)` of the fee just paid.
3. Net result: attacker's realized swap fee cost is reduced roughly by the fraction of the pool they temporarily controlled, while pre-existing LPs receive only `L/(L+M)` of the fee instead of the full amount they would have earned from that trade had the attacker simply swapped without flash-depositing.
