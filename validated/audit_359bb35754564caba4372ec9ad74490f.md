### Title
Bad-debt supply-index floor clamp resurrects wiped suppliers' shares, letting them drain fresh deposits - ([File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs](contracts/pool/src/interest.rs))

### Summary
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` socializes bad debt by lowering the market supply index, but clamps the result at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`) instead of zeroing supplier shares. After a full wipeout, every pre-existing scaled position retains a phantom claim worth `scaled_amount * RAY/1000 / RAY`. Because withdrawal only checks `require_reserves` (cash on hand), a wiped-out supplier can later `withdraw` and pull real tokens deposited by fresh suppliers — a classic use-after-free shape: the position's backing was destroyed, but the dangling share object is still dereferenced for payout.

### Finding Description
In `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89`, bad debt is capped at `total_supplied_value`, the reduction factor drives `new_supply_index` toward zero, but `cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)))` clamps it up to `RAY/1000` whenever the write-off consumes ≥99.9% of supplied value.

Scaled supplier positions in `AccountPosition.scaled_amount` and the market's `supplied` share counter are not touched by this write-down; the index is the only conversion factor. On withdrawal, `contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:93-101` resolves `gross = unscale_supply_floor(position)` via `Cache::resolve_withdrawal`, and `gate_and_debit` (`withdraw.rs:111-119`) only enforces `require_reserves(net_transfer)` — cash ≥ payout — plus utilization/supply-for-debt guards that say nothing about whether the burned shares were ever backed after the clamp.

The pool's own unit test proves the theft: `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-427` (`test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard`) shows that after a wipeout writes the index down to the floor, the old scaled position still unscale-withdraws `gross > 0` equal to a fresh deposit, leaving pool cash below the fresh supplier's claim (`cache.cash() < fresh_claim`). The stranded claim drains "exactly the fresh deposit."

Reachable entirely by unprivileged calls:
1. Attacker (or anyone) calls controller `clean_bad_debt` / liquidation that triggers a pool seizure covering ≥99.9% of a market's supplied value → index clamps at floor, all supplier shares become nominally worthless but retain `scaled * FLOOR` claims.
2. Any holder of a pre-wipeout supply position (including revenue shares minted into `supplied`) calls `withdraw`/`withdraw_all` once new liquidity exists → `require_reserves` passes against the fresh cash → tokens paid out.
3. Insolvency is now socialized onto fresh suppliers, whose claims exceed pool cash.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency: wiped-out suppliers recover a residual ~0.1%-of-notional claim per share that is unbacked; each such withdrawal transfers fresh depositors' principal to the stale-share holder, and the pool ends unable to cover live claims. The unit test demonstrates a wiped position paying out `gross == fresh_cash` and leaving `cash < fresh_claim`.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a near-total bad-debt wipeout (severe price collapse or oracle lag driving HF far below 1 before liquidation), which is exactly the tail event the cleanup path exists for, plus new liquidity entering the same market afterward — plausible since markets are not disabled by wipeout and `supply` remains open. No privileged action is needed by the exploiter; they merely hold an old position and call `withdraw` at the right time.

### Recommendation
When `capped == total_supplied_value` (full wipeout), zero out the market's `supplied` share counter (and revenue shares) rather than clamping the index, or record a per-market wipeout flag/epoch so pre-wipeout scaled positions unscale to zero. Alternatively, gate `resolve_withdrawal`/`require_reserves` on the unscaled claim being backed by `supplied * supply_index` rather than cash alone, so floor-clamped phantom shares cannot withdraw real tokens.

### Proof of Concept
From `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-427`:
```rust
// market: supplied = 1000*RAY scaled, index = RAY, cash = 0
apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(&mut cache, Ray::from(5_000 * RAY));
assert_eq!(cache.supply_index().raw(), SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW); // clamped UP

let stranded = cache.unscale_supply_floor(old_scaled);
assert!(stranded > 0); // phantom claim survives wipeout

// fresh supplier deposits `stranded` tokens
cache.mint_supply(cache.calculate_scaled_supply(fresh_cash));
cache.credit_cash(fresh_cash);

// wiped supplier withdraws: require_reserves passes against fresh cash
let (burn, gross) = cache.resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, old_scaled);
cache.require_reserves(gross);
cache.debit_cash(gross);
assert_eq!(gross, fresh_cash);            // stole the fresh deposit
assert!(cache.cash() < fresh_claim);      // pool now insolvent vs fresh supplier
```
On-chain: `clean_bad_debt` → wait for new `supply` → `withdraw(i128::MAX)` on the stale position.
