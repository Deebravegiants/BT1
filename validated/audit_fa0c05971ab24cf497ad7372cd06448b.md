### Title
Supply-index floor resurrects wiped supply shares and allows fresh deposits to be drained - (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs`)

### Summary
`clean_bad_debt` can socialise debt greater than the market’s total supplied value, but `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps a fully wiped supply index to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead of invalidating or zeroing the residual shares. [1](#0-0) 

### Finding Description
The permissionless `Controller::clean_bad_debt` path emits every remaining debt position to `pool_seize_positions_call` as a `Borrow` seizure. [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2)  The pool converts the seized debt shares to a RAY debt amount, socialises it through `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, and then burns the debt shares. [4](#0-3)  Inside `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, `bad_debt` is capped at `total_supplied_value`, `remaining` is calculated with checked subtraction, and the resulting zero `reduction_factor` produces a zero index before the index is clamped upward to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. [1](#0-0)  Consequently, a debt write-down intended to consume 100% of supplier value leaves every pre-existing scaled supply share with a positive residual claim, while total `supplied` shares remain unchanged. [5](#0-4) 

### Impact Explanation
Once fresh liquidity is supplied after the wipeout, those stale scaled shares can be withdrawn through the ordinary withdrawal path because `resolve_withdrawal` unscales them at the floored index and `debit_cash` pays the resulting amount. [6](#0-5)  The repository’s regression test demonstrates the exact sequence: a residual Alice claim waits for a new deposit, withdraws the entire fresh deposit, and leaves the new depositor’s claim greater than remaining cash. [7](#0-6)  This permits theft of later suppliers’ funds and leaves the market insolvent. [8](#0-7) 

### Likelihood Explanation
An unprivileged caller can invoke `clean_bad_debt` when an account reaches the protocol’s cleanup condition, and the affected suppliers can subsequently invoke `withdraw` against their unchanged scaled positions. [2](#0-1) [9](#0-8)  The critical precondition is a `Borrow` seizure whose ceiled debt value is at least the total supplied value for that market, after which the clamp deterministically creates residual claims. [10](#0-9) 

### Recommendation
When `capped == total_supplied_value`, do not clamp the supply index upward for existing shares; instead explicitly invalidate the wiped share class, burn or zero the residual supply balance, and track any floor-only accounting separately so old shares cannot unscale to a positive claim. [11](#0-10)  Alternatively, mark the market as wiped out and block subsequent supply or withdrawals until governance performs an explicit recapitalisation or migration. [12](#0-11) 

### Proof of Concept
1. A market contains a victim or attacker-controlled supply position with scaled shares `S`, while an insolvent account’s debt is large enough that `unscale_borrow_ceil_ray(position) >= supplied * supply_index`. [13](#0-12) 
2. An unprivileged caller executes `Controller::clean_bad_debt(caller, account_id)`. [2](#0-1) 
3. The pool caps the bad debt to total supplied value, computes `reduction_factor = 0`, then changes `supply_index` from zero to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. [11](#0-10) 
4. The surviving `S` shares now unscale to `S * SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, producing a positive `stranded` claim despite the complete write-down. [14](#0-13) 
5. A new supplier deposits `stranded` units of the asset, creating cash backing. [15](#0-14) 
6. The holder of `S` withdraws all shares; `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, S)` returns the full stranded amount and `debit_cash` pays it out. [6](#0-5) 
7. The old share owner receives exactly the new deposit while the new supplier retains a claim that remaining cash cannot cover. [12](#0-11)

pub(crate) fn apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache: &mut Cache, bad_debt: Ray) {
    let total_supplied_value = cache.supplied().mul(cache.env(), cache.supply_index());

    if total_supplied_value == Ray::ZERO {
        return;
    }

    let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
    let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);

    let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
    let new_supply_index = cache
        .supply_index()
        .mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);

    cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
}

/// Burns `burned` from market supply and returns the user's remaining scaled position.
fn burn_position(env: &Env, cache: &mut Cache, position: Ray, burned: Ray) -> Ray {
    cache.burn_supply(burned);
    position.checked_sub(env, burned)
}

/// Enforces reserve, utilization, and solvency guards, then debits cash for
/// the net transfer. Liquidations and footprint-only closes skip utilization.
fn gate_and_debit(env: &Env, cache: &mut Cache, net_transfer: i128, skip_utilization_check: bool) {
    cache.require_reserves(net_transfer);

    if !skip_utilization_check {
        guards::require_utilization_below_max(env, cache);
    }
    guards::require_supply_for_debt(env, cache);
    cache.debit_cash(net_transfer);
}
