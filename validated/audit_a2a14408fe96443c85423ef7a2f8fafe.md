This leads to interest accrual and eventual forced liquidation for users who are unable to repay their loans. ### Impact Explanation

The impact is a loss of user funds due to their collateral being locked and inaccessible.  In the `extrafinance` analog, users lose collateral on debt they were able and willing to settle, as their positions degrade until they are liquidatable.  This is considered a defect because the function's name and documentation imply a narrower effect than its actual behavior.  The prompt's scenario directly mirrors this, as users minting dollars against a paused collateral will find their collateral permanently frozen, leading to financial loss. 

### Likelihood Explanation

The likelihood of this vulnerability being exploited is high, as it can be triggered by a routine administrative action.  The `disableVaultToBorrow` function in `extrafinance` is an owner-triggered action, but its name conceals the side effect of blocking repayment, making it a "footgun" for administrators.  Similarly, in the prompt's scenario, an administrator pausing redemption for a collateral token, a seemingly routine security measure, inadvertently creates a trap for new users. 

### Recommendation

The recommendation for the `extrafinance` vulnerability was to either remove the `borrowingWhiteList` requirement from `repay` or split the mapping into separate `borrowingWhiteList` and `repaymentWhiteList` to prevent stranding positions. 

Following this precedent, the recommendation for the prompt's vulnerability is to add a check in the `mintDollar()` function to prevent minting against collateral for which redemption is paused.  This would involve a `require` statement similar to the one suggested in the prompt: `require(poolStorage.isRedeemPaused[collateralIndex] == false, "Collateral redemption is paused");`.
