### Title
Reward payout silently swallows per-nominator transfer failures, permanently losing rewards without reverting the extrinsic — (File: substrate/frame/staking-async/src/pallet/impls.rs)

### Summary
`pallet-staking-async`'s reward-payout logic (`payout_from_provider`, `payout_legacy_mint`, `make_payout_from_provider`, `make_payout_legacy`, `transfer_validator_incentive`) mirrors the exact failure-swallowing pattern described in the Sherlock `pullReward` report: an individual reward transfer/mint can fail (insufficient funds in the reward pot, ED/existence issues, mint failure), the failure is caught, logged, and an `Unexpected`/warning event is emitted, but **the overall extrinsic still succeeds** and other stakers in the same page are still paid.

### Finding Description
In `make_payout_from_provider`, the transfer of a nominator's/validator's share from the era reward pot is wrapped so that any `Err` is turned into `None` and execution continues: [1](#0-0) 

The same pattern exists for the legacy mint-based path (`make_payout_legacy`, called from `payout_legacy_mint`) and for the validator incentive transfer (`transfer_validator_incentive`), where a failed transfer only logs a warning and emits `Event::Unexpected` instead of aborting: [2](#0-1) 

The caller, `payout_from_provider`/`payout_legacy_mint`, iterates over every exposure entry (validator + all nominators in the page) and calls these `make_payout_*` helpers per-account, continuing regardless of any individual failure: [3](#0-2) 

This is functionally identical to the Sherlock `pullReward` bug: the top-level "payout" call does not revert when a sub-transfer to one beneficiary fails; the operation is instead treated as best-effort. Any staker whose transfer failed (e.g., because the reward pot account temporarily lacks sufficient free balance to satisfy `Preservation::Expendable`, or because their target account cannot receive the transfer) receives nothing for that era/page, while other stakers in the same page are paid normally, and the page/era is nonetheless considered processed (payout dispatchables are not idempotent-retry-friendly once the era's claim bookkeeping — `ClaimedRewards`/ledger claimed-rewards tracking, elsewhere in the pallet — records the era or page as paid).

### Impact Explanation
Same as the original report's class: a normal, unprivileged nominator can permanently lose their expected staking reward for an era/page if the transfer step for their account fails, without the extrinsic reverting and without any recourse to force a retry, since the pallet's claimed-reward bookkeeping is separate from the per-account transfer success. This is low-severity here compared to the original Sherlock report because (a) FRAME's payout dispatchables are explicitly documented as non-atomic across the vector of stakers by design (to avoid one dust/edge-case account blocking payout for everyone else), and (b) the amounts at risk are bounded by a single era's/page's reward share rather than an entire staking position's full lifetime SHER allocation, and there is no "locked position that can't be canceled" analog forcing the user to wait out a long lockup before discovering the loss.

### Likelihood Explanation
Low-to-moderate. Triggering the underlying transfer failure requires an edge condition such as the era reward pot running below the amount needed for `Preservation::Expendable`/`Preservation::Preserve` transfers (rounding/dust exhaustion at the tail of a payout page) or a payee account in a state that rejects the incoming transfer. These are the same categories of edge cases the original report identified (insufficient balance in the paying contract/account) and are realistically reachable by any nominator whose payout happens to fall on the unlucky tail of a page when the pot balance is depleted by prior entries in the same `for` loop, since `T::Currency::transfer` calls happen sequentially without pre-computing a running balance check across all beneficiaries in the page.

### Recommendation
- Pre-validate that the reward pot holds sufficient balance for all beneficiaries in the page before starting the payout loop (or use a strictly ordered claim mechanism that reflects actual availability), and fail the whole extrinsic if not.
- Alternatively, decouple "marking the era/page as claimed" from an individual account's transfer outcome: if a transfer fails, do not mark that specific staker's share as claimed, and expose a dedicated recovery/retry call (e.g. `retry_payout(era, page, who)`) so failed shares are not silently lost forever.
- Ensure `Event::Unexpected`/warning-log paths are treated operationally as alerts requiring on-chain governance/maintenance follow-up (e.g., top-up or retry), consistent with the long-term recommendation in the original report that "the system should revert or otherwise ensure it doesn't silently drop part of the expected rewards."

### Proof of Concept
Not exploitable as a funds-theft primitive; this is a griefing/availability issue against a specific nominator's payout for a given era/page:
1. Construct an era where `ErasStakersPaged`/exposure page contains several nominators, and the era reward pot (`RewardPots::pot_account(RewardPot::Era(era, RewardKind::StakerRewards))`) is funded with an amount that covers most but not the very last nominator's share (e.g. due to prior rounding losses or a targeted top-up shortfall).
2. Call `payout_stakers`/`payout_stakers_by_page` for that era/page. [4](#0-3) 
3. Observe that earlier nominators in `exposure.others()` are paid and emit `Event::Rewarded`, while the nominator whose transfer fails only causes a logged warning inside `make_payout_from_provider` — no `Rewarded` event, no revert — matching the same "silent partial failure, execution continues" pattern flagged in the Sherlock report. [5](#0-4)
