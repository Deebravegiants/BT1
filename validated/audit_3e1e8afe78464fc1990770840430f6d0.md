### Title
Beneficiary can permanently grief cross-chain calldata delivery in `pallet-hyper-fungible-token` by bumping their own account nonce - (File: `modules/pallets/hyper-fungible-token/src/module.rs`)

### Summary
`pallet-hyper-fungible-token`'s `on_accept` executes an optional signed runtime call on behalf of the cross-chain transfer's beneficiary. The signature is bound to the beneficiary's **current destination-chain `frame_system` nonce**, read only at the moment the relayed ISMP message is finally delivered/executed [1](#0-0) . Because cross-chain delivery is asynchronous and relayer-dependent, the beneficiary can submit any ordinary, cheap transaction on the destination chain before the bridged message lands, incrementing their own account nonce and permanently invalidating the pre-signed calldata payload — exactly analogous to PartyA bumping `partyANonces[msg.sender]` via `allocate()` to invalidate a liquidator's pending nonce-bound signature.

### Finding Description
When a sender on the source chain wants the destination-chain recipient's tokens to trigger an additional runtime call, they embed a `SubstrateCalldata` structure (optionally signed by the beneficiary) inside the bridged `Message.data` field [2](#0-1) . On delivery, `on_accept`:

1. Mints/transfers the bridged amount to the beneficiary first [3](#0-2) .
2. Reads the beneficiary's **live** `frame_system` account nonce on the destination chain: `let nonce = frame_system::Pallet::<T>::account_nonce(beneficiary.clone());` [4](#0-3) .
3. Verifies the beneficiary's Ed25519/Sr25519/Ecdsa signature over `(nonce, runtime_call)` [5](#0-4) , returning `HftError::SignatureVerificationFailed` and aborting `on_accept` with `Err(...)` if the nonce does not match.

This mirrors the audited bug exactly: the signature is computed off-chain (by the beneficiary, ahead of time, for the intended future call) against a nonce whose authoritative value lives entirely under the beneficiary's own on-chain control. Since ISMP message delivery timing depends on relayers, consensus proof availability, and network conditions — often minutes to hours after the source-chain dispatch — the beneficiary has ample opportunity to submit any unrelated transaction, incrementing their nonce and permanently desynchronizing it from the nonce baked into the pre-signed calldata. Once the live nonce advances past the signed value, the signature can never validate again (nonces only increase), unlike the liquidation-nonce case where the liquidator could theoretically retry with an updated nonce — here, the calldata's signed nonce was fixed off-chain when the sender constructed the cross-chain message and cannot be refreshed without the original sender resubmitting the whole bridge transaction with a different nonce.

The test suite itself demonstrates that nonce invalidation is a one-shot event: after one successful call-data execution, an identical resubmission of the same `PostRequest` fails outright because the account nonce has already advanced [6](#0-5) , confirming that any nonce bump — whether from the intended execution, a replay, or a griefing transaction — is fatal to the signed payload's future validity.

### Impact Explanation
A malicious or merely active beneficiary can grief the "calldata" leg of a token-bridge message: the token mint/transfer to the beneficiary succeeds (or, depending on whether `on_accept`'s error is propagated transactionally by the ISMP message-handling pipeline, the whole delivery — including the token transfer — could revert), but the intended follow-up runtime call (e.g. distributing bridged funds further, executing a DeFi action, or completing a cross-chain intent) permanently fails and can never be retried since `nonce` values are monotonically increasing and the signed payload is immutable. This breaks composability guarantees promised by "Calldata Execution" (bridged tokens with an attached call), and — if `on_accept` failures cause the relayer's delivery transaction to fail/revert — it also burns relayer gas and can be repeated cheaply to censor specific cross-chain calls targeting a given beneficiary, a route-unable-to-deliver-messages condition.

### Likelihood Explanation
High: the beneficiary needs only to submit any ordinary signed extrinsic on the destination chain (trivial cost) before the cross-chain message is delivered. Given ISMP consensus-proof delivery latency (BEEFY/GRANDPA/etc. finalization plus relayer submission time), there is a comfortable window for the beneficiary to observe or simply routinely transact and thereby invalidate the pending calldata, with no special timing precision required (unlike a mempool front-run, this is not even a race — any transaction landing before the relayed message suffices).

### Recommendation
Do not bind the calldata signature to the beneficiary's live `frame_system` account nonce. Instead, use a nonce/sequence value scoped to the HFT pallet itself (e.g. a dedicated per-beneficiary calldata-execution nonce stored in pallet storage and only incremented by this pallet), or bind the signature to the immutable cross-chain message identifier (source chain id + PostRequest nonce/commitment) so that ordinary account activity on the destination chain cannot invalidate a payload the beneficiary already authorized off-chain.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice on chain A calls `HyperFungibleToken::send` with `call_data` containing a `SubstrateCalldata` whose `signature` is computed over `(nonce_A, runtime_call)` where `nonce_A` is Alice's current account nonce on destination chain B, per `modules/pallets/hyper-fungible-token/src/module.rs:283-286` pattern shown in the test [7](#0-6) .
2. Before the relayer delivers the proof of this request to chain B, Alice submits any unrelated transaction on chain B, incrementing her `frame_system` account nonce from `nonce_A` to `nonce_A + 1`.
3. When the relayer finally submits the ISMP message, `on_accept` reads Alice's now-incremented nonce, the signature check fails at `modules/pallets/hyper-fungible-token/src/module.rs:138/149/164`, and `HftError::SignatureVerificationFailed` is returned [8](#0-7) .
4. The signed calldata can never execute again because Alice's nonce will only continue to increase, permanently blocking the intended runtime call while the relayer's delivery either reverts (losing gas/fees) or the tokens land without the intended follow-up action, depending on pipeline atomicity — either outcome is a griefing/DoS on the intended cross-chain execution.
