### Title
Griefing: an attacker can sandwich a victim's `withdraw` between a max-size `borrow` and a `repay` to repeatedly revert it - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs)

### Summary

A supplier's `withdraw` is gated on the post-state of the market: it must leave cash ≥ reserves (`require_reserves`), keep utilization ≤ `max_utilization` (`require_utilization_below_max`), and keep supply nonzero while debt exists (`require_supply_for_debt`). An unprivileged attacker who holds collateral in any market can front-run a victim's withdrawal with a `borrow` that drains the pool's cash down to the liquidation buffer (or pushes utilization to the cap), causing the victim's `withdraw` to revert with `InsufficientLiquidity`/`UtilizationAboveMax`, then back-run it with a `repay` that returns the full principal. Interest accrual is chunked in milliseconds, so a same-ledger borrow→repay round trip costs the attacker only gas — a pure, repeatable grief on withdrawal availability.

### Finding Description

- `withdraw` → `ops::withdraw::accounting` → `gate_and_debit` runs `cache.require_reserves(net_transfer)` then `guards::require_utilization_below_max` for non-liquidation withdrawals before debiting cash (contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:111-119).
- `require_utilization_below_max` reverts with `UtilizationAboveMax` when `ceil(debt)/floor(supply) > max_utilization` (contracts/pool/src/guards.rs:19-34). Burning supply shares shrinks the denominator, so any withdrawal at high utilization fails regardless of size.
- `borrow` → `mint_debt` debits cash and is capped only by `require_liquidation_buffer` (2% of floored supply) and `require_utilization_below_max` (contracts/pool/src/ops/borrow.rs:63-79). Any account with sufficient collateral can borrow the pool down to those limits in one call.
- `repay` is permissionless and refunds excess payment (controller `repay`, documented in skills/xoxno-lending-contracts/abi.md), so the attacker recovers the principal immediately. Within one ledger the accrued interest on the loan is zero or dust, making the round trip near-free.

Unlike the Particle report's NFT swap, no special asset mechanics are needed: the attacker's own borrowed liquidity itself blocks the exit. `clean_bad_debt`, `claim_revenue`, and `net_settle` are also gated by `require_utilization_below_max`/`require_supply_for_debt`, but withdrawal is the clearest victim path.

### Impact Explanation

Temporary freezing of funds: a victim supplier cannot execute `withdraw` (or a `repay_debt_with_collateral`/`close_position` flow that embeds a withdraw) while the attacker keeps the pool at the liquidity/utilization boundary. The attacker loses only gas; no funds are stolen and the block lifts when the attacker stops, matching a Medium-severity grief/availability impact.

### Likelihood Explanation

Requires the attacker to post enough collateral (in any listed market) to borrow the pool's free cash up to `max_utilization`/the 2% buffer — real capital, but fully recoverable via `repay`, so the only net cost is gas plus one-ledger interest. There is no economic gain; motivation is pure grief (e.g., DoS-ing a competing liquidator unwind, a vault redemption, or a specific user). Feasible whenever a market's `max_utilization < RAY` or cash is thin relative to the victim's withdrawal.

### Recommendation

Adopt the analogous mitigation: let the victim withdraw through the congestion rather than being gated by a state the attacker controls. Concretely, exempt cash-increasing/neutral exits from `require_utilization_below_max` by applying the utilization check to the *pre-withdrawal* state (or only when utilization worsens), so a withdrawal that lowers utilization is never blocked by an already-at-cap market. Alternatively, make `require_utilization_below_max` compare utilization before vs. after the leg and only reject if it increased, and/or charge a non-refundable minimum fee on `borrow` to raise the per-grief cost.

### Proof of Concept

1. Bob supplies 10,000 USDC (victim supplier). Carol supplies collateral (e.g., USDC in another spoke or ETH market) giving her borrow capacity.
2. Alice (victim) submits `controller.withdraw(alice, id, [(USDC, amount)], None)`.
3. Griefer front-runs: `controller.borrow(griefer, gid, [(USDC, cash_available - buffer)], None)` — `mint_debt` passes `require_liquidation_buffer` and lands utilization at `max_utilization` with cash ≈ 2% reserve.
4. Victim's `withdraw` executes `gate_and_debit`: `require_reserves` fails with `InsufficientLiquidity` (or `require_utilization_below_max` fails with `UtilizationAboveMax`, since burning shares raises `debt/supply`).
5. Griefer back-runs: `controller.repay(griefer, gid, [(USDC, borrowed + ε)])`; excess is refunded, net cost ≈ gas.
6. Repeat each ledger the victim broadcasts, keeping the withdrawal blocked indefinitely at near-zero cost.

Key code: `gate_and_debit` ordering (contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:111-119), `require_utilization_below_max` (contracts/pool/src/guards.rs:19-34), borrow caps only via `require_liquidation_buffer`+utilization (contracts/pool/src/ops/borrow.rs:63-79), and permissionless `repay` with refund (confirmed by `poc_permissionless_repay_any_caller`, tests/test-harness/tests/controller/security_audit.rs:423-445).
