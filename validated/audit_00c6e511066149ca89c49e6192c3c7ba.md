### Title
Emporium `EMPORIUM_SIGNATURE_TYPEHASH` omits `feeStructure`/`relay`, letting the tx submitter redenominate the signed `maxFee` into an arbitrary, more valuable token pulled from the wallet - ([File: contracts/external-actions/emporium/upgradeable/EmporiumUpgradeable.sol])

### Summary
`verifyWallet` computes the EIP-712 digest strictly from `circomData.emporiumMessage`, the hashed `ops`, `stack.maxFee` and `stack.deadline`; it never binds `circomData.feeStructure` (in particular `feeStructure.feeToken`) or `circomData.relay` into the signed payload. Because `maxFee` is a raw `uint256` with no token/denomination context in the signature, whoever calls `Hinkal.transact` (any unprivileged caller, since the Emporium action is reached via `onlyAllowedRecipient`, not owner-gated) can pick `feeStructure.feeToken` to be any ERC20 the wallet holds and charge up to `maxFee` raw units of that token via `payRelayFees` → `sendToRelayFromWallet` → `IHinkalWallet.doSendToRelay`.

### Finding Description
The claimed invariant is: *(assets leaving the wallet, their destination) == (ops, maxFee) the owner signed*. The signed digest is built here: [1](#0-0) 

and the typehash only names `message, ops, maxFee, deadline`: [2](#0-1) 

The only guard tying `feeStructure` to the signature is a raw numeric comparison, with no token binding: [3](#0-2) 

`circomData` (including `feeStructure` and `relay`) is supplied fresh by whoever calls `runAction`/`Hinkal.transact`, not recovered from any signed record - the owner signs a stack (`ops`, `maxFee`, `deadline`) off-chain, but the actual fee token and the flat fee's real-world value are decided later, at submission time, by the transaction submitter. In the stateful path (`stack.signerAddress != address(0)`), the fee charged is exactly `flatFee` (capped by the unrelated numeric check against `maxFee`) and is pulled straight from the signer's wallet: [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

An attacker who is merely the caller of `Hinkal.transact` (the relay/submitter role, not the owner) can construct `circomData.feeStructure.feeToken` = a high-value token the wallet holds (e.g. WETH instead of the low-value gas/fee token the owner had in mind when agreeing to a numeric `maxFee`), keep `flatFee <= maxFee` to pass the only check, and cause `doSendToRelay` to pull `flatFee` units of that arbitrary, more valuable token out of the signer's wallet to `circomData.relay` (also unsigned, so it can be the attacker's own address). Neither the token identity nor the recipient of the fee was part of what the owner's EIP-712 signature attested to - only an opaque number was.

`performHinkalChecks`, `dimensionsCheck`, `verifyProof`, nullifier/root checks and the circuit's `inTotal + amountChanges === outTotal` constraints only govern the shielded-pool bookkeeping and do not constrain which ERC20 the wallet's direct fee transfer (`doSendToRelay`) is denominated in, since that transfer happens outside the shielded balance/UTXO accounting for the "ops" themselves.

### Impact Explanation
The attacker (any unprivileged caller submitting the transaction) can redirect wallet funds - in an asset the owner never specified and to a relay address the owner never approved - up to the numeric `maxFee` bound, effectively moving/spending value the owner's signature never denominated. This matches the "temporary/permanent freezing or theft of protocol/relay fees" / "executing calls or moving assets a wallet owner ... never authorised" High category. It is repeatable on every signed stack the attacker can get relayed, and costs the attacker nothing beyond gas since they are typically the one who would collect the relay fee anyway.

### Likelihood Explanation
Preconditions: the attacker must be able to submit the `Hinkal.transact` Emporium call with a `CircomData` of their choosing (attacker explicitly has this capability per the threat model) while reusing a validly-signed `EmporiumStack` (obtained from any legitimate flow, e.g. a wallet owner who signed and broadcast/leaked their stack to a relay network, which is the normal intended flow for this feature). No special role is required; the wallet just needs to hold a balance of the substituted token. This is straightforward and highly feasible.

### Recommendation
Include `feeStructure.feeToken` (and ideally the full `FeeStructure`, or at minimum the token/denomination the `maxFee` refers to) in the `EMPORIUM_SIGNATURE_TYPEHASH` and the hashed message, so the signer explicitly commits to which asset/token the fee will be denominated and paid in. Optionally also bind `relay` if the owner should control who directly receives wallet-pulled fees, or otherwise document/restrict this as an accepted design trade-off only if bounded per-token allowances are enforced elsewhere.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry plan:
1. Deploy `EmporiumUpgradeable`, a mock `IHinkalWallet` implementation for the signer, and two ERC20 mocks: `LowValueToken` and `HighValueToken`, funding the wallet with both.
2. Owner signs an `EmporiumStack` (`ops` = benign no-op call, `maxFee = 100`, `deadline = future`) intending `feeStructure.feeToken = LowValueToken`.
3. Attacker calls `Hinkal.transact` → `runAction` with `circomData.feeStructure = {feeToken: HighValueToken, flatFee: 100, variableRate: 0}` and `circomData.relay = attacker`, reusing the owner's untouched signature/stack.
4. Assert `verifyWallet` does not revert (since only `flatFee <= maxFee` numeric check applies), and assert `HighValueToken.balanceOf(wallet)` decreases by 100 units while `HighValueToken.balanceOf(attacker)` increases by 100 - i.e., the owner's signature (bound only to `LowValueToken` in intent) was used to authorize a transfer of `HighValueToken`, demonstrating `(asset, destination) actually moved != (asset, destination) the owner signed`.
