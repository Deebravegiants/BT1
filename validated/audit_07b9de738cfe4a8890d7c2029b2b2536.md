### Title
RAY-value overflow in `scaled_to_original` permanently freezes a market once debt value exceeds `i128` - (`common/src/rates/simulate.rs`)

### Summary
Analog to CVE-2021-28090 (remote assertion failure crashing the system): an unprivileged-reachable arithmetic panic in the interest-accrual path permanently disables a pool market. `accrue_step` (`common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-62`) computes `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` and `scaled_to_original(supplied, supply_index)`, which call `Ray::mul` → `mul_div_half_up` (`common/src/math/fp_core.rs:108-118`), panicking with `MathOverflow` when `scaled * index / RAY` no longer fits `i128`. Because every pool mutation runs `interest::global_sync` first (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33`), once the debt or supply RAY value crosses `i128::MAX` the market is frozen forever: `supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`-driven settlement, `clean_bad_debt` and `recapitalize` all abort on the same panic. The borrow-index cap at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` never engages because the value overflows before the index does.

### Finding Description
The domain bound is ~1.7e11 whole tokens (`i128::MAX / RAY`); `Ray::from_asset` and position conversion admit inputs up to that bound, but the *accrued value* `scaled × index / RAY` can cross it later as indexes compound. Index caps (10^36 raw) bound the index, not the product. `docs/reference/formulas.md:432-437` explicitly acknowledges "Value overflow can occur before the index ceiling and block repayment/withdrawal because those operations accrue first," and the test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-360`) demonstrates the freeze end-to-end: after the index passes ~170x, `try_update_indexes_for`, `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` all revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW`.

Two factors widen reachability beyond the harness's lifted caps: (1) `calculate_scaled_cap` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:26-33`) saturates to `i128::MAX` instead of reverting — after a bad-debt write-down pushes the supply index toward its `10^24` floor (`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89`), any configured cap above ~1/1000 of the admitted maximum fails open, so a whale can supply up to the raw `i128` bound; (2) `update_indexes` is permissionless, and utilization drifts upward on an untouched book because debt compounds faster than supply, so a large high-utilization market reaches the cliff without further attacker action.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds on the affected (hub, token) market. Once `borrowed × borrow_index` (or `supplied × supply_index`) overflows, no accrue-first entrypoint can execute: suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot liquidate the market's debt, and `recapitalize` cannot restore solvency since it too accrues. All supplied, revenue, and collateral cash backing that market is locked permanently.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a market whose token-unit debt reaches ~1.7e11 whole tokens times index growth — far above current mainnet caps (e.g., AQUA's 5e8 cap is ~200x below the bound), and on the steep stress curves takes years of compounding at high utilization. No single transaction triggers it, and caps under a normal index do constrain entry. However, caps can be configured at the admitted maximum, cap enforcement saturates open after index write-downs, utilization drifts up autonomously, and nothing (no alarm, no guard) halts accrual before the cliff. Medium likelihood at best for currently listed markets; nonzero for high-supply tokens.

### Recommendation
Bound the *value*, not just the index: in `accrue_step`/`update_borrow_index`, clamp `new_borrow_index` to `min(cap, i128::MAX_RAY_value / borrowed)` so accrual stops accruing interest (index pinned) rather than panicking — mirroring how the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` ceiling already degrades to zero-interest accrual. Similarly clamp `update_supply_index` against `i128::MAX / supplied`. Alternatively, enforce a hard market-size ceiling in `supply`/`borrow` (`supplied × index < RAY_value_bound`) so the product can never reach `i128::MAX` regardless of caps.

### Proof of Concept
Reproduced by the committed test `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-360`:

1. Supply ~1e9 whole tokens (18 decimals) into `BIG18`; supply collateral and borrow ~98% of it (index ≈ 1 RAY).
2. Advance time yearly; each call of the permissionless `update_indexes` accrues in ≤1-year chunks.
3. Once `borrowed × borrow_index / RAY > i128::MAX`, `scaled_to_original` panics `MathOverflow` inside `accrue_step`.
4. From then on `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay` on that market all revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW` — the market is permanently frozen while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (the cap never engaged).

On a live path, the equivalent trigger is: wait for index growth (or exploit a saturated cap after a bad-debt write-down to enlarge `supplied`/`borrowed`), then call `controller.update_indexes` / `pool.update_indexes` — no privilege required.
