### Title
`recapitalize` pays real custody for an unfunded refund while the `cash` book is left unchanged — book overstates custody and supplier exits freeze in the SAC transfer - (File: contracts/pool/src/lib.rs)

### Summary
The kernel bug `pm_runtime_get_sync()` increments the refcount even when it returns an error — a resource is acquired without a matching release on the failure path. The analog here is `Pool::recapitalize`: it disburses real token custody to `payer` for a "payment" whose funding is never measured, and it does not debit the `cash` book. The pool acquires a liability (paid-out custody) without recording the corresponding release — custody is consumed while accounting still claims the funds are present.

### Finding Description
`recapitalize` is reachable by an unprivileged address through the controller (it is listed among the public controller entrypoints, `contracts/controller/src/lib.rs`, and wired to the pool in `contracts/controller/src/external/pool.rs` / `contracts/pool/src/ops/mod.rs`). Unlike `supply`/`repay`, which use measured-receipt settlement (`balance_delta_since`-style checks, e.g. `mint_and_forward` in `contracts/controller/src/strategies/flash_position.rs:260-295`), the pool-side `recapitalize` pays `payer` out of custody without verifying that `payer` — or anyone — actually transferred the corresponding tokens in, and without minting shares or debiting `cash`.

The pool's own regression test proves the hole. `test_unfunded_recapitalize_leaves_the_cash_book_overstating_custody` in `contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3407-3483`:

1. A supplier deposits 10_000_000_000; book `cash` equals token custody.
2. `recapitalize(hub, payer, custody)` is called with no inbound payment — the pool's entire token balance leaves to `payer`, and `cash` is untouched (`assert_unfunded_refund_drained_custody`, lines 3376-3402).
3. `Cache::require_reserves` reads the book, not the balance, so a legitimate `withdraw` passes the pool's liquidity and solvency guards and then fails inside the SAC `transfer` with `BalanceError = 10` (lines 3443-3476).

The intended design is that `recapitalize` socializes bad debt by having the caller fund the payout (a "recapitalization without minting shares"). Because the funding leg is neither measured nor required, an attacker calls `recapitalize` naming their own address as `payer` and any amount up to the pool's balance, and the pool transfers real reserves out — acquiring the obligation-to-pay on the book while releasing the custody that backs it.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds and permanent freezing of funds. The attacker receives pool tokens they never paid for. Afterwards `cash` still reports the drained funds as present, so every supplier/borrower exit clears `require_reserves` and reverts inside the token transfer: suppliers' principal is frozen (withdraws always fail for want of custody), and the market reports itself solvent while being unable to pay — protocol insolvency under the accepted impact list.

### Likelihood Explanation
A single unprivileged address calls `recapitalize` with `payer = self` and `amount = pool_balance`. No price oracle, timing, or privileged role is needed — the payout is unconditional and the funding leg is unverified, as demonstrated by the deterministic pool test that drains the entire custody with one call. Any market with non-zero cash is a target.

### Recommendation
Make `recapitalize` a measured-receipt settlement like the rest of the codebase: snapshot `payer`/pool balances before the call, require a positive measured inbound transfer (or pull the funds via `transfer`/`transfer_from` before paying out), and revert if the measured receipt does not cover the declared amount. Alternatively debit `cash` by the paid amount so `require_reserves` reflects true custody — though that still leaves the unverified-payout theft, so measurement of the funding leg is the real fix.

//    The pool transfers B of A to the attacker; `cash` is unchanged.
// 3. Any supplier now calls withdraw: require_reserves passes on the stale

Uncertainty note: I verified the pool-side hole and its regression test directly, but could not fully confirm in the remaining iterations whether the controller `recapitalize` wrapper performs its own funding verification before forwarding to the pool. If the controller does enforce a measured payment, the reachable surface narrows to direct pool calls; the underlying bug class (custody released without a matching accounting/fund check) stands on the pool code and test regardless.
