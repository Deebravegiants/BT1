### Title
Flash-loan stale `Cache::commit` overwrites market mutations performed during the receiver callback - ([File: contracts/pool/src/ops/flash.rs])

### Summary
The pool's flash loan loads the market `Cache` (interest-accrued) *before* paying out principal and invoking `execute_flash_loan` on the receiver, then commits that pre-callback snapshot afterward in `finalize`. The two `require_balance` checks only verify the token balance; they do not detect market-state changes made during the callback. Any same-market mutation committed during the callback (accrual/index advance, fee revenue, cash, share totals) is silently overwritten by the stale commit — a classic use-after-free/dangling-snapshot analog: state is "freed" (superseded by a newer committed state) but the old in-memory copy is still written back.

### Finding Description
`ops::flash::apply` executes in this order:

1. `prepare` loads `Cache` via `ops::renewed_market` (which runs `interest::global_sync`), so the cache holds accrued indexes, `cash`, `supplied`, `borrowed`, `revenue`, `last_timestamp` as of time T.
2. `asset.transfer` pays principal; `require_balance` asserts `balance_after_payout`.
3. `invoke_receiver` calls the arbitrary WASM receiver's `execute_flash_loan`.
4. `require_balance` again asserts only the *token balance* equals `balance_after_payout`.
5. `collect_repayment` pulls principal+fee; `finalize` → `book_fee` → `cache.commit()` writes the whole stale snapshot back.

`Cache::commit` (`contracts/pool/src/cache/mod.rs`) writes all state fields unconditionally, and `Cache` explicitly "does not hold a storage lock; callers must not interleave commits for the same market without reloading." The flash flow does exactly that: it interleaves an external callback between load and commit.

Pool mutators are `#[only_owner]` (the controller), so the receiver cannot call the pool directly. However, the receiver is an arbitrary contract chosen by the initiator (`require_wasm_receiver` only checks it is a WASM contract). During the callback it can invoke controller entrypoints that do not require the receiver's own `require_auth` — e.g., `update_indexes`, `clean_bad_debt`, `claim_revenue`, `recapitalize`, or permissionless legs of liquidate/flash_position on *other* accounts touching the same hub-asset market. The controller authorizes its pool sub-calls itself (`authorize_as_current_contract`), so a nested controller call can commit fresh state for the same market while the outer flash cache is still live. When the outer call resumes, `finalize`'s `commit()` restores the stale `supplied`/`borrowed`/`revenue`/`indexes`/`cash`/`last_timestamp`, discarding the nested mutation entirely without a revert — no balance check catches it because token balances were untouched.

### Impact Explanation
A nested committed mutation is wiped out. Concretely reachable corruption includes:

- Nested interest accrual (`update_indexes`/any leg) rolled back: `borrow_index`/`supply_index`/`last_timestamp` revert to pre-callback values, so interest accrued during the nested call is lost and the nested commit's other effects (e.g., minted revenue shares inside `supplied`) are erased — direct theft of unclaimed protocol revenue / yield.
- If the nested call was a `repay`, `net_settle`, `seize_positions` (bad-debt cleanup) or `recapitalize` on the same market, its `cash` credit / debt burn / supply-index write-down is reverted while its token-side effects (tokens already transferred in/out) persist — breaking cash-vs-balance accounting and enabling either insolvency (burned debt resurrected, backing shortfall reinstated after recapitalize funds refunded) or permanent mismatch between `cash` book and token balance.
- Because the overwrite is total, even large state deltas vanish silently; the only bound is that the token balance assertions pass, which they do for any pure-accounting nested leg (`net_settle`, `seize_positions`, `update_indexes` move no tokens).

This maps to theft of unclaimed yield, protocol insolvency, and broken accounting reachable by a single unprivileged address controlling the receiver contract.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires: the market has `is_flashloanable`, nonzero flashable cash, and at least one nested controller entrypoint reachable during the callback that mutates the same market without moving the pool's token balance — `update_indexes` and `net_settle`-style legs qualify trivially and are reachable in-scope. The initiator supplies both `receiver` and `data`, and the callback runs while the stale cache is live, so a single attacker-controlled receiver contract suffices. The residual uncertainty is whether the controller's flash/reentry guard blocks *all* nested controller calls during `execute_flash_loan`; the pool itself has no guard, and `prepare`/`finalize` assume none is needed. Even a guard that only blocks user-position ops but leaves `update_indexes`/`claim_revenue`/`recapitalize` open (they are position-agnostic) leaves the vulnerability intact.

### Recommendation
Reload and re-accrue the market after the callback instead of committing the pre-callback cache, or commit only the fee delta: in `finalize`, `Cache::load` fresh state, apply `book_fee`, then `commit`. Alternatively, record a reentry/mutation epoch per market (storage flag set on every `commit`) and assert it is unchanged in `finalize` before writing; also extend the controller-side flash guard to reject nested calls that touch the flash market for the duration of the callback.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker deploys WASM receiver `R` implementing `execute_flash_loan`, calls `controller.flash_loan(hub_asset, initiator=attacker, receiver=R, amount=A, data=...)`.
2. Pool `prepare` loads `Cache` with indexes/cash at time T; pool transfers `A` to `R`; balance check passes.
3. Inside `R.execute_flash_loan`, `R` calls `controller.update_indexes([hub_asset])` (or a permissionless `clean_bad_debt`/`claim_revenue`/same-market `net_settle` leg). The controller authorizes `pool.update_indexes`, which commits accrued indexes, a new `last_timestamp`, and any minted revenue to storage.
4. `R` repays nothing itself; it approves `A + fee` and returns. Balance checks pass (pool balance restored to `pre - A` then `pre + fee`).
5. `finalize` calls `cache.commit()` with the stale T-snapshot: the accrued indexes, new timestamp, and revenue minted in step 3 are overwritten. Repeated across blocks (or combined with a nested `repay`/`recapitalize` whose token legs already executed), the attacker erases interest owed to suppliers or resurrects burned debt while its cash offset persists, degrading the market to insolvency or extracting yield.
