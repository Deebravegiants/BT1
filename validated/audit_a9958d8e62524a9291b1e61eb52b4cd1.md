### Title
Accrued debt can overflow RAY valuation and permanently freeze a market - ([File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs](common/src/rates/simulate.rs))

### Summary
A borrower can place a market in a state where a later accrual panics while converting scaled debt to its RAY-denominated value. Because every borrow, repayment, withdrawal, liquidation, and explicit index update synchronizes the market before applying its operation, the resulting `MathOverflow` permanently blocks exits and repayments. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` first calls `scaled_to_original` on both `borrowed * borrow_index` and `supplied * supply_index`; either multiplication can exceed `i128::MAX`. [3](#0-2) 

The borrow index itself is capped only after multiplication by the interest factor, but that cap does not guarantee that the already-stored scaled debt can still be safely unscaled. [4](#0-3) 

The in-repo regression test demonstrates the exact condition and outcome: a one-billion-token market at 98% utilization eventually reaches the RAY-value ceiling before the borrow-index cap; the next `update_indexes` returns `MathOverflow`, and subsequent withdraw and repay calls fail for the same reason. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

### Impact Explanation
Once the stored `borrowed` and `borrow_index` combination can no longer be multiplied in `i128`, the market is permanently bricked. Suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot service the debt, and explicit synchronization cannot recover the market because each path accrues before acting. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7) 

Even replacing the rate model is not a recovery path, because `update_params` accrues under the old model before writing new parameters. [9](#0-8) 

### Likelihood Explanation
An unprivileged borrower can reach this state through normal `supply` and `borrow` calls when the market's decimals and configured caps permit a sufficiently large scaled debt and utilization remains high. The demonstrated attack uses ordinary deposits and a 98% draw; afterward, the public `update_indexes` entrypoint is enough to trigger the overflow. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10) 

This is capital-intensive and only affects markets whose cap and decimals make the scaled-value ceiling reachable, but it requires no privileged runtime action, malformed token behavior, or third-party oracle manipulation. [10](#0-9) 

### Recommendation
Bound the market by the maximum representable value, not merely by `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. Before committing an index increase, calculate the maximum index compatible with the current scaled debt, for example `i128::MAX / borrowed`, and clamp to that bound; alternatively, perform the accrual's scaled-value calculations in `I256` and enforce an explicit terminal state without panicking. Add a regression test proving that a market near the bound remains withdrawable and repayable after the index cap engages. [12](#0-11) [4](#0-3) 

### Proof of Concept
1. Create or use a high-decimal market whose configured caps permit a very large scaled balance.
2. Supplier liquidity is added to that market.
3. The attacker supplies sufficient collateral in another listed market and calls `borrow` for 98% of the debt market's liquidity.
4. Interest accrues until `borrowed * borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX`.
5. Call `update_indexes` for the debt market; `accrue_step` panics while unscaling the debt.
6. `withdraw`, `repay`, liquidation, and later `update_indexes` calls all execute the same synchronization and continue to revert. [13](#0-12) [6](#0-5)
