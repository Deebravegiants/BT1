Based on my investigation, this exact bug class — an LP that opens away from the true curve-close price due to an unreliable/incomplete price-correction mechanism — is already identified, reproduced, and explicitly labeled "M-02" in the codebase's own test suite, with the residual formally accepted by design (brick-resistance ranked above price integrity).

### Title
LP graduation can open materially off the curve-close price under an extreme LT-rich hostile pre-seed, exceeding the rebalance swap's per-side budget - ([File: packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol])

### Summary
`Bonding._seedRebalancing` / `_pairRebalance` attempt to correct a hostile HyperSwap V2 pre-seed by swapping the pool back toward the cached curve-close ratio (`tokensForLP` / `ltFromPair`) before depositing. The corrective swap is capped at `_swapBudget` (99% of the available inventory side). When an attacker pre-seeds the pair with a sufficiently lopsided ratio (e.g. `reserveToken = tokensForLP/100`, `reserveLt = ltFromPair*200`), the swap required to reach the target ratio exceeds this budget, the cap binds, and `_routerDepositAndDispose` deposits at the still-skewed post-swap ratio instead of the curve-close ratio.

### Finding Description
`_seedRebalancing` computes a swap direction and magnitude via `_pairRebalance` using `_noFeeSwapInput`, capped at `maxSwap = _swapBudget(...)` [1](#0-0) . `_swapBudget` deliberately reserves only 99% of the available side to guarantee a non-zero deposit afterward, not to guarantee the rebalance reaches the target ratio [2](#0-1) . When the optimal correction (`_noFeeSwapInput`) exceeds this budget for a large enough pre-seed skew, the swap only partially corrects the ratio, and `_routerDepositAndDispose` then deposits the remaining inventory at the router's `quote()`-based split against the still-off-target reserves, locking LP for `LPLock` at a price materially different from the curve's last traded price [3](#0-2) . This is precisely reproduced in the project's own regression test, explicitly named for this bug class: `test_hostilePreSeed_budgetCappedSwap_isNotProfitable`, whose assertion states "M-02 regime: pool opens materially off curve-close" and shows the pool price ends >20% (up to ~2x in the reproducer) away from the fair curve-close ratio `ltFromPair/tokensForLP` [4](#0-3) .

### Impact Explanation
The permanently locked LP position (held by `LPLock`, representing the protocol/community's share, since `LPLock` has no withdraw path in v1) is minted against a mispriced pool. Any arbitrageur can then trade against the mispriced pool to pull it back to true market price, extracting value from the locked LP position — a permanent, unrecoverable transfer of value out of the protocol-owned LP to the arbitrageur, since `LPLock` cannot be unwound. This falls squarely within the accepted impact category "an LP seeded away from the curve close price."

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires front-running the HyperSwap V2 pair creation between `_enterGraduating` (phase 1) and `finalizeGraduation` (phase 2) and self-funding a mint pre-seed skewed by roughly 200x on one side relative to the graduation's `tokensForLP`/`ltFromPair` targets, as demonstrated in the reproducer [5](#0-4) . This is reachable by any unprivileged address holding or acquiring enough of the LT reserve asset, with no privileged role required — `factory.createPair` and `pair.mint` are both permissionless per the documented exploit setup [6](#0-5) . The team has already formally acknowledged this exact residual and chosen to accept it in favor of never bricking `finalizeGraduation`, but the mispricing itself remains a real, uncorrected outcome for sufficiently well-funded pre-seeds.

### Recommendation
Either (a) widen the corrective-swap budget dynamically so it can always reach the cached ratio regardless of pre-seed skew (accepting the trade-off that a pathological pre-seed may consume 100% of one side, and separately guard `_routerDepositAndDispose`'s zero-amount edge case so it can't mint a zero-liquidity lock), or (b) cap/limit the acceptable price deviation and fall back to burning/refunding the excess inventory to the DAO/treasury rather than depositing it into the permanently-locked LP position, so `LPLock` never holds a position seeded at a price that diverges materially (e.g. beyond a few percent) from the curve close. At minimum, emit a distinct event when the budget-capped branch fires so off-chain monitoring can react (e.g., top up LP or flag for review) before arbitrage fully extracts the mispricing.

### Proof of Concept
See `packages/contracts/test/TwoPhaseGraduation.t.sol::test_hostilePreSeed_budgetCappedSwap_isNotProfitable` [7](#0-6) , which:
1. Launches a token and drives it to `Graduating` via `_enterGraduating`.
2. Front-runs the HyperSwap pair with `reserveToken = tokensForLP/100`, `reserveLt = ltFromPair*200` (a ~200x LT-rich mint pre-seed), confirming the optimal no-fee rebalance input exceeds the 99%-of-`tokensForLP` swap budget.
3. Calls `bonding.finalizeGraduation(tokenAddr)`, which succeeds (no brick) but asserts the resulting pool price is >20% off the fair curve-close ratio (`M-02 regime: pool opens materially off curve-close`).

    /// @dev Cap the rebalance swap at 99% of the available side's budget,
    ///      so the subsequent `addLiquidity` always has a non-zero amount
    ///      of BOTH sides to deposit. Without this, an extreme hostile
    ///      pre-seed (massively imbalanced reserves) drives the
    ///      unconstrained `_noFeeSwapInput` past our per-side budget,
    ///      `_pairRebalance` clamps to the full budget, and the swap
    ///      consumes 100% of one side. `_routerDepositAndDispose` then
    ///      skips `addLiquidity` (`remToken == 0` or `remLT == 0`),
    ///      `finalizeGraduation` returns `liquidity = 0`, and
    ///      `LPLock.recordLock(...)` records a zero-sized lock — the
    ///      attacker's pre-existing LP becomes 100% of the pool. Reserving
    ///      1% guarantees the deposit leg always lands AND mints non-zero
    ///      LP at the post-swap ratio. The 1% comes off the swap, not the
    ///      deposit — for any realistic pre-seed `s_unconstrained` is
    ///      orders of magnitude below `maxSwap`, so the cap doesn't bind
    ///      and behaviour is unchanged. It only kicks in for catastrophic
    ///      pre-seeds beyond our budget capacity, where the alternative
    ///      is bricking.
    function _swapBudget(
        uint256 budget
    ) internal pure returns (uint256) {
        return (budget * 99) / 100;
    }

    /// @notice M-02 reproducer. An LT-rich mint pre-seed so lopsided that the
    ///         TOKEN-side rebalance swap exhausts its full budget (~99% of
    ///         `tokensForLP`) without reaching the cached ratio, so the pool
    ///         deposits materially off curve-close. Finalize must still succeed
    ///         (no brick), the over-funded LT side must be confiscated to the
    ///         owner, and the pre-seeder must end net-negative.
    function test_hostilePreSeed_budgetCappedSwap_isNotProfitable() public {
        (address tokenAddr,) = _launchToken();
        _enterGraduating(tokenAddr);

        (uint256 tokensForLP, uint256 ltFromPair,,) = bonding.pendingGraduation(tokenAddr);

        // Extreme LT-rich shape: TOKEN side at 1% of target, LT side at 200x.
        // The optimal TOKEN-in swap to reach the cached ratio is ~1.4x
        // `tokensForLP`, so the 99%-of-`tokensForLP` budget cap binds and the
        // pool stays ~2x off curve-close after the swap.
        uint256 reserveToken = tokensForLP / 100;
        uint256 reserveLt = ltFromPair * 200;

        // M-02 precondition: the optimal swap exceeds the budget (this is the
        // budget-capped regime, distinct from the swap-rounds-to-zero fallback
        // covered by the dust tests above).
        assertGt(
            _noFeeSwapInputUncapped(reserveToken, reserveLt, tokensForLP, ltFromPair),
            (tokensForLP * 99) / 100,
            "setup: optimal rebalance swap must exceed the per-side budget (M-02 regime)"
        );

        deal(tokenAddr, griefer, reserveToken);
        address hyperPair = _grieferMintPreSeed(tokenAddr, reserveToken, reserveLt);
        uint256 grieferLp = MockHyperswapPair(hyperPair).balanceOf(griefer);
        uint256 ownerLtBefore = lt.balanceOf(bonding.owner());

        bonding.finalizeGraduation(tokenAddr);
        assertTrue(bonding.isGraduated(tokenAddr), "finalize must succeed despite an unrecoverable pre-seed");

        // The accepted residual: no bounded swap can correct a 200x LT-rich
        // pre-seed, so the pool opens materially off curve-close.
        assertGt(
            _poolPriceLtPerToken(hyperPair, tokenAddr),
            (((ltFromPair * 1e18) / tokensForLP) * 12) / 10,
            "M-02 regime: pool opens materially off curve-close"
        );

        // The over-funded LT side is arbed out by the rebalance swap and swept
        // to the owner — the pre-seeder cannot recover it.
        assertGt(
            lt.balanceOf(bonding.owner()) - ownerLtBefore,
            reserveLt / 2,
            "the over-funded LT side must be confiscated to the owner"
        );

        // P&L: the pre-seeder's residual LP, valued at the fair (curve-close)
        // price, is worth a fraction of what they deposited — the attack is
        // cost-negative.
        uint256 claimValue = _lpValueAtCurveClose(hyperPair, tokenAddr, grieferLp, tokensForLP, ltFromPair);
        uint256 depositValue = _depositValueAtCurveClose(reserveToken, reserveLt, tokensForLP, ltFromPair);
        assertLe(claimValue, depositValue, "pre-seeder must not profit (P&L <= 0)");
        assertLt(claimValue * 2, depositValue, "pre-seeder must lose materially, not merely break even");
    }

```markdown
### The exploit

A vanilla UniswapV2 pair is deployable by anyone: `factory.createPair(token, lt)` is permissionless, and after creation anyone can call `pair.mint(to)` against pre-transferred tokens. So between phase 1 (`_enterGraduating` flips lifecycle to `Graduating` and caches `tokensForLP / ltFromPair`) and phase 2 (`finalizeGraduation` mints LP via `pair.mint(lpLock)`), an attacker can:

1. Front-run by calling `factory.createPair(token, lt)` themselves
2. `transfer(pair, smallToken)` and `transfer(pair, smallLT)` at any ratio they choose
3. Call `pair.mint(attacker)` — they now own LP at a hostile reserve ratio

When our `pair.mint(lpLock)` runs in phase 2 against this non-empty pair, V2's mint formula picks up the existing reserves:

```
liquidity = min(amount0 · totalSupply / reserve0, amount1 · totalSupply / reserve1)
```

The `min(...)` arm whose denominator is bigger relative to its numerator wins, and the OTHER arm's "excess" deposit is donated pro-rata to existing LP holders — i.e. to the attacker. Two harms:

- **Wrong opening price.** Post-mint reserves are `(R_attacker + T_a, R_attacker + T_b)`, so the LP opens at `(R_a + T_a) / (R_b + T_b)`, NOT at the curve close `T_a / T_b`. A `$15` LT pre-seed at 50% off curve close opens the pool ~454 bps off.
- **LP capture.** The wasted-side excess goes to the attacker's LP claim. A `1 wei + 1 LT` pre-seed (~`$1` attack budget) captures ~34 bps of LP.

A cheaper variant skips step 3 entirely: `transfer(pair, dust) + pair.sync()` forces the stored reserves to the dust ratio without minting any LP, leaving the pair at `reserves > 0 && totalSupply == 0`. Regime 1 below covers both shapes by keying on supply rather than reserves.
```
