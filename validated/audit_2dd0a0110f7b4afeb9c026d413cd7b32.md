### Title
Permanent market freeze from i128 RAY-value overflow in index accrual — an unprivileged whale can brick a market and lock all supplier/borrower funds - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
The bug class is heap/memory corruption via an overflowed numeric slot. The analog in XOXNO Lending is i128 RAY-value overflow: share-unscaling (`scaled × index`) silently panics once the product leaves i128, long before the borrow/supply index caps engage. Because every mutating pool verb accrues indexes first, once a market's `supplied * supply_index` (or `borrowed * borrow_index`) crosses the i128 ceiling the market can never accrue again — supply, borrow, withdraw, repay, liquidate, flash loan, and bad-debt cleanup all revert forever.

### Finding Description
`update_supply_index` computes `supplied.mul(env, old_index)` in `i128` (common/src/rates/index.rs:34), and `Cache::calculate_utilization` similarly computes `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` and `scaled_to_original(supplied, supply_index)` for every operation (contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:23-26). RAY values scale `asset_amount × index`; a billion whole tokens at 18 decimals is `1e36` RAY of scaled shares, and `i128::MAX ≈ 1.7e38` is only ~170× of headroom. On a steep rate curve (XLM-style, ~98% utilization, up to 200% APR cap) the index grows past that factor in a few years — before `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`/`MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` caps can engage. The harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-360) demonstrates exactly this: `update_indexes` panics with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` calls panic identically because `global_sync` (contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33) runs accrual before any verb. Unlike the heap-overflow primitive, the corruption here is permanent: there is no recovery path — accrual can never complete, so no state-mutating entrypoint can ever succeed on that market again.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds. All suppliers' deposits, all borrowers' collateral-seizable positions, and accrued protocol revenue in that market are locked forever — the contract is unable to operate for that market. This falls under the accepted "permanent freezing of funds" / "contract unable to operate" impacts, not a transient fail-closed DoS.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by a single unprivileged address: `supply`/`borrow` are permissionless. The attacker supplies a very large notional of a high-decimal listed market (18-decimal assets reach the RAY ceiling at ~1e9 whole tokens) and borrows to ~98% utilization to put the market on the steep segment of the curve, then waits for index growth to cross the i128 ceiling. The capital cost is large and the attacker's own funds are frozen too (griefing-only, no profit), which is why this is Medium rather than High — but nothing technically prevents it, and even without an attacker, an organically large market at sustained high utilization hits the same cliff, as the test shows.

### Recommendation
- Use `mul_div_floor_saturating` (or a saturating `Ray::mul`) for `supplied * old_index` and `borrowed * borrow_index` in `common/src/rates/index.rs` and `scaled_to_original` in `common/src/rates/scaling.rs`, and clamp the value (not just the index) at a documented ceiling.
- Alternatively, cap total scaled shares per market (`supplied`, `borrowed`) at `i128::MAX / MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` at supply/borrow time so the product can never overflow.
- Add an accrual-time guard that detects the ceiling and freezes new borrows while still permitting repay/withdraw with a clamped index.

### Proof of Concept
Existing in-repo demonstration: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs::a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (lines 320-360). It supplies `1e9 * 1e18` raw units of an 18-decimal asset, borrows 98% of it, advances time yearly, and observes `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay` all revert with `errors::MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` — proving the value ceiling, not the index cap, is what bricks the market. The overflow site is `supplied.mul(env, old_index)` at common/src/rates/index.rs:34 and `scaled_to_original` calls in contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:23-26.
