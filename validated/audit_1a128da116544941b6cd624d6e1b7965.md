Based on the investigation, alt.fun's `Zap` contract is the only place using EIP-2612 permits (`buyWithPermit`, `sellWithPermit`, `createTokenWithPermit`), and in every case `_tryPermit` is invoked with `msg.sender` as the owner: [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

Unlike the reported `PledgeManager::pledge` bug — where the vulnerable function accepted a caller-supplied `data.signer` field that was decoupled from `msg.sender`, letting an attacker submit someone else's address as the "signer" while calling the function themselves — none of Zap's permit-flavored entry points expose such a decoupled owner/signer parameter. The owner passed into `permit()` is hard-coded to `msg.sender` in `_tryPermit`, and the subsequent `_buyInternal`/`_sellOnCurve`/`_createTokenInternal` flows likewise pull tokens via `msg.sender`'s allowance (as seen in the `buy`/`sell`/`createToken` non-permit variants and their `*WithPermit` counterparts).
