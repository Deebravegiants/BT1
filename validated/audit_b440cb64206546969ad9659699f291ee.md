Whale-scale market hits the i128 RAY-value ceiling inside accrual before the borrow-index cap engages, permanently freezing the market - (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary
The SDL2 CVE is a crash-class bug (SEGV inside `SDL_free`). The analog on XOXNO Lending is an arithmetic crash: every pool verb begins with `global_sync` → `accrue_step`, which computes `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` and `scaled_to_original(supplied, supply_index)` as plain `scaled * index` RAY products. For a very large market these products overflow `i128` and panic with `MathOverflow` *before* `update_borrow_index` can clamp the index at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (1e36). Once reached, every subsequent call that touches the market panics in the same place — the freeze is permanent, not fail-closed-with-recovery.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` at `common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-61` calls `scaled_to_original`, which is just `scaled.mul(env, index)` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`), a panicking checked multiply. The index ceiling is applied later inside `update_borrow_index` (min of product and `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`), so it never gets a chance to run: the utilization valuation at the top of the step already overflows when `scaled * index > i128::MAX` (~1.7e38). A market with a RAY-scaled book on the order of 1e36 (e.g., ~1 billion whole units of an 18-decimal asset) crosses this ceiling once the borrow index reaches roughly 170x, which the steep segment of a kinked rate curve reaches after a few years at sustained high utilization. `global_sync` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33`) runs at the head of `supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `net_settle`, `seize_positions`, `flash_loan`, `claim_revenue`, and the permissionless `update_indexes`, so all of them hit the panic. The in-repo test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-361`) demonstrates exactly this: `update_indexes` fails with `MathOverflow`, the borrow index is still below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and both `withdraw` and `repay` then fail identically.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds: every supplier's deposit, every borrower's repayment path, and every liquidation route into that market revert in accrual, forever. No entrypoint can skip accrual, `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` also accrue first, and governance has no unfreeze path short of a contract upgrade (out of scope). This matches the "permanent freezing of funds" and "contract unable to operate" acceptance criteria, and it is a genuine analog of the report's crash class — a memory-safety SEGV mapped onto Soroban's checked-arithmetic panic domain.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reaching the cliff requires a whale-scale book (≈10^36 RAY-scaled units) at sustained ~98% utilization for multiple years on a steep rate curve — capital-intensive and slow, so Medium rather than High. However, no privileged action is needed: `supply`, `borrow`, and `update_indexes` are all permissionless, and once organic market growth plus accrual crosses the threshold the freeze is irreversible by anyone. The bug is a wrong-ordering design flaw (cap applied after the overflowing valuation), not an edge case in parameter validation.

### Recommendation
Make accrual overflow-safe at the ceiling: saturate `scaled_to_original` for the utilization read (e.g., a `mul_floor`-saturating variant like `mul_div_floor_saturating` already used in `calculate_scaled_cap`), or check `borrow_index == MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` / cap short-circuit *before* computing `borrowed_original`/`supplied_original` in `accrue_step`. More robustly, cap the effective index used in valuation at `i128::MAX / scaled` so the utilization computation can never panic while index semantics remain monotone.
