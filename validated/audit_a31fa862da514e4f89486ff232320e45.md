### Title
Timestamp-skewed oracle legs inflate collateral through midpoint blending - ([File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs])

### Summary
A dual-source oracle can combine a fresh primary price with an older anchor price by averaging the two observations. The cross-leg timestamp-spread check applies only when both sources are marked `FeedNature::Market`; otherwise, an anchor may lag by hours while remaining inside its own staleness window. [1](#0-0) 

During a market decline, the older anchor remains above the fresh market price. If the leg difference remains inside the configured tolerance, `blend` returns their midpoint rather than rejecting the observation. [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
`Controller::borrow` is reachable by an authenticated account owner or delegate and delegates solvency checks to `positions::process_borrow`. [3](#0-2) 

The controller caches prices fetched from the price aggregator for the duration of the invocation and later uses those cached prices for account valuation. [4](#0-3) 

Provider observations preserve their source timestamps and only reject future-dated data; ordinary staleness is evaluated afterward at the oracle level. [5](#0-4) 

For two legs, the outcome is stale only if either leg exceeds its own staleness limit, or if both legs are market feeds separated by more than `MAX_LEG_AGE_SPREAD_SECONDS`. [6](#0-5)  The spread bound is 3,600 seconds, while a feed may remain individually usable for up to 93,600 seconds. [7](#0-6) 

The price is then set to the integer midpoint of the primary and anchor legs. [8](#0-7)  `Outcome::failure` rejects stale or deviating outcomes, but accepts a positive midpoint inside the configured sanity range. [9](#0-8) 

### Impact Explanation
An attacker can supply the affected asset as collateral and call `Controller::borrow(caller, account_id, borrows, to)` while the primary leg has updated downward and the anchor still carries a permitted older price. The blended midpoint overstates collateral value and permits borrowing above the amount supported by the current primary price.

This can create immediately undercollateralized debt and protocol insolvency once liquidation bonus, market movement, or residual bad debt prevents complete recovery. The existing regression test demonstrates a realistic instance: a fresh primary of `$0.91`, a 23-hour-old `$1.00` anchor, 10% tolerance, 100,000 XLM collateral, and a successful 70,000 USDC borrow; the fresh-anchor control rejects the same borrow as insufficient collateral. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10) 

At the current `$0.91` value, that collateral supports only 68,250 USDC at a 75% LTV; the `$0.955` blended value supports 71,625 USDC. The borrow therefore exceeds true collateral capacity by approximately 1,750 USDC before interest or liquidation effects.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack does not require privileged protocol access. It requires a configured dual-source market where:

- the primary leg updates before the anchor;
- the anchor is old enough to differ materially but remains within its configured staleness window;
- the two prices remain within tolerance;
- at least one leg is not classified `Market`, bypassing the cross-leg timestamp-spread rejection.

Delayed oracle updates, heartbeat differences, and temporary feed outages can produce these conditions without an attacker manipulating the oracle contract. The window can persist for hours because individual feeds may allow staleness up to 93,600 seconds. [12](#0-11) 

### Recommendation
Do not blend legs whose observation timestamps are separated by more than a small, protocol-defined window. In `contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs`, apply `MAX_LEG_AGE_SPREAD_SECONDS` to every two-source outcome rather than only `Market`/`Market` pairs, or mark the outcome stale whenever the configured sources were not observed during the same freshness epoch.

For risk-sensitive collateral valuation, consider using the lower of the two valid leg prices instead of their midpoint when the timestamps differ materially. Continue rejecting outcomes outside the existing tolerance and sanity checks.

### Proof of Concept
The repository contains a reproducer in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_borrow_withdraw_liquidate_stale_anchor_blend.rs`:

1. Configure XLM with a Reflector primary source and a RedStone anchor using an 86,400-second anchor staleness limit and 1,000-basis-point tolerance. [13](#0-12) 
2. Set the fresh primary price to `$0.91` while retaining a `$1.00` anchor observation from 82,800 seconds earlier. [14](#0-13) 
3. Supply 100,000 XLM as the attacker and 500,000 USDC as lender liquidity. [15](#0-14) 
4. Call `borrow` for 70,000 USDC. The stale-anchor execution succeeds, while the fresh-anchor control fails with `INSUFFICIENT_COLLATERAL`; collateral valuation is inflated by more than 4%. [11](#0-10)
