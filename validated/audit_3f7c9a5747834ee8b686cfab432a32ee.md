### Title
Unaccounted Decimal-Precision Dust Trimming in `pallet-hyper-fungible-token` Cross-Chain Transfers - ([File: modules/pallets/hyper-fungible-token/src/impls.rs])

### Summary
`pallet-hyper-fungible-token` bridges tokens between substrate chains (e.g. `nexus`, 12 decimals for `BRIDGE`) and EVM chains (18 decimals, per `BridgeToken.sol`). Like Wormhole's `normalizeAmount`/`deNormalizeAmount`, the pallet performs a decimal-precision conversion at the trust boundary — but only accounts for the truncation in one direction. Amounts arriving from a higher-precision chain (EVM, 18 decimals) are floor-divided down to the local lower-precision balance (12 decimals) in `convert_to_balance`, silently discarding any remainder as unaccounted dust, with no refund, no event, and no re-crediting path.

### Finding Description
The scaling logic lives in `convert_to_erc20`/`convert_to_balance`: [1](#0-0) 

`convert_to_erc20` (used in the substrate→EVM `send` path) multiplies up, which is always exact and lossless: [2](#0-1) 

However `convert_to_balance` (used in `on_accept` and `on_timeout`, i.e. for messages arriving from an EVM chain such as `BridgeToken.sol`, whose `decimals()` is 18 while nexus's `BRIDGE` is 12) performs integer division by `10^(erc_decimals - local_decimals)`: [3](#0-2) 

This is functionally identical to Wormhole's `normalizeAmount(amount, decimals)` truncation described in the report. On the EVM side, `HyperFungibleToken`/`BridgeToken.sol` burns the *exact* ERC20 amount the sender specifies (18-decimal precision) and encodes that exact amount in the `Message.amount` field dispatched to nexus. There is no pre-truncation on the EVM side that rounds the burn amount down to a multiple of `10^6` (the scaling factor documented in `BridgeToken.sol`): [4](#0-3) 

When `on_accept` on nexus receives this message, `convert_to_balance` floor-divides the 18-decimal amount by `10^6` to obtain the 12-decimal local balance to mint/release. Any remainder below `10^6` wei of the ERC20 amount (up to `999_999` units) is simply dropped — it was already burned on the EVM side (destroying real value), but no equivalent credit is minted or escrowed on nexus, and no dust-tracking/refund mechanism exists to recover it: [5](#0-4) 

The same unaccounted truncation occurs in the timeout/refund path, compounding potential loss on refund of an already-lossy transfer: [6](#0-5) 

### Impact Explanation
Every EVM→nexus transfer whose ERC20 amount is not an exact multiple of `10^(erc_decimals - local_decimals)` (e.g. `10^6` for the 18-vs-12-decimal `BRIDGE` pair) permanently destroys the truncated remainder: the full amount is burned on the EVM side, but only the floor-divided amount is credited on nexus. Unlike the Wormhole report where dust merely accumulates uncredited inside `WormholeTunnel` (recoverable in principle), here the burn on the EVM side is irreversible — the value is unbacked-burned with no corresponding credit anywhere, a permanent, unrecoverable loss of user funds. Because `BRIDGE`'s supply on the EVM side is meant to be fully backed by nexus's escrow (per `BridgeToken.sol`'s NatSpec: "the supply of this token is always backed by the pallet's escrow account"), this systematic under-crediting also creates a supply/backing mismatch over time.

### Likelihood Explanation
This triggers on essentially every ordinary user-initiated transfer, since ERC20 amounts (18 decimals) are very unlikely to be exact multiples of `10^6` in practice (e.g. typical wallet UIs and `parseEther` inputs routinely produce non-round values). No malicious actor is required — any unprivileged user or a token bridger routing arbitrary amounts through this pallet will trigger the loss.

### Recommendation
Mirror the Wormhole fix: before burning/dispatching on the EVM contract (`HyperFungibleToken.sol`/`BridgeToken.sol`), round the amount down to the nearest representable unit at the destination's precision (`amount - (amount % 10^(erc_decimals - local_decimals))`) and only burn/dispatch that rounded amount, returning the truncated remainder to the sender instead of burning it. Alternatively, track and escrow any truncated remainder on nexus (e.g. in a dedicated dust-recovery account) rather than silently discarding it in `convert_to_balance`.

### Proof of Concept
1. On an EVM chain, a user holds `BRIDGE` (18 decimals) and calls `send`/transfer-back-to-nexus with `amount = 1_000000000000000001` wei (`1 BRIDGE + 1 wei`).
2. `HyperFungibleToken`/`BridgeToken.sol` burns the full `1_000000000000000001` wei from the user (see `evm/src/apps/BridgeToken.sol` burn/send behavior for this token) and dispatches a `Message` with `amount = 1_000000000000000001`.
3. On nexus, `on_accept` calls `convert_to_balance(1_000000000000000001, 18, 12)` → `1_000000000000000001 / 10^6 = 1_000000000000` (12-decimal units) — exactly `1 BRIDGE`, with the extra `1` wei's worth of precision truncated.
4. Result: user burned `1 BRIDGE + 1 wei` on the EVM chain but received only `1 BRIDGE` on nexus. The `1 wei` (in EVM precision) is permanently lost — no event, no refund, no escrow entry recovers it. Repeating this at scale (e.g. programmatic transfers with non-round amounts) causes systematic, unrecoverable value loss across the bridge.

pub fn convert_to_balance<B: core::str::FromStr>(
	value: U256,
	erc_decimals: u8,
	local_decimals: u8,
) -> Result<B, B::Err> {
	let dec_str = (value /
		U256::from(10u128.pow(erc_decimals.saturating_sub(local_decimals) as u32)))
	.to_string();
	dec_str.parse::<B>()
}

/// Converts a local u128 balance to an ERC20 U256 amount
///
/// Multiplies by 10^(erc_decimals - local_decimals) to scale up to ERC20 precision
pub fn convert_to_erc20(value: u128, erc_decimals: u8, local_decimals: u8) -> U256 {
	U256::from(value) * U256::from(10u128.pow(erc_decimals.saturating_sub(local_decimals) as u32))
}
