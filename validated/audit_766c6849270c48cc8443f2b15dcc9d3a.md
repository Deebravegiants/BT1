### Title
Flash-loan skew of Aquarius pool reserves deflates the LP share fair price and enables underpriced liquidation of LP-collateral accounts - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs)

### Summary
The Aquarius LP price source computes `price = 2·sqrt(value_a·value_b) / total_shares` (constant product) or `D·min(price_a, price_b) / total_shares` (stable) from reserves and share supply read live from the pool contract inside the same transaction that consumes the price. This is the same bug class as the Array Finance incident: the collateral price is derived from AMM state an unprivileged trader can move within one transaction. The fair-value formula is only manipulation-resistant in the inflation direction — skewing reserves away from balance strictly lowers the computed LP value, so an attacker can flash-borrow, push the pool off balance, liquidate LP-collateral positions at the deflated price, and restore the pool, all in one transaction.

### Finding Description
`aquarius::read` fetches `reserve_a`, `reserve_b`, and `total_shares` via `aquarius_pool_reserves_call` / `aquarius_total_shares_call`, i.e., the pool's own `get_reserves`/`get_total_shares` at call time [1](#0-0) . The constant-product fair value is `2·sqrt(value_a·value_b)` [2](#0-1) , which for fixed external leg prices is maximized when the two leg values are equal and decreases monotonically as the pool is skewed — so a large swap through the pool strictly lowers the reported LP price. `total_shares` cannot be inflated for free (minting shares requires a proportional deposit), so the Array-style dilution leg is closed, but the reserve-skew leg is not.

The only on-chain backstop is the configured sanity band: LP oracles are sole-source and explicitly waive tolerance and smoothing checks — "Sole-source by construction, so the band is the only backstop" [3](#0-2) . Deployed LP bands are wide — e.g., `AQUAUSDC_LP` admits a range of roughly 0.0129 to 0.129 WAD, about a 10× band [4](#0-3) . Because the pool's skewed reserves still satisfy `min_pool_value_wad` easily and leg prices come from external feeds that the swap does not move, the manipulated price is accepted whenever it lands inside the band.

The controller consumes this price in `Context` when computing health factor for `liquidate`. Any account supplying the Aquarius LP share token as collateral can therefore be pushed below HF = 1 by a within-transaction reserve skew, and the liquidator seizes LP collateral valued at the depressed price while the real redemption value is unchanged.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency: the attacker liquidates LP-collateral borrowers that are actually solvent at the true fair price, seizing their LP shares plus the HF-based liquidation bonus. The seized collateral redeems for more than the debt repaid once the pool is restored. The theft is bounded by (a) the sanity band width (up to ~10× deflation on configured LP markets), (b) the liquidation bonus curve, and (c) how much debt the victim accounts carry — consistent with Medium severity.

### Likelihood Explanation
Fully unprivileged: the attacker needs only (1) a flash loan or own capital in one pool leg, (2) a swap on the Aquarius pool itself (`own trades on Aquarius` is in scope), (3) a `controller::liquidate` call, and (4) a swap back. Aquarius pools for the listed LP collateral can be shallow relative to the collateral they back; `is_flashloanable` on the borrowing side and `flash_position` provide capital-free leverage. The attack fails only if the skewed price exits the sanity band or the pool's `min_pool_value_wad` floor — on the 10×-banded markets there is ample room. The main residual uncertainty is real Aquarius pool depth on mainnet, which determines the swap size needed.

### Recommendation
Make the LP price skew-resistant, not just band-clamped:
- Price LP shares from virtual/TWAP reserves rather than instantaneous `get_reserves` (e.g., Aquarius TWAP or a cached reserve snapshot older than the transaction), or compute per-leg value against the balanced invariant using only the smaller leg, e.g., `2·min(value_a, value_b)` for constant product.
- Tighten `MAX_LP_SANITY_BAND_BPS` so the band cannot admit a multi-X depression (the current LP cap permits the ~10× `AQUAUSDC_LP` band).
- Consider requiring LP-collateral liquidations to resolve a second independent price source, removing the `has_aquarius_lp_source` waiver of dual-source tolerance checks.

### Proof of Concept
1. Victim supplies Aquarius `AQUA/USDC` LP shares as collateral in hub 3 and borrows a flashloanable asset; HF ≈ 1.1 at the true LP price.
2. Attacker calls `controller::flash_loan` (or `flash_position`) to borrow a large amount of USDC, or uses own funds.
3. In the flash receiver, attacker swaps USDC → AQUA on the Aquarius pool `CA6GAFOJCW4MGQQBUCQUSA3CLIH25G4SNKB2JHYKZCVWZTNW5VXMSC4O`. Reserves become `(USDC + x, AQUA − y)`, and `2·sqrt(value_usdc·value_aqua)` falls proportionally to the imbalance — e.g., a 4:1 value skew yields roughly a 20% price depression, well inside the 0.0129–0.1285 WAD band.
4. In the same transaction, attacker calls `controller::liquidate(liquidator_account, victim_account, debt_asset, amount, ...)`. The `Context` resolves the LP collateral through `aquarius::read`, which reads the skewed reserves [1](#0-0) , prices the victim's collateral ~20% low, pushes computed HF below 1, and the multi-leg seizure transfers LP shares to the attacker at the depressed valuation plus bonus.
5. Attacker swaps back on the pool (reserves restore, the attacker's AQUA returns to USDC minus swap fees), repays the flash loan plus fee, and keeps the liquidation profit: `seized_LP_value_true − repaid_debt − 2·swap_fee − flash_fee`.
6. Net result: victim loses collateral exceeding their true under-collateralization (they were solvent at fair price); attacker profit is bounded only by victim debt size, the bonus curve, and the band width.
