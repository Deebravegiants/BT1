# No vulnerability found for this question.

**Analysis supporting this conclusion:**

The scenario hinges on the caller explicitly using `createNearWithdrawalRoute(msg)`, which is a documented, exported factory function [1](#0-0)  that produces `{ route: RouteEnum.NearWithdrawal, msg }`. This is the *only* way `routeConfig.route` becomes `NearWithdrawal`, and the SDK's bridge selection is deterministic based on this field, not ambiguous or coincidental.

`PoaBridge.supports()` explicitly rejects any `routeConfig` whose `route` isn't `PoaBridge`: [2](#0-1)  — so when the caller passes `createNearWithdrawalRoute(msg)`, `PoaBridge.supports()` returns `false` and is skipped entirely, never falling through by accident.

`DirectBridge.supports()` accepts any `nep141` asset (including `bch.omft.near`) when `routeConfig.route === NearWithdrawal`: [3](#0-2) , and `parseAssetId` for `DirectBridge` always reports `blockchain: Chains.Near`: [4](#0-3) . This is consistent for both the `supports()` selection stage in `IntentsSDK.createWithdrawalIntents` and any subsequent `createWithdrawalIdentifier` call — both resolve to `DirectBridge`/`Chains.Near`, with no divergence between the bridge chosen for intent-building and the bridge later used for status reporting.

`DirectBridge.createWithdrawalIntents` forwards the caller-supplied `msg` verbatim into the `ft_withdraw` intent, which becomes an `ft_transfer_call(receiver_id, amount, msg)` on the NEAR token contract: [5](#0-4) . This is exactly the intended, documented behavior of the "direct withdrawal" escape hatch — it lets a caller bypass the PoA/Omni bridge automation entirely and directly interact with the NEAR-side token contract (e.g., to call `ft_transfer_call` on an arbitrary NEAR receiver with an arbitrary `msg`), which is precisely why the factory function exists and is exported. The `destinationAddress`/`receiver_id` and `msg` are all supplied and controlled by the same caller who chose this route — there is no other party's funds misdirected by a third party's unrelated action; the caller is choosing where their own signed intent sends the tokens on NEAR.

Per the audit rules, this is "an integrator deliberately misusing a documented escape hatch" (`createNearWithdrawalRoute`) rather than a genuine bridge-selection/custodian-mismatch bug: the bridge `supports()` chosen and the bridge used for `createWithdrawalIdentifier`/status agree, and match what the caller explicitly requested via `routeConfig.route`. There is no divergence between the bridge selected and the custodian implied by the caller's own explicit route choice.
