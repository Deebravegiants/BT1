### Title
Unbounded accrual math permanently freezes any market once `shares × index` exceeds `i128` — attacker-seedable via `update_indexes` - (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary
The CVE-2020-23333 class (unchecked size/arithmetic overflow → denial of service) maps onto XOXNO Lending as an `i128` overflow in the interest-accrual step. Every mutating pool path runs `global_sync` → `accrue_step` first, and `accrue_step` computes `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` and `scaled_to_original(supplied, supply_index)`, i.e. `shares × index / RAY`. Both `borrow_index` and `supply_index` are only capped at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`/`MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` (10^36 raw, i.e. ~10^9×), while the *value* `shares × index / RAY` must fit `i128` much earlier. Once a market's scaled book is large enough that `scaled_shares × current_index / RAY > i128::MAX`, `mul_div_half_up`/`Ray::mul` panics with `MathOverflow` inside accrual — before the index cap can ever engage — and the market is bricked forever: `update_indexes`, `supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `recapitalize` all accrue first and all revert. The codebase's own long-horizon test demonstrates exactly this cliff and that "the index cap never engages" while "no repay, no withdraw, no liquidation" succeed.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` (common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-62) unconditionally evaluates:

- `borrowed_original = scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` → `borrowed.mul(index)` → `mul_div_half_up(borrowed, index, RAY)`
- `supplied_original = scaled_to_original(supplied, supply_index)`
- `update_supply_index` additionally computes `supplied.mul(old_index)` again.

`mul_div_half_up`/`Ray::mul` panic with `GenericError::MathOverflow` when the exact quotient does not fit `i128` (`try_mul_div_half_up` returns `None` on `to_i128()` failure). The panic is *not* a function of inputs the caller can shrink: it depends only on committed market state (`borrowed`, `supplied`) and the monotone `borrow_index`. `borrow_index` only grows (`update_borrow_index` is its sole writer and is monotone), so once `borrowed × borrow_index / RAY > i128::MAX`, no state change can ever bring it back under the limit — the panic is permanent.

`global_sync` (contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33) runs this accrual at the head of every market mutation, and the permissionless `update_indexes` entrypoint runs it too. The dedicated harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-360) confirms: after the cliff, `try_update_indexes_for`, `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` all return `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, i.e. the designed safety cap is unreachable.

Reachability by a single unprivileged address: the attacker supplies the dominant share of a market (any listed asset — caps are set per market and validated only against the representable asset domain, i.e. up to `i128::MAX / 10^(27-d)` units), borrows a large fraction of it against collateral in another market (hub/spoke allows this with the attacker's own collateral leg), then lets accrual run — or simply calls `update_indexes` periodically. Because `borrowed` in RAY shares can reach ~10^37-10^38 for high-decimal assets at lifted caps, `borrow_index` need only grow to low single digits (≈1 year at the steep `MAX_BORROW_RATE_RAY` segment, sooner for larger books) before `borrowed × index` overflows. No privileged action is needed: `update_indexes` is permissionless and the panic is self-sustaining once triggered.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds / contract unable to operate. Once the market crosses the ray-value ceiling, every entrypoint touching it reverts in `global_sync` before any state transition: suppliers cannot `withdraw`, borrowers cannot `repay` (so positions can't be closed voluntarily), liquidators cannot `liquidate`, and `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` cannot be used to unstick it. All supplier principal and accrued yield in that market is frozen permanently; underwater borrowers can never be liquidated, so the market is also insolvent-in-practice. This is worse than a transient DoS because `borrow_index` is monotone — there is no recovery path short of a contract upgrade.

### Likelihood Explanation
Low-to-moderate per market, but the precondition is purely economic, not privileged. It requires (a) a scaled share book large enough that `shares × index` can exceed `i128::MAX` before `index` hits the 10^9× cap — i.e. books approaching the representable ceiling, most plausible for high-decimal listed assets with lifted/lenient caps, exactly the configuration the protocol's own test exercises — and (b) sustained utilization so `borrow_index` keeps compounding. The attacker controls both: their own supply fills the book, their own borrow keeps utilization high, and permissionless `update_indexes` advances accrual. Index growth cannot be reversed, so the condition, once reached, is permanent. The protocol documents the ceiling exists (INV-IDX-01: "Debt-value overflow can still revert accrual before that ceiling is reached") but treats it as an edge bound rather than an attacker-steerable freeze.

### Recommendation
Make accrual fail-safe instead of panicking on the value ceiling:

- In `accrue_step`, compute `scaled_to_original` through `try_`/`saturating` variants (`mul_div_floor_saturating` already exists in `common/src/math/fp_core.rs`) and clamp the unscaled value to `i128::MAX` rather than reverting; utilization saturates at 100%, which naturally drives `borrow_index` toward its 10^36 cap — the designed terminal state — instead of a panic.
- Alternatively/additionally, enforce at supply/borrow time that `supplied` and `borrowed` stay below `i128::MAX / MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` and `i128::MAX / MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` respectively (a hard share-book cap), so `shares × index` can never overflow at any reachable index.
- Ensure `update_supply_index`'s `supplied.mul(old_index)` gets the same treatment, since it overflows on the same precondition.

### Proof of Concept
The repository already contains an executable PoC: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs::a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (lines 320-360). It:

1. Lists an 18-decimal market, lifts caps to the representable domain, and has a single account supply `10^9 × 10^18` units and borrow 98% of it (`supply_raw`, `borrow_raw` — both unprivileged).
2. Advances time in yearly steps calling the permissionless `update_indexes` (`try_update_indexes_for`) until it returns `MATH_OVERFLOW`, with `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` — the value ceiling is hit before the designed cap.

pub fn accrue_step(
    env: &Env,
    params: &MarketParams,
    borrowed: Ray,
    supplied: Ray,
    borrow_index: Ray,
    supply_index: Ray,
    delta_ms: u64,
) -> AccrualStep {
    let borrowed_original = scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index);
    let supplied_original = scaled_to_original(env, supplied, supply_index);
    let util = utilization(env, borrowed_original, supplied_original);
    let borrow_rate = calculate_borrow_rate(env, util, params);
    let interest_factor = compound_interest(env, borrow_rate, delta_ms);

    let new_borrow_index = update_borrow_index(env, borrow_index, interest_factor);

    let (supplier_rewards, protocol_fee) =
        calculate_supplier_rewards(env, params, borrowed, new_borrow_index, borrow_index);

    let new_supply_index = update_supply_index(env, supplied, supply_index, supplier_rewards);
    let supplier_shortfall = supply_index_reward_shortfall(
        env,
        supplied,
        supply_index,
        new_supply_index,
        supplier_rewards,
    );

    let protocol_reward = protocol_fee.checked_add(env, supplier_shortfall);
    // Shares are valued at the new supply index, which the caller stores for
    // this step.
    let revenue_shares = if protocol_reward == Ray::ZERO {
        Ray::ZERO
    } else {
        protocol_fee_shares(env, protocol_reward, new_supply_index, supplied)
    };

    AccrualStep {
        borrow_index: new_borrow_index,
        supply_index: new_supply_index,
        revenue_shares,
    }
}

pub(crate) fn global_sync(env: &Env, cache: &mut Cache) {
    if !cache.needs_accrual() {
        return;
    }

    let mut remaining = cache.elapsed_ms();
    while let Some(nonzero) = NonZeroU64::new(remaining) {
        let chunk = nonzero.get().min(MAX_COMPOUND_DELTA_MS);
        accrue_chunk(env, cache, chunk);
        remaining = remaining.saturating_sub(chunk);
    }

    cache.mark_accrued();
}

/// Applies one compound step of `delta_ms` to indexes and protocol revenue.
///
/// The arithmetic lives in [`accrue_step`], shared with the read-only
/// `simulate_update_indexes` so the view and the mutator cannot drift.
fn accrue_chunk(env: &Env, cache: &mut Cache, delta_ms: u64) {
    let step = accrue_step(
        env,
        cache.params(),
        cache.borrowed(),
        cache.supplied(),
        cache.borrow_index(),
        cache.supply_index(),
        delta_ms,
    );

    cache.set_borrow_index(step.borrow_index);
    cache.set_supply_index(step.supply_index);
    cache.accrue_revenue(step.revenue_shares);
}

pub fn mul_div_half_up(env: &Env, x: i128, y: i128, d: i128) -> i128 {
    // The zero check runs first so debug and release builds agree on a zero
    // divisor: both surface `DivisionByZero` rather than tripping the assert.
    require_nonzero_divisor(env, d);
    debug_assert!(
        x >= 0 && y >= 0 && d > 0,
        "mul_div_half_up: non-negative x, y and positive d"
    );
    try_mul_div_half_up(env, x, y, d)
        .unwrap_or_else(|| panic_with_error!(env, GenericError::MathOverflow))
}

/// Computes `x * y / d` rounded half up. Returns `None` if `x < 0`, `y < 0`, `d <= 0`, or the
/// result does not fit in `i128`.
pub fn try_mul_div_half_up(env: &Env, x: i128, y: i128, d: i128) -> Option<i128> {
    if x < 0 || y < 0 || d <= 0 {
        return None;
    }
    let half = d / 2;

    // Fast path: the biased product fits `i128`, so the whole computation is
    // native. `x * y + half` is non-negative here, so `/` is the floor the
    // widened path would produce.
    if let Some(biased) = x
        .checked_mul(y)
        .and_then(|product| product.checked_add(half))
    {
        return Some(biased / d);
    }

    let (x256, y256, d256) = to_i256_operands(env, x, y, d);
    x256.mul(&y256)
        .add(&I256::from_i128(env, half))
        .div(&d256)
        .to_i128()
}

/// `i128::MAX`, 170 times that. At the XLM curve's steep segment the index
/// grows past 170x in a few years, and the next accrual panics inside
/// `scaled_to_original`. Every verb accrues first, so the market freezes:
/// no repay, no withdraw, no liquidation. The index cap never engages.
#[test]
fn a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap() {
    let mut t = LendingTest::new()
        .with_market(big("BIG18", 18, xlm_curve()))
        .with_market(col())
        .with_max_utilization_disabled_all_markets()
        .build();
    lift_caps(&t, "BIG18", 18);
    lift_caps(&t, "COL", 7);
    let principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18);
    t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);
    let debt = principal / 100 * 98;
    t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
    t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);

    let mut years = 0u32;
    let failure = loop {
        years += 1;
        assert!(
            years <= 40,
            "no cliff within 40 years; the bound in docs/reference/formulas.md is wrong"
        );
        t.advance_time(YEAR_SECS);
        if let Err(e) = t.try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"]) {
            break e;
        }
    };

        last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY,
        "the index cap did not engage before the value overflow"
    );
    // The market is frozen: exits and repayments accrue first and hit the same panic.
    assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    assert_contract_error(t.try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    std::println!(
        "ray-value cliff reached after {years} years at 98 percent utilization on the XLM curve; last index x{:.1}",
        last.borrow_index as f64 / RAY as f64
    );

Both indexes start at one RAY. Successful accrual with validated rate parameters
cannot lower the borrow index and caps it at the protocol constant 10^36 raw
