### Title
Accrual-time `i128` overflow in `scaled_to_original` permanently freezes a market before the borrow-index cap engages - (common/src/rates/scaling.rs)

### Summary
The CVE-2020-15572 class is a reachable crash that aborts processing (OOB memory access → remote DoS on Tor+NSS). The on-chain analog in XOXNO Lending is a reachable, uncatchable arithmetic trap in the accrual path: `accrue_step` computes `borrowed * borrow_index` via `scaled_to_original`, which panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` once the scaled debt value exceeds `i128::MAX`. Because every state-changing entrypoint on a market accrues first, a market that crosses this RAY-value ceiling can never be touched again — no supply, borrow, repay, withdraw, liquidate, clean_bad_debt, recapitalize, or update_indexes — permanently freezing all supplier and collateral funds held by the pool for that market. The `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap that should bound index growth never engages, because the value multiplication traps first.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` (common/src/rates/simulate.rs) unscale multiplies the scaled borrow total before doing anything else:

- `common/src/rates/simulate.rs` — `accrue_step` calls `scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)` and `scaled_to_original(env, supplied, supply_index)` at lines 60-61.
- `common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16` — `scaled_to_original` is `scaled.mul(env, index)`, a checked `Ray` multiply that panics on overflow (via `fp_core` / `checked_add`-family arithmetic).
- `common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19` — `update_borrow_index` clamps the *index* at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (10^36 raw), but that clamp runs after the value multiplication in `calculate_supplier_rewards` (lines 80-83) and after the unscale at line 60 of `accrue_step`. A market whose scaled `borrowed` is ~1e36 RAY (e.g., one billion units of an 18-decimal token) hits the `i128::MAX` ceiling (~1.7e38) at a borrow index of only ~170 RAY — far below the 1e36 index cap.
- `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33` — `global_sync` runs this accrual at the head of every mutation on the market. Once `borrowed * borrow_index` no longer fits `i128`, every subsequent call traps identically; `last_timestamp` never advances past the fatal chunk and no state transition is possible.

The panic is not caller-caused and not recoverable by argument choice: any `update_indexes`, `supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, or `recapitalize` touching the market invokes `global_sync` on the same stored `(borrowed, borrow_index)` pair and traps at the same multiplication. The repo's own harness test pins this exact behavior: `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:315-356` asserts `MathOverflow` on `update_indexes` and that `withdraw`/`repay` then revert with the same error — "the market is frozen: exits and repayments accrue first and hit the same panic. The index cap never engages."

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds. All supplier balances in the affected market — and any collateral positions whose operations require that market's accrual — are locked in the pool contract indefinitely, and outstanding debt can never be repaid or liquidated. This matches the "permanent freezing of funds" and "contract unable to operate" impact classes. Unlike a fail-closed oracle outage (ADR-0005), this freeze is not conditional on external state and does not resolve.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by unprivileged addresses through `supply` + `borrow` + time, but with real preconditions: an attacker (or organic whale activity) must drive a market's scaled debt to near the `i128` value ceiling and sustain high utilization long enough for `borrow_index` to multiply the scaled total past 1.7e38. The documented test scenario uses a billion whole tokens of an 18-decimal asset at ~98% utilization on a steep rate curve, reaching the cliff in a few years of compounding — before the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap. The condition is protocol-reachable without privilege (caps permitting), but requires extreme scale or long horizons, which is why `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` was presumably assumed to be the binding bound. The boundary is arithmetic, not governance-enforced, so no privileged action is needed to trigger it and none exists in-scope to unwind it (delisting/`recapitalize` still accrues first).

### Recommendation
Make the accrual step fail-safe at the value ceiling rather than trapping:

- In `accrue_step`, saturate the unscaled debt/supply values instead of panicking — e.g., clamp `scaled * index` at `i128::MAX` (utilization is already capped at 1 RAY by rate selection, so a saturated numerator still yields a correct-or-capped rate decision). Alternatively, reorder so `update_borrow_index` clamps the index to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` *before* any `borrowed * new_borrow_index` multiplication, and cap `new_total_debt`/`interest` at the representable bound.
- The same saturation must be applied in `update_supply_index` (`supplied.mul(old_index)`) and `supply_index_reward_shortfall` for the supply side.
- Add a regression test that accrual at the value ceiling continues to update `last_timestamp` and permits `repay`/`withdraw` even if index growth has effectively stopped — mirroring the existing cliff test in `large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs` but asserting continued operability.

### Proof of Concept
Encoded in the repo's own test `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-356`:

1. Unprivileged `supply` of ~1e9 whole units of an 18-decimal listed asset (scaled `supplied`/`borrowed` ≈ 1e36 RAY) and `borrow` of ~98% of it.
2. Advance ledger time; each `update_indexes` call compounds `borrow_index` through `global_sync` → `accrue_step`.
3. Once `borrow_index` exceeds ~170 RAY, `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` overflows `i128` and panics `GenericError::MathOverflow` — while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (the cap never engages).
4. Thereafter `withdraw` and `repay` both revert with `MathOverflow` because they accrue first; the market's funds are permanently frozen, confirmed by the test's assertions at lines 354-356.
