### Title
Unprivileged third-party `supply` top-up force-restamps a victim's LTV/LT tuple and can force the account into liquidation - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs)

### Summary
Analogous to CVE-2018-1000077 (improper input validation on a gem attribute), `controller::supply` accepts a `caller`/`account_id` combination without validating that the caller controls the account when the call has a side effect beyond crediting tokens: merging the leg invokes `refresh_supply_risk_params` with `RiskRefreshScope::FullTuple`, which overwrites the position's cached `loan_to_value` **and** `liquidation_threshold` with the currently listed values. Unlike `update_account_threshold`, which reverts unless the account clears a health-factor floor when restamping risk parameters, the supply path performs no solvency or health check after the restamp. Any unprivileged address can therefore force a victim's collateral leg onto newer, stricter risk parameters.

### Finding Description
`process_supply` calls `require_third_party_existing_supply`, which only checks that each supplied `hub_asset` already exists in `account.supply_positions` (contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs:78-97). After `process_deposit`, `merge_supply_leg` unconditionally runs `refresh_supply_risk_params(..., RiskRefreshScope::FullTuple)` before writing the position back (contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs:288-296), and `finalize_position_flow` is invoked with `PositionSides::Supply` and no post-pool solvency/health-factor enforcement (supply.rs:65-72). The permissionless-entrypoint registry confirms only INV-AUTH-03 is intended (a third party "cannot open a new asset slot") — nothing bounds the risk restamp (scripts/permissionless_entrypoints.txt:69). The differential test `poc_third_party_top_up_force_restamps_ltv` already pins the LTV half of this behavior: a 1-unit third-party top-up restamps the leg to a newly listed LTV of 5000 and blocks the victim's borrow with `INSUFFICIENT_COLLATERAL` (tests/test-harness/tests/controller/security_audit.rs:507-532).

### Impact Explanation
The same `FullTuple` restamp also writes `liquidation_threshold`, which the health factor reads. After governance tightens a market's LT (a routine, timelocked-but-frequent operation), any account sitting near the boundary retains its old cached tuple until a keeper `update_account_threshold` restamps it — and that path is guarded by a health-factor floor precisely so it cannot push an account into liquidation (permissionless_entrypoints.txt:76). The supply top-up bypasses that guard entirely: an attacker dusts 1 unit of the victim's existing collateral asset, the leg is restamped to the lower LT, HF drops below 1, and the attacker immediately calls `liquidate` to seize collateral at the HF-based bonus — direct theft of user funds. When LT is unchanged but LTV is lowered, the same call temporarily freezes the victim's borrow capacity (pinned by the existing test).

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires only: (1) the victim holds a supply position in the asset (required for the third-party slot check anyway), and (2) governance has tightened that asset's listed LTV/LT since the victim's tuple was last stamped. Both are common operational states. The attacker needs only dust of the asset; `supply` is permissionless and `liquidate` is permissionless. One caveat I could not fully verify within this analysis: whether `refresh_supply_risk_params` internally floors the new tuple at the old one — the restamp-to-lower-LTV behavior is confirmed by the pinned test, and `merge_supply_leg` passes `RiskRefreshScope::FullTuple` with no intervening guard, but the params file itself was not read, so the LT-decrease-to-HF<1 leg should be confirmed against `contracts/controller/src/risk/params.rs`.

### Recommendation
Gate the risk restamp, not the supply. In `merge_supply_leg`, when `caller` is not the account owner or an active delegate (state already computed in `require_third_party_existing_supply`), either skip `refresh_supply_risk_params` or use a scope that preserves the existing cached tuple. Alternatively, after a third-party top-up, enforce the same post-update health-factor floor that `update_account_threshold` applies, reverting the supply if the restamp would drop the account below the floor — mirroring the `leg_may_restamp_risk_params` eligibility pattern already used on the withdraw path (supply.rs:379-391).

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice supplies 10,000 USDC and borrows up to near her liquidation threshold; her USDC leg caches `loan_to_value = LTV0`, `liquidation_threshold = LT0`, HF ≈ 1.05.
2. Governance lists a config change lowering USDC's `liquidation_threshold` to `LT1 < LT0` such that Alice's debt / (collateral × LT1) > 1. No keeper restamps Alice's account (or `update_account_threshold` correctly reverts on the HF floor).
3. Attacker Bob calls `controller::supply(bob, alice_account_id, spoke_id, [(usdc_hub_key, 1)])`. The slot check passes (USDC leg exists), `merge_supply_leg` restamps the leg to `LT1`, and the call succeeds with no solvency check.
4. Alice's HF is now < 1. Bob calls `controller::liquidate(bob, alice_account_id, [(debt_key, amount)], SeizeMode::Transfer)` and seizes her collateral at the bonus — profit extracted, caused entirely by the unvalidated third-party write to her risk tuple.

(If only LTV is lowered, step 3-4 becomes: Alice's subsequent `borrow` reverts `INSUFFICIENT_COLLATERAL` until she manually restamps — the temporary-freeze variant already pinned by `poc_third_party_top_up_force_restamps_ltv`.)
