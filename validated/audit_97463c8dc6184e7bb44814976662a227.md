## Analog Found

### Title
Native-token fee payments in `EvmHost.dispatch()` price against a manipulable Uniswap V2 spot price with no TWAP/oracle guard - ([File: evm/src/core/EvmHost.sol])

### Summary
`EvmHost.dispatch(DispatchPost)`, `dispatch(DispatchGet)`, and `fundRequest()` — the core entry points any unprivileged message dispatcher (app or user) uses to pay Hyperbridge's relayer fee in native token — convert `msg.value` into `feeToken()` by calling the local Uniswap V2 router's `swapETHForExactTokens` at the pool's instantaneous spot price. There is no TWAP, no external oracle, and no deviation/staleness check, exactly the anti-pattern described in the Deriverse report: a critical financial computation (how much native token a fixed fee-token amount costs) is derived from a spot AMM price that any actor can move within the same block.

the same unguarded pattern repeats in `dispatch(DispatchGet)` and `fundRequest()`: [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

`uniswapV2` is a single configured Uniswap V2 pool/router read straight from `_hostParams.uniswapV2` and used for a live, unguarded spot quote: [4](#0-3) 

The SDK confirms this is the intended, documented client-side pricing path (no oracle involved, just the live router quote), and that a *different* router can even be used than the "canonical" one the SDK otherwise assumes: [5](#0-4) 

Unlike the deliberate `checkPriceGuard`/`maxDeviationBps` protections Simplex's off-chain solver applies before quoting against a Uniswap V4 pool, `EvmHost` — the actual on-chain contract every app and relaying dispatcher transacts against — has no equivalent reference-price/deviation check on its Uniswap V2 spot swap: [6](#0-5) 

Because `swapETHForExactTokens` buys an *exact* `post.fee` amount of feeToken at whatever the current pool price is (bounded only by the caller's own `msg.value`), an attacker can sandwich a victim's `dispatch{value: ...}()` call: push the ETH/feeToken pool price up immediately before the dispatch (via a swap or flash loan) so the router consumes far more of the dispatcher's ETH than a fair price would require, then reverse the price move afterward, capturing the difference as MEV profit extracted directly from the message dispatcher's `msg.value`. In the opposite direction, an attacker can push the price so far that the router requires more ETH than `msg.value` covers, causing `dispatch()` to revert (`INSUFFICIENT_OUTPUT_AMOUNT`/`EXCESSIVE_INPUT_AMOUNT`), denying native-token-funded dispatch and effectively blocking that route until the attacker's manipulation unwinds — a route rendered unable to deliver messages, one of the explicitly accepted impacts.

### Impact Explanation
Any application or user dispatching an ISMP request and paying with native token (the flow explicitly documented as the standard path in `docs/content/developers/evm/messaging/post-requests.mdx`) is exposed to value extraction proportional to the attacker's ability to move the configured Uniswap V2 pool's price within a block, and to denial-of-service on the native-payment dispatch path whenever the attacker widens the price enough to exceed the sender's `msg.value`. Because `EvmHost.dispatch` is the base primitive invoked by every downstream Hyperbridge app (`HyperApp`, `IntentGatewayV2`, `TokenGateway`, etc.), this is not confined to one integration — it's systemic to native-fee-funded dispatch across the protocol.

### Likelihood Explanation
Uniswap V2 pools with modest liquidity (the kind typically paired with a project's feeToken) are cheap to move within a single block via a flash-loan-funded swap, and `dispatch()` is a public, unprivileged, frequently-called entry point with no minimum-liquidity or TWAP requirement enforced on `_hostParams.uniswapV2`. Any relayer, bot, or MEV searcher watching the mempool for native-funded `dispatch()` calls can profitably sandwich them.

### Recommendation
Do not price native-to-feeToken conversion off the live Uniswap V2 spot reserves inside `dispatch()`/`fundRequest()`. Either: (1) require callers to pre-quote off-chain and pass a `maxIn`/slippage bound that `dispatch()` enforces (currently `msg.value` is the only bound, which is caller-supplied and does not protect against adversarial pool state), (2) source the native/feeToken rate from a TWAP (e.g., Uniswap V2's own cumulative price accumulators) or an external oracle with staleness/deviation checks, similar to the guard Simplex applies to its own Uniswap V4 venue pricing (`referencePrice`/`maxDeviationBps`), or (3) restrict native-token payment entirely and require pre-funded feeToken transfers for security-sensitive dispatch paths.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker identifies the Uniswap V2 pool at `_hostParams.uniswapV2` (WETH/feeToken) backing a target `EvmHost`.
2. Attacker observes a pending `dispatch{value: X}(DispatchPost{fee: F, ...})` transaction in the mempool from a victim dispatcher.
3. Attacker front-runs with a swap (optionally flash-loan funded) that pushes the WETH→feeToken price up sharply.
4. Victim's `dispatch()` executes `IUniswapV2Router02(uniswapV2).swapETHForExactTokens{value: X}(F, [WETH, feeToken], address(this), block.timestamp)` at the now-manipulated price, consuming close to the full `X` ETH to acquire `F` feeToken (versus the fair-price cost), or reverting outright if `X` is now insufficient.
5. Attacker back-runs to restore the pool price, pocketing the ETH captured by the artificially inflated swap as arbitrage profit — value extracted from the victim's dispatch payment — or, if the transaction reverted, the victim's message dispatch is denied until the price normalizes.

```text
## Uniswap price guards

Pool-based pricing trusts the live pool, which leaves the solver exposed to a manipulated, stale, or thin pool returning a bad quote. To bound that risk, give a position a **`referencePrice`** and **`maxDeviationBps`**. Whenever the pool quote on that chain drifts more than `maxDeviationBps` above or below the reference, the solver refuses to fill — the order is rejected before any bid is submitted.

`referencePrice` is expressed in **exotic tokens per USD**, the same units as the bid/ask curves. The two fields must be set together; omit both to leave the chain unguarded.
```
