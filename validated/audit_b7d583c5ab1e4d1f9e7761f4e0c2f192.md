### Title

Large scaled balances overflow `i128` during accrual and permanently freeze a market - (File: `common/src/rates/simulate.rs`)

### Summary

Interest accrual multiplies scaled debt and supply by their indexes before applying the borrow-index cap, so a sufficiently large market can make `scaled_to_original` overflow `i128` and revert every subsequent settlement path. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description

`accrue_step` first calls `scaled_to_original` for both `borrowed` and `supplied`, before calculating utilization, the interest factor, or the capped `new_borrow_index`. [3](#0-2) 

`scaled_to_original` delegates to `scaled.mul(env, index)`, while the underlying half-up multiply-divide panics with `MathOverflow` when the exact result does not fit in `i128`. [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

For example, 1 billion whole units of an 18-decimal asset normalize to `1e36` RAY; a 98% borrowed share therefore leaves approximately `9.8e35` scaled debt, and a borrow index above roughly `174 * RAY` makes `borrowed * borrow_index` exceed `i128::MAX`. [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) 

The cap in `update_borrow_index` is unreachable in that state because the overflow happens before `update_borrow_index` is invoked. [8](#0-7) [3](#0-2) 

### Impact Explanation

Once the market crosses the overflow boundary, any operation that accrues that market reverts, including `Controller::supply`, `Controller::borrow`, `Controller::withdraw`, `Controller::repay`, liquidation paths, and explicit `update_indexes`. [9](#0-8) [10](#0-9) 

The pool’s mutating accrual path loops through `accrue_step`, and the standalone `update_indexes` path calls the same synchronization before committing state. [11](#0-10) [12](#0-11) 

Because debt cannot be reduced without first accruing, suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, and liquidators cannot unwind the position; this permanently freezes the market’s funds absent an out-of-scope contract upgrade or migration. [13](#0-12) 

### Likelihood Explanation

The attacker does not need to modify configuration or obtain privileged access after the market exists: they can supply a large supported asset, borrow up to the configured utilization limit, and wait for normal interest accrual to cross the `i128` boundary. [14](#0-13) [15](#0-14) 

The attack requires substantial capital and a market whose configured caps permit balances large enough for `scaled_amount * index` to exceed `i128::MAX`, but the repository’s own long-horizon test demonstrates a reachable configuration and confirms that the index cap does not engage first. [16](#0-15) 

### Recommendation

Do not derive utilization by converting potentially unrepresentable scaled totals into `i128` before applying index bounds. Perform the total-value multiplication in `I256`, cap or reject the next index before any conversion that can overflow, and enforce per-market scaled-supply/debt caps such that `scaled_amount * MAX_INDEX` remains representable. A recovery path should also handle an already-overlarge market without first requiring the overflowing `scaled_to_original` calculation.

### Proof of Concept

1. Call `Controller::supply(caller, 0, spoke_id, [(debt_asset, 1_000_000_000 * 10^18)])` on an 18-decimal market whose cap admits that amount. [17](#0-16) 
2. Supply sufficient collateral in another market and call `Controller::borrow(caller, account_id, [(debt_asset, principal * 98 / 100)], None)`, leaving approximately `9.8e35` scaled debt. [18](#0-17) [15](#0-14) 
3. After enough high-rate accrual for the borrow index to exceed about `174 * RAY`, call `Controller::update_indexes(caller, [debt_asset])`; `accrue_step` reaches `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` and reverts with `MathOverflow`. [10](#0-9) [3](#0-2) 
4. Subsequent `withdraw`, `repay`, liquidation, or another `update_indexes` transaction repeats the same accrual and remains blocked; the checked-in scenario demonstrates the resulting `MathOverflow` on both withdraw and repay. [13](#0-12)

/// `i128::MAX`, 170 times that. At the XLM curve's steep segment the index
/// grows past 170x in a few years, and the next accrual panics inside
/// `scaled_to_original`. Every verb accrues first, so the market freezes:

#[test]
fn a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap() {
    let mut t = LendingTest::new()
        .with_market(big("BIG18", 18, xlm_curve()))
        .with_market(col())
        .with_max_utilization_disabled_all_markets()
        .build();
    lift_caps(&t, "BIG18", 18);
    lift_caps(&t, "COL", 7);
    let principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18);
    t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);
    let debt = principal / 100 * 98;
    t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
    t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);

    let mut years = 0u32;
    let failure = loop {
        years += 1;
        assert!(
            years <= 40,
            "no cliff within 40 years; the bound in docs/reference/formulas.md is wrong"
        );
        t.advance_time(YEAR_SECS);

        }

        "the index cap did not engage before the value overflow"

    // The market is frozen: exits and repayments accrue first and hit the same panic.

pub(crate) fn global_sync(env: &Env, cache: &mut Cache) {
    if !cache.needs_accrual() {
        return;
    }

    while let Some(nonzero) = NonZeroU64::new(remaining) {
        let chunk = nonzero.get().min(MAX_COMPOUND_DELTA_MS);
        accrue_chunk(env, cache, chunk);
        remaining = remaining.saturating_sub(chunk);
    }

    cache.mark_accrued();
