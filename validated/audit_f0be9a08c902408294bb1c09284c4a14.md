### Title
RAY-scaled debt value overflows `i128` inside `accrue_step` before the borrow-index cap engages, permanently freezing the market - (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary
The CVE class is "crafted input drives the program into a fatal trap" (DoS via crash). On XOXNO Lending the analog is a deterministic arithmetic panic in interest accrual: `accrue_step` unscales `borrowed` debt shares by `borrow_index` via `scaled_to_original`, which panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` when `borrowed * borrow_index / RAY` exceeds `i128::MAX`. Every pool and controller verb accrues before acting (`interest::global_sync` chunks the gap and calls `accrue_step` first), so once the RAY-scaled debt value crosses the representable bound, every entrypoint touching that market — `supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `update_indexes`, `recapitalize`, `claim_revenue` — reverts permanently. The intended safety net, `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` clamping inside `update_borrow_index`, runs *after* the overflowing multiply and never engages.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` computes `borrowed_original` and `supplied_original` before any index update, at common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-62. `scaled_to_original` is `scaled.mul(env, index)` (common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16), a RAY mul-div that panics on overflow (common/src/math/fp_core.rs:205-234). `update_borrow_index` does clamp the index at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY = 1e36` (common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19), but the clamp applies to the index, not to `borrowed * index`, so a market with large scaled debt hits the `i128` value ceiling while `borrow_index` is still far below the cap. `global_sync` runs `accrue_chunk` → `accrue_step` unconditionally at the head of every mutation (contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-53), so the panic poisons all verbs on that book. The repository's own integration test demonstrates exactly this: `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-361) asserts `MATH_OVERFLOW` from `update_indexes`, and that subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` calls revert identically while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. docs/reference/invariants.md:235-236 concedes the gap ("Debt-value overflow can still revert accrual before that ceiling") but treats it as an invariant caveat rather than a bounded condition, and the test itself notes the documented bound in formulas.md is wrong.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of all funds in the affected market. Suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot liquidate (liquidation also accrues first), bad debt cannot be cleaned or recapitalized, and revenue cannot be claimed — for every position, forever, since no subsequent state change can reduce `borrowed` or `borrow_index` (both only grow or stay). This is the "permanent freezing of funds" impact class.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable entirely by unprivileged transactions: an attacker (or organic usage) supplies a large position and borrows to high utilization on a high-decimals market, then ordinary interest accrual over time pushes `borrowed * borrow_index` past `i128::MAX` — the test reaches the cliff in well under 40 years at 98% utilization on the XLM curve, and faster on steeper curves or higher-rate markets. The preconditions are a large book (caps lifted/high-decimals token) and sustained high utilization, which accrual itself drives once borrow capacity saturates; no privileged call is needed to trigger the freeze, only to have configured a market large enough. Medium severity: high setup cost, unrecoverable outcome.

### Recommendation
Make accrual saturate instead of panicking when scaled value overflows: use a saturating `scaled_to_original` variant (e.g., `mul_div_floor_saturating`) for the `borrowed_original`/`supplied_original` computation in `accrue_step`, or clamp `borrowed` debt shares and force `borrow_index` to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` when the product would exceed `i128::MAX`. The index cap should engage *before* the value representation limit — i.e., derive the cap as `i128::MAX / borrowed` per market rather than a fixed `1e36` — so the market degrades to "no further interest" instead of a hard-freeze. Alternatively, give `repay`/`withdraw`/`liquidate` an accrual-free fallback path that can shrink `borrowed` even when accrual overflows.

### Proof of Concept
1. On a high-decimals market (e.g., 18 decimals) with caps allowing a ~10^9-whole-token book, call `controller.supply` and `controller.borrow` to reach ~98% utilization (plus collateral supply in a second market for the borrow). The harness test does this with `supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 10^27)` and `borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", 0.98·principal)`.
2. Advance ledger time (or wait) so `borrow_index` grows to ~170× RAY — the index itself is still far below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
3. Any call — `update_indexes`, `repay(1)`, `withdraw(1)`, `liquidate` — enters `global_sync` → `accrue_step` → `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` and reverts with `GenericError::MathOverflow` (`Error(Contract, #33)`), because `borrowed * borrow_index / RAY > i128::MAX`. Confirmed verbatim by `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:343-356`: `try_update_indexes_for`, `try_withdraw_raw`, and `try_repay` all fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and `last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` proves the cap never engaged.
