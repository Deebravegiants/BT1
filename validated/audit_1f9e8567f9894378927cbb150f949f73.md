### Title
Webhook shop/topic identity spoofing via headers excluded from HMAC signature - (File: `lib/shopify_api/webhooks/request.rb`)

### Summary
`ShopifyAPI::Webhooks::Request` computes its HMAC-verifiable content solely from the raw request body (`to_signable_string` returns `@raw_body`), while `shop`, `topic`, `webhook_id`, and `api_version` are parsed from HTTP headers that are never included in the signed payload. `Registry.process` trusts `request.shop` and `request.topic` to route and attribute the webhook to a merchant/tenant without any cryptographic binding between those values and the HMAC signature.

### Finding Description
The identity binding that should hold is: `hmac_signed_bytes == bytes_that_determine_tenant_and_topic`. In this gem it does not: `HmacValidator.validate_signature` computes the expected signature over `verifiable_query.to_signable_string`, and for `Request` that is exactly `@raw_body` [1](#0-0) [5](#0-4) . Meanwhile `shop`, `topic`, `webhook_id`, and `api_version` used by the application are read straight from the `x-shopify-*`/`shopify-*` headers with no participation in the HMAC computation [2](#0-1) . `Registry.process` validates only the body HMAC, then dispatches based on `request.topic` for handler lookup and forwards `request.shop` unmodified into `WebhookMetadata` for the app's handler to act on [3](#0-2) .

Because the headers are outside the signed scope, any party who has previously received (or can otherwise obtain) one genuine, HMAC-valid webhook body/signature pair — for example by installing the app on their own low-privilege development store and receiving a real webhook for it — can replay that exact body+signature to the app's webhook endpoint while substituting the `x-shopify-shop-domain` header for an arbitrary target shop domain and/or the `x-shopify-topic` header for a different registered topic. The HMAC check still passes because it never inspected those headers, yet the app processes the payload as if it authentically originated from, and pertains to, the victim shop and chosen topic.

### Impact Explanation
This breaks the tenant boundary the gem is expected to enforce for webhook delivery: an app relying on `WebhookMetadata#shop` (which is populated straight from the unauthenticated header) to select per-tenant credentials, records, or business logic can be tricked into performing actions attributed to, or scoped to, a shop the attacker does not own. This is a cross-tenant identity confusion / spoofing vector classified as Critical under the given rubric (cross-tenant access), since the attacker fully controls which tenant and topic the verified payload is attributed to despite passing HMAC validation.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation requires the attacker to possess at least one valid raw-body/HMAC pair, which is trivially obtainable by any developer who installs the target app on their own store (a normal, unprivileged action) and captures the resulting webhook request. No access to `api_secret_key`, access tokens, or the merchant's environment is required — only replaying/forging the unsigned headers on an otherwise-legitimate signed body.

### Recommendation
Include the security-relevant headers (`shop-domain`, `topic`, and ideally `api-version`/`webhook-id`) in the HMAC-signed content, or otherwise cryptographically bind them to the payload (e.g., validate `shop`/`topic` against values embedded in the JSON body when the topic guarantees such fields are present, or require the app to independently confirm the shop domain against its own tenant registry before trusting `WebhookMetadata#shop`). At minimum, document prominently that `WebhookMetadata#shop` and `#topic` are unauthenticated and must not be used as the sole tenant/topic identity source.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker installs the target Shopify app on their own store `attacker.myshopify.com` and triggers a webhook (e.g., `orders/create`) with body `B`. Shopify sends a request with header `x-shopify-hmac-sha256: H` where `H = HMAC-SHA256(secret, B)`, and header `x-shopify-shop-domain: attacker.myshopify.com`.
2. Attacker captures `B` and `H` verbatim.
3. Attacker crafts a new POST to the app's webhook endpoint with the same raw body `B` and same `x-shopify-hmac-sha256: H`, but sets `x-shopify-shop-domain: victim.myshopify.com` (and/or a different `x-shopify-topic`).
4. `ShopifyAPI::Webhooks::Registry.process` calls `Utils::HmacValidator.validate(request)`, which recomputes the HMAC over `request.to_signable_string` (`= B`) and matches `H` — validation succeeds [6](#0-5) .
5. The handler receives `WebhookMetadata.new(topic: ..., shop: "victim.myshopify.com", body: parsed(B), ...)` and performs its business logic believing the event genuinely came from `victim.myshopify.com` [7](#0-6) .
