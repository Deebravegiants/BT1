### Title
`paused` spoke flag freezes withdrawals of non-collateral supply and blocks repayment of paused debt, trapping users who cannot improve their position - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/mod.rs)

### Summary

In Wise Lending, `checksWithdraw` reverts with `OpenBorrowPosition` when a blacklisted (unusable-as-collateral) token is withdrawn while debt is open, locking funds that can never serve to improve the borrow position. XOXNO Lending has the same shape: the per-listing `paused` flag unconditionally blocks the exit leg of *every* supply position in that listing — including assets that are not collateralizable — and simultaneously blocks `repay` and the debt leg of `liquidate` when set on a borrowed asset. Because flags ratchet (they can only be tightened by an instant guardian call and cleared only by a timelocked owner call), affected users face an unbounded freeze of assets that serve no risk function.

### Finding Description

`execute_withdrawal` applies `FreezePolicy::AllowOnExit` to the withdrawn asset before any accounting, and `AllowOnExit` asserts `!sa.paused` (`SpokeError::SpokeAssetPaused`, #315) with no exception for assets whose `is_collateralizable` is false or whose withdrawal cannot change the account's risk totals (`contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs:226-232`, `contracts/controller/src/positions/mod.rs:264-277`). The post-pool solvency gate (`require_post_pool_risk_gates`, `contracts/controller/src/risk/validation.rs:29-60`) is the only mechanism meant to couple withdrawal permission to open debt; an `is_collateralizable = false` leg contributes nothing to `ltv_collateral` or `health_factor`, so gating its exit serves no risk purpose — exactly the blacklisted-token situation in the Wise report.

Worse, the same `paused` flag applied to a *debt* asset blocks `repay` and the debt leg of `liquidate`: `repay` is permissionless and remains callable under global pause, but reverts with #315 on a paused debt listing (confirmed by `test_liquidation_of_paused_debt_reverts` and `test_repay_debt_with_collateral_paused_debt_reverts` in `tests/test-harness/tests/strategy/edge/pause_bypass.rs:23-36,75-86`). So a borrower whose debt asset is paused cannot repay, cannot be liquidated on that leg, and cannot use `repay_debt_with_collateral` or `swap_debt` (both go through the same exit/repay leg), while interest keeps accruing (ADR-0008 acknowledges "Interest continues during listing pauses"). If collateral prices move against them during the pause, the account can go underwater with no ability to deleverage; on reopen there is no grace period and a liquidator can act in the next transaction.

Flags are a one-way ratchet: `set_spoke_asset_flags` and `edit_asset_in_spoke` can tighten instantly via the GUARDIAN role with no delay, but clearing requires the owner-only timelocked `relax_spoke_asset_flags` bound to a `flags_epoch` that any later guardian flag write invalidates (`SpokeFlagsEpochMismatch`, #319) (`contracts/controller/README.md:51-55`, `docs/explanation/decisions.md` ADR-0007). Users who don't front-run the guardian transaction — which can be executed in the same transaction as flag tightening — cannot exit.

### Impact Explanation

Temporary freezing of user funds (all supply in a paused listing, including non-collateral positions that could otherwise be withdrawn) plus forced inability to repay paused debt while interest accrues, leading to avoidable liquidation losses after reopening. Matches the report's impact class: user funds locked with no unprivileged exit path.

### Likelihood Explanation

Requires only a guardian flag-tightening action (an intended emergency power reachable instantly), not a bug or mistake. Any market stress event that prompts pausing an asset triggers it for every holder of that listing, including accounts whose paused supply is non-collateralizable and accounts whose debt is in the paused asset. Users cannot front-run reliably because tighten calls are immediate.

### Recommendation

Exempt non-collateral supply exits from the `paused` gate: in `execute_withdrawal`, skip or soften `AllowOnExit` when the listing's `is_collateralizable` is false (or when the leg contributes zero LTV weight), since such withdrawals cannot worsen risk. For debt legs, allow `repay` (which is permissionless and purely risk-reducing) to bypass `AllowOnExit` so borrowers can always deleverage; keep `paused` enforcement on entry and on the withdrawal of collateral-bearing legs.

### Proof of Concept

1. Alice supplies USDC (collateral) on spoke S and supplies/mints an `is_collateralizable = false` asset X (or later governance sets `can_collateral = false` on X via `edit_asset_in_spoke`). Alice borrows ETH.
2. Guardian calls `set_spoke_asset_flags` to set `paused = true` on X (instant, no delay).
3. Alice calls `withdraw(caller, account_id, [(X_key, 0)], None)` → reverts `SpokeAssetPaused` (#315) even though X contributes nothing to her LTV/HF, and even though X cannot be used to improve her borrow position (it is not collateral). Her X is frozen until governance passes a timelocked `relax_spoke_asset_flags` — which reverts if any intervening flag write bumped the epoch.
4. Separately, guardian sets `paused = true` on ETH (the debt asset). Alice calls `repay(caller, account_id, [(ETH_key, amt)])` → reverts #315; `liquidate` on the ETH leg reverts #315 (`test_liquidation_of_paused_debt_reverts`). Interest accrues; a collateral price drop pushes HF < 1 while Alice can neither repay nor top up meaningfully, and she is liquidated immediately upon reopen with no grace period.

Relevant test evidence already in-repo: `paused_blocks_withdraw_repay` (`contracts/controller/tests/positions/flags.rs:56-60`), `test_paused_spoke_asset_blocks_supply_and_withdraw` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/spoke.rs:1087-1098`), `test_swap_collateral_paused_collateral_reverts` (`tests/test-harness/tests/strategy/edge/pause_bypass.rs:5-20`).

Note: the freeze-and-repay-blocking behavior under `paused` is partially acknowledged in ADR-0008 and the halt-flags table; if the scan treats documented ADR choices as out of scope, the residual finding is specifically that `AllowOnExit` gives no exemption for supply legs that carry no collateral value — a gap not addressed by the ADR.
