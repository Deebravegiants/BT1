### Title
Relayer fee withdrawals zero the source-chain balance before confirming the destination `HostManager`/host-executive holds enough fee tokens, permanently burning the relayer's reward when the destination lacks liquidity - (File: `modules/pallets/relayer/src/withdrawal.rs`)

### Summary
`pallet-ismp-relayer::withdraw` dispatches an ISMP POST request instructing the destination chain (via `HostManager`/`EvmHost` on EVM, or `HYPERBRIDGE_MODULE_ID` on Substrate) to pay `available_amount` of fee tokens to the relayer, and then unconditionally zeroes the relayer's `Fees` entry on Hyperbridge itself, with no check that the destination side actually holds that much liquidity.

### Finding Description
`Pallet::withdraw` reads the relayer's full accumulated balance, `Fees::<T>::get(dest_chain, address)`, builds a `WithdrawalParams`/`WithdrawRelayerFees` payload for the full amount, dispatches it, and then immediately zeroes the source-side accounting: [1](#0-0) 

There is no query of the destination `EvmHost`/`HostManager` fee-token balance (or the corresponding substrate pallet account) before dispatch, and no cap on `available_amount` versus what liquidity actually exists on the destination. The Rust integration test confirms the failure mode: when the `HostManager` fee-token balance on the destination is zero, the `SafeERC20` transfer in the withdrawal handler reverts: [2](#0-1) 

Because `Fees::<T>::insert(..., U256::zero())` at line 177 runs on Hyperbridge as soon as the dispatch is accepted into the outbound queue — not after destination confirmation — the relayer's balance is wiped from the source of truth regardless of what happens on delivery. If the destination `HostManager`/`EvmHost` is under-funded (which is exactly the "receiving-side liquidity" scenario the LiFi report warns about for LIFuelFacet), the payout call reverts on delivery and the relayer has no remaining record of the fee to re-claim: the accounting that would let them re-derive `available_amount` was already zeroed at dispatch time.

This mirrors the LIFuelFacet finding precisely: a module moves value cross-chain based purely on an internally tracked balance/limit with no verification that the receiving side has sufficient funds to actually deliver it, and the sending side commits/burns its accounting optimistically before delivery succeeds.

### Impact Explanation
This is a permanent loss-of-funds bug for relayers, not merely a stuck-message issue: the relayer irrevocably loses their accrued, previously-earned fee balance the moment they call `withdraw_fees` against an under-funded destination `HostManager`. Unlike a normal ISMP POST request (which can be resubmitted while the receipt is unset, as shown for `BridgeToken` deliveries), the relayer-side ledger entry that represents entitlement to the funds is deleted regardless of destination outcome, so there is nothing left to retry against. Any relayer operator who has accumulated a large fee balance on Hyperbridge and withdraws to a chain whose `HostManager`/host-executive account is momentarily under-collateralized in the fee token loses that entire balance.

### Likelihood Explanation
`HostManager` fee-token balances on each destination are externally funded/topped-up out-of-band by governance/host-executive operations and are not guaranteed to always cover the full sum of all relayers' accumulated, unclaimed fees for that destination (there is no reserve/solvency invariant enforced across `Fees` sums and destination balances). Any relayer whose accumulated `available_amount` exceeds the `HostManager`'s current fee-token balance on the target destination — plausible during periods of high fee accrual or thin host-executive funding — will trigger this loss on their next `withdraw_fees` call. This requires no special privilege: any relayer with a signed withdrawal request can trigger the vulnerable path.

### Recommendation
Do not zero the `Fees` balance until destination delivery is confirmed, or make the withdrawal amount recoverable/replayable on failure — e.g., only decrement `Fees` by the amount actually settled (confirmed via a delivery/settlement callback or a `RequestPayments`-style intermediate escrow tied to a source-provable claim), or hold the withdrawal in a pending state keyed by commitment until the destination side either succeeds or explicitly signals a failure/refund path back to Hyperbridge. Alternatively, cap `available_amount` per withdrawal against a known-good destination liquidity figure (analogous to LiFi's suggestion of conservative fixed limits), and require governance to keep `HostManager`/host-executive fee-token reserves audited against total outstanding `Fees` per destination.

### Proof of Concept
1. A relayer accumulates `Fees::<T>::get(Evm(84532), relayer_address) = 250 * 1e18` fee tokens via `accumulate_fees` (see `test_withdrawal_fees_evm`). [3](#0-2) 
2. Separately, the destination `HostManager` contract on `Evm(84532)` holds `0` fee tokens (e.g., host-executive has not yet funded it, or a prior withdrawal already drained it) — reproduced directly by `test_host_manager_insufficient_balance`, which shows the `SafeERC20` transfer reverting when the host has no fee tokens. [2](#0-1) 
3. The relayer calls `withdraw_fees` with a valid signature. `Pallet::withdraw` dispatches the `WithdrawalParams` POST request and, on the same call, executes `Fees::<T>::insert(dest_chain, address, U256::zero())` — the relayer's balance is gone from Hyperbridge's books immediately. [4](#0-3) 
4. When the request is relayed to `Evm(84532)` and executed against `HostManager`, the transfer reverts due to insufficient balance (as in step 2). The relayer has no accumulated `Fees` entry left to reclaim and no automatic retry/refund mechanism restores it — the fee is permanently lost.

#[test]
fn test_host_manager_insufficient_balance() {
	let mut env = TestEnv::new();
	let manager = host_manager_of(&mut env);

	// Host has no fee tokens; withdraw attempt should fail on SafeERC20 transfer
	let params = WithdrawalParams {
		beneficiary_address: H160::random().as_bytes().to_vec(),
		amount: SubstrateU256::from(500_000_000_000_000_000_000u128),
		token: H160::from_slice(env.fee_token.as_slice()),
	};

	let post = router::PostRequest {
		source: StateMachine::Kusama(2000),
		dest: StateMachine::Evm(1),
		nonce: 0,
		from: env.sender.as_slice().to_vec(),
		to: vec![],
		timeout_timestamp: 100,
		body: params.abi_encode().expect("20-byte beneficiary"),
	};
	let evm_request: EvmPostRequest = post.into();

	let host_addr = env.host;
	let calldata = onaccept_calldata(evm_request, env.sender);
	let err = env
		.call_as_may_revert(host_addr, manager, calldata)
		.expect_err("expected revert");
	assert!(!err.is_empty(), "expected non-empty revert data");
}
