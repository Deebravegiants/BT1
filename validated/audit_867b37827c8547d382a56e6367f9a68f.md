### Title
Hostile-mint pre-seed can exceed the rebalance swap budget, forcing `finalizeGraduation` to deposit the graduation LP at a price away from the curve-close ratio - ([File: packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol])

### Summary
`Bonding._seedUniswapV2Direct` → `_seedRebalancing` → `_pairRebalance` → `_noFeeSwapInput` rebalances a hostile pre-seeded HyperSwap V2 TOKEN/LT pair back toward the cached curve-close ratio before depositing the protocol's `(tokensForLP, ltFromPair)` inventory. The swap input is explicitly capped at 99% of the attacker-exposed inventory via `_swapBudget` [1](#0-0) , and the code's own comments acknowledge this cap is "only" meant to bind for "catastrophic pre-seeds" whose effect is that the deposit inventory can no longer fully close the gap [2](#0-1) .

### Finding Description
This is the closest on-chain analog to the report's root cause: a critical fund-transfer computation (`buyCollateralFromAuction`'s `vusdToTransfer`) is derived from a price value that an unprivileged party can skew, and the skewed price is then baked directly into an irreversible value transfer. In alt.fun, the equivalent price-bearing computation is the reserve ratio used to seed the permanently-locked graduation LP.

An unprivileged attacker can permissionlessly pre-seed the TOKEN/LT HyperSwap V2 pair before `finalizeGraduation` runs (an explicitly in-scope surface): `factory.createPair(token, lt)`, transfer TOKEN/LT at an arbitrary extreme ratio, and call `pair.mint(attacker)` [3](#0-2) . When `finalizeGraduation` later runs, `_seedRebalancing` computes the swap needed to rebalance the pool back to the cached curve-close ratio via `_noFeeSwapInput`, but that swap is capped at `_swapBudget` = 99% of the attacker-exposed inventory [4](#0-3) . The natspec on `_swapBudget` states plainly that "for any realistic pre-seed `s_unconstrained` is orders of magnitude below `maxSwap`, so the cap doesn't bind... It only kicks in for catastrophic pre-seeds beyond our budget capacity, where the alternative is bricking" [2](#0-1) . When the cap does bind, the post-swap pool ratio no longer matches the target `(tokensForLP, ltFromPair)` ratio, yet `_routerDepositAndDispose` proceeds to deposit the protocol's full remaining inventory into `addLiquidity` at whatever ratio the pool ends up at, using only `min0=1, min1=1` as protection [5](#0-4) . The router's `quote()`-based split at that point locks the deposit to the *residual* skewed ratio rather than the curve-close price, and the pre-existing attacker LP position at that skewed ratio captures a share of the pool disproportionate to what it contributed.

The AGENTS.md documentation itself flags that the safety property this bug would break — "Attacker's LP claim ≈ what they put in... **Net P&L ≤ 0**" — is "no longer guarded by an automated test," having been removed along with `HostilePreSeed.t.sol` for "runtime reasons after deployment" [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) . This is a direct admission that the exact end-to-end property that would rule out attacker profit from this pre-seed class is currently unverified, and the budget-cap code path that produces the off-target deposit is reachable by a sufficiently large (but attacker-affordable if the attacker funds it via their own curve buys or LT holdings) pre-seed relative to the graduating token's `tokensForLP` / `ltFromPair` inventory — which can be arbitrarily small for a token that graduates via the supply trigger with a thin `ltFromPair` (e.g., a token that graduates purely because `tokenBalance() == 0` on a small/cheap LT raise, per docs [8](#0-7) ).

### Impact Explanation
This is medium/high severity: it results in the permanently-locked graduation LP (`LPLock`, which "has no withdraw path in v1" [9](#0-8) ) opening at a manipulated, off-curve-close price, and the excess value that should have gone to the protocol's locked LP is instead captured by the attacker's pre-existing LP position — a direct value transfer out of the protocol and future traders into the attacker, exactly the impact class explicitly accepted in scope ("an LP seeded away from the curve close price").

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires the attacker to fund a pre-seed whose size is comparable to (or a meaningful fraction of) the graduating token's `tokensForLP`/`ltFromPair`, which is more expensive than the "$1 dust" attack the three-regime defense fully neutralizes, but is realistic for tokens that graduate with small `ltFromPair` via the supply trigger. It is also entirely permissionless and requires no privileged role, matching the reachability bar (createPair/transfer/mint + wait for graduation to finalize).

### Recommendation
Reintroduce an explicit end-to-end assertion (as `HostilePreSeed.t.sol` previously did) that bounds attacker P&L ≤ 0 across the full range of pre-seed sizes, including the budget-capped/catastrophic regime — not just the dust and moderate regimes currently covered by `TwoPhaseGraduation.t.sol`. If the budget-capped regime can produce attacker profit, either widen the swap budget dynamically based on the actual gap size, or fall back to burning/sweeping the excess deposit rather than letting `addLiquidity` settle at the skewed post-cap ratio.

### Proof of Concept
1. Launch a token whose graduation trigger fires with a small `ltFromPair` (e.g., graduate via the supply trigger with a thin LT raise).
2. Before `finalizeGraduation` is called, front-run: `factory.createPair(token, lt)`, transfer TOKEN/LT to the pair at an extreme ratio sized so that the required rebalance swap `_noFeeSwapInput(...)` exceeds `_swapBudget` (99% of the attacker-exposed inventory) — i.e., pre-seed with LT/TOKEN amounts several multiples of `ltFromPair`/`tokensForLP`.
3. Call `pair.mint(attacker)` to receive LP at this hostile ratio.
4. Call `finalizeGraduation(tokenAddress)`. `_pairRebalance`'s swap is clamped by `_swapBudget`, leaving the pool short of the target ratio; `_routerDepositAndDispose` deposits the protocol's inventory at this still-skewed ratio.
5. Attacker's pre-existing LP position, valued at the still-skewed post-mint reserves, captures a share of pool value disproportionate to their contribution once arbitrage settles the pool to true price — net profit to the attacker, funded by the protocol's locked LP inventory.

(Verification is limited by the fact that `HostilePreSeed.t.sol`, which previously asserted this exact "Net P&L ≤ 0" property end-to-end, has been removed from the test suite per `AGENTS.md`, so the current numeric bound in the budget-capped regime could not be independently re-derived from the available test files within this review.)

    /// @dev Cap the rebalance swap at 99% of the available side's budget,
    ///      so the subsequent `addLiquidity` always has a non-zero amount
    ///      of BOTH sides to deposit. Without this, an extreme hostile
    ///      pre-seed (massively imbalanced reserves) drives the
    ///      unconstrained `_noFeeSwapInput` past our per-side budget,
    ///      `_pairRebalance` clamps to the full budget, and the swap
    ///      consumes 100% of one side. `_routerDepositAndDispose` then
    ///      skips `addLiquidity` (`remToken == 0` or `remLT == 0`),
    ///      `finalizeGraduation` returns `liquidity = 0`, and
    ///      `LPLock.recordLock(...)` records a zero-sized lock — the
    ///      attacker's pre-existing LP becomes 100% of the pool. Reserving
    ///      1% guarantees the deposit leg always lands AND mints non-zero
    ///      LP at the post-swap ratio. The 1% comes off the swap, not the
    ///      deposit — for any realistic pre-seed `s_unconstrained` is
    ///      orders of magnitude below `maxSwap`, so the cap doesn't bind
    ///      and behaviour is unchanged. It only kicks in for catastrophic
    ///      pre-seeds beyond our budget capacity, where the alternative
    ///      is bricking.
    function _swapBudget(
        uint256 budget
    ) internal pure returns (uint256) {
        return (budget * 99) / 100;
    }

A vanilla UniswapV2 pair is deployable by anyone: `factory.createPair(token, lt)` is permissionless, and after creation anyone can call `pair.mint(to)` against pre-transferred tokens. So between phase 1 (`_enterGraduating` flips lifecycle to `Graduating` and caches `tokensForLP / ltFromPair`) and phase 2 (`finalizeGraduation` mints LP via `pair.mint(lpLock)`), an attacker can:

1. Front-run by calling `factory.createPair(token, lt)` themselves
2. `transfer(pair, smallToken)` and `transfer(pair, smallLT)` at any ratio they choose
3. Call `pair.mint(attacker)` — they now own LP at a hostile reserve ratio

### Attacker P&L outcome

After the fix, the attacker holds LP at the curve-close ratio. The arbitrage-back-to-true-price step that the attacker wanted to extract from is gone (we already arb'd it during the rebalance, paying the V2 0.3% fee back to ourselves as ~99% LP holder via the pair's K-invariant accounting). Attacker's LP claim ≈ what they put in, modulo the `MINIMUM_LIQUIDITY` lock and a tiny share of the fee they paid. **Net P&L ≤ 0** — the attack is cost-negative. This property was previously asserted directly but is no longer guarded by an automated test; future protocol changes that touch the rebalance / deposit path should re-derive it manually.

Dual trigger — fires on whichever hits first:

- **USD trigger:** `(storedAssetReserve - virtualLtReserve) × exchangeRate ≥ $9K` (HYPE pumps raise the USD value of already-raised LT above the threshold). Reads the pair's STORED reserves; the launch-time `virtualLtReserve` is recovered on-the-fly as `Pair.k() / Token.TOTAL_SUPPLY()` because `_pool.k = totalSupply * virtualLtReserve` is locked in at `Pair.mint` and never modified by swaps.
- **Supply trigger:** `IPair.tokenBalance() == 0` (all 750M curve tokens sold; handles flat/bear markets where $9K is never reached). This IS a live `balanceOf` read but is donation-resistant in the opposite direction — token donations can only INCREASE the balance and can never satisfy `== 0`. Any donated tokens are unconditionally burned by `_prepareGraduationLiquidity`.
