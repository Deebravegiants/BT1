### Title
Unfunded overpayment refund drains pool custody — `repay`/`recapitalize` pay out the declared excess, not the received excess - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs)

### Summary
`ops::repay::apply` and `ops::recapitalize::apply` transfer a "refund" derived from the caller-declared `amount`, not from the pool's measured balance delta. When the declared amount exceeds the outstanding debt (or the backing shortfall), the excess is paid out of the pool's own token custody via `transfer_out`, with no `cash` debit and no reserve check. An attacker who calls the pool's `repay` directly with a declared amount on a market carrying no debt receives the entire declared amount back as a refund funded by real depositor custody — the refund "pointer" references money that was never received, the same shape as a UAF where a stale reference is consumed after its backing is gone.

### Finding Description
In `contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs`:

- `accounting` resolves the declared `action.amount` into `net_repay` and `overpayment` against the position's debt (`cache.resolve_repay(amount, position)`, line 44). `net_repay` is credited to the `cash` book (line 57); `overpayment` is returned to `apply`.
- `apply` then executes `outcome.cache.transfer_out(payer, outcome.overpayment)` (line 32) — a real token payout from the pool address to the caller.
- Nothing in `apply` or `accounting` verifies that `amount` tokens were actually received. The measured receipt is the controller's responsibility (`transfer_amount_measured` in `common::token`, invoked in `contracts/controller/src/strategies/legs.rs:50` and the controller repay path), but the pool entrypoint itself is callable and computes the refund purely from the declared argument.

`contracts/pool/src/ops/recapitalize.rs` has the identical structure: `applied = amount.min(backing_shortfall)` (line 52), `refund = amount - applied` (line 53), then `outcome.cache.transfer_out(&payer, outcome.refund)` (line 34). A healthy market has zero shortfall, so `refund == amount` — the full declared amount is paid back from custody.

The refund also bypasses solvency checks: `transfer_out` in `cache/cash.rs` moves tokens without debiting `cash`, and the pool's own test suite documents that neither path passes `require_reserves` (`contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3578-3588`).

The test `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` (`contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3590-3611`) demonstrates the primitive directly: a repay of `custody_before` against a zero-debt market, with nothing transferred in, results in `actual_amount == 0` credited and custody drained to `payer`.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds (Critical). The pool's `repay`/`recapitalize` are public entrypoints; the payer argument is attacker-controlled and only requires the attacker's own auth. By declaring `amount` equal to the pool's token balance on a market with no outstanding debt (for `repay`) or no backing shortfall (for `recapitalize`), the attacker receives a refund of the full declared amount paid out of supplier deposits. Repeatable until custody is exhausted; the `cash` book is never debited, so the loss is socialized onto suppliers at withdrawal time.

### Likelihood Explanation
- Reachability: the attacker supplies only their own signature as `payer` and a chosen `amount`; no privileged role, no position, no prior deposit required. The cleanest path is a debt-free market (a freshly listed market, or any market whose borrows were repaid), where `resolve_repay` takes the full-close branch and the entire amount becomes `overpayment`.
- Even on the controller-mediated path, `recapitalize` on any healthy market routes the full declared amount back as a refund — but the direct pool call already suffices, so no funding is needed.
- No oracle, timing, or liquidation preconditions apply.

### Recommendation
- Measure the inbound transfer inside the pool leg (`transfer_amount_measured` against the pool's own balance, or a `received` value passed and verified by the pool), and cap `overpayment`/`refund` at that measured receipt rather than the declared `amount`.
- Alternatively, require the refund to debit `cash` and pass `require_reserves`, so an unfunded refund cannot exceed the booked inflow.
- At minimum, gate `repay`/`recapitalize` on the controller address and enforce that the controller always prefunds before invoking, though defense-in-depth at the pool layer is preferable since the refund primitive itself is unsound.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual trace, mirroring `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody`:

1. Attacker picks a pool market with `borrowed == 0` and pool token balance `B`.
2. Attacker calls `pool.repay(payer = attacker, actions = [PoolAction { position: any, amount: B }])` — or `pool.recapitalize(attacker, hub_asset, B)` on a healthy market — transferring nothing.
3. `resolve_repay` returns `overpayment = B`, `net_repay = 0`; `RepayRoundsToZeroShares` passes on its `net_repay == 0` disjunct (repay.rs:48-52).
4. `transfer_out(attacker, B)` pays `B` from pool custody; `cash` is unchanged (repay.rs:32).
5. Suppliers' claims (`supplied` shares vs. `cash`) are now unbacked by `B`; withdrawals revert on `require_reserves` or are impaired.
