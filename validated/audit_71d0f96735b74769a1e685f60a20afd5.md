### Title
RAY value overflow in `accrue_step` permanently freezes a large utilized market - (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary
Market accrual multiplies scaled balances by their current indexes before applying the borrow-index ceiling, so a sufficiently large supplied or borrowed RAY balance can make `scaled.mul(index)` overflow while both indexes remain below their caps. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1)  Because every pool mutation loads the market through `synced_market`, which always invokes `global_sync`, the failed accrual prevents subsequent repayments, withdrawals, liquidations, revenue operations, and direct index updates for that market. [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` first computes `borrowed * borrow_index` and `supplied * supply_index` through `scaled_to_original`, and only afterward calculates utilization, the interest factor, and the capped `new_borrow_index`. [5](#0-4)  `scaled_to_original` is a checked multiply, while the index ceiling of `10^36` is applied only after those potentially overflowing value calculations. [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6)  The documented domain explicitly notes that accrued market values can exceed the RAY domain before either index reaches its ceiling, blocking repayment and withdrawal because those operations accrue first. [8](#0-7) 

An unprivileged account can reach the preconditions through `Controller::supply` and `Controller::borrow`: create or use a Normal account, supply a very large borrowable market balance and separate collateral, then borrow most of that market’s liquidity subject to its listed caps, utilization limits, collateral, and solvency checks. [9](#0-8)  After enough index growth, the permissionless `Controller::update_indexes` call for the affected `HubAssetKey` reaches `pool::ops::market::accrue`, which calls `global_sync` and traps on the oversized value product. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10) 

### Impact Explanation
Once the first overflowing state is reached, every mutation touching the market calls `Cache::load` followed by `global_sync`, so even risk-reducing operations fail before their accounting code runs. [12](#0-11)  In particular, repayment performs `ops::load_leg` before burning debt, and withdrawal performs `ops::load_leg` before resolving or paying the position. [13](#0-12) [14](#0-13)  The result is permanent freezing of supplier and borrower funds in that market, along with an inability to liquidate or otherwise normalize its debt. [15](#0-14) 

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires an unusually large token position and sustained high utilization, but no privileged role, leaked key, malformed oracle, upgrade, or third-party callback is required. [16](#0-15)  The harness demonstrates the reachable state with a one-billion whole-token 18-decimal market and 98% utilization, then shows `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay` all failing with `MathOverflow` while `borrow_index` remains below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. [17](#0-16) 

### Recommendation
Constrain admitted scaled supply and debt so `scaled * MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` and `scaled * MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` remain representable, rather than validating caps only against the token’s decimal-domain maximum. [18](#0-17)  Additionally, bound or split the value calculations in `accrue_step` so an approaching representability limit cannot make the shared accrual prelude permanently unre executable; the market must retain a recoverable repay, withdraw, liquidation, or governance remediation path. [19](#0-18) 

### Proof of Concept
The repository’s regression test constructs the condition with `principal = BILLION * 10^18`, supplies it to `BIG18`, supplies collateral in `COL`, and borrows `principal * 98 / 100`. [16](#0-15)  It then advances ledger time until `try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"])` returns `MathOverflow`; afterward both `try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1)` and `try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0)` fail with the same error. [17](#0-16)

    if !cache.needs_accrual() {
        return;
    }

    let mut remaining = cache.elapsed_ms();
    while let Some(nonzero) = NonZeroU64::new(remaining) {
        let chunk = nonzero.get().min(MAX_COMPOUND_DELTA_MS);
        accrue_chunk(env, cache, chunk);
        remaining = remaining.saturating_sub(chunk);
    }
