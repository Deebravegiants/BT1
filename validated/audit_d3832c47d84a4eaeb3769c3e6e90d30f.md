### Title
Sub-3-decimal single-collateral accounts have a debt band where every liquidation offer reverts, manufacturing unliquidatable bad debt - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/math.rs)

### Summary
`whole_unit_repayment` keeps the raw curve quote when raising it to back one whole collateral unit would reach or exceed the account's total debt (`unit_repayment >= snap.total_debt`) while a full close is not yet justified (`unit_at_bonus < full_close_ceiling`). In that band the liquidation plan seizes zero whole units, the plan is left with no seizure, and `liquidate` reverts. An unprivileged borrower can deliberately construct and hold an account inside this band, making its debt unliquidatable while interest accrues, until it becomes protocol bad debt.

### Finding Description
For a solvent account whose single supply position is an asset with `asset_decimals < MIN_BORROWABLE_ASSET_DECIMALS`, `normalize_repayment_plan` replaces the curve quote with `whole_unit_repayment` [1](#0-0) . That helper returns the untouched curve quote in the gap between two regimes: when `unit_repayment` (the repayment that backs one whole unit plus the `1e-6` margin) is already `>= snap.total_debt`, but `unit_at_bonus < full_close_ceiling`, so the plan is neither promoted to a one-unit seizure nor to a full close [2](#0-1) .

Because `full_close` is false, any offer above the kept quote is trimmed back to it by `process_excess_payment` [3](#0-2) . The seizure for the low-decimal leg then floors to whole units in `calculate_seized_collateral`, producing zero units [4](#0-3) , the leg is dropped (`seizure_ray <= Ray::ZERO → continue`) [5](#0-4) , and a plan with no payable seizure reverts. The protocol documentation itself states that "while the debt sits in the narrow band where neither change applies ... every offer reverts until accrual or a price move ends that state" [6](#0-5) .

This is not a transient oracle outage: the band is a deterministic arithmetic gap reachable by a single unprivileged address through `supply` (one unit of a listed low-decimal collateral as the account's only supply leg) and `borrow` (sizing the debt into the band), entered passively as interest accrual pushes HF below 1 while `whole_unit_repayment` keeps declining to promote the quote.

### Impact Explanation
While the account sits in the band, `liquidate` reverts for every possible `debt_payments` input, so no liquidator can reduce the debt. The position is temporarily unliquidatable; meanwhile `borrow_index` accrual grows the debt toward and past the collateral value. Once the account is deeply insolvent it exits the band only into full bad debt, which is then socialized via `clean_bad_debt`/index write-down onto suppliers — protocol insolvency borne by other users. The borrower's collateral (one unit) is also effectively frozen inside the position during the band.

### Likelihood Explanation
An attacker needs only one listed collateral asset with `decimals < MIN_BORROWABLE_ASSET_DECIMALS`, a fresh account, and a borrow sized so that the HF<1 curve quote backs less than one collateral unit while `unit_repayment >= total_debt`. Because `unit_at_bonus` is fixed by the unit price and bonus, and the curve quote shrinks as HF falls, ordinary interest accrual naturally walks the position into the band after it crosses HF = 1 — no oracle manipulation or privileged action is required. The window's existence is acknowledged by the invariants and threat-model docs, so feasibility depends on concrete listed asset prices/decimals, keeping it Medium rather than High.

### Recommendation
Close the gap in `whole_unit_repayment`: when `unit_repayment >= snap.total_debt` but `unit_at_bonus < full_close_ceiling`, promote the quote to `snap.total_debt` (a full close that rounds the seizure up to one held unit) instead of returning the unexecutable curve quote. Alternatively, allow a full-close offer to bypass the `ideal_repayment_usd` trim whenever the account's only collateral leg is sub-3-decimal, so an offer of the whole debt always seizes the unit.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance has listed asset `LOW` with `asset_decimals < MIN_BORROWABLE_ASSET_DECIMALS` and unit price `U` USD; liquidation threshold `LT`, base bonus `b`.
2. Attacker calls `supply` to deposit exactly one unit of `LOW` (position must be the account's only supply leg so `supply_positions.len() == 1`), then `borrow` of a debt asset so total debt `D` satisfies `U/(1+b) < D + Σ leg units` (full-close promotion denied) while the future HF<1 curve quote `q` satisfies `q(1+b) < U + U·1e-6` and `ceil((U + margin)/(1+b)) >= D`.
3. Time/accrual moves HF below 1 with `D` in the band: `whole_unit_repayment` returns `q` unchanged (line 287-288).
4. Any liquidator call to `liquidate(liquidator, account_id, payments, SeizeMode::Transfer)` is trimmed to `q`, the `LOW` leg floors to 0 whole units, `seized` is empty, and the call reverts. Debt keeps accruing until `D > collateral`, at which point cleanup socializes the shortfall to `LOW`-market suppliers via the supply-index write-down.

When one unit's value divided by `1 + bonus` covers the whole debt plus one base
unit of each debt leg, the quote becomes the whole debt and the leg rounds up to
one unit. That full close can pay the liquidator more than the quoted bonus.
Otherwise, when the quote seizes less than one unit plus a `1e-6` margin, it
rises to the repayment that backs one unit plus the margin, if that repayment
is below the whole debt. The seizure then refunds the margin, rounded down to
whole debt-token units. Thus such an account stays liquidatable below `HF = 1`, by a one-unit
sale or by a full close. The exception is a debt in the narrow band where
neither change applies: if the curve quote backs less than one unit, every
offer reverts until accrual or a price move ends that state. See
[whole-unit legs](formulas.md#bonus-and-target-repayment).
