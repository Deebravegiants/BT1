### Title
Stale fundamental feed inflates blended collateral value and enables undercollateralized borrows - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs)

### Summary
A two-source oracle blends a fast market leg with a slow `Fundamental` leg by averaging their prices, while the cross-leg age-spread check applies only when both legs are `Market`. [1](#0-0) 

Consequently, an observation that remains within its own long freshness budget can pull the accepted price toward an outdated value, and controller `borrow` consumes that blended price for post-borrow solvency. [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

### Finding Description
`blend` marks a two-source result stale when either leg exceeds its own staleness bound, or when both legs have `FeedNature::Market` and their timestamps differ by more than `MAX_LEG_AGE_SPREAD_SECONDS`. [4](#0-3) 

When one leg is `Fundamental`, that timestamp-spread bound is skipped even if the two observations are many hours apart. [4](#0-3) 

Provided each leg is inside its individual `max_stale_seconds` and the prices remain inside the configured tolerance, `prices` accepts their midpoint. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

The mainnet XLM oracle pairs a Reflector TWAP leg capped at 3,600 seconds with a RedStone `Fundamental` leg capped at 46,800 seconds and permits approximately 10% cross-leg divergence. [7](#0-6) 

The controller loads the strict aggregator price, values collateral and debt from that cached feed, and `borrow` requires only that the resulting LTV-weighted collateral covers debt and HF is at least one. [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8) [10](#0-9) 

An unprivileged account owner can therefore call `supply` with a volatile collateral whose slow leg still reports an outdated high price, then call `borrow` against the inflated blended valuation. [11](#0-10) 

### Impact Explanation
The attacker can borrow more than the position's current-market collateral value supports, withdraw the borrowed assets, and leave the protocol with an undercollateralized position when the delayed leg catches up. [12](#0-11) 

The regression test demonstrates this exact economics: a lagging anchor at `$1.00` and fresh price at `$0.91` inflate measured collateral by more than 4%, allow a `$70,000` borrow, while the fresh-anchor control reverts with `InsufficientCollateral`. [13](#0-12) 

This can create bad debt and protocol insolvency rather than merely producing an inaccurate quote. [14](#0-13) 

### Likelihood Explanation
No oracle manipulation, privileged call, or signer compromise is required; the attacker only waits for normal market movement while a permitted slow fundamental observation remains within its configured freshness window. [1](#0-0) 

Mainnet markets intentionally permit slow fundamental observations, including 46,800 seconds for XLM and 57,600 seconds for USDC. [15](#0-14) [16](#0-15) 

The exploit requires price movement large enough to matter but small enough to remain within the tolerance band, making repeated extraction opportunistic rather than constantly available. [17](#0-16) 

### Recommendation
Apply `MAX_LEG_AGE_SPREAD_SECONDS`, or a stricter equivalent, to every dual-source blend rather than only to two `Market` legs. [4](#0-3) 

For blended valuations used by collateral risk, prefer the lower leg for collateral and the higher leg for debt instead of averaging economically divergent observations. [9](#0-8) 

At minimum, reduce `Fundamental` feed `max_stale_seconds` to a duration comparable to the market leg and tighten tolerance where a long-delayed leg is intentionally supported. [7](#0-6) 

### Proof of Concept
1. Suppose XLM previously traded at `1.00` USD and its RedStone `Fundamental` observation remains accepted for up to 46,800 seconds. [15](#0-14) 
2. The live Reflector leg later reports `0.91` USD while the old RedStone leg still reports `1.00` USD; the approximately 9.1% divergence remains inside the configured `9091..11000` reciprocal band. [18](#0-17) 
3. Because the legs are `Market` plus `Fundamental`, the timestamp spread is not bounded and the aggregator returns approximately `0.955` USD rather than the current `0.91` USD market price. [1](#0-0) 
4. The attacker supplies XLM through `supply` and then calls `borrow` for USDC at a size that passes against the inflated `0.955` valuation but would fail against the fresh `0.91` valuation. [11](#0-10) [12](#0-11) 
5. Once both legs converge near `0.91`, the account retains the borrowed assets while its collateral is worth less than the admission check implied, leaving liquidation insufficient to recover the full debt. [13](#0-12)

```rust
/// Requires debt coverage by LTV-weighted collateral, health factor >= 1, and
/// the configured collateral floor. Debt-free accounts skip all three checks.
pub(crate) fn require_post_pool_risk_gates(env: &Env, cache: &mut Context, account: &Account) {
    if account.debt_free() {
        return;
    }

    let totals = risk::calculate_account_risk_totals(
        env,
        cache,
        &account.supply_positions,
        &account.borrow_positions,
    );

    assert_with_error!(
        env,
        totals.ltv_collateral >= totals.total_debt,
        CollateralError::InsufficientCollateral
    );

    spec_hooks::solvency_gate_checked(account);

    assert_with_error!(
        env,
        totals.health_factor >= Wad::ONE,
        CollateralError::InsufficientCollateral
    );
```
