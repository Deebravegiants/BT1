### Title
Attacker can pre-seed the HyperSwap TOKEN/LT pair before `finalizeGraduation` to permanently open the graduated LP away from the curve-close price - (File: `packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol`)

### Summary
The reported bug class is "front-run a state-changing setter that gates a restricted action, and land your own transaction in the gap before the restriction takes effect." Alt.fun's closest reachable analog is the gap between graduation phase 1 (`_enterGraduating`, which freezes trading and caches the LP-seed target) and phase 2 (`finalizeGraduation`, the permissionless call that actually mints LP into the HyperSwap V2 pair). An unprivileged attacker can front-run phase 2 by permissionlessly creating the pair and calling `pair.mint(attacker)` against a self-funded, extremely skewed TOKEN/LT ratio. The protocol's own test suite documents that its rebalancing defense is budget-capped and, for sufficiently large skews, cannot fully correct the ratio — `finalizeGraduation` still succeeds (no brick), but the LP is permanently opened at a price materially different from the curve-close price.

### Finding Description
`_enterGraduating` (phase 1) drains the curve, computes and caches `tokensForLP`/`ltFromPair` at the last curve price, freezes trading, and emits `TokenGraduating` [1](#0-0) . Phase 2, `finalizeGraduation`, is explicitly **permissionless** and can be called by anyone in a separate, later transaction [2](#0-1) .

Because a vanilla UniswapV2/HyperSwap pair is deployable and mintable by anyone (`factory.createPair` is permissionless, and `pair.mint(to)` can be called by anyone against pre-transferred balances), the documented threat model itself acknowledges an attacker can, between phase 1 and phase 2: create the pair, transfer TOKEN/LT at a hostile ratio, and call `pair.mint(attacker)` to capture LP at that hostile ratio before the protocol's own `pair.mint(lpLock)` runs [3](#0-2) . The protocol added a "three-regime defense" to keep `finalizeGraduation` from bricking and to push the pool back toward the curve-close ratio, but the fix is explicitly a swap with a bounded per-side budget, not a guarantee of price correctness [4](#0-3) .

The test suite documents the residual directly: for a pre-seed ratio where the optimal rebalancing swap would need to exceed the per-side budget (the "M-02 regime"), `finalizeGraduation` still succeeds, but the pool is asserted to open at a price that is *materially* off the curve-close price (more than 1.2x the target ratio in the test) [5](#0-4) . The over-funded LT side gets confiscated to the protocol owner and the attacker's own residual LP is designed to be worth less than they deposited (so the specific attacker is not directly profitable) [6](#0-5) , but the test does not — and by construction cannot — restore the price to curve-close; it only bounds the attacker's own P&L to be non-positive.

### Impact Explanation
Even though the attacker who mints the hostile pre-seed cannot walk away with a profit (per the protocol's own P&L invariant test), the graduated LP — which is permanently locked in `LPLock` with no withdraw path in v1 [7](#0-6)  — opens at a price materially different from the token's fair curve-close price. This is exactly the impact category the validation rules call out as acceptable: "an LP seeded away from the curve close price." Every trader who buys/sells post-graduation via `Zap`/HyperSwap trades against this mispriced pool, and value is permanently transferred to/from the pool relative to fair value with no path to correct it, since `LPLock` cannot be unwound. This is a permanent, protocol-level mispricing of the post-graduation market for that token, triggerable by any unprivileged wallet.

### Likelihood Explanation
- `factory.createPair` and `pair.mint` are permissionless on HyperSwap V2, so any wallet can pre-seed a pair for a token that is about to graduate.
- The attacker only needs to watch for a threshold-crossing buy (`_executeBuy` → `_enterGraduating`, emitting `TokenGraduating`) and then front-run/precede the (also permissionless, keeper-driven) `finalizeGraduation` call within the same window.
- The protocol's own test explicitly demonstrates the "budget-capped" regime exists and produces a materially-off-curve-close price outcome — this is not a hypothetical, it is a documented, tested residual behavior (`testFuzz_hostilePreSeed_neverProfitable_neverBricks` sweeps `seedMultiple` from 1–1000 and accepts that price stays off-target for large multiples) [8](#0-7) .
- The cost to the attacker is bounded by the size of the dust/skewed deposit needed to exceed the rebalance budget, not by the value they misprice into the pool — a comparatively small attacker deposit can permanently misprice a much larger locked LP position.

### Recommendation
Do not rely solely on a budget-capped best-effort swap for graduations where the pre-seed skew exceeds the rebalance budget. Options: (1) widen the per-side rebalance budget dynamically based on the actual pre-seed size so any hostile ratio can always be corrected before minting into `LPLock`; (2) if the correction would exceed budget, route the excess through a second `pair.swap` leg (chained rebalancing) rather than accepting a capped, materially-off-ratio deposit; (3) add an owner/keeper rescue path on `LPLock` (or a follow-up rebalancing entrypoint) so a detected M-02-regime graduation can be corrected post-hoc instead of being permanently locked at an off-price ratio.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker watches for a buy that pushes a token past the graduation threshold, firing `_enterGraduating` and emitting `TokenGraduating(token, tokensForLP, ltFromPair, ...)`.
2. Attacker calls `hsFactory.createPair(token, lt)`, then `token.transfer(pair, hugeTokenAmount)` and `lt.transfer(pair, tinyLtAmount)` (or the reverse) at a ratio chosen so the required no-fee rebalancing swap `_noFeeSwapInputUncapped(...)` exceeds `tokensForLP * 99 / 100` (mirrors `test_hostilePreSeed_...M02...` setup) [9](#0-8) .
3. Attacker calls `pair.mint(attacker)`, capturing LP at the hostile ratio.
4. Anyone calls `bonding.finalizeGraduation(token)`. It succeeds (no revert), but the final pool price is materially off the curve-close price, as asserted by the protocol's own `assertGt(_poolPriceLtPerToken(...), (curveClosePrice * 12) / 10, ...)` check [10](#0-9) .
5. The resulting LP is locked forever in `LPLock` at the wrong price; all subsequent post-graduation trades via `Zap`/HyperSwap execute against this permanently mispriced pool.

This is the most bespoke piece of the protocol. Full rationale + invariants live in [`docs/contracts-scope.md`](../../docs/contracts-scope.md#graduation); the short version:

- **Two-phase split.** Graduation is split across two transactions to fit HyperEVM's small-block (~2M gas) ceiling.

A Cloudflare Worker keeper handles the happy path; anyone can call to rescue a stuck token.
- **Brick resistance.** Phase 2 must never revert under any pre-seed shape. Empty/donation pairs use direct pair calls; hostile mint pre-seeds use direct `pair.swap` for rebalance plus router `addLiquidity` for the canonical quote-based deposit. ### The exploit

A vanilla UniswapV2 pair is deployable by anyone: `factory.createPair(token, lt)` is permissionless, and after creation anyone can call `pair.mint(to)` against pre-transferred tokens. So between phase 1 (`_enterGraduating` flips lifecycle to `Graduating` and caches `tokensForLP / ltFromPair`) and phase 2 (`finalizeGraduation` mints LP via `pair.mint(lpLock)`), an attacker can:

1. Front-run by calling `factory.createPair(token, lt)` themselves
2. `transfer(pair, smallToken)` and `transfer(pair, smallLT)` at any ratio they choose
3. Call `pair.mint(attacker)` — they now own LP at a hostile reserve ratio

When our `pair.mint(lpLock)` runs in phase 2 against this non-empty pair, V2's mint formula picks up the existing reserves:

```
liquidity = min(amount0 · totalSupply / reserve0, amount1 · totalSupply / reserve1)
```

The `min(...)` arm whose denominator is bigger relative to its numerator wins, and the OTHER arm's "excess" deposit is donated pro-rata to existing LP holders — i.e. to the attacker. Two harms:

- **Wrong opening price.** Post-mint reserves are `(R_attacker + T_a, R_attacker + T_b)`, so the LP opens at `(R_a + T_a) / (R_b + T_b)`, NOT at the curve close `T_a / T_b`. A `$15` LT pre-seed at 50% off curve close opens the pool ~454 bps off.
- **LP capture.** The wasted-side excess goes to the attacker's LP claim. A `1 wei + 1 LT` pre-seed (~`$1` attack budget) captures ~34 bps of LP.

A cheaper variant skips step 3 entirely: `transfer(pair, dust) + pair.sync()` forces the stored reserves to the dust ratio without minting any LP, leaving the pair at `reserves > 0 && totalSupply == 0`. Regime 1 below covers both shapes by keying on supply rather than reserves.

### What we shipped — the three-regime defense

`_seedUniswapV2Direct` branches on the pre-seed shape:

#### Regime 1 — no LP minted yet (~99% of graduations)

Gated on `pair.totalSupply() == 0`, which covers both a pristine empty pair and a dust pre-seed flipped to non-zero reserves via `transfer + sync()` (no `mint`, so supply is still zero). Pristine path: `transfer(pair, tokensForLP) + transfer(pair, ltFromPair) + pair.mint(lpLock)`. With zero supply V2 mints from our deposit amounts alone, so the pool opens at exactly `ltFromPair / tokensForLP` (zero gap by construction) and any synced dust becomes reserves with no LP claim. Bypasses the V2 router entirely. Keying on supply rather than reserves keeps the dust shape out of the rebalance path, where a tiny seed could otherwise let the deposit land at the attacker's ratio.

#### Regime 2 — pure-donation pre-seed

Attacker called `IERC20(token).transfer(pair, X)` without ever calling `pair.mint`. Reserves stay at zero; only the pair's balance moved. Attacker called `pair.mint(attacker)` against a self-funded dust seed. Reserves are non-zero at a hostile ratio. We:

1. **Compute the swap input** that would drive the pool ratio back to the curve-close ratio under the no-fee constant-product model: `s = sqrt(reserveIn · reserveOut · targetN / targetD) − reserveIn`, capped at our per-side budget.
