### Title
A stale-feed collateral leg planted via `supply` bricks liquidation, withdrawal, and bad-debt cleanup for the account until the feed recovers - (File: contracts/controller/src/context.rs)

### Summary
The price aggregator is strictly fail-closed: `Outcome::failure` returns `PriceFeedStale` whenever a resolved leg exceeds its staleness bound, and `fetch_prices` panics the entire valuation. Every valuation-dependent controller path (liquidation, withdraw, `clean_bad_debt`) prices *all* of an account's positions through `Context::load_markets` / `fetch_prices`, but `supply` does not require a fresh price for the supplied asset. Because third-party top-ups to another account are permitted, an unprivileged address can attach a dust-sized supply leg of an asset whose oracle feed has gone stale (or simply wait for any existing leg's feed to go stale) onto a borrowing account, making that account unliquidatable and unable to withdraw or be bad-debt-cleaned for the duration of the outage.

### Finding Description
- `contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs:133-135` — any stale reading fails resolution with `PriceFeedStale`, and the strict `prices` entrypoint panics rather than skipping the key.
- `contracts/controller/src/context.rs:70-74` — `load_markets` fetches prices for every unique hub token in the position set; `:142-151` — `fetch_prices` calls the strict aggregator `prices` endpoint, so one stale asset among the account's collateral/debt legs aborts the whole call.
- `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_supply_stale_shield.rs` — proves the full chain: WBTC's feed is aged past its staleness bound, `try_supply(ALICE, "WBTC", 0.001)` still succeeds (supply performs no price read for the leg), after which `try_liquidate`, `try_clean_bad_debt_by_id`, and even `try_withdraw(ALICE, "WBTC", 0.0001)` all revert with `PRICE_FEED_STALE` until the feed is refreshed.
- `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/security_audit.rs:489-490` — `try_supply_to_account(BOB, ALICE, ...)` confirms third-party supply into another account is allowed, so the poisoned leg can be planted on a victim.

This is the XOXNO analog of the USSD finding: an oracle going down halts the peg-protection equivalent (liquidation/bad-debt cleanup), and here it additionally freezes the victim's own exits because valuation scans every position leg rather than only the legs involved in the operation.

### Impact Explanation
While any collateral or debt asset's feed is stale, the affected account cannot be liquidated and cannot withdraw collateral — a temporary freezing of funds. If the outage coincides with a real price crash, underwater accounts accrue bad debt the protocol cannot clean (`clean_bad_debt` reverts too), which can translate into realized protocol insolvency by the time the feed recovers. Attackers can also deliberately plant a dust leg of an already-stale-feed asset onto a victim account to extend or trigger the freeze.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. It requires an oracle feed outage/staleness, which is an external precondition, but feed halts do occur (e.g., Chainlink pausing UST/ETH), and the protocol's own long staleness budgets (up to 26h per `MAX_PRICE_STALE_SECONDS`) widen the window. No privileged action is needed; `supply` to an arbitrary account is unprivileged.

### Recommendation
Skip unpriceable legs in valuation when a safe bound exists: e.g., value a stale collateral leg at its sanity-band floor (`min_sanity_price_wad`) instead of aborting, or exempt dust legs below the bad-debt threshold from blocking the operation. Alternatively, reject `supply` of an asset whose strict price read fails so a stale leg can never enter an account's position set.

### Proof of Concept
See `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_supply_stale_shield.rs:4-70`: age WBTC's Reflector observation past `max_stale_seconds`, `try_supply` 0.001 WBTC to the account succeeds, then `try_liquidate`, `try_clean_bad_debt_by_id`, and `try_withdraw` all revert with `PRICE_FEED_STALE`; refreshing the price restores the identical calls. The same pattern on a victim via `supply` to another's account ID demonstrates the plantable variant.
