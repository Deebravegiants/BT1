### Title
`max_utilization` can be permanently bypassed by inflating pool supply before borrowing and withdrawing it afterward - (File: contracts/pool/src/guards.rs)

### Summary
The pool enforces `max_utilization` as a ratio of outstanding debt to total supply at the moment a borrow is executed, in `require_utilization_below_max` (`contracts/pool/src/guards.rs:19-34`). The check is a point-in-time gate on debt mints; exits are not re-checked against it (the same module documents that `require_liquidation_buffer` applies to debt mints and "Exits do not", `contracts/pool/src/guards.rs:36-47`, and the utilization guard lives in the same borrow-time guard set). An attacker can therefore supply a large amount to inflate the `supplied` denominator, borrow up to the inflated limit, then withdraw the temporary supply in a separate call, leaving the market with utilization persistently above `max_utilization` — the same bug class as M-11, where a limit computed from a transient contribution (`userAllocation.bought` / the attacker's deposit) survives after the contribution is consumed.

### Finding Description
`require_utilization_below_max` computes `borrowed.div_ceil(supplied)` against the current market totals and asserts it is `<= params.max_utilization` (`guards.rs:24-33`). `supplied` is the total scaled supply, which includes the borrower's own deposit. The borrower's deposit is not locked to the resulting debt: `withdraw` only runs the account-level post-risk gate (`require_post_pool_risk_gates`: LTV collateral ≥ debt, HF ≥ 1, min-borrow floor), which is satisfied by other collateral the attacker holds. Once the temporary supply is withdrawn, the market sits above `max_utilization` and no later operation restores the intended bound, because the guard only fires on new debt mints and `apply_exit` in `SpokeUsageContext` (`contracts/controller/src/spoke_usage.rs:119-139`) decreases usage without re-checking caps. This mirrors M-11: the sell limit counted `bought` forever even after those tokens vested; here the utilization limit counts the attacker's supply even though that supply is withdrawn immediately after the borrow.

### Impact Explanation
`max_utilization` exists to guarantee residual cash so suppliers can exit. Pinning utilization above the configured maximum means the market's free cash is permanently below the intended floor: honest suppliers attempting to withdraw get `InsufficientLiquidity` / insufficient pool cash until the attacker repays. This is temporary freezing of user funds reachable by a single unprivileged address through `supply` → `borrow` → `withdraw` on the controller.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attacker needs enough capital to (a) post collateral backing the borrow and (b) temporarily supply the borrowed asset — the latter can be recycled across markets or partially covered by flash liquidity since only the borrow-time snapshot matters. No privileged role, oracle manipulation, or timing race is required; the bypass is deterministic. The constraint "supply cap" on the borrowed asset's spoke listing may bound how much the attacker can inflate the denominator, which limits the achievable overshoot but does not eliminate it when caps are loose or unset.

### Recommendation
Enforce the utilization bound against the supply that is committed to the market, not the instantaneous total. Concretely: either re-check `require_utilization_below_max` on withdrawals that would push the market above `max_utilization` (rejecting exits that leave `borrowed / supplied > max_utilization`), or exclude the borrower's same-transaction supply when sizing the borrow limit, so a deposit that is about to leave cannot count toward the borrow ceiling.

### Proof of Concept
Market X has `max_utilization = 0.8`. Honest supply is 1,000 X, debt is 800 X (at the cap — new borrows correctly revert).

1. Attacker supplies 9,000 X (`controller::supply`). Market: supply 10,000 X, debt 800 X, utilization 8%.
2. Attacker supplies collateral Y in the same account and borrows 7,200 X (`controller::borrow`). Guard passes: debt 8,000 / supply 10,000 = 0.8.
3. Attacker calls `controller::withdraw` for the 9,000 X. Account risk gate passes because collateral Y covers the 7,200 X debt; the pool has 1,000 X of supply left against 8,000 X of debt — utilization 800%, far above `max_utilization`, and it stays there until repayment.
4. Honest suppliers of X cannot withdraw: only ~1,000 X cash exists (minus the liquidation buffer) against 1,000 X of claims plus the attacker's withdrawn share already gone; any meaningful exit reverts until the attacker repays.

Note: I was unable to fully verify within the iteration budget that `withdraw`/`repay` paths never invoke `require_utilization_below_max`; the finding rests on the guard's documented placement as a debt-mint check in `guards.rs` and the absence of a re-check in `SpokeUsageContext::apply_exit`. If withdraw does re-run the utilization guard, this analog is invalid.
