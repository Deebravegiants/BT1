### Title
Webhook `shop`, `topic`, and `webhook_id` are not covered by the HMAC, allowing cross-tenant webhook forgery via replay - ([File: lib/shopify_api/webhooks/request.rb])

### Summary
`ShopifyAPI::Webhooks::Request#to_signable_string` returns only the raw HTTP body, so the HMAC verified in `Utils::HmacValidator.validate` binds nothing but the body bytes to the app's secret. The `shop-domain`, `topic`, `webhook-id`, and `api-version` headers are read directly off the (unauthenticated) HTTP headers and are never included in the signed content.

### Finding Description
The identity binding this gem is supposed to enforce is: *the shop whose HMAC-authenticated bytes were received* == *the shop the handler acts on*. Here that equality is broken because the HMAC only covers `@raw_body` [4](#0-3) , while `shop`, `topic`, and `webhook_id` are taken verbatim from HTTP headers with no cryptographic tie to the body or to each other [2](#0-1) .

`Registry.process` only checks `Utils::HmacValidator.validate(request)` (i.e., body-only HMAC) before selecting the handler by the unauthenticated `request.topic` and forwarding the unauthenticated `request.shop` as the tenant identifier to the handler: [3](#0-2) 

Because the `api_secret_key` is shared across every shop that installs the app (it is not shop-specific), any merchant who has installed the app can legitimately receive at least one real webhook — a valid `(raw_body, hmac)` pair signed by the app's secret for their own shop and some topic. An unprivileged internet user who controls their own store (no special access to the victim or the app's credentials) can capture that valid body+HMAC pair and replay it to the app's public webhook endpoint while substituting a different `shopify-shop-domain` header (a victim's shop) and/or a different `shopify-topic` header. Since the HMAC check only re-validates the body — which is unchanged in the replay — the check passes, and `Registry.process` invokes the handler under an attacker-chosen `shop` and `topic`, i.e., the tenant/topic metadata is not actually bound to the signed bytes.

### Impact Explanation
This crosses a tenant boundary: an app that trusts `WebhookMetadata#shop` (as documented — it is the field apps are expected to use to key data per-merchant, e.g., to mark a shop uninstalled, delete or upsert per-shop records) can be made to act on behalf of a different (victim) shop using a body the attacker fully controls (from their own legitimate webhook), without ever compromising the victim or the app's `client_secret`/access tokens. This matches the "Cross-tenant access" impact category.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires only: (1) attacker installs the app on their own (attacker-owned) shop — no special privilege beyond being a regular Shopify merchant, (2) attacker triggers or waits for one webhook delivery to capture a valid `(body, hmac)` pair, (3) attacker replays that exact body/HMAC to the app's public webhook endpoint with a forged `shop-domain`/`topic` header. No secrets, tokens, or victim interaction are required, making this reasonably likely for any app that keys business logic off `WebhookMetadata#shop` or `#topic`.

### Recommendation
Include `shop`, `topic`, and `webhook_id` (or equivalently, require the caller to independently confirm `shop` matches an already-known/authorized shop for the topic in question) in the HMAC-signed content, or otherwise cryptographically bind the header values to the signed body before trusting them in `WebhookMetadata`. At minimum, document that `shop`/`topic`/`webhook_id` are NOT covered by the HMAC and must not be trusted as authenticated tenant identifiers without additional verification (e.g., cross-checking against the shop's stored session/access-token registration).

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker installs the target app on their own shop `attacker.myshopify.com` and registers/receives a webhook for topic `orders/create`, obtaining a legitimate `raw_body` and `shopify-hmac-sha256` value signed with the app's `api_secret_key`.
2. Attacker sends a POST to the app's webhook endpoint reusing the exact same `raw_body` and `hmac` header, but sets:
   - `shopify-shop-domain: victim.myshopify.com`
   - `shopify-topic: app/uninstalled` (or any topic registered by the app)
3. `ShopifyAPI::Webhooks::Request.new` accepts the forged headers (only checks presence, not that headers match anything signed) [5](#0-4) .
4. `Registry.process` calls `Utils::HmacValidator.validate(request)`, which recomputes the HMAC over `to_signable_string` (the raw body only) and succeeds since the body is unchanged [6](#0-5) .
5. The registered handler for `app/uninstalled` (or whatever forged topic) is invoked with `WebhookMetadata.new(shop: "victim.myshopify.com", ...)`, causing the app to process attacker-controlled data as though it originated from the victim shop.

        end
