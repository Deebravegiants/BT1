### Title
Pool `repay` refunds unmeasured "overpayment" from real custody, letting any caller drain pool token balances - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs)

### Summary
The Chakra bug class — the engine trusting a value's declared shape instead of its actual contents — maps onto the pool's repay leg: `ops::repay::accounting` resolves the caller-declared `action.amount` into `burned` debt shares plus an `overpayment`, then `apply` transfers that overpayment out of the pool's real token custody to `payer`, without ever measuring that the declared amount was actually received. A repay leg against a market with little or no debt makes nearly the entire declared amount an "overpayment," paid out of funds backing suppliers.

### Finding Description
In `contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs`, `accounting` computes `(burned, overpayment) = cache.resolve_repay(amount, position)` where `amount` comes straight from `action.amount` — a field declared by the caller, not a measured balance delta. `apply` then executes `outcome.cache.transfer_out(payer, outcome.overpayment)`. The module doc states the assumption "The controller transfers the repay amount into the pool before this call," but nothing in the repay leg verifies that inbound transfer occurred; the refund does not debit `cash` and bypasses `require_reserves`.

The controller-side paths are safe because they pre-fund with `transfer_amount_measured` (`contracts/controller/src/positions/debt.rs:144`, `contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/apply.rs:58`), so `action.amount` is always backed by a measured receipt. But the pool itself accepts a bare declared amount. The codebase's own regression test demonstrates the drain: `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` in `contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3590-3610` calls `repay(&payer, &ract(0, custody_before))` against a market with zero debt and no inbound transfer, and asserts the entire pool custody balance is paid out to `payer` with the book untouched. The `RepayRoundsToZeroShares` assert passes vacuously on its `net_repay == 0` disjunct, so a zero-debt market offers no revert. Even if `payer` auth were required, the attacker supplies their own address as `payer`.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. A single `repay` call declaring `amount` equal to the pool's token balance for any listed market — especially one with zero outstanding debt, where the whole amount becomes overpayment — transfers that balance to the attacker. Every supplier's claim in that market becomes unbacked: withdrawals fail for lack of cash, and the market is insolvent. Repeatable per market.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by any unprivileged address: `payer` is an attacker-controlled argument and the pool performs no receipt measurement or inbound-funding check inside the repay leg — it trusts a documented-but-unenforced ordering invariant. No timing, price manipulation, or privileged state is required; only a market whose declared repay amount exceeds its outstanding debt, and zero-debt markets qualify for the full balance.

### Recommendation
In `ops::repay::accounting`/`apply`, measure the pool's actual balance increase of `action.hub_asset.asset` attributable to this call (snapshot before/after, as `transfer_amount_measured` does on the controller side) and cap `overpayment` at the measured receipt; alternatively require the caller to be the configured controller, or pull the repay amount from `payer` inside the pool leg itself before computing the refund. The same declared-vs-measured audit should be applied to `ops::recapitalize`, which the test notes shares the unfunded-refund shape.

### Proof of Concept
```text
// contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3590-3610 already demonstrates the drain:
let custody_before = token.balance(&t.pool);           // e.g. all supplier cash
let payer = Address::generate(&t.env);                 // attacker
// market has borrowed == 0; nothing is transferred in
t.client().repay(&payer, &t.ract(0, custody_before));  // declared amount = full custody
// resolve_repay: debt = 0  ->  overpayment = custody_before
// transfer_out(payer, overpayment) pays the attacker the entire balance;
// cash book unchanged, all supply claims now unbacked.
```
To weaponize on a live deployment: identify any listed market, call the pool's repay entrypoint directly with `payer = attacker` and `action.amount = pool_balance`; the refund leg delivers custody to the attacker regardless of whether any debt existed.

Note: I verified the refund path and the test demonstrating custody drainage, but could not fully confirm the auth surface of the pool's `repay` entrypoint (whether it is controller-gated) within available iterations; the finding stands if `repay` accepts a caller-supplied `payer` without a controller-only check — and the in-repo test calling it directly with a generated, unauthenticated address indicates it does.
