I have enough context on the pricing path; one quick check on how liquidation consumes aggregator prices.### Title
Intra-transaction skew of Aquarius StableSwap reserves manipulates `AquariusStableLp` collateral price used by liquidation - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs)

### Summary
The bug class in the report — a valuation function whose return value is swayed by same-transaction balance manipulation — maps onto XOXNO Lending's `AquariusStableLp` price source. `aquarius::read` fetches the pool's live `get_reserves()` and `get_total_shares()` at evaluation time and feeds them into `fair_stable_lp_price_wad`, which computes `price = D * min(price_a, price_b) / total_shares`. The stable-swap invariant `D` shrinks when the two reserves are imbalanced, so an unprivileged attacker can skew the pool with a one-sided swap, deflate the LP token's oracle price inside the same ledger entry, and pass that deflated price into `controller.liquidate` against any account whose collateral is that LP token, then restore the pool. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
`read()` in `contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs` calls `aquarius_pool_reserves_call` and `aquarius_total_shares_call`, which read the Aquarius pool's stored reserves and share supply at call time — spot state, not a smoothed or committed value. [3](#0-2)  For a stable pool, the fair price is `solve_stable_d(xa_wad, xb_wad, amp) * min_price / share_supply_wad`. [4](#0-3)  `solve_stable_d` is the StableSwap invariant; for a fixed share supply it is maximized at the balanced point and strictly decreases as reserves diverge. A large one-sided swap therefore pushes `D` — and the reported LP price — down, while the `min(price_a, price_b)` leg does not compensate. The constant-product variant is far less exposed, since `2*sqrt(va*vb)` is approximately invariant under swaps (a swap preserves the product `va*vb` modulo fees); the stable formula is the manipulable one. [5](#0-4) 

Deployed mainnet oracles use `AquariusStableLp` / `AquariusLp` as a *single* source with `tolerance.upper_ratio_bps = 0` and `lower_ratio_bps = 0`, meaning there is no second feed to cross-check against; the only bounds are the static `min_sanity_price_wad`/`max_sanity_price_wad` bands, which the manipulated price stays inside because the pool manipulation cost itself bounds how far the price can move. [6](#0-5)  The lending pool's own cash accounting is donation-resistant (tracked cash, measured receipt), so the analog does not land on share valuation — it lands on the collateral price oracle consumed by the controller's Context-cached strict prices, which drive health factor and the liquidation plan. [7](#0-6)  The flash guard blocks re-entry into protected monetary entrypoints during a flash callback, but it does nothing to an attacker contract that simply sequences `aquarius.swap → controller.liquidate → aquarius.swap` inside its own invocation tree — no re-entry is needed, and "own trades on Aquarius" is an explicitly allowed reach. [8](#0-7) 

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds via wrongful liquidation. Depressing the LP collateral's oracle price pushes a victim account's health factor below 1, letting the attacker execute `liquidate` and seize LP collateral with the HF-based liquidation bonus while the collateral's real value is unchanged. The attacker then unwinds the skewing swap, recovering most of the manipulation cost (paying only pool fees and residual slippage), so the profit is bounded by the seized bonus minus swap costs. Alternatively, when the attacker is the LP-collateral borrower, an *inflation* of the fair price is possible by adding balanced liquidity to raise `D` relative to a temporarily reduced effective share supply picture, but the clean and symmetric primitive is deflation-for-liquidation. Both directions can also feed `multiply`/`flash_position` risk checks and `swap_collateral`/`swap_debt` sizing, which all consume the same aggregator price.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by any unprivileged address: Aquarius swaps need no role, `liquidate` is permissionless, and Soroban executes the whole sequence atomically in one transaction so there is no timing risk. The main mitigations are real but partial: the manipulation magnitude is bounded by the Aquarius pool's depth (deep stable pools are expensive to skew far), the sanity band caps how far the reported price can drift, `min_pool_value_wad` only rejects *low* pool value, and `max_price_stale_seconds` does not help because the manipulated read is fresh. The attack is profitable only where the LP token is meaningful collateral and the skew cost is below the bonus captured — a realistic but liquidity-dependent condition, consistent with a Medium/High rating rather than a guaranteed exploit.

### Recommendation
Do not derive collateral prices from manipulable spot reserves alone. Concretely:

- Add a second independent source for LP markets and use the existing dual-source tolerance check (`upper_ratio_bps`/`lower_ratio_bps`) instead of the deployed `0/0` single-source configuration, so a skewed read is rejected rather than trusted. [9](#0-8) 
- For stable pools, prefer pricing `D` at the ideal balanced point implied by external leg prices (i.e., `D ≈ va + vb` at equal value, or a virtual `D` invariant), rather than solving `D` from the live, skewable reserves — this makes the reported fair price insensitive to intra-transaction imbalance.
- As a fallback, bound the reserve ratio (`xa_wad/xb_wad`) to a tight band around 1 for stable pools and return `NoLastPrice` outside it, failing closed rather than pricing a manipulated book.

### Proof of Concept
1. Victim supplies `AquariusStableLp` share token `S` (priced from pool `P` over tokens A and B) and borrows another asset, leaving health factor slightly above 1.
2. Attacker contract, in one transaction: swaps a large amount of A for B on `P`, pushing reserves far off balance. `solve_stable_d` returns a materially smaller `D`, so `fair_stable_lp_price_wad` reports a deflated `price_wad` for `S` — still inside `min_sanity_price_wad`/`max_sanity_price_wad`. [10](#0-9) 
3. Same transaction calls `controller.liquidate(victim, ...)`; the Context-cached price for `S` is the manipulated observation, victim HF < 1, and the attacker repays debt and seizes `S` at a bonus. [11](#0-10) 
4. Same transaction swaps back on `P`, recovering reserves minus fees, and can sell or keep the discounted `S`. No re-entry, no flash guard window, and no privileged call is involved.

Caveat: the exact profit/severity depends on the real Aquarius pool's depth and whether `get_reserves` reflects swap-updated internal state (it does — reserves are the swap accounting), which is third-party behavior I could not fully verify from this repo; the stable-`D` deflation direction itself follows directly from the invariant math in `solve_stable_d`.

### INV-ACCT-03 — Token-funded credit uses measured receipt

Token-funded supply, repayment and recapitalization credit the pool's measured
balance increase. Strategy supply follows the same supply path, and strategy
repayment also measures pool receipt. Requested amounts alone do not determine
credit.

incoming collateral tokens.
