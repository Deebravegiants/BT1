### Title
Market-wide i128 value overflow permanently freezes withdrawals, repayments, and liquidations - ([File: common/src/rates/index.rs](common/src/rates/index.rs))

### Summary
A sufficiently large active market can reach a state where converting scaled debt or supply shares back to RAY-denominated value overflows `i128` before the configured index ceiling is reached. Because `interest::global_sync` runs before every pool mutation, the first such overflow permanently blocks all state-changing operations for that market.

### Finding Description
The pool stores `supplied` and `borrowed` as RAY-scaled share balances. During accrual, `common/src/rates/index.rs::calculate_supplier_rewards` computes:

- `borrowed.mul(env, old_borrow_index)`
- `borrowed.mul(env, new_borrow_index)`

At [common/src/rates/index.rs:80-83](common/src/rates/index.rs#L80-L83), these products are represented as `i128` RAY values. `common/src/rates/scaling.rs::scaled_to_original` performs the same multiplication when unscaling positions or market totals.

`update_borrow_index` caps the index at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, but it does so only after multiplying the old index by the interest factor and does not ensure that `borrowed * borrow_index` remains representable. Consequently, the debt-value domain can overflow before the index cap engages.

Every market mutation follows the sequence `Cache::load → interest::global_sync → mutate`, as documented in `contracts/pool/README.md`. Therefore, once accrual reaches the overflow condition, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `recapitalize`, and `update_indexes` all revert before reaching their operation-specific logic. `update_params` also accrues before changing the interest model, so governance cannot correct the curve through the normal parameter path.

### Impact Explanation
This is permanent freezing of all funds in the affected hub/asset market:

- Suppliers cannot withdraw underlying tokens.
- Borrowers cannot repay or close debt.
- Liquidators cannot repay debt or seize collateral.
- `clean_bad_debt` cannot socialize the frozen position.
- `recapitalize` cannot repair the market because it accrues first.
- Protocol revenue in the market cannot be claimed.

The market remains stranded absent a privileged contract upgrade, which is outside the accepted remediation model.

### Likelihood Explanation
The trigger requires an extremely large market relative to the asset’s decimals and sustained interest accrual. For an 18-decimal asset, the existing test demonstrates the condition with approximately one billion whole tokens supplied and 98% borrowed under a high-utilization curve. The required market size makes exploitation dependent on asset supply, admitted caps, and sustained utilization rather than being arbitrarily triggerable on a small market.

No malicious parameter, oracle manipulation, or privileged action is required after such market state exists. Any unprivileged caller can invoke `update_indexes` and permanently push the market across the representable-value boundary. A user can also contribute to the precondition through ordinary `supply` and `borrow` calls where liquidity and caps permit.

### Recommendation
Make index accrual safe at the market-value domain boundary:

1. Before applying an index increase, check both resulting values:
   - `borrowed * new_borrow_index`
   - `supplied * new_supply_index`
2. Clamp the index to the largest value for which all stored scaled balances remain representable, rather than multiplying first and reverting afterward.
3. Alternatively, track market totals in a wider internal representation and only convert to `i128` at token-transfer boundaries.
4. Add an explicit recovery path that can lower utilization or burn/write off debt without performing the overflowing accrual multiplication.
5. Add regression coverage near the value-overflow boundary and before `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.

### Proof of Concept
The repository’s harness already contains a directed reproduction in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs`:

1. Create an 18-decimal market using the XLM-style interest curve and disable the utilization cap.
2. Supply `1_000_000_000 * 10^18` base units.
3. Supply enough separate collateral to borrow `98%` of that market.
4. Borrow the debt.
5. Advance ledger time in yearly intervals and call `update_indexes`.
6. Accrual eventually reverts with `MathOverflow` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
7. Subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` calls also revert with `MathOverflow` because both execute `global_sync` before resolving the requested operation.

This demonstrates that the cap on `borrow_index` does not protect the downstream `scaled_shares * index` value calculation, leaving the market permanently unable to mutate once the RAY-valued total exceeds `i128::MAX`.
