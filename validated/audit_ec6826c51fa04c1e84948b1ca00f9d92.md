### Title
Stale `Account.owner` grants withdrawn-NFT holders full account control after position transfer — authentication bypass on `withdraw`/`borrow` - (File: contracts/controller/src/account.rs)

### Summary
The controller maintains two different notions of account ownership: the position-NFT `owner_of(account_id)` (the transferable source of truth) and the `owner` field frozen inside the `Account` record at mint time. Privileged position actions (`withdraw`, `borrow`, `swap_collateral`, `swap_debt`, `multiply`, etc.) authorize against the stale `Account.owner`, while NFT `transfer`/`approve` in `position-nft` never resyncs that field. This mirrors CVE-2021-41303: two layers interpret the same request's identity differently, so the authority check passes for a caller who no longer owns the position.

### Finding Description
`Account.owner` is written once at mint in `create_account_with` and is never updated afterward — the only write site is account creation [1](#0-0) . Yet the sensitive entrypoints authorize via `require_owner_or_delegate(env, account_id, caller, &account.owner)`, which compares the caller against that stored field [2](#0-1) . This is the check guarding `process_withdraw` [3](#0-2)  and `process_borrow` [4](#0-3) .

Meanwhile, the controller itself acknowledges NFT ownership as the real authority: `require_account_owner` separately resolves `storage::account_owner(env, account_id)` — the NFT `owner_of` — for `renew_account` and delegate management [5](#0-4) , and the NFT contract documents that "the token owner (`owner_of`) is the account owner" [6](#0-5) . The NFT contract exposes the stock OZ `NonFungibleToken` interface (`transfer`, `approve`, `transfer_from`) with no hook back into the controller [7](#0-6) .

So after `position-nft.transfer(victim → buyer, account_id)`:

1. `storage::account_owner` returns `buyer`, but `account.owner` remains `victim`.
2. `victim` still passes `require_owner_or_delegate` and can call `controller.withdraw(account_id, [(collateral, 0 /* withdraw-all */)], Some(victim))`, draining all collateral the buyer just paid for — `process_withdraw` honors a caller-chosen `to` recipient [8](#0-7) .
3. `victim` can also `borrow` against the buyer's collateral to the LTV limit and route proceeds to itself, instantly making the bought account liquidatable.
4. The `buyer` cannot call `withdraw`, `borrow`, or `add_delegate` on their own account — `require_owner_or_delegate` rejects them — permanently freezing the position except for `repay`-style inbound paths.

### Impact Explanation
- **Theft of user funds (Critical):** any account NFT sold/transferred (e.g., via a marketplace using `transfer_from`) lets the previous owner withdraw 100% of collateral and extract max-LTV borrows. Every secondary-market trade of a leveraged position is backdoored.
- **Permanent freezing of funds:** the legitimate new owner cannot perform any owner-gated action; only the stale recorded owner (or its registered delegates) can ever touch the position.
- Delegation compounds it: `is_owner_or_delegate` resolves delegates under the stale `account.owner` key, so delegates appointed by the old owner retain authority over the new owner's funds [9](#0-8) .

### Likelihood Explanation
Fully unprivileged and deterministic. The attacker needs only to have minted an account (own `supply` call with `account_id = 0`) and transfer its NFT — both are in-scope entrypoints (`supply`, `position-nft.transfer`). No timing, oracle, or price dependency. Any protocol or marketplace flow that transfers a position NFT (the explicit purpose of making positions non-fungible) triggers it.

### Recommendation
Make NFT `owner_of` the single authority in `require_owner_or_delegate` (resolve `storage::account_owner(env, account_id)` instead of `account.owner`), or drop the cached `Account.owner` field entirely and key delegates to `account_id` rather than owner address. If the cached field must remain for gas reasons, add a controller-invoked ownership resync on every owner-gated path. Note the same staleness applies to `storage::get_delegates(env, account_id, owner)` keyed by the old owner.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice calls `controller.supply(caller=alice, account_id=0, spoke_id=1, [(hub_asset_X, 1_000e7)])` → mints account `A`, `account.owner = alice`.
2. Alice sells/transfers the position: `position_nft.transfer(alice, bob, A)`. Now `owner_of(A) = bob`.
3. Alice calls `controller.withdraw(account_id=A, withdrawals=[(hub_asset_X, 0)], to=Some(alice))`.
   - `caller.require_auth()` passes (alice signs).
   - `require_owner_or_delegate(A, alice, account.owner=alice)` passes on the stale field.
   - `amount == 0` maps to `WITHDRAW_ALL_SENTINEL`; the pool pays the full collateral to `alice` [10](#0-9) .
4. Bob, the actual NFT owner, calls `controller.withdraw(...)` → `require_owner_or_delegate` panics `NotAuthorized`; his funds are already gone and were never under his control.

Caveat: this finding assumes `storage::account_owner` resolves to the NFT `owner_of` and that no transfer hook resyncs `Account.owner`. The split check in `require_account_owner` and the NFT contract's lack of any controller callback strongly support this, but `contracts/controller/src/storage/account.rs` and the OZ transfer internals were not fully read in this pass; if a sync path exists elsewhere it would need to be verified before final severity assignment.

pub(crate) fn is_owner_or_delegate(
    env: &Env,
    account_id: u64,
    caller: &Address,
    owner: &Address,
) -> bool {
    if caller == owner {
        return true;
    }
    let active_manager =
        storage::get_position_manager(env, caller).is_some_and(|config| config.is_active);
    active_manager && storage::get_delegates(env, account_id, owner).contains(caller)
}
