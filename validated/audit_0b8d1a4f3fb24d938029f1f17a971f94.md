### Title
Suppliers front-run `clean_bad_debt` to dodge the bad-debt supply-index write-down, pushing the full loss onto remaining suppliers - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs)

### Summary
The Carapace issue is a sandwich around an event that discontinuously changes the share↔asset exchange rate. XOXNO Lending has the mirror-image primitive: `seize_positions` on the borrow side calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, which scales `supply_index` down by `1 − bad_debt/total_supplied_value` to socialize unpaid debt pro-rata across all suppliers. `clean_bad_debt` is a permissionless controller entrypoint, and suppliers can `withdraw` at any time subject only to cash/utilization guards. A supplier that withdraws immediately before triggering `clean_bad_debt` redeems at the pre-write-down index and exits with full value, while the same loss is then concentrated on the suppliers who remain — violating the same fair-distribution principle.

### Finding Description
- `contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs` (`apply`): for `AccountPositionType::Borrow` entries it computes `bad_debt = unscale_borrow_ceil_ray(position)` and calls `interest::apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, then burns the debt. This is invoked via `pool_seize_positions_call` from `execute_bad_debt_cleanup` in `contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/bad_debt.rs`.
- `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs` (`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`): multiplies `supply_index` by `floor((total_supplied_value − bad_debt) / total_supplied_value)`, floored at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. Every share still in the market loses value proportionally.
- `contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs` (`accounting`/`gate_and_debit`): withdrawal redeems shares at the *current* supply index and is gated only by `require_reserves` (cash), `require_utilization_below_max`, and `require_supply_for_debt`. Nothing forces a supplier to remain in the book while pending bad debt is written down.
- `clean_bad_debt` requires no special authorization — the keeper flow exists precisely because anyone may trigger cleanup of an underwater account (see `tests/test-harness/src/keeper.rs` and `scripts/permissionless_entrypoints.txt`). So the attacker controls the ordering themselves: withdraw first, then call `clean_bad_debt(account_id)`, with no dependence on mempool timing.

Attack shape (single unprivileged address, two calls):
1. An account sits with undercollateralized/bad debt in hub asset `(hub_id, asset)`. The market is under-backed: `floor(supplied × supply_index) > cash + ceil(debt × borrow_index)`; the eventual write-down is unavoidable.
2. Attacker, a supplier in that market, calls `withdraw` (controller) for their full supply position (amount `0` = withdraw-all sentinel), paying out `floor(shares × supply_index)` from `cash` at the pre-loss index.
3. Attacker calls `clean_bad_debt(account_id)`, which seizes the dead account's borrow leg and lowers `supply_index` by the bad-debt factor.
4. Remaining suppliers' shares are now worth the reduced index. The attacker extracted pre-loss value from the common cash pool, so the socialized loss falls disproportionately on those who didn't exit.

The asymmetric ordering is structural: the write-down happens at seize time, not when the debt went bad, so whoever exits before the permissionless cleanup pays nothing toward it. Notably `supply` is guarded by `require_backed_market` (can't deposit into an already-under-backed book), but `withdraw` has no equivalent "write down pending bad debt before letting me out" step — the cleanup must be invoked separately and the exit beats it.

### Impact Explanation
Theft/dilution of other suppliers' funds: early withdrawers take more than their pro-rata share of actual backing, increasing every remaining supplier's realized loss beyond the intended pro-rata socialization. In the extreme, a dominant supplier (or several racing to exit, draining `cash` until `require_reserves` fails) forces small suppliers to absorb nearly the entire bad debt via the index write-down. This is the identical economic harm class as the reference report — exploiting a discrete exchange-rate-changing event to redistribute value unfairly — realized through the rate *decreasing* event instead of an increasing one. Severity: Medium (requires pre-existing bad debt and available cash to exit, but needs no privilege and the attacker controls both legs).

### Likelihood Explanation
- Bad debt arises whenever a position's collateral is exhausted before full liquidation — a normal occurrence in volatile markets, and `clean_bad_debt` exists specifically for this.
- The exit leg is a normal `withdraw`, permissionless, needing only sufficient `cash` and utilization headroom; suppliers can monitor hub state via `get_sync_data`/`get_bulk_indexes` and risk views to detect accounts awaiting cleanup.
- The attacker need not own the bad account — any supplier benefits from exiting first, so there is a natural race (first-come-first-served loss avoidance) whenever underwater accounts await cleanup.
- Limiting factors: exit is capped by pool `cash` (`InsufficientLiquidity` past reserves), a high-utilization market may block `require_utilization_below_max`, and if liquidation seizes the position fully there is nothing to clean. Where withdrawal is still possible, the dodge is mechanically guaranteed.

### Recommendation
Order the write-down before the exit, or make exit prices account for pending bad debt:
- In `withdraw` (or the controller's withdrawal path), evaluate whether any account in the same market carries unclaimable/dead debt and force the cleanup (or an equivalent `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` adjustment) before paying out at the current index.
- Alternatively, gate withdrawals with a "bad-debt-aware" backing check: compute the withdrawable index against `cash + recoverable debt` (excluding positions eligible for `clean_bad_debt`) so exiting shares are priced post-socialization.
- Short-term mitigations analogous to the original report's suggestions also apply: batch/atomic cleanup triggers, or crediting seized value through `recapitalize`-style flows rather than letting the index jump discretely.

### Proof of Concept
1. Hub `H`, asset `A`. Suppliers S1 (attacker, large) and S2 (victim) hold scaled supply shares; borrower B is deeply underwater: `debt_value(B) >> collateral_value(B)`, and B's collateral has already been seized or is dust.
2. Attacker observes via `get_sync_data(H, A)` that B's debt is unbacked (or simply that `clean_bad_debt(account_id_B)` is callable).
3. Attacker calls controller `withdraw` on their `(H, A)` supply leg with amount `0` (withdraw-all). `ops/withdraw.rs::accounting` burns their shares and pays `floor(shares × supply_index)` in tokens at the pre-write-down index.
4. Attacker calls `clean_bad_debt(account_id_B)`. `execute_bad_debt_cleanup` builds `PoolSeizeEntry{side: Borrow, position: B's debt}` → `pool.seize_positions` → `ops/seize.rs::apply` → `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache, bad_debt)` → `supply_index *= (total_value − bad_debt)/total_value`, then `burn_debt`.
5. S2's shares now redeem at the reduced index. Attacker's tokens already left the pool. Attacker's net gain versus honest ordering equals `attacker_shares × (index_before − index_after)`, taken from the shared cash pool — i.e., S2's excess loss.

Key code path: `contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/bad_debt.rs::execute_bad_debt_cleanup` → `contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs::apply` (Borrow side) → `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs::apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`; exit path `contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs::accounting`/`gate_and_debit` pays at the untouched index with no pending-bad-debt check.

Caveat: I verified the pool-side mechanics and the controller cleanup call graph; I did not fully trace every controller-side gate on `withdraw`/`clean_bad_debt` (e.g., whether cleanup is restricted to keeper accounts in the deployed `lib.rs` dispatch). The entrypoint lists and test-harness keeper usage indicate it is permissionless, but that auth check should be confirmed against `contracts/controller/src/lib.rs` before relying on the finding.
