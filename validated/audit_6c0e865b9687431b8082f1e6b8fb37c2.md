### Title
Market-wide RAY value overflow in accrual permanently freezes all repay/withdraw/liquidate paths - ([File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs](contracts/pool/src/interest.rs))

### Summary
The Chakra memory-corruption class maps here to unchecked numeric state corruption: every pool mutation first accrues interest via `global_sync` → `accrue_chunk` → `accrue_step`, which values scaled debt with `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` — a half-up `mul` that panics with `MathOverflow` when `borrowed_shares × borrow_index / RAY` exceeds `i128::MAX`. Once a market's scaled-debt times index crosses that bound, accrual traps forever, and since every verb accrues first, the entire market is bricked: no `repay`, no `withdraw`, no `liquidate`, no `clean_bad_debt`, no flash paths.

### Finding Description
`global_sync` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20` runs before any state change and loops `accrue_chunk` (`interest.rs:39`) over elapsed milliseconds. Each chunk calls `accrue_step` (`common/src/rates/simulate.rs:51`), whose first operation is `scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14`), implemented as `scaled.mul(env, index)` — a checked half-up multiply-divide in `common/src/math/fp_core.rs` that panics on overflow. The borrow index itself is only capped at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY = 10^36` after the multiply, and the index cap does not bound the *product* `borrowed × index`: a whale-scale market at sustained high utilization grows the debt value past `i128::MAX` well before the index ceiling engages (confirmed by `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321`, which asserts `MATH_OVERFLOW` on `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay`, with `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`). `docs/reference/formulas.md:435` concedes "value overflow can occur before the index ceiling and block repayment/withdrawal because those operations accrue first."

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds and effective market insolvency: suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot liquidate, and bad debt cannot be cleaned — the panic is in shared market accrual, not per-account state, so a single over-limit market DoSes all positions in it irreversibly. There is no unwind path that skips accrual.

### Likelihood Explanation
Fully reachable by a single unprivileged address with sufficient capital: supply a near-cap amount of a high-decimals market token, supply collateral, and `borrow` up to ~98% utilization, then let time pass (or repeatedly call `update_indexes`). No privileged action, oracle manipulation, or parameter misconfiguration is required — the cliff is a function of principal size and the configured rate curve. The documented bound (~170 billion whole tokens, `formulas.md:429`) is large but within the admitted cap domain, and the token-to-RAY upscale accepts it. Likelihood is bounded by the capital required, hence Medium rather than High.

### Recommendation
Saturate rather than panic in the accrual valuation path: cap `borrowed_original`/`supplied_original` at the representable maximum, or engage a debt-value ceiling before `scaled_to_original` can overflow, so exits and liquidations remain executable. Alternatively, gate `supply`/`borrow` entries so that `scaled × index_ceiling` stays inside `i128` (a tighter effective cap derived from `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`), converting the cliff into a rejectable entry condition.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker calls `supply` on market `BIG18` (18 decimals) with ~10²⁷ base units (within the admitted cap after `lift_caps`-equivalent listing).
2. Same account supplies `COL` collateral and calls `borrow(BIG18, ~98% of principal)`.
3. Time advances (a few years at the steep utilization segment); attacker or anyone calls `update_indexes`.
4. `accrue_step` → `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` panics `MathOverflow`; every subsequent `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `flash_loan`, `clean_bad_debt` on `BIG18` reverts identically, permanently. Reproduced by `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360`.
