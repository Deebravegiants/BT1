### Title
Permissionless `update_account_threshold` lets any address restamp a lowered LTV onto a victim's collateral, freezing their withdrawals - (File: contracts/controller/src/risk/params.rs)

### Summary
`update_account_threshold` requires only `caller.require_auth()` and accepts an arbitrary `account_ids` vector with no ownership or delegate check. With `has_risks = false` (`RiskRefreshScope::LtvOnly`) it overwrites every listed supply position's cached `loan_to_value` with the current spoke config, and — unlike the `FullTuple` path — performs no health-factor gate. If governance has lowered a market's LTV after users deposited, a griefer can force-apply the reduced LTV to any victim, flipping them over their borrow limit and making `withdraw` (and `borrow`) revert until the position is repaid or governance restores the LTV.

### Finding Description
- `controller::update_account_threshold` is a permissionless entrypoint (`contracts/controller/src/lib.rs:386`) that delegates to `risk::params::update_account_threshold`.
- `update_account_threshold` calls `validation::require_authorized_caller` (which only does `caller.require_auth()`), then loops over caller-supplied `account_ids` and calls `sync_account_thresholds` on each (`contracts/controller/src/risk/params.rs:124-143`). There is no check that the caller owns or delegates the listed accounts — contrast with the INV-AUTH-02 owner/delegate guard applied to `withdraw`, `borrow`, etc.
- `sync_account_thresholds` (`params.rs:149-219`) loads the victim's supply positions and calls `refresh_supply_risk_params`, which unconditionally writes `position.loan_to_value = effective_config.loan_to_value` (`params.rs:35`) and persists via `set_supply_positions` (`params.rs:218`).
- The HF >= 1.05 gate exists only to protect the *liquidation tuple* restamp (`FullTuple` path, `params.rs:221-234`); `LtvOnly` restamps borrow-limit parameters with no solvency check at all, and `apply_gated_liquidation_params`'s `favors_liquidator` skip does not apply to LTV.

### Impact Explanation
Withdrawal solvency is re-proven against the position's cached LTV (`require_owner_or_delegate` + post-op solvency in `controller::withdraw`). After a legitimate governance LTV reduction, an unprivileged attacker calls `update_account_threshold(attacker, false, [victim_ids])` to stamp the lower LTV onto victims ahead of any natural restamp. Accounts whose debt now exceeds `collateral × new_ltv` cannot withdraw any collateral or borrow — a temporary freezing of user funds lasting until the account deleverages or the config changes. Because the liquidation threshold tuple is *not* restamped in this path, victims can be frozen without becoming liquidatable, so the freeze has no self-resolving exit for leveraged-but-healthy users. This is a griefing DoS on withdrawals, matching the CVE's "improper input validation → denial of service" class: the missing validation is authorization of `account_ids`.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a governance LTV decrease to have occurred after deposits (a routine risk-parameter action, e.g., risk-off tightening). The attack costs one unprivileged call; `account_ids` is a caller-chosen batch so many accounts are bricked per transaction. Impact is temporary freezing rather than theft, so severity is Medium — aligned with the source CVE's Medium rating.

### Recommendation
Restrict `update_account_threshold` to the account owner or an active delegate for `account_ids` it does not own, or only apply LTV restamps that do not worsen an account's borrow limit (analogous to the `favors_liquidator` gate already used for liquidation params). Alternatively, apply restamps lazily inside each user's own operation flow so third parties cannot force a downgrade.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice supplies USDC and borrows ETH near the LTV limit; her supply position caches `loan_to_value = LTV_old`.
2. Governance (legitimately) lowers the USDC listing's LTV to `LTV_new < LTV_old` via a delayed operation.
3. Attacker (any address) calls `controller::update_account_threshold(caller=attacker, has_risks=false, account_ids=[alice_id])`.
4. `sync_account_thresholds` writes `loan_to_value = LTV_new` into Alice's stored supply position with no HF check.
5. Alice's `withdraw(alice_id, …)` now fails the post-withdrawal solvency proof (`debt > collateral × LTV_new`), reverting every exit until she repays debt — despite never consenting to the restamp and despite the account not yet being liquidatable (her stale liquidation threshold was untouched by the `LtvOnly` scope).
