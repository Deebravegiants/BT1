Audit Report

## Title
`WouldBurn` strict-zero-balance check in `do_refund_other` can permanently block existence-deposit reclamation - (File: substrate/frame/assets/src/functions.rs)

## Summary
`do_refund_other`, used by `refund_other` to reclaim a `DepositFrom` existence deposit on behalf of another account, unconditionally requires the target account's balance to be zero and has no `allow_burn` override unlike the self-service `do_refund`. Any holder of the asset can permissionlessly transfer a nonzero (even minimal) amount into the target account to keep this check failing indefinitely, blocking deposit reclamation until an admin performs a privileged `burn`.

## Finding Description
`do_refund_other` gates the refund on `ensure!(account.balance.is_zero(), Error::<T, I>::WouldBurn)` at [1](#0-0) , with no way to force a burn as part of the call, unlike `do_refund`'s `ensure!(account.balance.is_zero() || allow_burn, Error::<T, I>::WouldBurn)` at [2](#0-1) . Because asset transfers/mints to a holder's own account are permissionless for anyone already holding units of that asset, a non-depositor/non-admin/non-freezer third party can dust the target account to keep `account.balance.is_zero()` false forever, causing `refund_other` to fail with `Error::WouldBurn` regardless of who calls it. The existing regression test demonstrates exactly this: minting into the target account causes `refund_other` to fail with `WouldBurn` even for the asset owner/admin, and only the privileged `burn` call clears the balance [3](#0-2) .

## Impact Explanation
The depositor's reserved existence deposit becomes stuck as long as the target balance stays nonzero, which a griefer can maintain cheaply by re-dusting after any burn attempt. This is a low-severity griefing/DoS on deposit reclamation, not a loss of principal or a broken accounting invariant, since the asset's admin/Issuer can always resolve it via `burn` before calling `refund_other`.

## Likelihood Explanation
Triggering requires only that a `DepositFrom`-based account exists (via `touch_other`) and that the attacker holds any amount of the same asset to transfer/dust the target — both trivially satisfiable for any permissionless asset, making the grief cheap and repeatable, though bounded by the always-available privileged `burn` remediation path.

## Recommendation
Add an `allow_burn`-style parameter or equivalent forced-burn path to `refund_other`/`do_refund_other`, mirroring the self-service `refund` extrinsic's `allow_burn` flag, so the depositor/admin can force-clear dust balances and reclaim the deposit in one call.

## Proof of Concept
1. Admin/depositor `X` calls `Assets::touch_other(origin, asset_id, victim)` to create an asset account for `victim` and reserve a deposit.
2. Attacker `Y`, holding units of `asset_id`, calls `Assets::transfer(origin, asset_id, victim, 1)` (or any nonzero amount).
3. `X` calls `Assets::refund_other(origin, asset_id, victim)` → fails with `Error::WouldBurn` per the check at `substrate/frame/assets/src/functions.rs:432`, reproducible as shown by the existing test at `substrate/frame/assets/src/tests.rs:1247-1253`.
