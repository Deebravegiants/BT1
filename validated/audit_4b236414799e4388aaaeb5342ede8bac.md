### Title
Stale `account.owner` field retains spending authority after position NFT transfer - ([File: contracts/controller/src/account.rs])

### Summary
The controller keeps two sources of truth for account ownership: `Account.owner`, written once at mint inside `create_account_with` (`contracts/controller/src/account.rs:64-70`), and the position-NFT's live `Owner(token_id)` entry read by `storage::account_owner`. `require_owner_or_delegate` (`account.rs:130-140`) authenticates callers against the stale `Account.owner` snapshot, while `require_account_owner` (`account.rs:143-148`) checks the live NFT owner. No code path rewrites `Account.owner`, so after the position NFT is transferred the previous owner retains delegate-equivalent spending authority over the account and the new owner cannot satisfy `require_owner_or_delegate`. This is the direct analog of the Fleet bug class: the effective identity is taken from a privileged/stale credential store instead of the authoritative one in a second code path.

### Finding Description
- `create_account_with` sets `account.owner = owner` at mint and stores it in the `Account` map; nothing ever updates that field afterward (`account.rs:44-74`).
- `is_owner_or_delegate`/`require_owner_or_delegate` compare `caller` against `account.owner` plus the per-owner `Delegates` map (`account.rs:115-140`). Delegates are keyed under `storage::add_delegate(env, account_id, caller, delegate)` with `caller` being the owner at grant time (`account.rs:260-264`).
- Every unprivileged spending entrypoint routes through `require_owner_or_delegate` or `AccountGuard::Migrate`/`Multiply`, which call it (`account.rs:101-109`): `borrow`, `withdraw`, `multiply`, `flash_position`, `swap_debt`, `swap_collateral`, `repay_debt_with_collateral`, `migrate_from_blend`, and the liquidation receiver path in `positions/liquidation/mod.rs`. All perform `caller.require_auth()` then trust `account.owner` as the identity root.
- `require_account_owner` (used by `add_delegate`, `remove_delegate`, `renew_account`) instead resolves ownership via `storage::account_owner` — the NFT ledger (`account.rs:143-148`). The two sources diverge permanently after a `position-nft` transfer, because the NFT contract updates `Owner(token_id)` but has no hook that mutates `Account.owner`.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds. A user who sells or transfers their position NFT to a buyer retains full borrow/withdraw authority over the account: they still pass `require_owner_or_delegate` because `account.owner` still equals their address, and they still pass `caller.require_auth()` with their own signature. They can call `withdraw` to pull the buyer's collateral and `borrow` to drain pool liquidity against it. Symmetrically, the legitimate new NFT owner is denied `require_owner_or_delegate` paths (their delegates were granted under a different owner key and they are not `account.owner`), so they cannot even withdraw to safety — they can only be liquidated. This maps to the "theft of user funds" / "temporary freezing of funds" acceptance classes.

### Likelihood Explanation
Only requires `position-nft::transfer`, which is an in-scope unprivileged entrypoint, followed by `controller::borrow`/`withdraw` by the seller. NFT trading/transfers are a normal intended feature (approve/transfer exist for exactly this). No privileged action, oracle manipulation, or timing dependency is needed; the desync is deterministic on first transfer.

### Recommendation
Derive spending authority from a single source: make `require_owner_or_delegate`/`is_owner_or_delegate` resolve the owner via `storage::account_owner` (the NFT ledger) and key the `Delegates` map by account_id rather than by stored owner address, or add a controller-authorized `on_transfer` hook in the position NFT that rewrites `Account.owner` and clears `Delegates` on every transfer. Until then, the delegate list must also be invalidated on ownership change, since stale `Delegates` entries keyed under the old owner give the seller's managers continued access.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice supplies `USDC` collateral into `account_id = 7` via `controller::supply` (or `multiply`), creating `Account{owner: alice, ...}`.
2. Alice sells the position NFT: `position_nft::transfer(alice → bob, token_id = 7)`. `Owner(7)` now reads `bob`; `Account.owner` still reads `alice` (`account.rs:64-70` — field is never rewritten).
3. Alice calls `controller::borrow(caller=alice, account_id=7, ...)`. `caller.require_auth()` succeeds (her own signature); `require_owner_or_delegate` compares `alice == account.owner` and passes (`account.rs:115-123`). Funds are drawn against Bob's collateral.
4. Alice calls `controller::withdraw` similarly and exits with the collateral.
5. Bob calls `controller::withdraw(caller=bob, account_id=7, ...)`: `require_owner_or_delegate` fails (`bob != account.owner`, `panic_with_error!(NotAuthorized)`, `account.rs:139`) — he is locked out of his own account despite owning the NFT.
