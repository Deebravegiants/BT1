### Title
Permissionless accrual permanently freezes a high-utilization whale market via i128 overflow panic - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
`accrue_step` computes `borrowed * borrow_index` and `supplied * supply_index` as raw `i128` products with no saturation. Once a market's scaled debt value approaches `i128::MAX`, every subsequent accrual panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow`. Because every mutating verb (`supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, flash paths, `update_indexes`) runs `global_sync`/`accrue` first, the market is frozen forever: suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot seize. The `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap in `update_borrow_index` is applied *after* the value multiplication, so it never engages to stop the panic. This is the on-chain analog of the Node advisory: untrusted input drives the runtime into a crash path — here the "crash" is a permanent, unrecoverable contract panic that bricks a live market.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` in `common/src/rates/simulate.rs` (lines 60-61) calls `scaled_to_original`, which is `Ray::mul` over raw `i128` values — `borrowed * borrow_index / RAY` in fixed point, computed via `fp_core` checked multiplication that panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` on overflow (see `checked_add_raw`/`checked_sub_nonneg` in `common/src/math/fp.rs:13-25` and `Ray::mul`'s checked semantics). The same panic propagates through `calculate_supplier_rewards` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:80-81`) and `update_supply_index` (`index.rs:34`). The pool's mutating accrual `global_sync` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33` calls `accrue_chunk` → `accrue_step` before any state change, and `update_indexes` is permissionless, so anyone can trigger the panic — and once the stored `borrow_index` and `borrowed` are large enough, the panic repeats on every call with no recovery path, since accrual is unconditionally first.

`update_borrow_index` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19`) caps the index at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY = 10^36` raw RAY, but the cap is checked on the index product, not on `borrowed * index`: with `borrowed` scaled shares of a billion whole 18-decimal tokens (`~1e36` raw units), the value ceiling is `i128::MAX ≈ 1.7e38`, i.e. only ~170x headroom — the index reaches the value ceiling long before the index cap.

The repository's own test demonstrates the end state in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-361`: after a few years at 98% utilization on the XLM curve, `update_indexes` panics with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (cap never engaged), and subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` both panic — "The market is frozen: exits and repayments accrue first and hit the same panic."

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds: once the market crosses the i128 value ceiling mid-accrual, no verb can ever execute again for that market because accrual precedes everything and deterministically panics. All supplier principal and accrued interest in the market becomes permanently unwithdrawable; borrower collateral posted in other markets cannot be released through repayment of this debt; bad debt cannot be cleaned. For a billion-token-scale market this is total loss of liveness for all funds in that book.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by unprivileged users through ordinary `supply` + `borrow`: an attacker (or organic whale activity) supplies a very large principal to a high-decimals market and borrows near max utilization, then simply waits for compounding — or calls `update_indexes` — until the accrued index pushes `borrowed * borrow_index` past `i128::MAX`. No privileged action, oracle manipulation, or special parameters are required; cap lifting shown in the test (`lift_caps`) corresponds to governance-set supply caps, but even standard caps on high-decimal tokens (e.g. 18-decimals, billion-scale books) place the ceiling within reach of sustained high utilization, since required growth is only ~170x of index while the XLM-style steep curve exceeds that within a few years at ~98% utilization. Likelihood is moderate (requires a large market and time at high utilization), hence High rather than Critical.

### Recommendation
Saturate rather than panic in the accrual-path value computations: use `mul_div_floor_saturating`-style math (already used by `protocol_fee_shares` and `update_supply_index`'s division) or explicit `checked_mul` fallback for `scaled_to_original` and `borrowed.mul(new_borrow_index)` inside `accrue_step`, and clamp accrued debt value at `i128::MAX` while still advancing `last_timestamp` so the market remains operable. Alternatively, pre-check in `update_borrow_index`/`global_sync` whether `borrowed * new_borrow_index` would overflow and cap the index at the largest value that keeps the product representable. Also consider a per-market `i128`-scaled supply cap or an accrual path that skips interest booking when the product would overflow instead of reverting.

### Proof of Concept
Existing executable PoC: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361` (`a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap`).

1. Create market `BIG18` (18 decimals, XLM curve); lift caps.
2. `BOB` supplies `1e9 * 10^18` raw units via `supply` (unprivileged).
3. `ALICE` supplies collateral and borrows ~98% of the book via `borrow` (unprivileged).
4. Advance ledger time year by year, calling permissionless `update_indexes(["BIG18"])`.
5. Once `borrowed * borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX`, `accrue_step` → `scaled_to_original` panics `MathOverflow` (`common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60`, `common/src/math/fp.rs:13-15`).
6. `borrow_index` remains below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (`index.rs:15`), so the cap never stops the panic; every later `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, and `update_indexes` panics identically — the market is permanently frozen (asserted at test lines 354-356).
