### Title
Hostile HyperSwap V2 pre-seed beyond the graduation swap-rebalance budget locks the launched token's LP far from the curve close price - ([File: packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol])

### Summary
`Bonding.finalizeGraduation` seeds the HyperSwap V2 `TOKEN/LT` pair via `_seedUniswapV2Direct` → `_seedRebalancing` → `_pairRebalance`. Any unprivileged address can permissionlessly `factory.createPair(token, lt)` and `pair.mint(attacker)` against a self-chosen, arbitrarily large and skewed reserve ratio before `finalizeGraduation` runs. The corrective swap that is supposed to bring the pool back to the curve's closing ratio is capped by `_swapBudget` at 99% of `Bonding`'s own fixed graduation inventory (bounded by the ~$9K USD graduation threshold), not at whatever swap size is required to correct the attacker's pre-seed. A pre-seed sized to dwarf that fixed budget makes the rebalance swap bind at the cap, leaving the pool materially off the curve close price when `LPLock.recordLock` permanently locks it.

### Finding Description
`_seedRebalancing` reads the pre-existing pool reserves and, for the "mint pre-seed" regime, computes a no-fee rebalancing swap via `_noFeeSwapInput`, then executes it through `_pairRebalance` with `maxSwap` capped to 99% of Bonding's *own* inventory: [1](#0-0) 

The cap itself is explicitly sized off `Bonding`'s balance, not off the attacker's pre-seed size: [2](#0-1) 

The natspec on `_swapBudget` explicitly documents that "for any realistic pre-seed `s_unconstrained` is orders of magnitude below `maxSwap`, so the cap doesn't bind... It only kicks in for catastrophic pre-seeds beyond our budget capacity" — i.e. the defense's price-correction guarantee is conditioned on the pre-seed staying within an unenforced size bound. The AGENTS.md pre-seed writeup confirms the resulting opening-price precision is only bounded ("~50 bps") under the assumed pre-seed size, and that the dedicated end-to-end regression suite asserting "attacker P&L ≤ 0" was removed and is "no longer enforced by automated tests": [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

Once the swap under-corrects, `_routerDepositAndDispose` deposits `Bonding`'s remaining inventory at whatever ratio the capped swap left the pool at (not the cached curve-close ratio), and `finalizeGraduation`/`LPLock.recordLock` finalize the lock unconditionally — there is no re-check that the post-seed price matches the cached curve close, and no retry path once the LP is minted and locked: [5](#0-4) 

Because pair creation and `pair.mint` are fully permissionless and unbounded in size (an attacker can size the pre-seed with borrowed/flash-loaned LT or Token, and the pre-seed capital is recovered as their own LP position — no capital is destroyed, only the ratio matters), the attacker's pre-seed size is not constrained by the protocol's fixed, small graduation-time budget.

### Impact Explanation
This produces exactly the impact class the task calls out: "an LP seeded away from the curve close price." A catastrophically-sized hostile pre-seed forces the corrective swap in `_pairRebalance` to bind at its 99%-of-Bonding's-inventory cap, so `_routerDepositAndDispose` locks the launched token's canonical HyperSwap V2 pool at a ratio far from the bonding curve's true closing price. Post-open, the token's market price on its only trading venue can be dramatically depressed (or inflated) relative to its LT-denominated fair value — mirroring the CUPID incident's severe single-token price dislocation driven by an oversized, single-transaction reserve manipulation. The attacker holds the (unlocked) pre-existing LP minted at the hostile ratio and can withdraw/dump it once arbitrageurs move the price back, extracting value from anyone who trades the mispriced pool in between, while `LPLock.recordLock` (a one-shot call `finalizeGraduation` cannot skip or retry) has already permanently locked the protocol's own LP at the bad price.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is non-trivial: `factory.createPair` and `pair.mint` are permissionless HyperSwap V2 operations reachable by any wallet, requiring no privileged role, and the target token/LT pair address is deterministically known ahead of graduation (needed to front-run phase 1→phase 2). The only barrier is capital size relative to `Bonding`'s fixed graduation-time inventory (bounded by the small `$9K` USD graduation threshold), which is a modest amount of capital for a motivated attacker (optionally flash-loan funded, since the pre-seed capital converts into the attacker's own LP claim rather than being spent).

### Recommendation
Bound the acceptable pre-seed size relative to the graduation's own inventory (e.g., revert or fall back to a bounded-loss path — not a silent partial correction — when the required rebalance swap would need to exceed a fraction of `Bonding`'s inventory that can't guarantee the resulting price stays within an acceptable band of the cached curve-close ratio), and/or re-derive and re-add the removed `HostilePreSeed.t.sol` end-to-end assertions (wrong-opening-price bound, attacker-no-profit, LP-capture bound) as enforced CI regressions rather than relying on manual re-derivation, so a catastrophic pre-seed is caught rather than silently locked via `LPLock.recordLock`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker computes the deterministic HyperSwap V2 pair address for `(token, lt)` and calls `factory.createPair(token, lt)` before the token's threshold-crossing buy lands (or immediately after phase 1 `_enterGraduating` fires, before phase 2).
2. Attacker transfers a very large amount of `lt` (e.g., borrowed via flash loan or otherwise sized far beyond `Bonding`'s cached `ltFromPair`/`tokensForLP` for this token) and a small/dust amount of `token` into the pair, then calls `pair.mint(attacker)` — mirroring the existing `test_brick_resistance_frontRun_dust_seed` / `test_hostilePreSeed_*` scaffolding in `test/TwoPhaseGraduation.t.sol` but scaled so the pre-seed's implied `_noFeeSwapInput` far exceeds `_swapBudget(_ltSwapInventory(...))` / `_swapBudget(tokenAddress.balanceOf(...))`.
3. Anyone calls `finalizeGraduation(token)`. `_seedRebalancing` detects the pool is TOKEN/LT-rich, computes the required rebalance swap, and `_pairRebalance` clamps it to `maxSwap` (99% of Bonding's fixed inventory) — insufficient to restore the cached `tokensForLP/ltFromPair` ratio.
4. `_routerDepositAndDispose` deposits the remaining inventory at the still-skewed post-swap ratio; `LPLock.recordLock` locks the resulting LP unconditionally, and `TokenGraduated` fires with the pool open materially off the curve's last price — reproducible by extending the existing `test_hostilePreSeed_*` tests in `test/TwoPhaseGraduation.t.sol` with a pre-seed size well beyond `_swapBudget`'s bound and asserting the post-`finalizeGraduation` pool ratio deviates from `tokensForLP/ltFromPair` by more than the documented ~50 bps ceiling.

```text
    /// @dev Cap the rebalance swap at 99% of the available side's budget,
    ///      so the subsequent `addLiquidity` always has a non-zero amount
    ///      of BOTH sides to deposit. Without this, an extreme hostile
    ///      pre-seed (massively imbalanced reserves) drives the
    ///      unconstrained `_noFeeSwapInput` past our per-side budget,
    ///      `_pairRebalance` clamps to the full budget, and the swap
    ///      consumes 100% of one side. `_routerDepositAndDispose` then
    ///      skips `addLiquidity` (`remToken == 0` or `remLT == 0`),
    ///      `finalizeGraduation` returns `liquidity = 0`, and
    ///      `LPLock.recordLock(...)` records a zero-sized lock — the
    ///      attacker's pre-existing LP becomes 100% of the pool. Reserving
    ///      1% guarantees the deposit leg always lands AND mints non-zero
    ///      LP at the post-swap ratio. The 1% comes off the swap, not the
    ///      deposit — for any realistic pre-seed `s_unconstrained` is
    ///      orders of magnitude below `maxSwap`, so the cap doesn't bind
    ///      and behaviour is unchanged. It only kicks in for catastrophic
    ///      pre-seeds beyond our budget capacity, where the alternative
    ///      is bricking.
    function _swapBudget(
        uint256 budget
    ) internal pure returns (uint256) {
        return (budget * 99) / 100;
    }
```

```markdown
### Attacker P&L outcome

After the fix, the attacker holds LP at the curve-close ratio. The arbitrage-back-to-true-price step that the attacker wanted to extract from is gone (we already arb'd it during the rebalance, paying the V2 0.3% fee back to ourselves as ~99% LP holder via the pair's K-invariant accounting). Attacker's LP claim ≈ what they put in, modulo the `MINIMUM_LIQUIDITY` lock and a tiny share of the fee they paid. **Net P&L ≤ 0** — the attack is cost-negative. This property was previously asserted directly but is no longer guarded by an automated test; future protocol changes that touch the rebalance / deposit path should re-derive it manually.

### Pool-open precision

Honest graduations open at 0 bps gap (Regime 1, exact direct mint). Dust mint pre-seeds also open at ~0 bps — they take the `_seedDirectMint` fallback, which deposits at the cached ratio with only a vanishing skew from the dust reserves. Larger mint pre-seeds (where the rebalance swap fires) open within ~50 bps — the structural ceiling is the V2 fee landing between rebalance and deposit (~30 bps) plus integer rounding in the router's `quote()`-based split (~10 bps). 50 bps is well inside "exploit denied" — arbitrage closes the gap within blocks and the attacker still loses pre-seed value.
```
