### Title
Sustained high-utilization accrual overflows the RAY value domain and permanently freezes a market - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
Interest accrual converts scaled RAY debt into token-denominated value before applying the borrow-index cap. For a sufficiently large debt book, a high borrow index makes this intermediate conversion exceed `i128`; `scaled_to_original` panics with `MathOverflow`. Every state-changing market operation accrues first, so once the boundary is reached, repayment, withdrawal, liquidation, and index updates all revert.

### Finding Description
Pool debt accounting stores positions and market totals as RAY-scaled amounts and periodically rescales them by the accrued borrow index. The vulnerable calculation multiplies a RAY-scaled principal by an elevated index inside `scaled_to_original`.

The repository’s regression test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` demonstrates the boundary:

- an 18-decimal market uses the XLM rate curve;
- utilization is held at approximately 98%;
- repeated yearly `update_indexes` calls eventually fail with `MathOverflow`;
- the stored borrow index remains below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`;
- subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` calls fail with the same error.

The failure occurs before the index cap can engage, meaning the configured cap does not bound the vulnerable conversion.

### Impact Explanation
The affected market becomes permanently unusable under the deployed code. Suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot liquidate, and keepers cannot update indexes. Since every verb performs accrual before its operation, there is no unprivileged recovery path. User funds remain frozen unless governance performs a contract upgrade.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is constrained by the extreme scale required: the market must combine whale-sized scaled debt with sustained high utilization over a long accrual horizon. An unprivileged borrower can establish the required utilization if sufficient liquidity and collateral exist, then any caller can trigger the failing conversion through `update_indexes`. The issue does not depend on malformed input, privileged access, or external contract behavior, but it requires an unusually large and persistent market state.

### Recommendation
Perform scaled-value conversion with widened intermediate arithmetic, such as an `I256`/`u256` multiply-divide, before checking the result against `i128`. Apply the borrow-index ceiling before the potentially overflowing conversion and ensure accrual reaches a deterministic terminal state rather than panicking. Add a production-level invariant covering maximum scaled debt multiplied by `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and keep the existing regression test as a boundary case.

### Proof of Concept
1. Supply collateral and call `Controller::borrow(caller, account_id, borrows, to)` to establish a very large debt position in a high-utilization market.
2. Advance ledger time while utilization remains high.
3. Repeatedly call:

   `Controller::update_indexes(caller, vec![HubAssetKey { hub_id, asset }])`

   for the debt market.

4. Once `scaled_amount * borrow_index` exceeds the `i128` conversion domain, `scaled_to_original` panics with `MathOverflow` even though `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
5. Calls to `repay`, `withdraw`, `liquidate`, and subsequent `update_indexes` all reach accrual first and fail identically, leaving the market frozen.
