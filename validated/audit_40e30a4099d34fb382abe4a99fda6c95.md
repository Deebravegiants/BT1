### Title
Excess native token sent to `EvmHost.dispatch()`/`fundRequest()` is refunded to the `EvmHost` contract instead of the depositor and is permanently lost - (File: `evm/src/core/EvmHost.sol`)

### Summary
`EvmHost.dispatch(DispatchPost)`, `EvmHost.dispatch(DispatchGet)`, and `EvmHost.fundRequest()` all allow paying the relayer fee with native token by forwarding the caller's entire `msg.value` into `IUniswapV2Router02.swapETHForExactTokens`, requesting only the exact `fee` amount of `feeToken`. Any unspent native token from that swap is refunded by the router to `msg.sender` of the swap call — which is `EvmHost` itself, not the original account that called `dispatch()`/`fundRequest()`. `EvmHost` has no logic to forward that refunded ETH back out to the original depositor, so the excess becomes permanently stuck in the contract.

and identically in `dispatch(DispatchGet)`: [2](#0-1)

In each case, the entire `msg.value` supplied by the caller is forwarded to `swapETHForExactTokens{value: msg.value}(fee, path, address(this), block.timestamp)`. The canonical `UniswapV2Router02.swapETHForExactTokens` implementation only consumes the ETH needed to produce the exact `fee` output amount and refunds `msg.value - amountIn` to `msg.sender` of that call. Since `EvmHost` itself is the caller of the router (it forwards `msg.value` from its own balance), the refund lands back on the `EvmHost` contract's own balance, not on the depositor who originally called `dispatch()`/`fundRequest()` with an excess `msg.value`.

This is structurally identical to the wfCash root cause described in the report: a "pay in native, swap/lend for an exact required amount" flow refunds leftover native token to the *calling contract* rather than the *end depositor*, and the calling contract has no mechanism to detect or forward that specific refund back to the correct user. There is no accounting of per-call refund amounts, so even a privileged sweep of stray ETH from `EvmHost` could not correctly attribute the refund to the right depositor.

### Impact Explanation
Any user who overestimates the native-token amount needed to cover the relayer fee when calling `dispatch()` or `fundRequest()` (which is a normal usage pattern, since callers must guess the swap rate in advance without knowing the router's exact execution price) will have the unspent portion of their `msg.value` permanently trapped in `EvmHost`. This is unbacked/frozen ETH within the protocol's core dispatch path, reachable directly by any unprivileged message dispatcher on a single transaction. Because no event or storage records the amount refunded per caller, the funds cannot be reliably returned even by a future upgrade or governance action.

### Likelihood Explanation
High likelihood: any caller providing native token payment via `dispatch()`/`fundRequest()` who overpays even slightly (e.g., due to slippage buffering, since the exact router execution price is not known ahead of time) triggers this loss on every such call. This is a core, frequently used entry point (native token payment for message dispatch), not a rare edge case.

### Recommendation
Track `EvmHost`'s ETH balance before and after the `swapETHForExactTokens` call (similar to `UniV4UniswapV2Wrapper.swapETHForExactTokens`'s balance-delta pattern) and forward any leftover ETH back to `_msgSender()` in `dispatch(DispatchPost)`, `dispatch(DispatchGet)`, and `fundRequest()`.

### Proof of Concept
1. A user calls `EvmHost.dispatch{value: X}(post)` where `post.fee` requires only `Y < X` worth of native token to swap for the exact `feeToken` fee.
2. `EvmHost` forwards the full `X` to `swapETHForExactTokens{value: X}(post.fee, path, address(this), block.timestamp)`.
3. The router consumes `Y` and refunds `X - Y` in ETH to `msg.sender`, which is `EvmHost`'s own address (since `EvmHost` made the external call).
4. `EvmHost.dispatch()` returns without ever transferring the `X - Y` refund back to the original caller; it remains in `EvmHost`'s balance with no accounting of who is owed it and no built-in withdrawal path back to that user.
