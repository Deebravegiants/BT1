### Title
Uniswap V4 spot-price used as a market-rate oracle for Simplex intent fills enables two-way manipulation arbitrage - ([File: sdk/packages/simplex/src/funding/uniswapV4/UniswapV4FundingPlanner.ts])

### Summary
Simplex, the reference intent-filler ("solver") for `IntentGatewayV2` orders, prices curveless cross-asset legs directly off the *instantaneous* Uniswap V4 pool mid-price (`sqrtPriceX96`) rather than an exchange-rate/TWAP-style feed. This is the same class of bug as the stETH/ETH market-rate-feed finding: a single, freely tradable spot price is used symmetrically for both directions of value transfer, and the only defense is a static-deviation guard that bounds price level but not the cost of moving it — so an attacker who can nudge the pool within the allowed band can extract value from the solver on a fill, then reverse the trade.

### Finding Description
`resolveLegRates` in `sdk/packages/simplex/src/strategies/fx.ts` prices any curveless pair whose `token0` is a USD stable directly from the venue quote: [1](#0-0) 

The venue quote comes from `UniswapV4FundingPlanner.getExoticTokenPrice`, which selects the most-liquid position and computes the price via `computeDirectPoolPriceUsd`: [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

As the project's own flow documentation states, this is the raw current-block pool mid, with no TWAP, no fee-tier adjustment, and no size/impact term, and it is used as a *single* price for both bid and ask: [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

The only mitigation is `checkPriceGuard`, which rejects a fill only if the venue quote drifts more than a static `maxDeviationBps` from an operator-configured `referencePrice` — it bounds the *level* of the manipulated price, not the *cost* an attacker must pay to move it there: [6](#0-5) 

This mirrors the stETH/ETH report exactly: a market (spot, tradeable) rate feed is used where an exchange-rate-style, execution-cost-aware price is required. Whatever the current pool price is, it is achievable by any actor willing to pay the price-impact cost of a swap that stays within `maxDeviationBps` — and once inside that band, the guard cannot tell a "real" quote from a manipulated one.

### Impact Explanation
Because the same instantaneous mid is used for pricing outputs the solver commits (its own escrowed/withdrawn funds via `UniswapV4FundingPlanner.planWithdrawalForToken`) against a cross-chain order that is irrevocable once filled (`IntentGatewayV2` settlement dispatched over Hyperbridge), an attacker can:
1. Push the V4 pool's spot price for the exotic token away from its fair value (e.g., via a flash-swap) while staying within the configured `maxDeviationBps` band.
2. Submit or trigger a real fill of a favorable order while the price is skewed, causing the solver to pay out (or accept) tokens at the manipulated rate — a direct value transfer from the solver's escrowed/LP-funded inventory to the attacker.
3. Reverse the manipulating trade in the same or a following transaction/block, recovering the capital used to move the price and keeping the arbitrage profit.

This is a direct theft-of-funds vector against the solver's committed liquidity (and, transitively, order counterparties relying on a fair fill), reachable by any unprivileged actor who can trade against the referenced Uniswap V4 pool and submit/trigger an intent fill — no privileged role is required.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is Medium: it requires (a) a `[vault.uniswapV4]`-funded pair configured without bid/ask curves (an explicit, documented Simplex mode), and (b) a pool with liquidity thin enough, or an attacker with capital large enough, to move the price within the configured `maxDeviationBps` while the fill executes. Both conditions are realistic for "exotic" long-tail tokens, which is precisely the use case this pricing mode targets (e.g., cNGN in the shipped example config).

### Recommendation
Replace or supplement the raw pool mid with a manipulation-resistant reference: use a time-weighted average price (TWAP) over multiple blocks/observations instead of `slot0`'s instantaneous `sqrtPriceX96`, apply the pool's own fee tier and a size/impact term to the quote (rather than extending the mid linearly across the whole notional in `computeLegPolicyOutput`), and make `checkPriceGuard` bound divergence against the TWAP baseline rather than a static, operator-set reference alone. Consider requiring a minimum observation window/liquidity depth before trusting a V4-pool-priced pair for a fill of a given size.

### Proof of Concept
1. Configure a curveless pair funded by `[vault.uniswapV4]`, e.g. USDC/cNGN as in `docs/content/developers/evm/simplex/pricing.mdx`, with a `referencePrice`/`maxDeviationBps` guard set loosely enough to allow normal price movement.
2. Attacker performs a swap against the referenced V4 pool (`BASE_USDC_CNGN_V4_POOL_ID`-style pool as exercised in `sdk/packages/simplex/src/tests/strategies/fx.mainnet.test.ts`) to move `sqrtPriceX96`/tick within the allowed deviation band, skewing `token0Price`/`token1Price`.
3. Attacker (or an accomplice) places/wins an intent order on this pair while the skew is live; Simplex's `resolveLegRates` → `computeDirectPoolPriceUsd` prices the fill off the skewed mid, and `checkPriceGuard` passes because the skew is inside `maxDeviationBps`.
4. The solver fills at the bad rate, funding the output via `UniswapV4FundingPlanner.planWithdrawalForToken` (withdrawing LP inventory priced at the skewed rate).
5. Attacker reverses the initial swap, recovering their capital and keeping the differential extracted from the solver's inventory — the two-way arbitrage described in the original stETH/ETH report, replayed against Simplex's venue-priced legs.

	private async resolveLegRates(
		orderId: string | undefined,
		leg: ResolvedLeg,
		cappedPairNotional: Decimal,
		venueUsdPrice: (chain: string, token1Address: string) => Promise<Decimal | null>,
	): Promise<LegRates | null> {
		// Explicitly configured curves always win — the venue only prices pairs
		// with no curves at all (and never same-token pairs, where a venue quote
		// would just be the asset's own USD price, not a spread).
		const curveless = !leg.pair.bidPricePolicy && !leg.pair.askPricePolicy
		if (curveless && !isSameTokenPair(leg.pair) && USD_STABLE_SYMBOLS.has(normalizeSymbol(leg.pair.token0))) {
			const venueUsd = await venueUsdPrice(leg.token1Chain, leg.token1Address)
			if (venueUsd) {
				// Guard compares the venue's token1-per-USD quote against the static reference.
				if (!this.checkPriceGuard(orderId, leg.token1Chain, new Decimal(1).div(venueUsd))) {
					return null
				}
				// A pool mid is ONE price, not a book: there is no opposite side
				// to report a round-trip margin against. The price guard above is
				// the venue-specific defense.
				const venueRate = new Decimal(1).div(venueUsd)
				return { rate: venueRate, oppositeRate: null, priceSource: "venue" }
			}

	async getExoticTokenPrice(chain: string, exoticToken: string): Promise<Decimal | null> {
		const state = this.stateByChain.get(chain)
		if (!state || !state.isHydrated()) return null

		try {
			await state.refresh()
		} catch (err) {
			this.logger.error({ err, chain }, "Failed to refresh state for price query")
			return null
		}

		const tokenLower = exoticToken.toLowerCase()
		let bestPrice: Decimal | null = null
		let bestLiquidity = 0n

		for (const pos of state.allPositions()) {
			if (pos.currency0.toLowerCase() !== tokenLower && pos.currency1.toLowerCase() !== tokenLower) continue
			const sdkPool = state.getSdkPool(pos.tokenId)
			if (!sdkPool) continue

			const result = this.computeDirectPoolPriceUsd(pos, sdkPool, chain)
			if (result && result.exoticToken.toLowerCase() === tokenLower) {
				const poolLiquidity = state.getPoolLiquidity(pos.tokenId)
				if (poolLiquidity > bestLiquidity) {
					bestPrice = result.priceUsd
					bestLiquidity = poolLiquidity
				}
			}
		}

		if (bestPrice) {
			this.logger.debug({ chain, token: tokenLower, priceUsd: bestPrice.toString() }, "Exotic token price computed")
		}
		return bestPrice
	}

	private computeDirectPoolPriceUsd(
		pos: HydratedV4Position,
		sdkPool: V4Pool,
		chain: string,
	): { exoticToken: string; priceUsd: Decimal } | null {
		const usdc = this.configService.getUsdcAsset(chain).toLowerCase()
		const usdt = this.configService.getUsdtAsset(chain).toLowerCase()
		const c0 = pos.currency0.toLowerCase()
		const c1 = pos.currency1.toLowerCase()

		if (c0 === usdc || c0 === usdt) {
			// currency0 is stable → exotic is currency1
			// token1Price = "token0 per token1" = USD per exotic
			return {
				exoticToken: c1,
				priceUsd: new Decimal(sdkPool.token1Price.toFixed(18)),
			}
		}

		if (c1 === usdc || c1 === usdt) {
			// currency1 is stable → exotic is currency0
			// token0Price = "token1 per token0" = USD per exotic
			return {
				exoticToken: c0,
				priceUsd: new Decimal(sdkPool.token0Price.toFixed(18)),
			}
		}

		return null
	}

    -> rate = 1 / venueUsd

`computeDirectPoolPriceUsd` returns the **raw pool mid** derived from `sqrtPriceX96`. The pool's
fee tier is read and stored on the hydrated position (`pos.fee`) but never applied to the price,
and there is no size or impact term — `computeLegPolicyOutput` extends the mid linearly across the
whole priced quantity. `checkPriceGuard` is the only defense on this path, and it checks deviation
from a static reference, not execution cost. A venue-priced pair that has to swap through its own
pool to source inventory pays a fee tier it never quoted against.

## Pool-Based Pricing

When **`[vault.uniswapV4]`** lists at least one position, cross-asset pairs without curves derive bid/ask prices from **Uniswap V4 pool state** (current tick). The pool acts as the price oracle instead of a static curve. Note this yields a **single** price used in both directions — a venue-priced pair has no bid/ask spread of its own, so its margin comes from `order.fees` alone.

With Uniswap V4 positions configured, you can **omit** `bidPriceCurve` and `askPriceCurve` on the pair. Pool pricing requires the pair's `token0` to be a USD stablecoin, and same-token pairs always need their curve. The optional **`spreadBps`** field (basis points) sets the slippage tolerance for on-chain LP redemptions; defaults to `50` (0.50%).

Uniswap V4 venue pricing uses pools that pair the exotic token with **USDC or USDT** (addresses from your chain config). When multiple positions exist for the same exotic token on a chain, the most-liquid qualifying pool's price is used.

## Uniswap price guards

Pool-based pricing trusts the live pool, which leaves the solver exposed to a manipulated, stale, or thin pool returning a bad quote. To bound that risk, give a position a **`referencePrice`** and **`maxDeviationBps`**. Whenever the pool quote on that chain drifts more than `maxDeviationBps` above or below the reference, the solver refuses to fill — the order is rejected before any bid is submitted.

`referencePrice` is expressed in **exotic tokens per USD**, the same units as the bid/ask curves. The two fields must be set together; omit both to leave the chain unguarded.

```toml lineNumbers
[vault.uniswapV4]
# referencePrice is the expected cNGN per USD;
# reject if the quote is more than 2% off.
# The two go together — one without the other is rejected.
[[vault.uniswapV4.positions]]
chain           = "EVM-8453"
tokenId         = "2087350"
referencePrice  = "1575"
maxDeviationBps = 200
