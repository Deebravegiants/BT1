### Title
Silent truncation of accumulated relayer fees via `U256::low_u128()` causes fund loss/DoS on withdrawal - ([File: modules/pallets/relayer/src/withdrawal.rs])

### Summary
`pallet-relayer`'s `withdraw` extrinsic reads the relayer's accumulated fee balance as a `U256` and truncates it to `u128` with `available_amount.low_u128()` when building the substrate-side withdrawal payload, silently discarding the high 128 bits instead of using checked/saturating conversion.

### Finding Description
`Fees::<T>::get(...)` stores accumulated relayer rewards as `U256`, and `Pallet::accumulate` credits it via plain `*inner += fee;` (unchecked/unsaturated `U256` addition) driven by attacker-relayable withdrawal proofs in `accumulate()`. [1](#0-0) 
When a relayer later calls `withdraw`, the substrate destination branch converts the full `U256` balance down to `u128` with `.low_u128()`, an operation that truncates rather than reverting when the value exceeds `u128::MAX`: [2](#0-1) 
This is the exact "integer overflow/wraparound" bug class from the reference CVE: a value that no longer fits its target width is silently reduced, corrupting downstream accounting instead of failing safely.

### Impact Explanation
If a relayer's accumulated `U256` fee balance ever exceeds `u128::MAX` (whether through legitimate high-volume accumulation over time, a fee-token with extreme decimal precision, or repeated `accumulate_fees` calls with large per-message fees), the `WithdrawalRequest.amount` sent to the substrate destination silently wraps to `available_amount mod 2^128`. The `Fees` storage entry is zeroed after dispatch regardless of the truncation, so the difference between the true accrued balance and the truncated payout is permanently lost — a freezing-of-funds condition for the relayer. Because `Fees` is `U256` while the wire format and destination pallet only support `u128`, this data loss is a structural minefield in the accounting path a relayer transaction can trigger.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reaching `u128::MAX` (~3.4×10^38) in raw token base units is astronomically unlikely under realistic fee-token economics, and the EVM-destination branch instead calls `.into()` on the full `U256` (an `alloy_primitives::U256`, so that leg does not truncate). This significantly limits real-world exploitability of the substrate-destination leg to extreme edge cases or misconfigured fee tokens with adversarial decimal counts, rather than a directly attacker-triggerable exploit from a single transaction under normal parameters.

### Recommendation
Replace `available_amount.low_u128()` with a checked conversion (e.g. `u128::try_from(available_amount)` or `TryInto`) and reject the withdrawal (returning an error) rather than silently truncating when the balance does not fit in `u128`. Consider also bounding `Fees` accumulation to `u128` from the start, or explicitly capping/rejecting `accumulate` when a credit would push the balance beyond `u128::MAX`, to fail closed instead of silently losing relayer funds.

### Proof of Concept
1. A relayer repeatedly submits valid `accumulate` withdrawal proofs (each backed by real cross-chain delivery receipts) for a substrate destination chain, driving `Fees::<T>::get(chain, relayer)` (a `U256`) above `u128::MAX` through legitimate accumulation over enough calls/fee amounts.
2. The relayer calls `withdraw` targeting that substrate `dest_chain`.
3. In the substrate branch, `available_amount.low_u128()` truncates the balance to its low 128 bits before encoding `WithdrawalRequest.amount` [2](#0-1) , while the `Fees` entry itself is expected to be zeroed for the full amount post-dispatch, permanently losing the excess above `u128::MAX`.
