### Title
Stale oracle prices survive the untrusted flash-position callback - (File: `contracts/controller/src/strategies/flash_position.rs`)

### Summary
`flash_position` caches oracle prices before invoking a caller-selected receiver, then uses the same `Context` for post-callback solvency checks. A malicious receiver can move an oracle venue during the callback, while the controller continues valuing the new position with the stale pre-callback prices.

### Finding Description
`process_flash_position` requires only caller authorization and a flashloanable debt market before accepting an arbitrary WASM receiver. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

The controller prefetches prices for the account, debt asset, and declared collateral assets before the callback. [3](#0-2)  `prefetch_strategy_prices` populates `Context::token_prices`, and `Context` explicitly retains already cached market values rather than refreshing them. [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

The debt is minted and forwarded to the receiver before `execute_flash_position` runs. [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6)  The receiver is then invoked with the account ID, debt asset, measured amount, controller address, and caller-controlled data. [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8) 

Inside that callback, the receiver can call an external venue such as an Aquarius or Soroswap pool and move the reserves used by the configured price leg; neither pool is on the controller call stack. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10) 

After the callback returns, the controller deposits the measured collateral and calls `strategy_finalize` using the original `Context`. [12](#0-11)  `strategy_finalize` immediately runs `enforce_post_pool_solvency` against that retained context, so its LTV, health-factor, and minimum-collateral checks consume the stale price snapshot. [13](#0-12) 

### Impact Explanation
The receiver keeps the forwarded debt tokens and only has to return enough declared collateral to satisfy the stale valuation. [7](#0-6) [14](#0-13)  If the callback lowers the collateral leg or raises the debt leg after the price snapshot, finalization can create an account whose current health factor is below one even though the stale snapshot passes the solvency gate. [15](#0-14) 

The attacker can abandon the undercollateralized account while retaining the borrowed assets, leaving liquidation proceeds or collateral value below the minted debt and causing protocol insolvency or socialized bad debt. [6](#0-5) [16](#0-15) 

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack is reachable by a single unprivileged caller through `flash_position`; it needs a deployed receiver contract, a flashloanable debt market, a collateral declaration, and enough venue liquidity or capital to move the configured pricing leg. [17](#0-16)  No controller or pool reentry is required because the price-moving venue is a separate contract. [18](#0-17) [8](#0-7) 

Profitability is bounded by the oracle’s venue liquidity and dual-leg tolerance, but the vulnerable ordering lets the attacker choose the trade size and loan notional for the specific market. [5](#0-4) [16](#0-15) 

### Recommendation
Do not carry cached token prices across the untrusted receiver callback. After `invoke_receiver` returns and before `strategy_finalize`, invalidate or replace `Context::token_prices` and refetch strict prices, while preserving the post-mutation market indexes and accounting updates already recorded in the cache. [19](#0-18) 

Equivalently, construct a fresh pricing context for the final solvency check and inject the pool-returned indexes into it, or explicitly reload the debt and collateral prices after the last external callback leg. [5](#0-4) [13](#0-12) 

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy a receiver `R` holding collateral asset `C`; choose a flashloanable debt asset `D` whose configured oracle leg can be moved by an attacker trade. [20](#0-19) 
2. Call `flash_position(caller=A, account_id=0, spoke_id=S, mode=Multiply, debt=(H,D), amount=B, receiver=R, data=attack_plan, collaterals=[((H,C), min_C)], refund_assets=[])`. [21](#0-20) 
3. Before invoking `R`, the controller caches the pre-manipulation prices for `C` and `D`, mints `B` debt onto the new account, and forwards the measured debt tokens to `R`. [22](#0-21) [7](#0-6) 
4. In `execute_flash_position`, `R` executes its own Aquarius or Soroswap trade that lowers `C`’s strict collateral price, transfers `min_C` of `C` to the supplied `controller` address, and keeps the forwarded `D`. [23](#0-22) [10](#0-9) 
5. The controller measures the `C` deposit and finalizes using the cached pre-trade price, accepting a position whose current oracle valuation is below the required health factor. [24](#0-23) [5](#0-4) 
6. The attacker retains `B` units of `D`; once valued at current prices, the collateral is insufficient to cover the account’s debt, leaving the pool with bad debt after liquidation or bad-debt cleanup. [16](#0-15)
