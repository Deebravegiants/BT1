### Title
Uniswap V4 phantom-bid liquidity backing can be spoofed by momentarily transferring in a borrowed position NFT — ([File: sdk/packages/sdk/src/protocols/intents/phantom-aggregation.ts])

### Summary
`aggregatePhantomBids` lets a solver back a phantom bid leg with Uniswap V4 LP position NFTs it merely *names* in its bid. The only defense against naming someone else's liquidity is a single, point-in-time `ownerOf` read taken when the aggregation runs. Because that check has no holding-period or lock requirement, an attacker can transiently acquire a position NFT (e.g. borrow it from a colluding party or an NFT-collateral facility, use it to pass the ownership check, then move it back out) to fabricate backed liquidity it never actually holds for the fill — the same "instant ownership at a checked instant" bug class as the referenced NFT-flashloan report, applied to on-chain LP-position ownership instead of ERC-721 sale eligibility.

### Finding Description
A phantom bid's `paymasterAndData` can declare Uniswap V4 `tokenId`s that "stand behind its quote" [1](#0-0) . The aggregator treats the declaration as "a POINTER, not a claim of size" and defends it purely by checking on-chain ownership at read time: `readV4Position` calls `ownerOf(tokenId)` via `eth_call` at `blockTag = "latest"` (or the indexer's supplied tag) and returns the current owner [2](#0-1) .

The verification step then filters declared positions to those whose `state.owner` equals the bidding solver at that exact block: [3](#0-2) 

There is no requirement that the solver has held the position for any prior duration, no check of a `Transfer` event history, and no lock/escrow of the NFT during the bid window. This is functionally identical to the report's core weakness: `nft.balanceOf(msg.sender) > 0` checked once, satisfiable by borrowing the asset for the instant of the check and returning it immediately after, because ownership itself — not sustained custody or economic exposure — is what gates the benefit.

Passing this check has real consequences beyond bookkeeping:
- It determines whether the bid is priced with the cheaper Uniswap pool-fee haircut instead of the protocol-fee haircut: `poolPriced = declaration.uniswapV4Positions.length > 0` [4](#0-3) .
- It contributes non-zero leg weight to the weighted median price other solvers and downstream consumers rely on, per the test asserting a leg is "weighted purely by the position" with zero actual ERC-20 balance [5](#0-4) .
- The verified `tokenId`s are persisted into the `SolverV4Positions` indexer entity and re-used by later inventory reads until the *next* bid re-declares or a re-read shows the owner has changed [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) . If the borrowed NFT is moved back out immediately after the check, the stored declaration continues to advertise it as the solver's inventory until the indexer happens to re-read that specific tokenId and finds a different owner.

### Impact Explanation
A solver with no genuine capital can fabricate Uniswap-V4-backed liquidity for a single block by momentarily holding a borrowed LP position, pass the aggregator's `ownerOf` snapshot check, and thereby (a) obtain the discounted pool-fee haircut it is not entitled to, (b) skew the weighted median price fillers and the protocol rely on for that leg, and (c) leave a stale, unbacked declaration recorded in `SolverV4Positions`/`LiquidityProviderBalanceV2` that other consumers treat as real inventory until the next independent re-read. Because Simplex actually "funds fills out of these positions" at settlement time, a fill relying on this phantom-backed capacity can fail or be under-collateralized once the borrowed NFT is gone, producing solver-selection distortion and potential fill griefing without the attacker ever committing real funds.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. No privileged role is required — any address can submit a phantom bid declaring arbitrary `tokenId`s, and the exploit only requires momentary possession of a Uniswap V4 position NFT (via a colluding counterparty transfer or any NFT-lending mechanism) timed to the aggregator's read. The attacker does need to synchronize the transfer with the specific block/time the aggregation runs its `ownerOf` call, which adds some execution difficulty but no special access.

### Recommendation
Do not accept a single-block ownership snapshot as sufficient backing. Options: (1) require the position to have been owned by the declaring solver for a minimum number of blocks prior to the bid (checked via a prior-block `ownerOf` read or `Transfer` event history) so a same-block borrow-and-return cannot pass; (2) require positions used to back bids to be escrowed/locked into a protocol-controlled or time-locked contract for the duration of the bid window; (3) re-verify ownership at multiple, unpredictable blocks spanning the bid window rather than a single "latest" read, mirroring the original report's suggestion of locking the asset for at least one block to defeat flash-style borrowing.

### Proof of Concept
1. Solver `S` (attacker) has no real Uniswap V4 liquidity.
2. `S` arranges for a counterparty `C` (or a same-block NFT loan) to transfer LP position `tokenId=X` to `S` at block `N`.
3. `S` submits a phantom bid declaring `uniswapV4Positions: [X]`, timed so the indexer's aggregation for the order's commitment reads `ownerOf(X)` at block `N`, per `readV4Position`'s `ownerOf` call [8](#0-7) .
4. The check passes: `entry.state.owner === normalizedSolver`, so the position counts toward `S`'s leg weight and the bid receives the pool haircut [9](#0-8) .
5. Immediately after (block `N+1` or later in the same block if ordering allows), `S` transfers `X` back to `C`.
6. `S`'s declaration is persisted in `SolverV4Positions` and counted as backing inventory in subsequent `inventoryReading.service.ts` sweeps until an independent re-read of `X` observes a different owner — a window during which `S` is treated as holding real, pool-backed liquidity it does not actually control.

// Uniswap V4 positions stand behind its quote. Bids declare both inside paymasterAndData, which
// the userOpHash covers, so the declaration is authenticated by the solver's existing bid
// signature (the `signature` field is excluded from the hash and therefore unusable). The overload
// applies to phantom bids only: a real fill's paymasterAndData keeps its functional EntryPoint
// semantics, and nothing on the real-fill path ever parses this format.
//
// Layouts, by leading version byte:
//
//   v1  0x01 ‖ chainCount(1) ‖ chainCount × ( len(1) ‖ utf8 state machine id )
//   v2  0x01-body ‖ posCount(1) ‖ posCount × ( len(1) ‖ big-endian tokenId )   with 0x02 leading
//
// v2 is v1 with a positions section appended, so decoding is one parser with an optional tail.
// The encoder emits v1 whenever there are no positions, which keeps existing solvers' bids
// byte-identical to what they produce today; a v1 blob decodes under v2 readers as "no positions".
// A v2 blob read by a pre-v2 decoder fails its version check and reads as no declaration at all —
// the same degradation as an absent blob, never a misparse, because both versions reject trailing
// bytes rather than half-reading.
//
// Declared positions are implicitly on the order's own chain: a phantom bid is submitted per
// chain, so the tokenIds a solver names in it are the ones it holds on that chain. The declaration
// is a POINTER, not a claim of size — the indexer reads each position's liquidity on-chain and
// checks the position is owned by the very solver that signed the bid, so a solver can point at
// its own positions but cannot inflate them, nor borrow someone else's.

"""
The Uniswap V4 positions one solver funds fills from, as named in its latest phantom bid.

A bid's `paymasterAndData` is the only place a position is ever named. The aggregation values the
declared positions while it has them in hand, but between bid windows nothing else knows the
tokenIds, so a balance re-read triggered by a fill could not see that inventory at all — and
carrying the last window's VALUE forward is worse than not seeing it, because simplex funds fills
out of these positions: such a fill drains the position inside the fill transaction while wallet and
vault balances barely move, so a carried value keeps advertising exactly what was just spent.
Recording the tokenIds lets the fill path re-read the positions instead of guessing.

One row per solver, replaced wholesale each time that solver bids, so the row is always the live
declaration and never a history to reconcile. A solver that stops bidding keeps its last row —
silence is not a withdrawal — and a position it has since burned or sold simply reads back as no
longer owned, which is the check every consumer applies before valuing one.
"""
type SolverV4Positions @entity {
	"""
	The solver's lowercase EVM address. One instance per LP by construction: the declaration is a
	single current fact about that solver, not a per-position or per-window record.
	"""
	id: ID!

	"""
	The liquidity provider these positions belong to. Same address as `id`; the link is what makes
	them reachable from a provider query.
	"""
	provider: LiquidityProvider!

	"""
	State machine ID of the chain the declaring bid was for — the only chain these positions are
	visible to, since a bid is per chain.

	This row assumes a solver declares on ONE chain, which holds while Uniswap V4 is configured for a
	single chain (see `src/addresses/uniswap-v4.addresses.ts`). If a second chain is ever configured,
	the writer warns rather than silently overwriting the other chain's declaration, and the key has
	to grow to (chain, solver).
	"""
	chain: String! @index

	"""
	PositionManager tokenIds the solver declared, verified on-chain as owned by it when the bid
	window closed. Empty when the solver bid without declaring any.
	"""
	tokenIds: [BigInt!]!
