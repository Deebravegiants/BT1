### Title
Accrual-time `scaled_to_original` overflow permanently freezes a market's repay, withdraw, and liquidation paths - (File: common/src/rates/scaling.rs)

### Summary
The GitLab CVE is a denial of service caused by an unhandled exception on a read path. The direct analog in XOXNO Lending is an unhandled i128 overflow inside `scaled_to_original`, reached through `accrue_step` in `common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-61`. Every state-changing entrypoint (`supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `update_indexes`, `recapitalize`, flash and strategy verbs) runs interest accrual first via the pool's `interest::global_sync`. Once `borrowed * borrow_index` (or `supplied * supply_index`) exceeds the i128/RAY domain, accrual panics before the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap can engage, and the market is permanently frozen: no repayment, no withdrawal, no liquidation, no bad-debt cleanup.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` computes `scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)` and `scaled_to_original(env, supplied, supply_index)` to derive utilization. `scaled_to_original` is `scaled.mul(env, index)` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`), which panics on `MathOverflow` when the RAY-scaled product exceeds i128. The borrow index is only clamped *after* this multiply (`update_borrow_index` clamps to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` in `common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19`), so value overflow hits before the index ceiling. The harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-361`) demonstrates the freeze end-to-end: `try_update_indexes_for`, `try_withdraw_raw`, and `try_repay` all revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. `docs/reference/formulas.md:432-437` concedes the bound: "valid caps and bounded indexes do not guarantee that future accrual fits. Value overflow can occur before the index ceiling and block repayment/withdrawal."

A single unprivileged address can drive this: `supply` of a very large position followed by near-total `borrow` at high utilization pushes `borrowed` (scaled) and the index upward together until the product crosses the overflow boundary on a later `update_indexes` call. Because accrual is a precondition of every exit and cleanup path, the panic is not transient — no input an unprivileged caller can craft un-freezes the market once `borrowed * borrow_index` overflows, and `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` also accrue first.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of all funds in the affected market: suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot liquidate, and bad debt cannot be cleaned or recapitalized. This matches the accepted impact classes (permanent freezing of funds / contract unable to operate).

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a whale-scale position (on the order of the i128/RAY domain, ~10^27 scaled units times an index) and sustained high utilization over multiple accrual periods. That is expensive but not privileged — any address holding or able to borrow the token supply can submit `supply`/`borrow` and later `update_indexes`. Medium likelihood; severity consistent with the source advisory.

### Recommendation
In `accrue_step`, saturate rather than panic: clamp `borrowed_original`/`supplied_original` at the representable maximum (which drives utilization to 100% and rate to max), or check the product before `scaled_to_original` and pin the borrow index to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` instead of reverting, so accrual degrades gracefully and exits remain possible.

### Proof of Concept
See `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-361`: supply `BILLION * 10^18` of an 18-decimal asset, borrow 98% of it, then advance time and call `update_indexes` — the call fails with `MathOverflow` inside `scaled_to_original` before `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` is reached, and subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` fail identically, permanently freezing the market.
