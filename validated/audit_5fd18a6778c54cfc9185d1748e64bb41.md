### Title
DAO treasury reserve share is permanently lost when interest accrual index advances faster than the fee-reserve mint rounds up - ([File: mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-stx.clar])

### Summary
Every vault's `accrue` function advances the borrow index (`index`) and liquidity index (`lindex`) unconditionally whenever a positive time delta produces any change, but only mints the DAO treasury's reserve share (`treasury-lp`) when the per-call `reserve-inc` (computed from the borrow-index delta since the *last* `accrue` call) rounds to a non-zero amount. Because the index advance and the reserve mint are gated by two different, decoupled conditions, an attacker (or normal high-frequency usage) can force many small `accrue` calls whose individual `debt-delta` is too small to produce a non-zero `reserve-inc`, while `index` still moves forward each time. Once `index` advances, that interest window can never be revisited by a future `debt-delta` calculation, so the treasury's reserve-factor share of that interest is permanently and irrecoverably lost — mirroring the reported Voter/Bribe pattern where an aggregate-threshold gate (`claimable/DURATION > 0`) silently drops a payout while the surrounding epoch state moves on regardless.

### Finding Description
`accrue()` in each vault contract (`v0-vault-stx.clar`, `v0-vault-sbtc.clar`, `v0-vault-usdc.clar`, `v0-vault-usdh.clar`, `v0-vault-ststx.clar`, `v0-vault-ststxbtc.clar`) computes: [1](#0-0) 

```clarity
(let ((next (next-index))
      (nliq (next-liquidity-index))
      (scaled-principal (var-get principal-scaled))
      (old-debt (mul-div-down scaled-principal idx INDEX-PRECISION))
      (new-debt (mul-div-down scaled-principal next INDEX-PRECISION))
      (debt-delta (if (> new-debt old-debt) (- new-debt old-debt) u0))
      (reserve-inc (mul-div-down debt-delta (var-get fee-reserve) BPS))
      (treasury-lp (if (> reserve-inc u0) (mul-div-down reserve-inc (total-supply) (- (total-assets-preview) reserve-inc)) u0)))
  (if (not (is-eq idx next)) (var-set index next) false)
  (if (not (is-eq lidx nliq)) (var-set lindex nliq) false)
  (if (> treasury-lp u0) (try! (ft-mint? zft treasury-lp .dao-treasury)) false)
  ...)

The identity that must hold across an accrual window is:
`interest generated by borrowers (Δindex × scaled-principal) = interest paid to suppliers (Δlindex effect) + reserve share minted to dao-treasury`.

`index` is committed to storage (`var-set index next`) whenever it changes at all — even by 1 unit — but the treasury's `reserve-inc`/`treasury-lp` computation is re-derived each call strictly from `old-debt` (the *just-committed* `idx`) versus `new-debt` (the freshly computed `next`). There is no persistent accumulator that carries forward any reserve fraction that rounded down to zero in a given call. Once `var-set index next` executes, that specific delta is baked into the new baseline (`old-debt` for the next call becomes the just-stored `next`), so any reserve share that was truncated to zero in the mint condition is gone permanently — it can never be recovered by a subsequent call.

Because `accrue()` is invoked as a side effect of nearly every public entry point (`system-borrow`, `system-repay`, deposit/redeem, etc., all call `(try! (accrue))` first), any actor can trigger `accrue()` at high frequency (e.g., every block, or even multiple times per block via dust-sized borrow/repay calls) to keep each individual `debt-delta` — and hence `reserve-inc = debt-delta * fee-reserve / BPS` — below the rounding threshold, while `index` still advances by a nonzero, committed amount each time. Over many such calls the cumulative `index` growth (and therefore cumulative real interest paid by borrowers) can be substantial, yet the treasury mint condition `(> treasury-lp u0)` never fires because each individual slice is sub-threshold.

This is structurally identical to the reported bug class: `Voter.distribute()` resets `claimable[_gauge] = 0` (the aggregate state) while gating the actual payout (`deliverBribes()`) on a separate threshold check (`claimable/DURATION > 0`) — so funds that exist can become permanently unreachable once the aggregate state moves past them. Here, `index` (the aggregate accrual state) advances unconditionally while the payout to `dao-treasury` is gated on a separately-rounded, per-call threshold, with no way to reclaim the lost slices afterward.

### Impact Explanation
This causes permanent, irrecoverable loss of protocol reserve yield (unclaimed yield belonging to the DAO treasury) with no path to recovery, since the underlying `debt-delta` history is not retained once `index` is overwritten. This falls under "permanent freezing/loss of unclaimed yield," a High-severity impact category, though the realistic magnitude depends on `fee-reserve` (BPS) and vault decimals — assets with coarse `mul-div-down` rounding (e.g., low `fee-reserve` bps or low-decimal debt deltas) are more exposed. Suppliers are unaffected because `lindex` growth is driven by `liquidity-rate`, computed independently of `reserve-inc`, and is applied every call regardless of the reserve mint outcome.

### Likelihood Explanation
No privileged access is required — any user (or the protocol's own routine transaction traffic) can trigger `accrue()` via ordinary vault operations. An attacker who deliberately submits many small state-changing transactions in short succession (block-by-block) to keep `debt-delta` below the reserve-mint rounding threshold can systematically suppress `treasury-lp` minting while `index` keeps advancing legitimately. The condition is easiest to trigger on vaults with lower-decimal underlying assets or lower `fee-reserve` settings, where `mul-div-down debt-delta fee-reserve BPS` rounds to zero more readily for realistic per-block interest deltas.

### Recommendation
Do not let `index` (and `old-debt`'s implicit baseline) advance independently of the reserve accounting. Either:
1. Maintain a persistent "unclaimed reserve dust" accumulator across calls (add current call's rounded-down remainder back into the next `debt-delta` before computing `reserve-inc`), so no fraction is silently dropped when `index` commits, or
2. Compute `reserve-inc` from the growth of a dedicated "last-reserve-index" checkpoint that only advances when `treasury-lp` is actually minted (so unminted deltas are not consumed by unrelated index updates), analogous to moving the reward-forwarding call outside the aggregate-reset in the original Voter.distribute() fix.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker observes a vault (e.g., `v0-vault-stx.clar`) with `fee-reserve` set such that `reserve-inc = debt-delta * fee-reserve / BPS` requires `debt-delta` above some threshold `T` to round to a non-zero value.
2. Attacker submits a sequence of minimal borrow/repay (or deposit/redeem) transactions in consecutive blocks, each of which calls `accrue()` via `system-borrow`/`system-repay`.
3. Each call's `next-index` computation yields a small `debt-delta` (because the elapsed `time-delta` since the previous call is only one block), keeping `reserve-inc` at `0` and skipping the `(try! (ft-mint? zft treasury-lp .dao-treasury))` branch, while `(var-set index next)` still executes because `idx != next`.
4. Repeating this over many blocks lets `index` accumulate real interest growth (verifiable via `total-debt`), while `dao-treasury`'s zft balance never increases from these accruals — the reserve-factor share of that accumulated interest is permanently unminted and unrecoverable, since subsequent `debt-delta` calculations only look at the (already advanced) `idx` going forward.
