Based on my investigation, I found a plausible analog in the same-chain intent fill path — `TRANSACTION_FEES` are paid out entirely to whichever solver happens to submit the fill that completes the order, regardless of how much of the order that solver actually filled.

### Title
Solver can construct a minimal completing fill to capture the entire `TRANSACTION_FEES` escrow that other solvers' partial fills funded - (File: evm/src/apps/intentsv2/IntrinsicIntents.sol, evm/src/apps/intentsv2/IntentsBase.sol)

### Summary
`_fillSameChain` releases proportional input escrow to each partial filler, but `_withdraw` only pays out the order's accumulated `TRANSACTION_FEES` (the relayer/fill fee escrowed at `placeOrder`) when `finalize == true`, i.e. only to the solver whose fill happens to push `isFullyFilled` to `true`. This mirrors the Tokemak bug class: a value that should accrue proportionally to the action taken (shares burned / fill amount contributed) is instead paid out in full to whoever triggers a particular state transition, letting an attacker construct a sequence of calls to capture value disproportionate to their contribution.

### Finding Description
`_withdraw` in `IntentsBase.sol` gates fee release strictly on the `finalize` flag: [1](#0-0) 

`finalize` is set to `isFullyFilled`, computed in `_fillSameChain`: [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

Because `isFullyFilled` is a boolean computed per-call from cumulative `_partialFills` state, any solver who supplies the last remaining sliver of the order's required output — even 1 wei — becomes the one whose call passes `finalize=true` into `_withdraw`, and thus receives 100% of `_orders[commitment][TRANSACTION_FEES]`, no matter how small their own contribution to the order was relative to earlier partial fillers.

This is structurally identical to the Tokemak issue: in both cases, a piece of accumulated value (destination-vault rewards in Tokemak; the escrowed transaction fee here) is released in full upon a particular triggering action (any withdrawal that pulls from a rewarding destination vault; the fill that completes the order) rather than pro-rata to the size of that action. An attacker can watch pending partial fills, or even coordinate with themselves across multiple addresses, to intentionally be the address that submits the final dust-sized completing fill and sweep the entire fee escrow that was economically earned by prior, larger fillers.

### Impact Explanation
This lets a solver extract fee value that was not economically earned by their contribution, at the expense of earlier partial fillers of the same order who receive none of the `TRANSACTION_FEES` payout despite having filled the bulk of the order. Given same-chain partial fills are explicitly a supported, permissionless feature (`docs/content/developers/evm/intent-gateway/overview.mdx` describes "escrow released proportionally" for partial fills, implying users would reasonably expect the fee to also track proportional contribution), this breaks the implicit fairness invariant and is a real (if bounded to `order.fees`) value-extraction vector reachable by any unprivileged solver submitting a fill transaction.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate: it requires an order to receive multiple partial fills from different solvers (a supported, expected flow) and an opportunistic (or self-colluding) solver to submit the final completing fill for a negligible amount. This is a rational, low-cost strategy for any actively-monitoring solver/MEV searcher racing same-chain fills, since dust-sized completing fills are cheap and gas-competitive.

### Recommendation
Track `TRANSACTION_FEES` proportionally to each filler's contribution (e.g., release `fees * fillAmount / totalRequired` at each partial fill, or accumulate a per-filler credit that is paid out immediately rather than only at `finalize`), instead of paying the entire escrowed fee to whichever address happens to trigger the finalize branch.

### Proof of Concept
1. User places a same-chain order with `order.fees = 100` (fee token) and `output.assets[0].amount = 1000`.
2. Solver A fills 999 of the 1000 required output, receiving proportional escrow via `_withdraw(body, false, false)` (finalize=false, no fee payout) per `IntrinsicIntents.sol:126-129`.
3. Solver B (attacker) observes the pending 1-unit remainder and submits a fill of `1`, causing `amountFilled == totalRequired`, `isFullyFilled = true`.
4. `_withdraw(body, false, true)` executes with `finalize=true`, transferring the full `100` fee-token escrow to Solver B per `IntentsBase.sol:472-477`, even though Solver B contributed only 0.1% of the order's fill.
