### Title
Bad-debt cleanup leaves phantom supply shares that permanently gate new deposits - ([File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs])

### Summary

Permissionless `clean_bad_debt` can reduce a market’s `supply_index` to the protocol floor without burning or resetting the surviving total-supply shares. Those shares retain a positive floored redemption value after the market has been fully written down, creating a permanent backing shortfall. Because `supply` rejects any market with a positive shortfall, future suppliers cannot enter until the stranded claims are recapitalized or otherwise removed.

### Finding Description

An unprivileged caller can invoke `Controller::clean_bad_debt` for an account whose debt exceeds its collateral while the collateral is below the configured dust threshold. The controller gathers every remaining borrow and deposit position and submits them to `Pool::seize_positions`. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

For each borrow-side seizure, the pool calculates the position’s bad debt and calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`. [3](#0-2)  That function caps the write-down at the total supplied value, but then clamps the resulting index upward to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`; it does not reduce `cache.supplied()`. [4](#0-3) 

After a full write-down, `supplied` therefore remains nonzero and each surviving share can still unscale to a positive asset claim at the floored index. The backing calculation compares those floored claims with cash plus outstanding debt, so the market retains a positive shortfall. [5](#0-4)  New deposits call `require_backed_market` before minting shares, meaning every subsequent `supply` reverts while the stranded claims remain. [6](#0-5) 

### Impact Explanation

A single attacker can turn accrued bad debt into a market-wide supply halt. The cleanup is intended to socialize losses, but the floor leaves unburned claims that exceed available backing indefinitely. New supply is blocked, recapitalization must cover claims that no longer correspond to real residual collateral, and existing suppliers race for whatever cash remains. This causes temporary freezing of funds and protocol insolvency for the affected market.

### Likelihood Explanation

The path is permissionless: `clean_bad_debt(caller, account_id)` only requires caller authorization, open debt, debt greater than collateral, and collateral at or below the dust threshold. [7](#0-6)  An attacker can create such a position on their own account and allow interest or adverse price movement to make the debt exceed collateral. When the unpaid debt reaches the market’s total supplied value, the same call drives the supply index to its floor and triggers the stranded-claim state.

### Recommendation

When a borrow-side seizure consumes the entire supplied value, explicitly burn or otherwise remove all remaining supply and revenue shares rather than preserving `supplied` at a nonzero total under a floored index. Alternatively, mark the market fully written down and special-case future supply/reopening only after all legacy shares are cleared. `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` should leave `backing_shortfall == 0` immediately after socialization.

### Proof of Concept

1. An honest supplier supplies `S` units of an asset through `Controller::supply`.
2. The attacker opens an account with collateral that later falls to or below `BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD`, then borrows the same asset.
3. Interest accrues until the attacker’s debt exceeds both their collateral and the market’s total supplied value.
4. The attacker calls `Controller::clean_bad_debt(attacker, attacker_account_id)`.
5. The controller submits the attacker’s borrow and deposit positions to `Pool::seize_positions`; the borrow leg invokes `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`.
6. `bad_debt` is capped at total supplied value, making `remaining == 0`; the new index is then set to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, while `supplied` remains unchanged.
7. `unscale_supply_floor(supplied)` still returns positive claims, so `backing_shortfall` is positive.
8. A new supplier calls `Controller::supply`; the pool reaches `require_backed_market` and reverts with `PoolInsolvent` before minting shares.

```rust
pub(crate) fn apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache: &mut Cache, bad_debt: Ray) {
    let total_supplied_value = cache.supplied().mul(cache.env(), cache.supply_index());

    if total_supplied_value == Ray::ZERO {
        return;
    }

    let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
    let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);

    let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
    let new_supply_index = cache
        .supply_index()
        .mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);

    cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
```

```rust
pub(crate) fn backing_shortfall(cache: &Cache) -> i128 {
    let supplied_claim = cache.unscale_supply_floor(cache.supplied());
    let outstanding_debt = cache.unscale_borrow_ceil(cache.borrowed());
    let backing = cache.cash().saturating_add(outstanding_debt);
    supplied_claim.saturating_sub(backing).max(0)
}
```
