## Title
Multisig timelock `override_threshold` is silently clamped by normal-threshold-authorized owner removals, letting a minority quorum erode the "supermajority" safeguard and bypass the timelock on high-value transactions - (File: `aptos-move/framework/aptos-framework/sources/multisig_account.move`)

### Summary
The Aptos `multisig_account` module supports an optional `MultisigAccountTimeLock` with an `override_threshold`, whose entire purpose is to require a *stronger* level of consensus than the normal `num_signatures_required` (`k`-of-`n`) before a transaction can bypass the timelock and execute immediately. This is a custody control: multisig accounts are resource accounts that hold live APT/fungible-asset balances, and the override threshold is meant to protect those assets from being drained quickly by only the minimum required signers. However, `update_owner_schema` — invoked by `remove_owner`/`remove_owners`, which only requires the *normal* `num_signatures_required` approvals — automatically clamps `override_threshold` down to the new (smaller) owner count whenever owners are removed, without any additional validation that the removal was intended to weaken this stronger safeguard. Because owner removal only needs the base threshold, a coalition that controls exactly `num_signatures_required` owners can chain multiple `remove_owners` calls to progressively shrink the owner set and force `override_threshold` down toward `num_signatures_required + 1`, destroying the intended elevated-consensus gate.

### Finding Description

and the invariant enforced at configuration time is that `override_threshold` must be strictly greater than `num_signatures_required`: [2](#0-1) 

Owner removal, however, is authorized purely through the *normal* multisig transaction flow (i.e., only `num_signatures_required` approvals, not `override_threshold`): [3](#0-2) 

`remove_owners` calls into `update_owner_schema`, and after the owner list shrinks, the function automatically re-clamps the timelock's `override_threshold` to the new owner count and emits an event — but performs no check that the *entities who authorized this removal* actually met the elevated `override_threshold` consensus that they are effectively weakening: [4](#0-3) 

This is confirmed directly by the module's own test, which shows a *single* `remove_owner` call (itself gated only by the normal threshold in the live proposal flow) silently dropping `override_threshold` from 3 to 2: [5](#0-4) 

The custody invariant being broken is directly analogous to the source report: in the external Wise Lending bug, each partial liquidation call individually satisfies a local health check, but the *sequence* of calls destroys the intended global cap (max 50% liquidatable without bad debt) because the attacker chooses which sub-state to shrink first. Here, each `remove_owners` call individually satisfies the local check (`num_owners >= num_signatures_required`), but the *sequence* of calls destroys the intended global custody invariant — that bypassing the timelock on a resource account holding live assets requires meaningfully more consensus than the normal execution path. Because `override_threshold` is auto-clamped as a side effect rather than being independently re-validated against its own elevated-consensus requirement, a coalition holding only the minimum `k` signatures can, over several chained calls, ratchet the override threshold down to `num_signatures_required + 1`, at which point the same minimal coalition (plus at most one cooperating/compromised extra owner) can immediately execute high-value transactions (e.g., transferring the multisig-held APT/FA balances out) without waiting out the timelock that the remaining honest owners relied on to detect and veto malicious proposals.

### Impact Explanation
Multisig accounts here are resource accounts that can hold APT and fungible-asset balances directly under the multisig's control, and the timelock/override design exists specifically to prevent a bare-minimum-threshold coalition from immediately draining those custody-held assets. This bug lets such a coalition erode the override safeguard purely through the ordinary, lower-privilege owner-removal path, ultimately achieving immediate (timelock-free) execution authority over asset-moving transactions with only marginally more than the normal signature threshold — a de facto authority escalation over resource-account-held funds that the timelock+override mechanism was explicitly designed to prevent. This is a custody-grade authority takeover on a live financial control (multisig-owned assets), qualifying as High impact.

### Likelihood Explanation
The precondition is that a multisig configures a timelock with `override_threshold` (an opt-in but supported and documented feature) and that an attacker/malicious coalition controls exactly the base `num_signatures_required` owners — a realistic scenario since compromise of exactly the operational quorum (not the full owner set) is the common threat model multisigs are built to resist. No special preconditions on external price oracles or protocol-specific state are needed (unlike the original report, which required manipulated collateral factors); the erosion path is entirely self-contained within `multisig_account.move`'s own owner-management and timelock logic, and is exercised by the module's own unit test (`test_owner_removal_clamps_override_threshold`), confirming the mechanics occur under normal execution, not just a contrived PoC.

### Recommendation
Do not silently auto-clamp `override_threshold` as a side effect of ordinary (normal-threshold) owner removal. Instead:
- Require that any owner-removal transaction that would reduce `override_threshold` below its configured value be itself approved by at least `override_threshold` signatures (i.e., require the elevated consensus to authorize weakening the elevated consensus requirement), or
- Automatically disable/void the override capability (rather than silently shrinking it) whenever owner count changes, forcing owners to explicitly reconfigure and re-approve a new `override_threshold` via `upsert_timelock` at the *original* elevated bar.

### Proof of Concept
1. Configure a multisig with `n=10` owners, `num_signatures_required = 2`, and `override_threshold = 9` (near-unanimous requirement to bypass the timelock), via `upsert_timelock`.
2. A coalition of 2 owners (meeting only `num_signatures_required`) creates, approves, and executes a `remove_owners` transaction removing 7 of the other owners, leaving 3 owners total. Per `update_owner_schema`'s clamp logic, `override_threshold` is automatically reduced to `3` (since it exceeded the new owner count), as directly demonstrated by `test_owner_removal_clamps_override_threshold`.
3. Repeat the process (or use `update_signatures_required` in tandem, subject only to the `EINVALID_TIMELOCK_OVERRIDE_THRESHOLD` bound `override_threshold > num_signatures_required`) to progressively narrow `override_threshold` toward `num_signatures_required + 1`.
4. With `override_threshold` reduced to a value the same 2-owner coalition (plus at most one more colluding/compromised owner) can trivially reach, the coalition creates a high-value asset-transfer transaction (e.g., draining the resource account's APT/FA balance) and executes it immediately, bypassing the timelock period that the remaining legitimate owners relied on to detect and reject the transaction — despite `override_threshold` originally having been configured to require near-unanimous consent for exactly this kind of immediate execution.
