### Title
Threshold-crossing buy dictates the graduation LP open price, letting an attacker profitably sandwich curve-tail slippage against fresh HyperSwap LP depth - (File: `packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol`)

### Summary
The DODO `adjustPrice` sandwich pattern (overpay buying against thin/pre-adjustment liquidity, force a liquidity-concentration event, then sell into the newly concentrated/deep liquidity at a better rate) maps onto alt.fun's graduation flow: the price at which the HyperSwap V2 LP opens is fixed to whatever the curve's reserves are immediately after the single threshold-crossing buy that triggers `_enterGraduating` [1](#0-0) , and that LP is a permissionless, immediate, deep constant-product pool the same trader can sell back into via `finalizeGraduation` + `Zap.sell`.

### Finding Description
`Router._computeBuy`/`_computeSell` implement a fee-free constant-product curve where price impact grows sharply as the virtual token reserve shrinks toward its floor [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) . `Bonding.canGraduate()` is evaluated at the end of every buy inside `_executeBuy`, and phase 1 (`_enterGraduating`) fires inline at the end of the exact buy that crosses the USD or supply threshold [4](#0-3) . `_prepareGraduationLiquidity` then reads the pair's reserves *immediately after that buy* to compute `tokensForLP`/`ltFromPair`, pinning the LP's opening price to the curve's marginal price at the exact moment the attacker's own trade lands [5](#0-4) . `finalizeGraduation` is explicitly permissionless — "anyone can call to rescue a stuck token" [6](#0-5)  — and seeds the HyperSwap pool at that cached ratio with "zero price gap" to the curve close [7](#0-6) .

An attacker who sizes a buy to be exactly the threshold-crossing trade absorbs the curve's worst (tail-end, thinnest-liquidity) slippage on that last chunk of tokens, since the constant-product curve's realized average price for a large marginal buy is materially worse than the instantaneous close price. Immediately after, they call `finalizeGraduation` themselves (no wait for the keeper is required — it is fully permissionless) to materialize a freshly-seeded HyperSwap pool sized at up to 250M tokens (`LP_RESERVE`) and the full LT raised, opened at exactly that closing price [8](#0-7) . They then sell the tokens just purchased back through `Zap.sell` → the new deep HyperSwap pool, at a lower price-impact than what the curve's shrinking virtual reserve charged them on the way in, capturing the delta between the curve's tail-end average buy price and the fresh pool's average sell price at the same nominal marginal rate.

This is structurally the same pattern as `adjustPrice`: an action taken by an ordinary participant (here, simply transacting near a threshold) triggers a liquidity-concentration event (fresh, deep LP at the closing price) that the same participant can immediately exploit by trading into it right after generating the price that seeded it.

### Impact Explanation
Profits are extracted from the protocol/LPs' `LP_RESERVE` allocation (up to 250M tokens plus the LT raised by the curve) — the same pool of value normal token buyers and the protocol intended to seed post-graduation liquidity with. Because the size of both the threshold-crossing buy and the LP being seeded are attacker-influenceable (the attacker chooses the buy size that crosses the threshold), this is a repeatable strategy against every graduating token, not a one-off edge case.

### Likelihood Explanation
Both preconditions are trivially reachable by an unprivileged address: `Zap.buy` is a normal user-facing entry point with no special permission required, and `finalizeGraduation` is documented as callable by anyone [9](#0-8) . No governance, keeper cooperation, or privileged role is needed; a bot can watch pending-graduation tokens and always be the one to size the crossing buy and immediately finalize+sell.

### Recommendation
Decouple the LP's opening price from the single threshold-crossing trade — e.g., use a short TWAP of curve reserves leading up to threshold-crossing rather than the instantaneous post-buy reserves, or require a cooldown/second-block confirmation before `finalizeGraduation` can execute so the crossing trade cannot be atomically or near-atomically unwound against the freshly seeded pool by the same address (e.g., disallow the same `tx.origin`/address from both triggering `_enterGraduating` and selling into the pool within N blocks of `finalizeGraduation`).

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual sequence (unprivileged, no special role needed):
1. Attacker monitors a `Curve`-lifecycle token approaching its USD/supply threshold.
2. Attacker calls `Zap.buy(token, usdcAmount, minTokensOut, referrer)` sized so this single buy crosses the graduation threshold inside `_executeBuy`, which inline-fires `Bonding._enterGraduating` and caches `tokensForLP`/`ltFromPair` at the post-buy (worst-slippage) reserves [10](#0-9) .
3. Attacker (or anyone, but nothing stops the same address) immediately calls `Bonding.finalizeGraduation(token)`, which is `nonReentrant` but otherwise unrestricted, seeding the HyperSwap V2 pair at the cached ratio via `_seedUniswapV2Direct`/`_seedDirectMint` [8](#0-7) .
4. Attacker calls `Zap.sell(token, tokensJustBought, minUsdcOut)`, which routes through HyperSwap post-graduation [11](#0-10) , realizing a better average price on the sell than was paid on the tail-end curve buy, net of the flat 0.75% Zap fee (paid symmetrically on both legs) and HyperSwap's 0.3% swap fee.

I was not able to fully quantify the exact numeric profitability threshold (it depends on curve parameters like `VIRTUAL_LIQUIDITY_USD`, `CURVE_BPS`, and the specific reserve state at threshold-crossing) without running the Foundry test suite / simulating actual curve numbers, so this should be validated with a concrete PoC test (e.g., extending `test/GraduationInvariants.t.sol` or `test/TwoPhaseGraduation.t.sol`) before treating the magnitude as confirmed.

    function _computeBuy(
        address pairAddr,
        uint256 amountIn
    ) internal view returns (uint256 amountInUsed, uint256 tokensOut) {
        IPair pair = IPair(pairAddr);
        (uint256 reserveToken, uint256 reserveAsset) = pair.getReserves();
        uint256 k = pair.k();

        amountInUsed = amountIn;

        uint256 newReserveAsset = reserveAsset + amountInUsed;
        tokensOut = reserveToken - (k / newReserveAsset);

        uint256 realBalance = pair.tokenBalance();
        if (tokensOut > realBalance) {
            tokensOut = realBalance;
            uint256 cappedReserveToken = reserveToken - tokensOut;
            if (cappedReserveToken == 0) revert OverflowCapDegenerate();
            uint256 cappedReserveAsset = (k + cappedReserveToken - 1) / cappedReserveToken;
            amountInUsed = cappedReserveAsset - reserveAsset;
        }
    }

    function finalizeGraduation(
        address tokenAddress
    ) external nonReentrant {
        BondingStorage storage $ = _s();
        TokenInfo storage info = $.tokenInfo[tokenAddress];
        if (info.lifecycle != Lifecycle.Graduating) revert NotGraduating();

        address lt = info.ltAddress;
        PendingGraduation memory p = $.pendingGraduation[tokenAddress];

        // Anything in this contract beyond `p.ltFromPair` belongs to a
        // concurrent graduation on the same LT (Phase 1 transferred it
        // via `Router.graduate`) or to stray dust. Either way it is
        // off-limits to this graduation's deposit and sweep — see
        // `_routerDepositAndDispose` and `_sweepLTToOwner`.
        // Saturating subtract: a balance below `p.ltFromPair` shouldn't
        // be reachable in normal operation, but we keep finalize from
        // bricking on a Panic if any future code path or non-canonical
        // LT briefly violates the invariant.
        uint256 ltBalance = IERC20(lt).balanceOf(address(this));
        uint256 protectedLT = ltBalance > p.ltFromPair ? ltBalance - p.ltFromPair : 0;

        address lpPair = _ensureUniswapV2Pair(tokenAddress, lt);
        uint256 liquidity = _seedUniswapV2Direct(tokenAddress, lt, lpPair, p.tokensForLP, p.ltFromPair, protectedLT);

        _sweepLTToOwner(lt, protectedLT);

        info.lifecycle = Lifecycle.Graduated;
        $.graduatedPair[tokenAddress] = lpPair;
        delete $.pendingGraduation[tokenAddress];

        LPLock($.lpLock).recordLock(tokenAddress, lpPair, liquidity);

        emit TokenGraduated(tokenAddress, lpPair, liquidity, p.tokensForLP, p.lpBurned, p.unsoldBurned);

    /// @dev Burns unsold curve tokens, drains real raised LT, computes
    ///      `tokensForLP` for zero-gap LP seeding, burns the LP excess.
    ///
    ///      Price equality: `tokensForLP / ltFromPair = tokenReserve / assetReserve`,
    ///      with `ltFromPair = assetReserve - virtualLtReserve` (the LT
    ///      actually raised by the curve, excluding the launch-time virtual
    ///      seed; the seed is recovered as `Pair.k() / TOTAL_SUPPLY`, see
    ///      `_launchTimeVirtualLtReserve`). Substituting gives LP price =
    ///      `assetReserve / tokenReserve` = curve close marginal price.
    ///      Donations of LT directly to the pair don't move the stored
    ///      `assetReserve`, so they're excluded from `ltFromPair`.
    ///
    ///      Token-side donations are handled by `unsoldBurned`: any tokens
    ///      sitting in the pair beyond the curve's accounting are burned.
    ///
    ///      With virtual `tokenReserve = totalSupply` and `curveSupply = 75%`,
    ///      the parabola `tokensForLP(sold) = sold·(S−sold)/S` peaks at
    ///      `S/4 = LP_RESERVE` when `sold = S/2`, so `tokensForLP ≤ LP_RESERVE`
    ///      is mathematically invariant. The cap is defensive.
    function _prepareGraduationLiquidity(
        address tokenAddress
    ) internal returns (uint256 tokensForLP, uint256 ltFromPair, uint256 lpBurned, uint256 unsoldBurned) {
        address pairAddr = _s().tokenInfo[tokenAddress].pair;
        (uint256 tokenReserve, uint256 assetReserve) = IPair(pairAddr).getReserves();

        unsoldBurned = IPair(pairAddr).tokenBalance();
        if (unsoldBurned > 0) {
            Token(tokenAddress).burn(pairAddr, unsoldBurned);
        }

        ltFromPair = assetReserve - _launchTimeVirtualLtReserve(tokenAddress, pairAddr);
        if (ltFromPair > 0) {
            _s().router.graduate(tokenAddress, ltFromPair);
        }

        tokensForLP = assetReserve == 0 ? 0 : (ltFromPair * tokenReserve) / assetReserve;
        if (tokensForLP > LP_RESERVE) tokensForLP = LP_RESERVE;
