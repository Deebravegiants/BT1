### Title
Raised `min_borrow_collateral_usd` floor retroactively blocks partial withdrawals for existing borrowers - (File: contracts/controller/src/risk/validation.rs)

### Summary
Governance can update the instance-wide `MinBorrowCollateralUsd` floor at any time via `set_min_borrow_collateral_usd_wad` (`contracts/controller/src/storage/protocol.rs:111`). The floor is enforced inside `require_post_pool_risk_gates` (`contracts/controller/src/risk/validation.rs:55-58`), which runs as a post-operation solvency gate for any account that still has debt (`account.debt_free()` early-return at line 30). Because the check compares the account's *current* LTV-weighted collateral against the *current* floor — not the floor that existed when the position was opened — raising the floor above an existing borrower's total collateral causes every `withdraw` call to revert with `CollateralError::MinBorrowCollateralNotMet`, even withdrawals that would leave the account extremely over-collateralized (HF ≫ 1).

### Finding Description
- `require_post_pool_risk_gates` is invoked from `contracts/controller/src/positions/mod.rs` after position mutations and gates on three things: LTV coverage, HF ≥ 1, and the configured collateral floor read fresh from instance storage (`get_min_borrow_collateral_usd_wad`, `contracts/controller/src/storage/protocol.rs:103`).
- The floor check at `validation.rs:56` uses `totals.ltv_collateral.raw() < floor`, i.e., the entire remaining collateral must exceed the floor. There is no exemption path for "reducing toward zero" or "position predates the floor."
- Contrast with `validate_bulk_position_limits` (`validation.rs:99`), which explicitly grandfathers existing positions when governance lowers limits — the USD floor has no equivalent grandfathering.
- Sequence: Alice supplies collateral and borrows such that her LTV collateral is, say, $200, satisfying the $100 floor. Governance executes a ready config operation setting the floor to $300. Alice's `withdraw` of even $1 now hits `ltv_collateral ($200) < floor ($300)` and panics. Her only exits are (a) repay 100% of debt to become `debt_free` and skip the gates, or (b) supply *more* collateral to exceed $300 — both requiring funds she may not have.

### Impact Explanation
Temporary freezing of user funds. Alice cannot reduce her collateral at all while the floor exceeds her position, even though her health factor may be far above liquidation. She is exposed to liquidation during the freeze and must either source external funds to fully repay or wait for governance to lower the floor — mirroring the Symmio report where a raised `minQuoteValue` stranded existing close requests.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a governance `execute` of a floor update — a plausible, routine config operation (the floor exists precisely to be tuned). Any borrower whose LTV collateral sits between the old and new floor is affected immediately and permissionlessly; no attacker action needed beyond the user calling `withdraw`.

### Recommendation
Skip the floor check when the operation strictly reduces risk (e.g., exempt withdrawals once HF and LTV checks pass, or apply the floor only to `borrow`/risk-increasing paths), or grandfather positions by storing the floor in effect at position-open time — the same pattern already used for position limits at `validation.rs:99`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance sets `MinBorrowCollateralUsd` = $100. Alice supplies $250 of collateral and borrows; `totals.ltv_collateral` = $200. A small `withdraw` succeeds.
2. Governance executes a config op raising the floor to $300.
3. Alice calls `withdraw` for any amount → `require_post_pool_risk_gates` → `totals.ltv_collateral ($200) < floor ($300)` → `MinBorrowCollateralNotMet`. All withdrawals revert until she fully repays or tops up above $300.

Note: I verified the floor check and its storage setter, but did not fully enumerate which entrypoints call `require_post_pool_risk_gates` (only `positions/mod.rs` was confirmed as a call site); if `repay`/`withdraw` route through it as the naming and docs imply, the finding holds as stated.
