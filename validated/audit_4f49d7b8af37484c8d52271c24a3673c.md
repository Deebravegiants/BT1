### Title
Suppliers can withdraw at the pre-write-down supply index and dump bad-debt losses onto remaining suppliers - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs)

### Summary
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` socializes a loss across suppliers only at the moment `seize_positions` runs (liquidation residual cleanup, `clean_bad_debt`, or `force_socialize_bad_debt`). Before that write-down executes, a supplier's shares unscale at the un-lowered `supply_index`, and `withdraw` pays out real `cash` as long as `require_reserves` and `require_utilization_below_max` pass. An unprivileged supplier who exits before socialization keeps 100% of their claim; the eventual index reduction is borne entirely by whoever stayed.

### Finding Description
Bad debt is latent: when a borrower's collateral collapses, `borrowed` still counts the unpayable debt at `borrow_index` and `supply_index` is untouched. The write-down happens inside `ops::seize::apply` on the `Borrow` side (`interest::apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`), which is only reached via `check_bad_debt_after_liquidation` (dust-capped), permissionless `clean_bad_debt` (dust-capped, `is_socializable_bad_debt`), or owner `force_socialize_bad_debt`.

Meanwhile `ops::withdraw::accounting` resolves the withdrawal at the current `supply_index` (`resolve_withdrawal`) and `gate_and_debit` checks only `cache.require_reserves(net_transfer)` plus `require_utilization_below_max` — there is no check that pending bad debt exists, and `require_backed_market` deliberately gates `supply` only, not exit (per the documented "backing shortfall blocks entry, not exit" asymmetry). When `max_utilization` leaves headroom (e.g., a large market where the insolvent position is a fraction of total debt, or `max_utilization >= RAY`), nothing blocks the exit. A supplier monitoring prices/liquidations can front-run the write-down, withdraw full value in cash, and after `clean_bad_debt`/`seize` lowers the index, re-supply at the depressed index to receive more shares — the same buy-back-cheap effect as the reference report. The exit window exists because socialization is event-driven (seize/cleanup) rather than realized at withdrawal time.

### Impact Explanation
Early exiters transfer their pro-rata share of the loss to remaining suppliers and to protocol revenue shares (which live inside `supplied` and are written down too). The pool's cash leaves with the exiters while `borrowed` stays unpaid, deepening `backing_shortfall` for those who remain — theft of value from non-exiting suppliers, analogous to the VaultV2 report. Magnitude is bounded by available `cash` and the utilization gate, so it is partial-loss socialization dilution rather than full drainage.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. Requires: (1) an insolvent position whose residual debt will be socialized, (2) enough idle `cash` in that market to cover the exit, and (3) post-exit utilization at or under `max_utilization`. The dust-capped permissionless path (`clean_bad_debt`) limits guaranteed same-transaction socialization to small positions, but liquidation-driven socialization and owner `force_socialize_bad_debt` extend it to arbitrary sizes; the exiter only needs to be faster than the cleanup transaction, which any on-chain observer can race. No privilege, no leaked key, no oracle manipulation needed — only `controller.withdraw` on the attacker's own account.

### Recommendation
- Realize pending bad debt inside the withdraw path, or block non-liquidation withdrawals on markets with an insolvent-but-not-yet-socialized account (a controller-level flag checked in `process_withdraw`).
- Alternatively, apply `backing_shortfall`-style gating to exits once a market has debt flagged for socialization, so exiters absorb a pro-rata haircut rather than dumping it on stayers.
- At minimum, socialize residual bad debt atomically within `liquidate` regardless of the dust cap (extending `check_bad_debt_after_liquidation`), shrinking the race window.

### Proof of Concept
Sequence on market `X` (single unprivileged attacker `BOB`, supplier `ALICE` stays):

1. `CAROL` supplies collateral and calls `controller.borrow(account_carol, [(X, amt)])`. `ALICE` and `BOB` each supply `100 X` via `controller.supply`.
2. Price crash (or borrow accrual) makes `CAROL`'s debt > collateral — bad debt pending, `supply_index` of `X` unchanged.
3. `BOB` calls `controller.withdraw(account_bob, [(X, i128::MAX)], Some(bob))`. `ops::withdraw::accounting` → `resolve_withdrawal` unscales BOB's shares at the un-written-down index; `gate_and_debit` passes `require_reserves` (cash present) and `require_utilization_below_max` (market not at cap). BOB receives `~100 X` — full claim.
4. Liquidator calls `controller.liquidate(liquidator, account_carol, payments, SeizeMode::Transfer)` then `controller.clean_bad_debt(caller, account_carol)` (or owner `force_socialize_bad_debt`). `ops::seize::apply` → `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` lowers `X`'s `supply_index`; only ALICE's shares (and revenue shares) absorb the loss.
5. BOB re-supplies `100 X` at the lowered index via `calculate_scaled_supply` (floor at `supply_index`) and holds strictly more shares than before — identical payoff structure to the VaultV2 PoC.
