### Title
Debt-value i128 overflow in `scaled_to_original` permanently freezes a market before the borrow-index cap engages - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
Analogous to CVE-2024-20967's repeatable server-side crash, the pool's shared accrual step panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` once `borrowed × borrow_index` (scaled debt value) exceeds `i128::MAX`. Because every pool verb runs `global_sync` first, a market that reaches this state freezes permanently: no repay, withdraw, borrow, liquidation, or bad-debt socialization can execute on it.

### Finding Description
`interest::global_sync` loops over elapsed chunks and calls `accrue_step`, which recomputes the borrow index and then calls `calculate_supplier_rewards`, internally scaling `borrowed` debt shares via `scaled_to_original` (a `mul` in RAY arithmetic that panics on `i128` overflow). `update_borrow_index` caps the index at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (10^36), but the *value* of a large book (`debt_shares × index`) overflows `i128` well before that cap — the cap never engages. `compound_interest` also panics on overflow when the scaled exponent doesn't fit `i128`. The panic is inside a `panic_with_error`/checked-mul path, so it aborts the transaction rather than producing a wrong index.

`global_sync` is invoked unconditionally at the head of every mutating pool call (supply, withdraw, borrow, repay, seize, flash, bad-debt application), so once the next accrual step overflows, every subsequent call re-panics at the same `accrue_step` — a repeatable crash identical in effect to the CVE's hang/crash DOS.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds / contract unable to operate. All suppliers in the affected (hub, token) book lose every withdrawal path: `withdraw` accrues first and reverts. Borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot seize, `clean_bad_debt`'s pool seize call cannot run, and even `recapitalize` cannot repair the market because it too accrues first. The funds in the pool are locked indefinitely with no governance escape documented (an upgrade is the only fix). The in-repo test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs`) proves the end state: after ~170× index growth at 98% utilization, `try_update_indexes_for`, `try_withdraw_raw`, and `try_repay` all fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and `last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` — the ceiling never protects the book.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by unprivileged addresses alone: an attacker (or organic growth) builds a large market via `supply`/`borrow` (caps can be set high by governance as normal operation), sustains high utilization, and waits — or any sufficiently large book simply ages into the cliff at the steep segment of its rate curve. The trigger requires a book whose scaled debt value approaches `i128::MAX / 10^27`, i.e. very large books at high utilization over multiple years, so likelihood is moderate-low; however no privileged action is needed and, critically, there is no bounded safe harbor — the index cap that appears to bound growth is unreachable before the value overflow fires. Severity: Medium/High.

### Recommendation
In `accrue_step` (or `global_sync`), detect the would-be-overflow condition and clamp: before calling `scaled_to_original`/`calculate_supplier_rewards`, check whether `borrowed.mul(borrow_index)` fits `i128`; if not, treat the step as the at-cap case — pin `borrow_index` at the largest representable value that keeps `borrowed × index ≤ i128::MAX` (or jump to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` semantics by freezing accrual) instead of panicking. Alternatively, cap `supplied`/`borrowed` share totals at admission so `value ≤ i128::MAX / MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` is an enforceable invariant, converting the freeze into a bounded entry rejection.

### Proof of Concept
Reproduced by the protocol's own test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs`:

1. Create an 18-decimal market with the XLM steep rate curve, lift caps (`lift_caps`), and seed a whale book: `supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 10^9 * 10^18)`, then `borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", 98% of it)`.
2. Advance ledger time year-by-year, calling permissionless `update_indexes`.
3. After index growth ≈ 170× (far below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`), `try_update_indexes_for` fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW` inside `scaled_to_original`.
4. Thereafter `try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1)` and `try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0)` both fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW` — the market is frozen for every caller, forever.

Root cause chain: `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs` `global_sync` → `accrue_step` (shared in `common/src/rates/simulate.rs`) → `calculate_supplier_rewards`/`scaled_to_original` checked `i128` multiply overflows before `update_borrow_index`'s `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` clamp can take effect; and since `global_sync` precedes every verb, the panic is permanent and repeatable.
