### Title
Permanent market freeze via i128 overflow in accrual utilization read — a whale supplier/borrower bricks every pool verb for the market - (File: common/src/rates/scaling.rs)

### Summary
The bug class from CVE-2025-21501 — a low-privileged actor forcing a frequently repeatable crash — maps onto XOXNO Lending as a **permanent, unrecoverable market freeze** caused by arithmetic overflow inside the interest-accrual path that every pool mutation runs first. An unprivileged attacker who can open a sufficiently large supply position and a large borrow position on a high-decimals market can drive the market's scaled debt value past `i128::MAX` through normal interest accrual. Once the accrual step overflows, `global_sync` panics on every subsequent call, so `supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and `flash_loan` all revert for that market forever. Suppliers' funds are permanently frozen and borrowers can never repay — the protocol has no code path that touches a market without accruing it first.

### Finding Description
`global_sync` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20` runs on every pool operation and calls `accrue_chunk` per elapsed `MAX_COMPOUND_DELTA_MS` window. Each chunk calls `accrue_step`, which computes utilization via `utilization(env, scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index), scaled_to_original(supplied, supply_index))` (`common/src/rates/simulate.rs:136-175`, `common/src/rates/curve.rs`). `scaled_to_original` is `scaled.mul(env, index)` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`), and `Ray::mul`/`mul_div` panics on `i128` overflow rather than saturating.

Because `borrowed` is a RAY-scaled share amount (asset units × 10^(27 − decimals)), the product `borrowed × borrow_index` overflows `i128` long before the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY = 10^36` cap in `update_borrow_index` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19`) engages. For an 18-decimal asset, `i128::MAX` corresponds to roughly 170× RAY in scaled value — a borrow index of ~1.7 on a 100-billion-token book at ~98% utilization crosses it. The regression test in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-361` demonstrates exactly this: after enough years at 98% utilization on the XLM curve, `update_indexes` fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` calls fail identically because every verb accrues first. The borrow-index cap never engages because the value multiplication overflows first.

The attacker needs no privilege: `supply` (controller entrypoint, caller-auth token pull) and `borrow` (owner-auth on their own account, collateralized by their own supply) are both in the unprivileged surface. The attacker's cost is capital and waiting for accrual; nothing resets `last_timestamp`, unwinds the scaled amounts, or skips the overflowing read. Even `clean_bad_debt` and `recapitalize` accrue first, so governance has no in-contract recovery path short of upgrading (out of scope for remediation but confirming the freeze is permanent on the deployed code).

### Impact Explanation
**Permanent freezing of funds**: all supplier claims on the market are unreachable — `withdraw` panics in accrual before reading positions. **Protocol insolvency**: liquidations cannot execute on the frozen market, so any underwater debt there can never be cleared; healthy markets in the same physical pool are unaffected for withdrawals of other tokens, but the frozen market's suppliers lose everything and any bad debt on it is permanently uncleansable. The freeze is not a fail-closed outage (it does not depend on external price availability — ADR-0005); it is a self-inflicted arithmetic trap reachable purely through protocol-owned state, matching the CVE's "low-privileged attacker causes a hang/crash" shape.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a whale-scale position on a high-decimals market at sustained high utilization: the test uses a 18-decimal market with ~10^29 asset units supplied and ~98% borrowed, reaching the cliff after accrual growth to ~1.7–170× index. Two mitigating factors reduce likelihood: (1) supply/borrow caps (`calculate_scaled_cap`, spoke usage caps) bound the scaled amounts governance allows — but caps are literal asset-unit ceilings set per market and can be configured high enough to admit the cliff, and `calculate_scaled_cap` saturates rather than fails; (2) the overflow requires years of accrual at high utilization, giving governance theoretical time to intervene — except intervention itself (withdraw, repay, update_indexes) hits the same panic once the value crosses the bound, so the only defense is acting *before* the cliff. This is the "medium" profile: significant preconditions (capital, caps, time) but irreversible catastrophic impact once reached.

### Recommendation
- In `accrue_step` / `utilization`, compute utilization on saturating or reduced-precision values instead of `scaled.mul(index)`: e.g., compute `borrowed × borrow_index / RAY` via `mul_div` with a saturating result, or compare `borrowed > i128::MAX / borrow_index` and clamp the rate input (utilization is already clamped to `Ray::ONE`, so saturating the numerator is semantics-preserving).
- Clamp the borrow index inside `accrue_step` *before* computing interest: `if borrow_index >= MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY { skip accrual }`, so the cap in `update_borrow_index` actually engages before value multiplication.
- Add a per-market scaled-supply/borrow ceiling in listing validation (`require_cap_within_asset_domain` analog) that enforces `scaled × MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY <= i128::MAX`, making the cliff unreachable regardless of configured caps.

### Proof of Concept
The shipped adversarial test is the PoC (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360`):

1. Governance lists an 18-decimal market (`big("BIG18", 18, xlm_curve())`) with lifted caps.
2. Attacker (unprivileged): `supply(caller, payments=[{BIG18, 1 billion × 10^18 units}])`, then `supply`/`borrow` on a collateral account: `borrow(account, [{BIG18, 98% of supply}])`.
3. Time passes at ~98% utilization; each `update_indexes` compounds the borrow index. Around index ≈ 1.7e27 the product `borrowed × borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX` and `accrue_chunk` panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` inside `scaled_to_original`.
4. Every subsequent operation on the market — `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `update_indexes` — reverts with the same error. Asserted in the test: `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` both return `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (the cap never fired).

Result: all supplier funds on that market permanently frozen; market permanently inoperable — the direct analog of "unauthorized ability to cause a hang or frequently repeatable crash," upgraded to permanence because every entrypoint accrues first.
