### Title
Dust top-up of a bad-debt account's existing supply leg permanently blocks permissionless `clean_bad_debt` - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/debt.rs, docs/reference/invariants.md)

### Summary
The bug class in the external report is an unprivileged, crafted request causing denial of service. The analog here is a griefing path: any authenticated user may `supply` into an existing supply leg of someone else's account (third-party top-ups of existing legs are explicitly allowed), and a single dust-sized top-up permanently raises the account's collateral above the fixed $5 dust threshold, disabling the permissionless `clean_bad_debt` path for that account forever.

### Finding Description
`clean_bad_debt` is the permissionless bad-debt cleanup entrypoint. INV-LIQ-04 states it requires "collateral at or below the fixed $5 dust threshold" and debt greater than collateral; only the privileged owner-forced cleanup omits the dust cap [1](#0-0) . On the supply side, the controller only requires an authorized *caller* (any signed address) and enforces position limits only on new slots — topping up an existing leg of an arbitrary `account_id` is allowed, as pinned by `regression_third_party_cannot_open_new_supply_slots`, where BOB tops up ALICE's USDC leg and only opening a *new* slot is refused with `NOT_AUTHORIZED` [2](#0-1) . The slot accounting in `validate_bulk_position_limits` confirms new-position checks do not apply to already-held legs [3](#0-2) .

Therefore, the moment a victim account reaches the bad-debt state (debt > collateral, collateral ≤ $5), an attacker calls `supply(caller=attacker, account_id=victim, spoke_id, assets=[(existing_collateral_leg, ~$5+)])`. The donation is booked as the victim's collateral shares, pushing `total_collateral` above the dust threshold. Every subsequent permissionless `clean_bad_debt` reverts on the dust check. Collateral is never reduced by a failed cleanup and bad-debt liquidation of a deeply insolvent account is economically unattractive (the insolvency quote caps repayment at what collateral backs at base bonus, `normalize_repayment_plan` [4](#0-3) ), so the collateral stays above $5 indefinitely.

### Impact Explanation
Bad debt that should be socialized via `clean_bad_debt` — which writes down the supply index and releases spoke usage — instead remains on the books permanently unless the protocol owner intervenes with the privileged forced cleanup. This delays/prevents the supply-index write-down, keeps the market's `borrowed` book inflated, blocks spoke-usage release, and leaves suppliers exposed to an unrecognized shortfall. The permissionless protocol mechanism is knocked out by a ~$5 unprivileged donation, leaving cleanup dependent on an operator action the design intends only as a fallback.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a bad-debt account to exist (normal consequence of price moves/insolvency) and an attacker willing to donate ~$5 worth of an asset the victim already supplies. The attacker needs no position, no special permissions, and only one transaction per victim account since the block is permanent once collateral exceeds the threshold. Motivation: preventing the supply-index write-down protects borrowers from socialized losses being recognized, or simply griefs suppliers/keep infrastructure.

### Recommendation
Exclude donations from the dust threshold by tracking attacker-distinguishable collateral, or simpler: measure the dust test against collateral *net of the block's own supply events* — e.g., allow `clean_bad_debt` to seize/ignore collateral added after the account first qualified, or evaluate the dust cap on collateral that excludes supply legs added by non-owners. Alternatively, require owner/delegate authorization for *any* third-party supply into an account that is liquidatable or in bad-debt state, or make the permissionless cleanup owner-independent by raising it above the dust check when debt growth (interest) alone keeps the account insolvent.

### Proof of Concept
1. ALICE supplies 10,000 USDC, borrows ETH; crash USDC price so `total_debt > total_collateral` and collateral value ≤ $5 (e.g., via a tiny residual after partial liquidation, or a small account).
2. Keeper calls `clean_bad_debt(ALICE)` — succeeds (precondition check: collateral ≤ $5 passes).
3. Attacker BOB calls `supply(caller=BOB, account_id=alice_id, spoke_id, [(alice's USDC hub_asset, 6 USDC)])`. This is permitted: BOB is a signed caller, USDC is an existing leg, so `validate_bulk_position_limits` adds no new slot and `require_authorized_caller` only binds BOB's own auth [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) .
4. Keeper retries `clean_bad_debt(ALICE)` — reverts: collateral now $6 > $5 dust cap [7](#0-6) .
5. The state is permanent: liquidation of the insolvent account is capped at the collateral-backed quote and leaves residue; collateral never falls back below $5; only the privileged owner-forced cleanup remains.

fn regression_third_party_cannot_open_new_supply_slots() {
    let mut t = LendingTest::new()
        .three_asset_usdc_eth_wbtc()
        .with_position_limits(2, 4)
        .build();

    t.supply(ALICE, "USDC", 1_000.0);
    t.assert_position_exists(ALICE, "USDC", PositionType::Supply);

    let new_slot = t.try_supply_to_account(BOB, ALICE, "ETH", 0.01);
    assert_contract_error(new_slot, errors::NOT_AUTHORIZED);

    let top_up = t.try_supply_to_account(BOB, ALICE, "USDC", 10.0);
    assert!(
        top_up.is_ok(),
        "third-party top-up of an existing supply leg must remain allowed; got {:?}",
        top_up
    );
    t.assert_supply_near(ALICE, "USDC", 1_010.0, 1.0);

    t.supply(ALICE, "ETH", 0.01);
    t.assert_position_exists(ALICE, "ETH", PositionType::Supply);
}
