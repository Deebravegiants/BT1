### Title
Dust repay can park a sub-3-decimal collateral account in the unliquidatable whole-unit band - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/math.rs)

### Summary
`process_repay` in `contracts/controller/src/positions/debt.rs:69` is permissionless: any caller can repay any account's debt in any positive amount. On a solvent underwater account whose only supply leg is a whole-unit asset (`asset_decimals < MIN_BORROWABLE_ASSET_DECIMALS`, ≥1 whole unit held), `whole_unit_repayment` in `contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/math.rs:235` admits a documented dead band: when the curve quote backs less than one collateral unit but one unit's discounted value is not enough to promote the quote to the full debt, and `unit_repayment >= total_debt`, the raw quote is kept. Every liquidation offer then floors its seizure to zero whole units, the leg is dropped, and `liquidate` reverts. An attacker can frontrun each liquidation with a repay just large enough to push `total_debt` back below `unit_repayment`, repeatedly re-entering the band.

### Finding Description
The liquidation planner works as follows for the relevant shape (single supply position, `asset_decimals < 3`, at least one whole unit held, solvent):

- `estimate_liquidation_amount` returns the curve quote (`curve.rs:102`).
- `whole_unit_repayment` (`math.rs:235-291`) normally raises the quote to `unit_repayment = ceil(unit_with_margin / (1 + bonus))` so the liquidator seizes exactly one whole unit.
- That raise is skipped at `math.rs:287-289`: `if unit_repayment >= snap.total_debt { return quote_usd; }`. The other escape, a full close, requires `unit_at_bonus >= total_debt + one unit per debt leg` (`math.rs:275-280`).
- With the raw quote kept, `calculate_seized_collateral` floors the sub-unit seizure to zero (`math.rs:401-416`), the leg is dropped, and every `liquidate` call reverts — the "narrow band" acknowledged in `docs/reference/invariants.md` (INV-LIQ-02): "if the curve quote backs less than one unit, every offer reverts until accrual or a price move ends that state."

The band's upper boundary is `total_debt <= unit_repayment`, i.e. roughly one collateral unit's USD value discounted by the bonus. A permissionless `repay` therefore moves `total_debt` into the band for a cost bounded by the gap between the current debt and `unit_repayment` — which can be dust when the debt is already near the boundary. Interest accrual pushes the debt back out of the band upward, at which point the attacker repays the accrued delta again, mirroring the Ion finding's "repeat to keep the position unliquidatable" behavior.

### Impact Explanation
While parked in the band, no `liquidate` invocation succeeds on the account: every plan keeps the raw quote, all seizure legs round to zero whole units, and the call reverts. The account's health factor is below 1 (the gate at `plan.rs:40-44` is satisfied) yet it cannot be liquidated. If the collateral price keeps falling or the band persists through repeated dust repays, the position drifts into insolvency and the resulting loss is socialized onto suppliers via `execute_bad_debt_cleanup` / the supply-index write-down, i.e. protocol-level bad debt attributable to a griefed liquidation window. Impact is bounded to accounts whose only collateral is a whole-unit (<3 decimals) asset and whose debt sits near the band boundary, and the attacker must continuously spend repayments to counteract accrual.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachability requires a specific account shape: exactly one supply position in a token with fewer than 3 decimals, at least one whole unit held, solvent (`C >= D`), HF < 1, and debt near the `unit_repayment` boundary. Such accounts exist naturally (e.g. NFT-priced or low-decimal collateral). Once an account drifts near the boundary — which happens automatically as debt accrues through it — the first dust repay is nearly free, and each subsequent top-up costs only the interest accrued since the last. The attacker needs no privileges and no cooperation from the borrower; `process_repay` performs no owner/delegate check on the repaid account. Blocking is temporary rather than permanent (accrual and price moves can end the state, and a sufficiently deep price crash turns the account insolvent where the whole-unit rule no longer applies), which keeps this at Medium.

### Recommendation
In `whole_unit_repayment`, do not leave the raw quote when `unit_repayment >= total_debt`. Prefer promoting the plan to `total_debt` (a full close that rounds the leg up to the held balance via `repays_all_debt` at `math.rs:405-407`), or alternatively allow the seizure to take the single held unit with the unbacked fraction refunded through the existing `release_unbacked_repayment` path (`math.rs:509`). Either removes the dead band so a repay cannot create a state where every liquidation offer reverts.

### Proof of Concept
1. Victim account: supplies `1` unit of a sub-3-decimal asset worth `U` USD; borrows debt token worth `D` with `quote < U/(1+bonus) < D <= unit_repayment` and `HF < 1` (reachable through normal price drift).
2. Liquidator submits `liquidate(victim, payments, SeizeMode::Transfer)`. Before the raise check, `whole_unit_repayment` computes `unit_repayment >= total_debt` and returns the raw `quote_usd` (`math.rs:287-289`).
3. `calculate_seized_collateral` computes `seizure_for_asset_usd = quote * (1 + bonus) < unit_with_margin`, floors to `0` whole units (`math.rs:409-410`), drops the leg, and the plan reverts.
4. Liquidation only becomes possible once accrual pushes `total_debt` above `unit_repayment`. Attacker monitors and calls `repay(account_id, [(hub_asset, delta)])` with `delta = total_debt - unit_repayment + ε`, re-entering the band for the cost of the accrued interest alone.
5. Repeat step 4 indefinitely; the account stays unliquidatable while its real collateralization deteriorates, eventually producing socialized bad debt.

Uncertain points: the exact size of the band depends on `unit_with_margin` (unit value + a 1e-6 relative margin) and the bonus, so the cheapest entry repay is only "dust" when the debt already sits near the boundary; I did not trace `require_authorized_caller` to confirm it imposes no further restriction on third-party repayment, though repayment on behalf of others is standard and nothing in `process_repay` checks ownership of the target account.
