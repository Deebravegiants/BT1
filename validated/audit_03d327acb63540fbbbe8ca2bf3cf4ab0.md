### Title
Hostile HyperSwap V2 pre-seed can outsize the rebalance budget, forcing graduation LP to seed away from the curve-close price - (`packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol`)

### Summary
`Bonding._seedRebalancing` / `_pairRebalance` attempt to neutralize a hostile pre-seed of the post-graduation HyperSwap V2 pair by swapping toward the curve-close ratio before depositing. The rebalance swap is capped at the graduation's own inventory (`_swapBudget`, 99% of the curve-raised token/LT amounts, which are bounded by the small, fixed `graduationThresholdUsd`/`LP_RESERVE`). An attacker who permissionlessly creates the TOKEN/LT pair and pre-seeds it with reserves far larger than the graduation's own capital can make that budget structurally insufficient to correct the ratio, so `finalizeGraduation` deposits the curve-raised liquidity at the attacker-chosen (post-swap, still skewed) ratio instead of the curve-close price, and burns/sweeps the off-ratio remainder.

### Finding Description
`finalizeGraduation` (`packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol:1000-1034`) calls `_ensureUniswapV2Pair` then `_seedUniswapV2Direct` (`packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol:1201-1234`). Both are reachable after phase 1 (`_enterGraduating`, itself reachable via any unprivileged buy that crosses the threshold, or via the permissionless `triggerGraduation`), and `finalizeGraduation` itself is explicitly permissionless (`function finalizeGraduation(address tokenAddress) external nonReentrant`, `packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol:1000-1002`). Nothing prevents an unprivileged address from calling `IUniswapV2Factory.createPair(token, lt)` directly and minting LP into it with an arbitrary self-funded ratio during the window between phase 1 (`Lifecycle.Graduating`) and phase 2 — this is exactly "Regime 3" that the code's own natspec describes handling (`packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol:1155-1176`).

The mitigation path is:
- `_seedRebalancing` (`packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol:1279-1354`) computes which side is rich and calls `_pairRebalance` with `maxSwap = _swapBudget(...)`.
- `_swapBudget` (`packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol:1374-1378`) caps the swap at `(budget * 99) / 100`, where `budget` is `_ltSwapInventory(lt, protectedLT)` (essentially the graduation's own raised LT, `ltFromPair`) or `IERC20(tokenAddress).balanceOf(address(this))` (the graduation's own `tokensForLP`, capped at `LP_RESERVE = 250M` tokens, `packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol:67`).
- `_noFeeSwapInput` (`packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol:1507-1522`) computes the *ideal* swap size to hit the curve-close ratio, then truncates it to `maxSwap` if it exceeds the budget: `return s > maxSwap ? maxSwap : s;` (`packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol:1521`).
- `_routerDepositAndDispose` (`packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol:1449-1486`) then deposits whatever remains via `addLiquidity(..., 1, 1, ...)` at the **post-swap** pool ratio (the router's `quote()`-based optimal split), not the cached curve-close ratio. Any unmatched remainder is burned (TOKEN) or swept to the owner (LT) — it does not get reinserted at the correct ratio.

Because `ltFromPair`/`tokensForLP` are bounded by the protocol's fixed, small graduation economics (`VIRTUAL_LIQUIDITY_USD = 3000 ether`, `graduationThresholdUsd` floored at that, `LP_RESERVE` = 25% of a 1B-token supply), while an attacker's own pre-seed capital in LT (an external, freely mintable/transferable BounceTech LT) and in TOKEN (bought on the curve pre-graduation) is not bounded by that same cap, the attacker can choose a pre-seed reserve ratio and size for which the ideal rebalance input `s` (from `_noFeeSwapInput`'s `sqrt(reserveIn*reserveOut*targetN/targetD) - reserveIn`) exceeds `maxSwap`. In that case the swap only partially corrects the ratio, and the deposit that follows locks in a pool price that is *not* the curve-close price — the exact "LP seeded away from the curve close price" outcome the surrounding natspec is trying to prevent, but the cap makes it achievable for a well-funded attacker.

### Impact Explanation
- The LP that `LPLock.recordLock` permanently locks (`packages/contracts/src/Bonding.sol:1031`, `packages/contracts/src/LPLock.sol:70-85`, one-shot, no rescue path) is minted at a manipulated ratio rather than the curve-close price.
- The attacker's own pre-existing LP position in the pool captures a disproportionate share of the newly deposited (curve-raised) TOKEN/LT because the deposit lands at the attacker-skewed ratio rather than the protocol-intended ratio.
- Off-ratio remainder is destroyed (`Token.burn`) or diverted to the owner (`_sweepLTToOwner`), meaning part of the economic value raised by legitimate curve traders is permanently lost rather than seeded into the LP that backs the graduated token.
- Post-graduation traders (via `Zap._swapOnUniswapV2` / direct pair interaction) trade against a mispriced pool, and the attacker can immediately arbitrage the gap for risk-free profit funded by the graduation's own liquidity — a permanent, uncompensated value transfer from LP/token holders to the pre-seeding attacker.

This satisfies the required impact bar ("an LP seeded away from the curve close price" / theft of trader-and-LP funds).

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium-High. Any unprivileged address can: (1) trigger phase 1 for a target token (buy across threshold, or call the permissionless `triggerGraduation`), (2) in the window before `finalizeGraduation` is called, create the HyperSwap V2 pair directly via the factory and mint into it with an extreme ratio funded by their own TOKEN (bought pre-grad) and LT (freely obtainable BounceTech LT), sized to exceed the fixed, small graduation budget (`VIRTUAL_LIQUIDITY_USD`-scale / `LP_RESERVE`-scale). Because `finalizeGraduation` is also permissionless, the attacker (or a colluding "keeper") can then call it immediately once the pre-seed is in place, before a legitimate keeper does. The required capital is modest relative to typical MEV/arbitrage budgets, since the protocol's own graduation economics are capped at a few thousand USD notional and a fixed 250M-token LP reserve.

### Recommendation
Do not size the rebalance swap budget purely off the graduation's own inventory. Either (a) detect when the ideal rebalance input `s` computed by `_noFeeSwapInput` before capping exceeds a safe multiple of the graduation's inventory and fall back to a defensive action (e.g., delaying finalize, or reverting/queuing until the pre-seed can be neutralized) rather than silently depositing at the truncated ratio, or (b) bound the acceptable post-swap ratio deviation from the cached curve-close ratio and revert (rather than deposit) if the deviation exceeds a tolerance, forcing manual/owner intervention for extreme pre-seeds instead of an automatic off-price deposit.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker buys TOKEN on the curve (or otherwise triggers `canGraduate`) so that `_enterGraduating` fires phase 1 for `tokenAddress`, freezing trading and caching `pendingGraduation` (`tokensForLP`, `ltFromPair`), both bounded by the fixed `graduationThresholdUsd`/`LP_RESERVE`.
2. Before anyone calls `finalizeGraduation`, attacker calls `IUniswapV2Factory(uniswapV2Factory).createPair(tokenAddress, lt)` and then mints LP into that pair with reserves funded from their own large TOKEN balance and freshly-minted/purchased BounceTech LT, choosing a ratio and absolute size deliberately far from `tokensForLP:ltFromPair` and large relative to those cached amounts.
3. Attacker (or anyone) calls `Bonding.finalizeGraduation(tokenAddress)`. `_seedUniswapV2Direct` detects `totalSupply() != 0` and routes to `_seedRebalancing`, which computes the needed swap via `_noFeeSwapInput` but clamps it to `_swapBudget(...)` (≤99% of the graduation's own `ltFromPair`/`tokensForLP`).
4. Because the attacker's pre-seed size dwarfs the graduation's inventory, the swap cannot reach the target ratio; `_routerDepositAndDispose` deposits the remaining balances at the still-skewed post-swap ratio into `lpLock`, and the mismatched remainder is burned/swept.
5. The resulting locked LP price differs materially from the curve-close price; the attacker's pre-existing LP share captures the mispriced deposit and/or the attacker arbitrages the pool immediately after graduation for profit funded by the curve's raised capital.
