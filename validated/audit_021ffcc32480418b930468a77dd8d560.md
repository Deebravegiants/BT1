### Title
RAY debt-value `i128` overflow in accrual permanently freezes a market — borrowed × borrow_index exceeds `i128::MAX` before `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` ever engages - (common/src/rates/scaling.rs)

### Summary
Analogous to a heap overflow, the fixed-point accrual path overflows its numeric domain: when a market holds a very large scaled debt and the borrow index grows under sustained high utilization, `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` overflows `i128` and panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow`. Because every user-facing verb accrues interest first, the whole market — repay, withdraw, liquidate, supply — becomes permanently uncallable, freezing all supplied funds. The borrow-index cap `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` is supposed to bound index growth, but the debt-value product overflows at roughly `i128::MAX / total_debt`, which is hit long before the index cap, so the protection never engages.

### Finding Description
- `scaled_to_original` is a plain half-up `Ray::mul` with no saturation path; an unrepresentable product panics: `common/src/rates/scaling.rs` lines 14-16 (`scaled.mul(env, index)`), backed by `fp_core` which raises `MathOverflow` when the `I256` result does not fit `i128`. [1](#0-0) 
- The panic site is on the utilization/accrual hot path: `Cache::calculate_utilization` computes `scaled_to_original(self.borrowed, self.borrow_index)` for every operation that refreshes the market, and the same unscaling runs inside interest accrual: `contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs` lines 19-27. [2](#0-1) 
- RAY (10^27) headroom over an 18-decimal asset is only 9 digits, so a whale-scale market (e.g., 10^9 whole units of an 18-decimal token at ~98% utilization on a steep curve) reaches the cliff purely through normal accrual.
- The test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs` (lines 316-362) proves end-to-end that `update_indexes` begins failing with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and that `withdraw` and `repay` then fail identically — "no repay, no withdraw, no liquidation". [3](#0-2) 
- Reachability is fully unprivileged: any user can `supply` a large amount and another user can `borrow` ~98% of it (caps can be lifted by governance, and on high-decimals assets the raw cap leaves enormous headroom); thereafter `update_indexes` — callable by anyone — alone advances the market to the cliff.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of user funds and an inoperative market. Once `borrowed * borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX`, accrual panics; since accrual is a precondition of every pool/controller verb, no supplier can withdraw and no borrower can repay, and the bad-debt/liquidation exits are equally blocked. There is no recovery path in-contract — the index only grows monotonically, so the state is unrecoverable.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a very large position and years of sustained high-utilization accrual (the test asserts no cliff within 40 years was *not* the bound — the loop breaks when overflow occurs, earlier than the documented index-cap bound). It needs whale-scale capital or a high-decimals low-value token plus a steep rate curve, but every step uses only unprivileged entrypoints (`supply`, `borrow`, `update_indexes`) and depends only on interest accrual, which rational borrowers on a steep curve cannot avoid triggering. Once approached, the freeze is irreversible, so the severity is High despite the demanding setup.

### Recommendation
Make the value computation saturate or bound instead of panicking:
- Clamp the borrow index during accrual at a cap that is *jointly* safe with total debt (e.g., cap the index at `i128::MAX / borrowed` or enforce `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` aggressively enough that the product can never overflow), rather than a fixed index cap.
- Alternatively, compute utilization and accrual in `I256` and only narrow to `i128` where the result is an asset amount; a saturating `scaled_to_original` for bookkeeping totals would let accrual continue and keep exits callable.
- Add an invariant test that `borrowed * borrow_index` stays representable for all admitted cap/decimals combinations.

### Proof of Concept
1. List (or use) an 18-decimals market on the steep XLM-style curve with caps effectively lifted.
2. Unprivileged user A: `supply(BIG18, 1e9 * 10^18)`; user B: `supply` collateral then `borrow(BIG18, 0.98 * principal)` — utilization ~98%.
3. Anyone calls `update_indexes` (or any verb that accrues) repeatedly as time advances; on the XLM curve the index grows past ~170x within a few years.
4. At the first accrual where `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` exceeds `i128::MAX`, the call panics with `MathOverflow` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
5. Subsequent `withdraw`, `repay`, and `liquidate` all panic at the same accrual site — the market is frozen, as demonstrated by the harness test asserting `MATH_OVERFLOW` on `try_update_indexes_for`, `try_withdraw_raw`, and `try_repay` in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs` lines 343-356.
