### Title
A permissionless execution of a ready market-parameter update can lower `max_utilization` after borrowing and freeze withdrawals and revenue claims - ([File: contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs])

### Summary
`Governance::execute` can be called by anyone for a ready operation when `executor = None`. A scheduled `upgrade_liquidity_pool_params` operation can replace a market’s `InterestRateModel`, including `max_utilization`, after suppliers and borrowers have already established positions. The new utilization ceiling is applied to ordinary withdrawals and revenue claims even when the existing market utilization was lawful under the previous parameters.

### Finding Description
`Governance::execute` performs no authentication or role check when `executor` is `None`, and it invokes the scheduled controller call once the operation is ready. `upgrade_liquidity_pool_params` first accrues indexes and then forwards the new model to the pool. The pool validates and stores the replacement parameters, but it does not check whether currently outstanding debt is compatible with the new `max_utilization`. Ordinary withdrawals burn supply shares before `require_utilization_below_max` evaluates the post-state utilization, while `claim_revenue` similarly burns claimable revenue shares before applying the same guard.

### Impact Explanation
Temporary freezing of user funds. If debt is 90% of supply and a ready update lowers `max_utilization` to 80%, every non-liquidation withdrawal fails because removing supply raises utilization further. `claim_revenue` can also revert after `burn_claimable_revenue` because removing revenue from total supply worsens the utilization ratio. Suppliers cannot exit normally until borrowers repay or liquidation becomes possible; protocol revenue can remain temporarily unclaimable for the same reason.

### Likelihood Explanation
This does not require the unprivileged executor to choose arbitrary parameters: the parameter update must first be scheduled by governance and become ready. However, once such a valid update exists, anyone can execute it at the moment the market is already above its new ceiling. `max_utilization` must only satisfy the model’s internal validation, including being at least `optimal_utilization`; there is no compatibility check against live utilization.

### Recommendation
When applying a lower `max_utilization`, either reject the update while current utilization exceeds the new limit, or exempt previously established positions from withdrawal blocking until utilization returns below the new ceiling. A safer design is to apply the cap only to new borrows and new strategy debt, since exits should not be penalized by a later policy change.

### Proof of Concept
1. A market starts with `max_utilization = 0.95 * RAY`.
2. Users supply 100 units and a borrower draws 90 units. Utilization is 90%, below the configured cap.
3. Governance schedules `AdminOperation::UpgradeLiquidityPoolParams` for that market with an otherwise valid model whose `max_utilization = 0.80 * RAY`.
4. After the timelock delay, any unprivileged address calls:

   `governance.execute(None, controller, "upgrade_liquidity_pool_params", args, predecessor, salt)`

5. The controller calls `pool_update_indexes_call` and then `pool_update_params_call`; the pool commits the new parameters.
6. A supplier calls `withdraw`. `resolve_withdrawal` burns supply shares, `require_utilization_below_max` computes `ceil(debt) / floor(remaining_supply) > 0.80`, and the transaction reverts with `UtilizationAboveMax`.
7. Even a minimal withdrawal cannot pass because every positive withdrawal decreases the denominator and therefore increases utilization.
8. `claim_revenue` can fail identically because it burns revenue shares before enforcing the updated utilization ceiling.
