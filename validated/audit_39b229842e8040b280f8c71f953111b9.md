### Title
Unfunded `repay` overpayment refund drains pool custody - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs)

### Summary
`ops::repay::apply` computes the overpayment from the caller-declared `action.amount` minus the outstanding debt and transfers that overpayment out of the pool, without measuring whether the declared amount was ever paid in. On a market with zero outstanding debt the entire declared amount is classified as overpayment and refunded, letting an unprivileged caller walk away with the pool's token custody.

### Finding Description
In `contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs`, `accounting` calls `cache.resolve_repay(amount, position)` to split the declared amount into `net_repay` and `overpayment`. When there is no debt, `current_debt_ceil` is zero, so `resolve_repay` takes the full-close branch: `net_repay == 0` and `overpayment == amount`. The `RepayRoundsToZeroShares` assertion passes via its `net_repay == 0` disjunct, `cache.credit_cash(0)` leaves the cash book untouched, and `apply` executes `cache.transfer_out(payer, overpayment)` — moving real tokens out of custody for a payment that never arrived. The test `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` (`contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3590`) confirms the refund is derived from the declared amount, not the cash book or a measured balance delta, and that a direct `repay` call drains the pool's entire custody with `cash` unchanged.

### Impact Explanation
This is theft of user funds. Any unprivileged address can call `repay` on a market carrying no debt with `amount` set to (or exceeding) the pool's token balance and receive the full balance as a "refund." Because `cash` is untouched, the market still reports itself solvent while its custody is gone — subsequent withdrawals/borrows pass the `require_reserves` book check and then fail inside the SAC transfer, compounding the loss into a frozen-market state.

### Likelihood Explanation
No special conditions are required: the attacker needs no collateral, no position, and no authorization beyond their own signature. The only requirement is a hub market whose outstanding debt is zero or below the attacker's chosen `amount`. The path is a single permissionless pool call.

### Recommendation
Measure the repay receipt: snapshot the pool's token balance before crediting and refund only the measured delta above the net repay, or require the controller to route the payment through a verified transfer leg. At minimum, gate `repay` so `overpayment` cannot exceed tokens actually received in the transaction, and debit `cash` (or call `require_reserves`) for any outbound refund.

### Proof of Concept
Existing test, `contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3590-3611`: seed a pool with real custody and zero debt, then call `client().repay(&payer, &t.ract(0, custody_before))` with no token transfer in. `actual_amount` is `0` and `assert_unfunded_refund_drained_custody` shows the payer's balance equals the declared amount and the pool balance is `0`.
