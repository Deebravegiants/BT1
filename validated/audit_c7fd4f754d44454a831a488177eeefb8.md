### Title
`Pool::recapitalize` books cash it never received, leaving the market unable to pay withdrawals — (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/recapitalize.rs)

### Summary
The kernel bug class is "allocation returns success even though the resource acquisition (ioremap) failed, with no rollback." The XOXNO Lending analog is `Pool::recapitalize(hub_asset, payer, amount)`: it inflates the market's cash book for a `(hub, token)` book without requiring that `payer` actually fund the pool. The pool's own test suite proves the divergence: `test_unfunded_recapitalize_leaves_the_cash_book_overstating_custody` mints a legitimate deposit, drains every token of custody via an unfunded `recapitalize`, and shows the cash book still claims the supplier's deposit while the SAC balance is zero (`contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3408-3482`). The book and custody — which the fixture asserts are identical after a real deposit — permanently decouple, and the function returns success rather than rolling back the unbacked credit.

### Finding Description
The pool tracks a per-`(hub, token)` cash book separate from physical SAC custody. `recapitalize` is a permissionless entrypoint intended to restore solvency after bad debt by crediting cash "without minting shares," but it does not perform or verify a token transfer from `payer` — the credit is booked even though no custody arrives, the same shape as `bnxt_qplib_alloc_dpi` returning success after `ioremap` fails. Every downstream liquidity check reads the book, not the balance: `require_reserves` and the `PoolInsolvent`/`InsufficientLiquidity` guards on `withdraw` consult the inflated cash figure. A supplier's exit therefore clears all pool guards and only fails inside the SAC `transfer`, which reverts with the token's own `BalanceError` (error 10) — confirmed by the test asserting that neither pool guard fires and the failed exit rolls back, leaving the overstated book intact for the next victim.

### Impact Explanation
Temporary freezing of funds and effective market insolvency. After a single unfunded `recapitalize`, the market reports itself solvent while holding zero custody. Every withdrawal sized against the book's liquidity passes the pool's guards and then traps inside the SAC transfer, so suppliers cannot exit even though the protocol's own solvency checks say they can. Because the failed withdrawal rolls back, the overstated book persists indefinitely and each subsequent exit hits the same wall — a fail-open accounting lie rather than a fail-closed pause, and no governance action short of a funded recapitalization or forced socialization reconciles it.

### Likelihood Explanation
`recapitalize` is in the permissionless entrypoint set and takes `payer` as a free argument, so any unprivileged address can submit `recapitalize(hub_asset, attacker_addr, large_amount)` and instantly overstate the cash book for any active market — no price manipulation, timing, or privileged role required. The overstatement directly suppresses the bad-debt/insolvency signals that keepers and the withdrawal path rely on, so the frozen-withdrawal condition is reachable in one call on any hub/token book.

### Recommendation
Require `recapitalize` to settle in cash: transfer `amount` of the hub token from `payer` to the pool (with `payer.require_auth()`), or at minimum measure the pool's balance delta inside the call and revert if custody did not increase by `amount`. Alternatively, make the liquidity guards (`require_reserves`, `PoolInsolvent`) validate against actual SAC balance, not only the cash book, so an unbacked book credit cannot admit unpayable exits.

### Proof of Concept
1. Supplier deposits via the normal `supply` path; test asserts `state_snapshot().cash == token.balance(pool)` — book equals custody.
2. Attacker calls `pool.recapitalize(hub_asset, payer, custody_amount)` where `payer` is any address that never funded the pool. The call succeeds; `token.balance(pool)` drops/reads as 0 while `cash` still claims ≥ the supplier's deposit.
3. Supplier calls `withdraw(receiver, false, withdrawals)`. The pool's `InsufficientLiquidity` and `PoolInsolvent` guards do not fire because they read the inflated book; the call reverts inside the SAC `transfer` with `BalanceError` (error 10). Receiver gets nothing; the rollback leaves the book still overstating custody, so every subsequent withdrawal fails identically — funds are frozen until external recapitalization actually arrives.

Reference: `test_unfunded_recapitalize_leaves_the_cash_book_overstating_custody` in `contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3405-3483` already encodes this exact sequence.
