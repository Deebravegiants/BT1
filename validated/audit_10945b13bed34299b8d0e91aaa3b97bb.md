### Title
Whole-unit liquidation promotion bypasses the bonus bound and over-seizes collateral - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/math.rs)

### Summary
For an account whose only collateral uses fewer than three decimals, `whole_unit_repayment` can promote a liquidation quote to the full debt when the discounted value of one indivisible unit covers that debt plus rounding headroom. The seizure path then rounds the planned fractional unit up to one whole unit. Consequently, the permissionless `liquidate` path can transfer collateral worth substantially more than `debt × (1 + bonus)`.

### Finding Description
`Controller::liquidate` is callable by any authenticated liquidator. [1](#0-0)  After the normal liquidation quote is calculated, `whole_unit_repayment` returns `snap.total_debt` when `floor(unit_value / (1 + bonus)) >= total_debt + per-leg-rounding`. [2](#0-1)  For a full debt repayment, `calculate_seized_collateral` rounds a sub-3-decimal seizure upward to a whole unit rather than keeping the value implied by the configured bonus. [3](#0-2) 

The reported and configured bonus is therefore not the effective economic bound: the liquidator pays `D` and receives one unit worth `U`, producing an effective bonus of `U / D - 1`. This is analogous to the report’s `!important` bypass: the ordinary bonus calculation is superseded by the special whole-unit rule.

### Impact Explanation
A liquidator can extract excess collateral directly from a borrower. For example, with a 0-decimal collateral unit worth $100, an 80% liquidation threshold, and a 5% bonus, an account with $100 collateral and $80 debt has `HF = 0.8`. Since `$100 / 1.05 ≈ $95.24` covers the $80 debt plus rounding headroom, the quote is promoted to full debt. The liquidator pays $80 and receives the entire $100 unit, an effective 25% bonus rather than the configured 5%, taking approximately $16 more than `D × 1.05`.

This is theft of user collateral, not merely a display discrepancy.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires a supported collateral market below three decimals, a one-asset collateral account, and a debt in the range where `HF < 1`, `C >= D`, and the discounted whole unit covers the debt. Those conditions can arise from ordinary price movement or interest accrual. Once present, exploitation requires only calling `liquidate` with the full debt payment and `SeizeMode::Transfer` or `Credit`.

### Recommendation
Do not allow whole-unit rounding to create an effective bonus above the configured liquidation bonus or HF-preserving cap. Concretely, suppress the full-debt promotion when `U > D × (1 + bonus)`, fall back to the partial whole-unit quote, or explicitly bound the seized value and add regression coverage for high-value 0–2 decimal collateral.

### Proof of Concept
1. Configure collateral `C` with `asset_decimals = 0`, price `U = $100`, liquidation threshold `80%`, and bonus `5%`.
2. Create an account whose only supply position is one unit of `C`; borrow debt valued at `$80`.
3. The account is liquidatable because `HF = 80 / 80 = 1` boundary aside, a small interest accrual or price move makes `HF < 1` while `C >= D`.
4. Call `liquidate(liquidator, account_id, [(debt_asset, 80)], SeizeMode::Transfer)`.
5. `whole_unit_repayment` promotes the quote because `floor(100 / 1.05) = 95 >= 80 + R`; seizure rounds `0.84` units up to one unit.
6. The liquidator pays `$80` and receives `$100`, exceeding the intended `$84` collateral payout by `$16`.
