### Title
`borrowed × borrow_index` overflows `i128` inside `accrue_step`, permanently freezing a large market before the borrow-index cap can engage - ([File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs])

### Summary
The bug class is an integer overflow that turns into uncontrolled state corruption; the analog here is an `i128` overflow in the RAY interest-accrual path that turns into an unrecoverable panic. Every pool verb accrues the market first, so once `borrowed * borrow_index / RAY` exceeds `i128::MAX`, the market can never be touched again — no repay, withdraw, supply, or liquidation — permanently freezing all supplier funds.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` (shared by the mutating `interest::global_sync` and the read-only `simulate_update_indexes`) begins by unscaling the market totals: `scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)` and `scaled_to_original(env, supplied, supply_index)`, where `scaled_to_original` is `scaled.mul(env, index)` — `mul_div_half_up` with an `i128` fast path and an `I256` widened path that still panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` if the quotient does not fit `i128`. The borrow index is only clamped afterward inside `update_borrow_index` at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (10^36, i.e. index ≤ ~10^9 RAY); the product `borrowed * index` is unbounded by that clamp because `borrowed` itself is a Ray-scaled token amount that grows with market size. For an 18-decimal market holding billion-scale principal at high utilization on a steep rate curve, the unscaled debt value crosses `i128::MAX` while the index is still far below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. The harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` demonstrates this concretely: after advancing time at 98% utilization, `update_indexes` fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` calls fail identically because `global_sync` accrues before any state change.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds: once the market crosses the cliff, every entrypoint that mutates the market reverts inside `accrue_step`, and the panic is time-independent — waiting longer only makes it worse. Suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot liquidate, and `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` also accrue first. All tokens held by the pool book for that market are bricked forever.

### Likelihood Explanation
Triggering requires (a) a very large market — billion-scale token principal on a high-decimal asset, reachable by any unprivileged `supply`/`borrow` caller subject to governance-set caps and available liquidity — and (b) sustained high utilization on a steep curve segment for an extended period so the index compounds into the overflow region. No privileged action is needed; `update_indexes` is permissionless and the freeze is a pure function of stored `borrowed`, `supply_index`, and elapsed time. The capital and duration requirements reduce likelihood, but the outcome is total and irreversible.

### Recommendation
Bound the value domain rather than the index alone. Options: (a) compute `scaled_to_original` for market aggregates through a saturating or `I256`-retained path in `accrue_step` and clamp `borrowed_original`/`supplied_original` to `i128`-safe values, letting `update_borrow_index` hit `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` and stop accrual gracefully; (b) enforce a protocol-wide maximum on `borrowed * RAY-scale` (a Ray-valued "total value ceiling" cap checked at `supply`/`borrow`) so `borrowed * MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY / RAY` is guaranteed to fit `i128` by construction; (c) make `update_borrow_index` saturate `borrow_index` before the product overflows and short-circuit accrual at the cap. Any of these converts the panic into a bounded, index-capped halt of interest accrual rather than a frozen market.

### Proof of Concept
Existing harness test, `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-361`:

```rust
let principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18);
t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);
let debt = principal / 100 * 98;
t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);
// advance one year per iteration at 98% utilization on the XLM curve
t.advance_time(YEAR_SECS);
// eventually:
assert_contract_error(t.try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"]), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
assert!(last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY); // cap never engaged
assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
assert_contract_error(t.try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
```

The overflow path is: `interest::global_sync` → `accrue_chunk` → `accrue_step` (`common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60`) → `scaled_to_original` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14`) → `Ray::mul` → `mul_div_half_up` (`common/src/math/fp_core.rs:108`) → `I256` widened product whose quotient exceeds `i128::MAX` → `GenericError::MathOverflow` panic (`fp_core.rs:300-302`).
