### Title
Monotonically growing borrow index overflows i128 in `scaled_to_original` before `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap engages, permanently freezing the market - (File: common/src/rates/scaling.rs)

### Summary
The external bug class is a counter that grows without a bound and eventually overflows, aborting the server. The XOXNO Lending analog is the RAY-scaled borrow index accrual path: `borrowed` (scaled debt in RAY units) is multiplied by `borrow_index` on every accrual step, and this product overflows `i128` before `update_borrow_index`'s `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY = 10^36` cap can stop index growth. Because every mutating entrypoint runs `global_sync` → `accrue_step` → `scaled_to_original` first, once the product exceeds `i128::MAX` the market panics on every subsequent call — supply, borrow, withdraw, repay, liquidate, and `update_indexes` itself. This is demonstrated by the repo's own harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs`, which observes `MATH_OVERFLOW` with `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` and confirms frozen `withdraw`/`repay`.

### Finding Description
`common/src/rates/scaling.rs::scaled_to_original` (lines 14–16) computes `scaled.mul(env, index)` via `fp_core::mul_div_half_up`, which panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` when `scaled * index` leaves the `i128` domain (`common/src/math/fp.rs` lines 49–52). `accrue_step` in `common/src/rates/simulate.rs` calls it at the top of every compounding chunk (`borrowed_original = scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)`, line 60), and `global_sync` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs` (lines 20–33) runs before every state mutation. `update_borrow_index` (`common/src/rates/index.rs` lines 13–19) only caps the index itself at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`; nothing bounds the product `borrowed * borrow_index`. A large scaled `borrowed` (e.g. a whale book in an 18-decimal asset, where `from_asset` rescales the amount up to RAY precision) multiplied by a moderately grown index reaches `i128::MAX` (~1.7e38) while the index is still far below its 10^36 cap — the harness test reaches the cliff at roughly 170x index growth under sustained ~98% utilization on the steep XLM rate curve. Like the CVE's refcount that is incremented on every lease-query response with no decrement, the borrow index is incremented by every accrual and has no effective bound on the value-domain where it is actually consumed.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds and effective protocol insolvency on the affected market. Once `borrowed * borrow_index` overflows, the panic occurs inside `accrue_step`, which runs at the head of `global_sync`, so all user-facing verbs on that market — `borrow`, `repay`, `withdraw`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, flash paths — revert unconditionally. Suppliers cannot exit, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot seize, and the pool's cash for that token is stranded. The test at lines 354–356 of `large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs` asserts exactly this: `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` both fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW`. There is no recovery path since `update_indexes` hits the same panic and no admin path bypasses accrual.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a large borrow book in a high-decimals asset plus sustained high utilization over a long accrual horizon (the harness used ~1B × 10^18 token principal at 98% utilization and needed years of compounding on the steep segment). Both preconditions are achievable by unprivileged actions — `supply`/`borrow` with the listed caps lifted, then passive waiting, with permissionless `update_indexes` — but the capital size and horizon make it a Medium-severity edge case rather than a cheap grief. Notably, nothing privileged is needed to trigger it once state drifts there.

### Recommendation
Bound the value domain, not just the index. Options: (a) in `accrue_step`/`update_borrow_index`, clamp or saturate when `borrowed * borrow_index` approaches `i128::MAX` rather than letting `scaled_to_original` panic; (b) add a `require_*` validation limiting scaled `borrowed` such that `borrowed * MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` stays in `i128`, enforced at listing/borrow time; (c) have `scaled_to_original` return a saturating/optional result on the accrual path so accrual degrades (e.g., stops accruing interest) instead of freezing the book. A freeze-free cap means the market degrades to "no more interest" rather than bricking every entrypoint.

### Proof of Concept
The repository contains an executable proof: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs::a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (lines 316–361). Steps:

1. List an 18-decimal market with the XLM rate curve; lift supply/borrow caps.
2. `supply(BOB, BIG18, BILLION * 10^18)`; supply collateral and `borrow(ALICE, BIG18, ~98% of principal)`.
3. Advance time year-by-year calling permissionless `update_indexes` until it returns `Err(MATH_OVERFLOW)` — observed with `borrow_index ≈ 170 × RAY`, well under `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (assert at lines 350–353).
4. Afterwards `withdraw(BOB, 1)` and `repay(ALICE, 1.0)` both revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW` (lines 355–356): the market is permanently frozen.

The panic originates in `common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14` (`scaled_to_original` → `Ray::mul` → `mul_div_half_up` overflow) invoked from `common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60` inside the `global_sync` loop at `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:39–53`.
