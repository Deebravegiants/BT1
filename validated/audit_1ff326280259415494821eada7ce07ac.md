### Title
Withdraw reverts entirely (permanently locking LP funds) when a computed side amount rounds down to zero - (File: program/src/processor.rs)

### Summary
`Processor::process_withdraw` computes `coin_amount` and `pc_amount` proportionally to the withdrawn LP amount using floor-rounded division, and then unconditionally rejects the entire withdrawal if either side is `0`, with no fallback path to recover the non-zero side or the underlying LP position.

### Finding Description
In `process_withdraw`, after validating ownership and LP amount bounds [1](#0-0) , the coin and pc amounts owed to the withdrawing LP are computed via `InvariantPool::exchange_pool_to_token` with `RoundDirection::Floor` [2](#0-1) . Immediately after, the function enforces:

```rust
if withdraw.amount == 0 || coin_amount == 0 || pc_amount == 0 {
    return Err(AmmError::InvalidInput.into());
}
``` [3](#0-2) 

This check is a blanket revert: it doesn't distinguish between "the whole withdrawal is worthless" and "one side floors to zero while the other side is a large, meaningful amount." Because `coin_amount` and `pc_amount` are each proportional to `withdraw.amount / amm.lp_amount` scaled by the *pool's* respective vault totals [2](#0-1) , a pool with a highly skewed coin/pc ratio (e.g., a token pair where one side has vastly larger total balance than the other, which is common for low-decimal or low-priced tokens) can cause the smaller side to floor to `0` even when the LP is withdrawing their entire, potentially large, balance. There is no partial-withdraw or single-side-withdraw fallback — `Withdraw` is the only instruction path to redeem LP tokens for underlying funds [4](#0-3) . `WithdrawPnl` is a distinct, privileged, protocol-fee-only path and does not help ordinary LPs [5](#0-4) .

Since the check reverts unconditionally whenever coin_amount or pc_amount is zero, and the ratio is dictated purely by pool state (not adjustable by the user, since `min_coin_amount`/`min_pc_amount` are optional slippage floors, not overrides of the zero-check), an LP holder can become permanently unable to redeem their LP tokens for either token if the pool's ratio makes one side compute to zero for their balance — this mirrors the reported EigenPod pattern where a minimum-amount gate makes fund recovery impossible, except here the "minimum" is an implicit zero-floor rather than an explicit constant.

### Impact Explanation
An LP's tokens (and the underlying coin/pc claims they represent) can become permanently frozen in the pool: the LP cannot call `Withdraw` successfully (it always reverts on the zero-amount check), cannot call `WithdrawPnl` (restricted to the protocol's `pnl_owner`/`amm_owner`) [5](#0-4) , and there is no other instruction that burns LP tokens for underlying assets. This is a permanent freezing-of-user-funds condition, matching the required impact bar.

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires a pool ratio skewed enough that one side's proportional payout floors to zero for a given LP balance — most likely for tokens with very different decimals/prices relative to their counterpart, or for LPs holding a comparatively small share of a large, imbalanced pool. It's a normal, permissionless `Withdraw` call with no privileged accounts, reachable by any LP holder; likelihood is pool/ratio dependent rather than universal, which is consistent with a Medium-severity, not universally-triggered, bug.

### Recommendation
Do not reject the entire withdrawal when only one side floors to zero. Either (a) allow the transfer of the non-zero side and skip the zero side (with an explicit event/log noting zero payout on that leg), or (b) round in favor of the LP (e.g., ceiling instead of floor, bounded by vault balance) so a real, non-dust LP balance is never entitled to a literal zero on either leg, while still preventing `withdraw.amount == 0` withdrawals as a true no-op guard.

### Proof of Concept
1. Create a pool where `amm_coin_vault.amount` is astronomically larger than `amm_pc_vault.amount` relative to `amm.lp_amount` (e.g., a token with 9 decimals paired against a token with very few effective pc units after fees/pnl deduction), such that for a given LP's `withdraw.amount`, `pc_amount = floor(withdraw.amount * total_pc_without_take_pnl / amm.lp_amount) == 0` while `coin_amount` computes to a large non-zero value.
2. LP calls `Withdraw` with their full LP balance (`withdraw.amount < amm.lp_amount`, satisfying the earlier bound checks) [1](#0-0) .
3. `process_withdraw` computes `coin_amount != 0` but `pc_amount == 0`; the check at [3](#0-2)  triggers `AmmError::InvalidInput`, and the whole instruction reverts — burning nothing and transferring nothing.
4. The LP's tokens remain locked with no other instruction available to redeem them, since `WithdrawPnl` requires the `pnl_owner`/protocol-owner signer [5](#0-4) .
