### Title
Tokens sent to the pool or controller outside a crediting call are permanently stranded — there is no recovery or sweep path - ([File: contracts/pool/src/lib.rs](contracts/pool/src/lib.rs), [File: contracts/controller/src/strategies/flash_position.rs](contracts/controller/src/strategies/flash_position.rs))

### Summary
The Soroban analog of "dangerous payable functions" is value delivery with no crediting: in XOXNO Lending, `cash` is a bookkeeping number the pool credits only when its owner (the controller) calls `supply`/`repay`/`recapitalize`. A direct `token.transfer` to the pool address increases physical custody without touching any market book, and no entrypoint — user-facing, controller-facing, or governance-facing — can release that surplus. The controller has the same blind spot: `flash_position` credits only declared collateral deltas and refunds only `refund_assets` deltas; any other token pushed to the controller during the callback is neither credited nor returned, and there is no controller sweep endpoint. Unlike the EVM report, where `msg.value` arrives inside a function call, here the trap is the custody/bookkeeping split plus the refund whitelist.

### Finding Description
- The pool books `cash` per `(hub_id, asset)` and only reconciles real balances inside `flash_loan`; every crediting path is `#[only_owner]` (the controller) and trusts the controller's measured amount. An unsolicited `token.transfer(user, pool, n)` lands in custody that belongs to no market and cannot be claimed, withdrawn, or booked afterward — confirmed by the docs ("direct donations do not automatically increase booked cash", "the pool has no separate ownership-transfer endpoint") and by `pool_all_money_paths_preserve_books_and_shared_token_custody`, which shows the donation sitting outside both markets' cash books. There is no `sweep`/`rescue`/`skim` function anywhere under `contracts/` (verified by search).
- Because one token's markets across hubs share the single physical pool balance, the stranded amount is also not attributable to any book; `claim_revenue` can only pay out *booked* revenue shares, so it cannot recover donations either.
- On the controller side, `process_flash_position` snapshots only declared `collaterals` and `refund_assets` before the receiver callback (`snapshot_balances` at `flash_position.rs:120-143`), deposits measured deltas of declared collaterals, and calls `refund_listed_assets` for whitelisted refund assets only. `refund_controller_balance_delta` refunds only the positive delta of listed assets; undeclared tokens pushed to the controller remain at `env.current_contract_address()` with no withdrawal path. The endpoints doc states this verbatim: "Undeclared callback assets receive neither credit nor refunds. There is no controller sweep endpoint."
- Relevant code: `contracts/pool/README.md` (trust table and in/out legs), `contracts/controller/src/payments.rs:41-52` (`refund_controller_balance_delta` only returns listed deltas), `contracts/controller/src/strategies/flash_position.rs:104-111` and `:372-384` (`validate_refund_assets` whitelist and `refund_listed_assets`), `contracts/controller/src/strategies/flash_position.rs:325-352` (only declared collaterals are collected as deposits).

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds (within the accepted impact list): tokens delivered to the pool or controller without a crediting path are unrecoverable by anyone, including governance. Two reachable variants for a single unprivileged address: (1) `token.transfer(victim, pool, amount)` — a direct donation permanently absorbed into shared custody; (2) a `flash_position` caller whose receiver callback pushes an asset not present in `collaterals` or `refund_assets` — the tokens stay in the controller forever. This mirrors the source report's "users can send ETH mistakenly" loss-of-funds mechanism, upgraded in severity terms because on Soroban there is not even an owner-only rescue.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. Mistaken transfers to contract addresses are a recurring wallet/integration error, and integrators building flash receivers must enumerate refund assets exactly — an omission loses the pushed amount atomically. It is user-error-driven rather than attacker-extractable, which caps it at Medium: no third party profits and no protocol accounting is corrupted (stranded funds are unbooked surplus, not insolvency).

### Recommendation
- Add an owner-gated rescue path, e.g. `pool.skim(asset, to)` guarded by the controller plus a governance-timelocked `controller.rescue(asset, to)` for tokens sent to the controller directly; restrict it to `token.balance(pool) − Σ cash` (or a per-market surplus computation) so booked funds can never be skimmed.
- For `flash_position`, either revert the call when the controller's balance of any non-declared asset increased during the callback (fail closed instead of silently retaining), or default the refund set to every asset whose controller delta is positive.

### Proof of Concept
1. Pool donation: call `token.transfer(alice → POOL, X)` on a listed asset. `get_reserves`/`get_sync_data` for every `(hub_id, asset)` market is unchanged, `token.balance(POOL) > Σ cash` forever, and no entrypoint can move the surplus — only booked `cash`/`revenue` outflows exist.
2. Controller push: call `flash_position(caller, account_id, spoke, Multiply, debt, amount, receiver, data, [(coll, min)], refund_assets=[])` where `receiver.execute_flash_position` additionally transfers token T (listed but undeclared) to the controller. The call succeeds; T sits on the controller with `refund_listed_assets` iterating an empty list, and no subsequent call retrieves it.
