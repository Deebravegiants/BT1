### Title
Pool `repay` refunds a declared "overpayment" out of real custody without debiting cash or checking what was actually paid in — ([File: contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs](contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs))

### Summary
The kernel report's bug class is an edge/error path that falls through a shared release routine while skipping the accounting that funds it. The pool's `ops::repay::apply` has the same shape: the overpayment refund is derived from the *declared* `action.amount`, not from measured inbound tokens, and is paid via `Cache::transfer_out` straight out of pool custody with no `cash` debit and no `require_reserves` check. On a market with little or no outstanding debt, an unprivileged direct `pool.repay` call makes nearly the entire declared amount "excess" and drains real supplier funds to the caller.

### Finding Description
`repay::apply` calls `accounting` and then unconditionally executes `outcome.cache.transfer_out(payer, outcome.overpayment)` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs:30-33`). Inside `accounting`, `resolve_repay` splits the declared `action.amount` into `burned` shares and `overpayment`; when `current_debt_ceil` is zero the full-close branch sets `net_repay == 0` and `overpayment == amount` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs:44-47`). The `RepayRoundsToZeroShares` guard passes on its `net_repay == 0` disjunct (`repay.rs:48-52`), so nothing stops a pure-refund call. Only `credit_cash(net_repay)` (zero) is booked; the refund leg is never charged against `cash` and never validated against an actual inbound transfer — the rustdoc's assumption that "the controller transfers the repay amount into the pool before this call" (`repay.rs:3`) is not enforced by the pool itself.

The repo's own test pins the exploit end-to-end: `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` calls `repay(&payer, custody_before)` on a zero-debt market where `before.cash == custody_before`, and asserts `credited == 0` while custody is drained to `payer` (`contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3584-3610`). The same unfunded-refund gap is documented for `ops::recapitalize::apply` (`flows.rs:3578-3582`).

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. A single unprivileged address calls `pool.repay(payer = attacker, action.amount = pool_token_balance)` on any market whose outstanding debt is below the declared amount; the excess is transferred out of custody that backs supplier claims (`cash`), leaving the market unable to honor withdrawals. No deposit, collateral, or prior position is required.

### Likelihood Explanation
Direct and permissionless: `repay` is a public pool entrypoint and nothing in `apply` authenticates that `amount` was actually transferred in — the controller normally does the inbound transfer, but the pool does not require the caller to be the controller nor measure a balance delta. The refund is computed entirely from the attacker-chosen `action.amount`, so no price oracle, timing, or governance precondition is needed; only a market with debt smaller than the custody balance.

### Recommendation
Either (a) measure the actual inbound token delta (or restrict `repay`/`recapitalize` to the controller and have it pass the measured amount), and clamp `overpayment` to `amount_received`, or (b) charge the refund against `cash`/`require_reserves` in `Cache::transfer_out` for these legs so an unbacked refund cannot exceed what was booked. `net_repay == 0` repays carrying a positive `overpayment` should also be rejected outright.

### Proof of Concept
1. Identify any initialized pool market with custody `C` and zero/low debt (or pre-repay residual debt first).
2. From a fresh address `attacker`, invoke `pool.repay(&attacker, &PoolAction { amount: C, .. })` directly — no token transfer in.
3. `resolve_repay` returns `overpayment = C`, `net_repay = 0`; the `RepayRoundsToZeroShares` assert passes; `transfer_out(attacker, C)` moves the pool's entire token balance to the attacker while `cash` is unchanged.
4. Result: supplier principal backing `cash` is gone; subsequent withdrawals fail. This is exactly what `contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3590-3610` already demonstrates and asserts.

Caveat: I could not fully re-verify `resolve_repay`'s branch conditions and whether the pool exposes a controller-only gate on `repay` elsewhere within the iteration budget; however, the in-repo test executes `repay` on a raw `payer` address with no controller hop and asserts the custody drain, which substantiates reachability as written.
