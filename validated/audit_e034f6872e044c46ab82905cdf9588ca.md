### Title
Dust top-up of a bad-debt account's existing collateral blocks permissionless `clean_bad_debt` above the $5 threshold - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs)

### Summary
The external report's bug class is: a batched entrypoint that increments a finite counter/admission state without enforcing a minimum value per leg, letting an attacker fill or manipulate that state at (near-)zero cost. XOXNO Lending rejects literal zero amounts on `supply`/`borrow`/`repay` via `aggregate_positive_payments` (`GenericError::AmountMustBePositive`), so the exact zero-fill shape does not exist. The same class does map onto the bad-debt cleanup gate: `clean_bad_debt` is permissionless but only while the account's unweighted collateral is at or below the fixed $5 dust threshold, while third-party `supply` is deliberately allowed to top up any *existing* supply slot on a foreign account. A bad-debt account by construction still holds supply positions, so an attacker can add a few dollars of an already-held asset to push collateral above $5 and disable the only unprivileged path that socializes the bad debt.

### Finding Description
- `process_supply` loads the target account and calls `require_third_party_existing_supply`, which for a caller that is neither owner nor delegate only requires that each supplied `(hub_id, asset)` already exists in `account.supply_positions`. An insolvent account awaiting cleanup necessarily has such positions (its residual collateral is exactly what cleanup reclassifies to revenue).
- The subsequent `process_deposit`/`finalize_position_flow(..., PositionSides::Supply, false)` path performs no health-factor or post-pool solvency check for pure supply (ordinary `supply` skips risk checks), and pool `supply::apply` only rejects positive amounts that mint zero shares — a dollar-scale top-up always mints shares.
- `clean_bad_debt` requires `ceil` risk debt > half-up unweighted collateral AND collateral ≤ $5; only the owner-gated forced cleanup omits the dust cap. Raising the account's collateral above $5 makes every permissionless `clean_bad_debt` call revert.
- Because supply shares accrue at the market supply index, the topped-up collateral does not decay back below $5 on its own — the block persists and worsens over time, while the unwritten-down debt keeps accruing borrow interest.

### Impact Explanation
Bad-debt socialization is the mechanism that keeps supplier claims honest: cleanup writes down the supply index and deletes the account. Blocking it leaves phantom debt on the books, prevents the supply-index write-down (so honest suppliers cannot see/realize the loss), and keeps the account's residual collateral locked instead of converted to protocol revenue. This is a griefing DoS on a core protocol function — temporary freezing of the market's loss-resolution path — achievable by any unprivileged address for roughly the dust threshold plus one unit, repeatable per account and per asset slot.

### Likelihood Explanation
- Cost is bounded and tiny: the attacker needs the account's existing collateral raised just above $5, i.e. ~$5 of a token the account already supplies, deposited via `supply(attacker, victim_id, spoke_id, [(hub_asset, ~$5+)])`.
- Preconditions occur naturally: any insolvent account whose remaining collateral sat at or under $5 (the exact population `clean_bad_debt` exists for) holds at least one supply slot, which is all `require_third_party_existing_supply` demands.
- Partial mitigations exist but are weak: a liquidator could repay debt to seize the dust collateral back below $5 (on an insolvent account the quote is `floor(C/(1+bonus))`, so seizing ~$5 costs ~$4.7 — marginally profitable but requiring the liquidator to absorb bad debt), and the owner-gated `force_socialize_bad_debt` can still clean regardless of the dust cap. Neither removes the griefing primitive; they shift cleanup to privileged or debt-absorbing actors while the attacker can repeat the top-up after every counter-action.

### Recommendation
Gate permissionless bad-debt cleanup on collateral *excluding* third-party top-ups, or bound the attack directly:
1. Track per-account "self-supplied" collateral (shares minted under owner/delegate authority) and evaluate the $5 dust threshold against that, or snapshot the collateral baseline when the account first becomes cleanup-eligible.
2. Alternatively, let `clean_bad_debt` treat collateral above the threshold by first liquidating/sweeping the excess into revenue internally, or
3. Reject third-party `supply` (and `repay`-adjacent credit paths such as liquidation `Credit` seizure into the account) once the account satisfies the insolvency condition (`ceil risk debt > half-up collateral`), since no legitimate user benefits from donating collateral to a doomed account.

### Proof of Concept
1. Borrower ALICE's account becomes deeply insolvent: debt `D` >> collateral `C` = $3 (under the $5 dust threshold). `clean_bad_debt(keeper, alice_id)` would now succeed.
2. Attacker MALLORY calls `supply(mallory, alice_id, alice_spoke, [((hub_id, asset_alice_already_supplies), ~$2.5 worth)])`. `require_third_party_existing_supply` passes because the asset is an existing supply slot; no risk check runs for pure supply.
3. Collateral is now ~$5.5 > $5. Every subsequent `clean_bad_debt` reverts on the dust-cap check.
4. If a liquidator repays bad debt to seize the dust back under $5, MALLORY repeats step 2. Supply-index accrual on the topped-up shares makes each repetition cheaper in relative terms. Bad debt remains unsocialized and keeps accruing interest until the owner runs forced cleanup.
