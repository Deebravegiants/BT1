### Title
Unpriceable Aquarius LP collateral blocks liquidation and bad-debt cleanup - (File: contracts/controller/src/risk/totals.rs)

### Summary
A borrower can attach a small listed Aquarius LP-token collateral leg to an otherwise ordinary leveraged account, then withdraw their separate liquidity from that Aquarius pool until its total value falls below `min_pool_value_wad`. The LP oracle then fails closed, and because account valuation fetches prices for every supply and debt asset, the poisoned leg makes `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `force_socialize_bad_debt`, and debt-bearing withdrawals revert. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
`Controller::supply` authenticates the caller, creates or loads the account, and deposits the listed collateral without requiring that its oracle currently resolves. [3](#0-2) 

Once the position exists, `calculate_account_risk_totals` loads market data for the concatenated supply and borrow keys and then reads `cached_price` for every collateral leg. [4](#0-3) 

`Context::load_markets` fetches every distinct token price through the aggregator's strict `prices` endpoint, so one failed key aborts the entire operation. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

The Aquarius provider computes the pool's LP-token value and returns `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` whenever `pool_value_wad < lp.min_pool_value_wad`. [7](#0-6) 

`liquidate` builds its liquidation plan from full account state and recalculates account risk after repayment and seizure, so it cannot skip the failing collateral price. [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8) 

Both permissionless cleanup and forced socialization call the same all-position risk-total routine before admitting the account. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10) 

### Impact Explanation
This is an attacker-controlled denial of liquidation: while the Aquarius pool remains below the configured liquidity floor, liquidators cannot reduce the borrower's debt through either `SeizeMode::Transfer` or `SeizeMode::Credit`, and bad-debt cleanup cannot remove the account. [12](#0-11) [13](#0-12) 

During the outage, collateral-market prices and debt indexes can continue moving against the protocol, so a position that should have been liquidated can become undercollateralized and produce supplier bad debt when the LP price is restored. [14](#0-13) 

The poisoned leg also blocks the borrower's withdrawals while debt remains because normal withdrawal performs a post-pool solvency check over the same account positions. [15](#0-14) 

### Likelihood Explanation
A single unprivileged liquidity provider can satisfy the trigger if they control enough of a listed Aquarius pool's shares: deposit a small LP amount as lending collateral, borrow against the account's total collateral, and withdraw the remaining pool liquidity below `min_pool_value_wad`. [16](#0-15) [2](#0-1) 

The test suite demonstrates the same account-shape weakness with a deliberately stale collateral feed: `supply` accepts the poisoned leg, after which both `liquidate` and `clean_bad_debt` revert. [17](#0-16) [18](#0-17) 

### Recommendation
Do not let one unpriceable collateral position poison every risk path unconditionally. Liquidation planning should isolate an oracle-failed collateral leg, exclude it from pro-rata seizure, and value usable collateral and debt separately; cleanup needs an explicit recovery path that can reclassify or seize the unpriceable shares without requiring their USD price for admission. Supply should additionally require a strict price when adding a collateral leg to an indebted account, although that check alone cannot prevent a later pool-liquidity outage. [4](#0-3) [2](#0-1) 

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker acquires LP tokens for a listed Aquarius pool while retaining enough separate LP shares to withdraw the pool below `min_pool_value_wad`. [2](#0-1) 
2. Attacker calls `supply(caller=attacker, account_id=0, spoke_id=S, assets=[(HubAssetKey{hub_id:H, asset:LP}, small_amount), (HubAssetKey{hub_id:H, asset:COLLATERAL}, collateral_amount)])`, followed by `borrow(caller=attacker, account_id=id, borrows=[(HubAssetKey{hub_id:H, asset:DEBT}, debt_amount)], to=None)`. [19](#0-18) 
3. After ordinary market movement makes the account unhealthy, the attacker withdraws their separate Aquarius liquidity until the LP provider returns `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity`. [7](#0-6) 
4. A liquidator calls `liquidate(liquidator=L, account_id=id, debt_payments=[(HubAssetKey{hub_id:H, asset:DEBT}, amount)], seize_mode=SeizeMode::Transfer)`; the operation fetches the LP price with all other account prices and reverts before debt is reduced. [8](#0-7) [4](#0-3) 
5. Any caller calling `clean_bad_debt(caller=C, account_id=id)`, or governance eventually calling `force_socialize_bad_debt(account_id=id)`, reaches the same strict valuation and also reverts. [13](#0-12) [11](#0-10) 
6. The existing harness pins the behavior: supplying a dust collateral leg with an unusable feed succeeds, then liquidation, cleanup, and removal of that leg all fail until the price becomes usable again. [20](#0-19) [21](#0-20)
