### Title
Debt-value overflow in interest accrual permanently freezes a market (all exits, repays, liquidations revert) - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs via contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
The analog of the missing-NULL-check crash (CVE-2021-44485) is a missing overflow guard in the accrual path: `accrue_step` values total debt as `borrowed_scaled × borrow_index` in `i128`, and once that product exceeds `i128::MAX` the multiplication panics with `MathOverflow`. Because every pool mutation runs `interest::global_sync` before doing anything else, the panic permanently bricks the market for all callers — an unprivileged borrower/supplier can push a market into this state by supplying and borrowing at very large scale on a high-rate curve.

### Finding Description
`global_sync` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-53` is executed at the head of every market entrypoint (`supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `net_settle`, `seize_positions`, `flash_loan`, `update_indexes`, `claim_revenue` — per the documented flow in `contracts/pool/README.md:159-168`). Each chunk calls `accrue_step`, which computes `new_debt = borrowed.mul(borrow_index')` through `scaled_to_original`/`Ray::mul` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`), a checked `i128` mul-div that panics on overflow.

The borrow index is only capped *after* the multiply (`update_borrow_index` clamps at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY = 1e36`, but the pre-clamp product `borrowed × index` is evaluated first). With a large enough scaled debt — reachable by an unprivileged account supplying and borrowing the maximum the market caps allow — ordinary index growth eventually makes `borrowed × borrow_index` exceed `i128::MAX`. From that point, every call that touches the market reverts inside accrual before any user logic runs.

The protocol's own harness demonstrates the cliff: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-356` supplies ~1e9·10^18 units, borrows at 98% utilization on the XLM curve, advances time, and asserts `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay` all panic with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (the index cap never engages). The invariant docs acknowledge the gap: "Debt-value overflow can still revert accrual before that ceiling is reached" (`docs/reference/invariants.md:235-236`).

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds. Once the product overflows, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`/`seize_positions`, `flash_loan`, and `recapitalize` on that market all revert inside `global_sync`, so suppliers cannot exit, borrowers cannot repay, and liquidators cannot seize — all cash and positions in the market are stuck forever. There is no admin escape hatch that skips accrual; even `claim_revenue` and `update_indexes` hit the same panic.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium-Low. The trigger is purely unprivileged (`supply` + `borrow`), but reaching the `i128` ceiling requires a debt scaled-share base near the RAY domain limit (~10^27 scaled) times index growth — i.e., an extremely large market (the test uses a ~10^27-unit supply on an 18-decimal asset) sustained at high utilization on a steep rate curve. Caps (`supply_cap`/`borrow_cap`) constrain this, but they are per-market parameters and can be large or zero (uncapped). An attacker with sufficient capital, or organic whale-scale usage over years on a high-APR market, reaches the cliff with no privileged action. No MEV, oracle, or third-party cooperation is needed.

### Recommendation
Value debt in the widened domain before narrowing, or clamp earlier:
- In `accrue_step`, compute `borrowed × borrow_index'` via `I256`/`mul_div` widening (the codebase already widens `fp_core::mul_div_half_up` to `I256` on overflow) and saturate/compare in the wide domain instead of letting `Ray::mul` panic.
- Alternatively, enforce a `borrowed` ceiling at borrow time (e.g., `require_cap_within_asset_domain`-style bound on scaled debt so `borrowed × MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY ≤ i128::MAX`), making the unreachable-product state impossible rather than relying on the index cap that engages too late.
- Add a regression test asserting `update_indexes`/`withdraw`/`repay` succeed (or fail with a recoverable path) at the maximum reachable `borrowed × index`.

### Proof of Concept
Adapted from the repo's own demonstration at `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-356`:

```rust
let mut t = LendingTest::new()
    .with_market(big("BIG18", 18, xlm_curve()))   // steep curve, 18-dec asset
    .with_market(col())
    .with_max_utilization_disabled_all_markets()
    .build();
lift_caps(&t, "BIG18", 18);
lift_caps(&t, "COL", 7);

let principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18);
t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);           // unprivileged supply
let debt = principal / 100 * 98;
t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);              // unprivileged borrow, 98% util

loop {
    t.advance_time(YEAR_SECS);
    if t.try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"]).is_err() { break; } // accrual panics
}

// Market permanently frozen — all unprivileged exits revert:
assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
assert_contract_error(t.try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
// seize_positions / flash_loan / recapitalize revert identically in global_sync.
```

The panic originates in `accrue_step` → `scaled.mul(env, index)` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`), invoked by `accrue_chunk` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:39-53`) at the head of every mutation.
