### Title
Attacker plants a dust supply leg in an unpriceable asset to make their insolvent account unliquidatable and uncleanable - (File: contracts/controller/src/risk/totals.rs)

### Summary
`liquidate` and `clean_bad_debt` both call `calculate_account_risk_totals`, which unconditionally prices **every** supply and debt leg of the target account via `cache.cached_price` — a single stale/missing feed reverts the whole call. Yet `supply` admits a brand-new collateral leg without requiring a fresh price for that asset (the `apply_gated_liquidation_params` gate only reads prices of pre-existing account assets). An unprivileged borrower whose position goes underwater can supply a dust amount of an asset whose oracle feed is stale/broken, permanently bricking both liquidation and bad-debt cleanup for their account. The repo's own audit test `audit_liquidate_and_clean_bricked_by_unpriceable_dust_leg` reproduces exactly this. This maps the CVE-2025-21536 class (attacker-triggered availability/DoS of a critical function) onto liquidation/cleanup availability.

### Finding Description
- `calculate_account_risk_totals_body` iterates all supply legs calling `cache.cached_price(&hub_asset.asset)`; any stale feed aborts valuation — `contracts/controller/src/risk/totals.rs:171-186`. [1](#0-0) 
- Liquidation builds its plan on these totals and cleanup gates on them in `socialize_bad_debt`, so both revert with `PriceFeedStale` — `contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs:222-235`. [2](#0-1) 
- `process_deposit`/`merge_supply_leg` create a new supply leg via `get_or_create_supply_position` and `refresh_supply_risk_params`; the gated HF check reads only existing position prices, never demanding the new asset's feed be fresh — `contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs:116-135`, `275-297`. [3](#0-2) 
- Proof in-repo: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_liquidate_and_clean_stale_leg.rs:26-40` supplies `0.001` WBTC with a 1-hour-stale feed (succeeds), then both `try_liquidate` and `try_clean_bad_debt_by_id` fail with `PRICE_FEED_STALE`. [4](#0-3) 

### Impact Explanation
While the planted leg's feed stays stale, no liquidator can repay any of the account's debt and permissionless `clean_bad_debt` cannot socialize it. Debt keeps accruing interest against a frozen account, deepening insolvency; once the write-down eventually lands, suppliers of the debt markets bear a larger loss than if timely liquidation had occurred. Additionally, the owner-only fallback `force_socialize_bad_debt` hits the same `cached_price` wall, so even governance cannot clean the account — a temporary freezing of funds with a direct path to protocol insolvency. Reachable by any unprivileged address via `supply(account_id, [(hub_asset, dust)])` on their own account.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a listed asset whose configured feed becomes stale (Reflector/RedStone outage, delisted feed, or a feed with a very short max-stale window) — a plausible oracle incident rather than attacker-controlled pricing. Cost to the attacker is one dust supply. No auth beyond the account owner is needed, and flags/pauses do not restore the path since cleanup bypasses listing flags but not pricing.

### Recommendation
At `supply` admission for a new leg on an account with debt, require a fresh valid price for the supplied asset (prefetch `cached_price` for all aggregated legs in `process_deposit`), or bound the blast radius by capping the number of supply legs whose stale price can block liquidation — e.g., value legs with invalid prices as zero in `calculate_account_risk_totals` for liquidation/cleanup purposes only, so an unpriceable dust leg cannot shield real debt.

### Proof of Concept
See `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_liquidate_and_clean_stale_leg.rs`: borrower holds USDC collateral + ETH debt; WBTC Reflector timestamp is set 1 hour stale; `supply(borrower, WBTC, 0.001)` succeeds; afterwards `liquidate(LIQUIDATOR, borrower, ETH, 1.0)` and `clean_bad_debt(account_id)` both revert `PRICE_FEED_STALE` until the feed recovers.
