### Title
Bad-debt supply-index floor clamp leaves unbacked residual claims that withdraw real cash after recapitalization - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
When bad-debt socialization writes the supply index down past its documented floor, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps the index **up** to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (10^24) instead of proportionally reducing or zeroing supply shares. Every supplier keeps their full `scaled_amount`, but the clamped index leaves claims that exceed the real loss absorbed — a phantom claim analogous to an attacker-influenced size/length field (the written-down index) letting an out-of-range value survive and corrupt later accounting, the structural analog of CVE-2014-8140's heap overflow via a crafted size field.

### Finding Description
The write-down formula (`docs/reference/formulas.md:391-394`) computes `new_supply_index = max(10^24, floor(old_index * reduction / RAY))`. When `bad_debt ≥ total_supply_value`, `reduction` collapses to 0 and the index is clamped to `10^24`, not to a value reflecting "everything was lost". Suppliers' `scaled_amount` shares are untouched, so each share retains `10^24 / old_index` of its original claim even though the corresponding tokens were lost.

`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:317-369` demonstrates the consequence at the raw-cache level: after a wipeout clamps the index to the floor, a stranded `scaled_a` position still unscales to a positive `stranded` claim (`cache.unscale_supply_floor(scaled_a) > 0`), and `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, scaled_a)` pays out non-zero `gross` equal to a fresh depositor's entire deposit — "userA drains exactly userB's fresh deposit out of the pool" and "pool cash can no longer cover userB's claim".

The gate that would stop this in production is `PoolInsolvent` on new `supply`, and the documented recovery path is `recapitalize`, which "fills that shortfall without restoring the lost index or deleted account" (`docs/reference/invariants.md:478-479`). Recapitalization injects cash without minting shares and without restoring the index — so after a successful `recapitalize`, the pool is solvent again, the backing gate reopens, and the stranded phantom shares become withdrawable against the recapitalizer's (and honest suppliers') cash. The old supplier's claim, which corresponded to tokens already lost, converts into a real payout.

Reachability for an unprivileged address: `clean_bad_debt(caller, account_id)` is permissionless (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs:196-243`, `BadDebtGate::DustCapped` — debt > collateral and collateral ≤ $5), and ordinary liquidation auto-triggers the same cleanup. An attacker can manufacture a dust-collateral, deeply insolvent account (own small account after a price move), call `clean_bad_debt` to clamp the index, and later withdraw their stranded shares once the market is recapitalized or re-opens. `recapitalize` is permissionless (`docs/reference/endpoints.md:40`) and can even be invoked by the attacker themselves with a tiny amount that refunds the surplus, as long as it restores enough backing to unblock withdraws — the phantom holder's own `withdraw` is not gated by `PoolInsolvent` in the same way supply is, and withdraws only need cash reserves.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency: residual claims backed by nothing withdraw real tokens injected by recapitalizers and honest suppliers. The unit test at `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:356-368` shows the drain goes to zero cash while an honest claim remains unpaid.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a full-wipeout write-down (bad debt ≥ total supply value in that market) so the index hits the floor — achievable in a thin market where the attacker creates a small borrow that becomes unbacked, or in a market whose other suppliers have exited. The permissionless `clean_bad_debt` path plus the permissionless `recapitalize` recovery path make the sequence reachable without any privileged action.

### Recommendation
On a write-down that clamps to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, proportionally burn or zero the stranded supply shares (or record an explicit per-market "stranded shares" haircut) so `scaled_amount × floor_index` never exceeds real backing; alternatively, make `withdraw` on positions whose value comes solely from the floor clamp fail until the market's claims are reconciled, and have `recapitalize` account for residual floor claims before reopening the backing gate.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker supplies dust collateral and borrows against it; a price move (or a thin market) leaves `debt > collateral` with collateral ≤ $5.
2. Attacker calls `clean_bad_debt(caller, account_id)`; `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes `reduction = 0` and clamps `supply_index` to `10^24` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs`, `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`).
3. An earlier supplier (or the attacker's second account) retains `scaled_amount` shares whose floor-unscaled value `> 0` with zero cash — proven by `test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:317`).
4. Anyone calls `recapitalize(payer, hub_asset, amount)`, refilling cash without changing the index or shares.
5. The stranded holder calls `withdraw` with `amount = i128::MAX`; `resolve_withdrawal` pays the phantom claim from real cash, leaving the recapitalizer's funds drained.
