### Title
Stable-pool LP collateral priced from live, trade-manipulable reserves — (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs)

### Summary
The price aggregator derives the fair value of an Aquarius stable-pool LP share by solving the StableSwap invariant `D` over the pool's **current** reserves (`get_reserves`), then multiplying `D` by the lower leg price (`common/src/oracle/lp_stable.rs:80-109`). Because `D` is recomputed from spot reserves on every price read, an attacker can skew the pool's reserve ratio with a single large swap of their own (an explicitly in-scope action), depress `D`, and thereby depress the LP token's oracle price within the transaction that liquidates LP-collateralized borrowers. This mirrors the Keycloak class: data fetched over an unauthenticated channel (here, a mutable pool's `get_reserves`/`a`/`share_id` view) is consumed as authentic without any manipulation-resistant verification (no virtual-price anchor, no reserve-sanity or skew bound, no TWAP).

### Finding Description
In `aquarius::read`, the provider re-validates metadata bindings (`bound_tokens`, `pool_kind_matches`, `decimals_match`) but then feeds `aquarius_pool_reserves_call` output directly into `fair_stable_lp_price_wad` (`contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs:90-114`). For stable pools the price is `D * min(price_a, price_b) / total_shares`, where `D` is Newton–Raphson-solved from WAD-normalized live reserves (`common/src/oracle/lp_stable.rs:30-68, 96-108`). `D` falls when reserves become imbalanced — that is precisely the stableswap invariant's behavior — so a swap that concentrates the pool into one coin lowers `D`, and the LP price falls with it. The only floors are positivity and `min_pool_value_wad` on *total* pool value (`aquarius.rs:118-122`), which does not constrain the reserve *ratio*. The observation is marked non-stale and returned to the engine, which hands it to the controller's Context-cached strict price used for health factor, `min_borrow_collateral_usd`, and liquidation eligibility. The constant-product path is safer by construction (`2*sqrt(vA*vB)` is the standard manipulation-resistant form), but the stable-pool `D * min_price` form is not: its entire manipulation resistance depends on reserves being honest, and nothing verifies them.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds via mispriced collateral. An attacker holding a wallet and some capital can, inside one transaction: (1) execute a large swap on the victim Aquarius stable pool to imbalance its reserves, lowering `D` and the LP oracle price; (2) call the controller's `liquidate` on accounts whose collateral is that LP token, now below their liquidation threshold, collecting the liquidation bonus pro-rata legs; (3) swap back to restore the pool. Alternatively the attacker can supply LP shares, imbalance the pool *toward* higher `D` is not exploitable, but the downward direction suffices for profitable liquidations and for triggering `clean_bad_debt`-eligible undercollateralized states. The swap cost is the pool's swap fee plus temporary price impact, recovered on the back-swap; the gain is the liquidation bonus on every liquidatable LP-collateralized position.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by any unprivileged address: `liquidate` is permissionless, Aquarius pool swaps are permissionless, and no allowlist, cooldown, reserve-ratio check, or historical-price anchor intervenes between the manipulated reserve state and the price read. Required conditions are a listed market whose oracle is `PriceSource::AquariusLp { stable: true }`, LP collateral posted by third parties near their liquidation threshold, and pool liquidity thin enough that the attacker can move `D` materially — the exact conditions stable-pool LP collateral markets are designed for. Severity is Medium: the attack needs capital and an imbalanced-move sufficient to both skew the price and keep it inside the sanity band (`min_sanity_price_wad`/`max_sanity_price_wad`), and deep stable pools resist it, but no privileged access is needed.

### Recommendation
Do not solve `D` from live reserves. Use the pool's reported virtual price (`get_virtual_price` / share-id-redemption quote) as the D-anchor, or bound the accepted reserve skew at read time (e.g., reject reads when `max(reserve)/min(reserve)` exceeds a configured ratio for a `stable`-typed pool), analogous to how `scaled.min_factor_wad`/`max_factor_wad` bounds the scaled factor. Additionally or alternatively, require a secondary confirmation leg (e.g., an external LP/constituent quote) via the existing dual-leg tolerance mechanism so a single-venue reserve snapshot cannot set the collateral price alone.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance lists LP share token `S` of Aquarius stable pool `P` (coins `A`, `B`, high `amp`) as collateral with oracle `PriceSource::AquariusLp{pool: P, key_a: A-oracle, key_b: B-oracle, stable: true}`.
2. Victim supplies `S` and borrows near max LTV; health factor ≈ 1.0x.
3. Attacker swaps a large amount of `A` for `B` on `P` (own trade, in-scope), leaving reserves heavily skewed toward `A`. `solve_stable_d` over the new reserves yields a lower `D`; `fair_stable_lp_price_wad` returns a lower `S` price, within the sanity band if sized correctly.
4. Attacker calls `controller.liquidate` on the victim; the Context-cached strict price for `S` is the depressed value, the victim's HF is below the threshold, and the attacker seizes discounted collateral across the pro-rata legs.
5. Attacker back-swaps `P` to restore reserves; net profit = liquidation bonus minus swap fees/impact.

Note on uncertainty: the exact sensitivity of `D` to reserve skew depends on the pool's `amp`; for very high `amp` the deviation needed is larger, but the code places no bound on either `amp` (beyond `<= 1_000_000`) or the reserve ratio, and `attest` only requires positive reserves and shares at configuration time (`aquarius.rs:37-48`).
