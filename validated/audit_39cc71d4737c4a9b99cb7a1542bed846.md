### Title
Unpriceable collateral dust leg blocks `liquidate` and `clean_bad_debt`, letting a borrower shield underwater debt from liquidation - ([File: contracts/controller/src/risk/totals.rs](contracts/controller/src/risk/totals.rs))

### Summary
XOXNO Lending's health/risk path fetches strict prices for *every* supply and borrow leg in an account. A borrower (an unprivileged address) can add a dust-sized supply position in an asset whose oracle subsequently reports zero, a stale timestamp, or a sanity-band violation. From that point on, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and even the borrower's own `withdraw` all fail closed on the price read — the protocol cannot seize the account's collateral or socialize its bad debt while the leg is unpriceable. This is the direct analog of the Y2K finding "de-peg trigger cannot fire when the oracle price is zero": the safety mechanism reverts exactly when it is needed most.

### Finding Description
`calculate_account_risk_totals` in `contracts/controller/src/risk/totals.rs` calls `cache.load_markets(portfolio_hub_keys(supply_positions.keys(), borrow_positions.keys()))` and then `cache.cached_price(&hub_asset.asset)` for **every** supply leg — with no way for the liquidator to exclude a leg (`liquidate` has no collateral-selection argument; seizure is pro-rata across all collateral).

The price aggregator fails closed on any unusable leg: `LegFailure::failure` in `contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs` returns `PriceFeedStale`, `UnsafePriceNotAllowed`, `InvalidPrice` (`price_wad <= 0`), or `SanityBoundViolated`, and `within_tolerance_band` in `contracts/price-aggregator/src/tolerance.rs` rejects a zero anchor. A single failing leg therefore poisons the whole account valuation.

Crucially, `supply` accepts a new leg even while that asset's feed is already stale/unpriceable (the risk gate only applies to risk-increasing actions when debt is added, and a dust supply of a listed asset is admitted). The harness test `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_supply_stale_shield.rs` proves the full sequence end-to-end:

1. Borrower supplies dust `WBTC` while its Reflector price is already stale — supply succeeds and a non-zero scaled share persists.
2. After the real collateral (USDC) crashes, `liquidate(LIQUIDATOR, alice, "ETH", 1.0)` reverts `PRICE_FEED_STALE`.
3. `clean_bad_debt_by_id(alice_id)` reverts `PRICE_FEED_STALE`.
4. The borrower's own `withdraw` of the dust leg also reverts — the poisoned leg cannot be removed by the borrower cheaply, and no permissionless path removes it.
5. Only when the WBTC feed recovers does the identical liquidation succeed — a twin account without the dust leg is liquidated normally.

`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_liquidate_and_clean_stale_leg.rs` confirms the same for `clean_bad_debt`, and `liquidation_coverage.rs` shows a zero price reverts `liquidate` with `NO_LAST_PRICE` before the health-factor check.

### Impact Explanation
A borrower facing liquidation can front-run it by supplying a tiny amount of an asset whose feed is already stale, down, or outside its sanity band (e.g., an illiquid long-tail collateral). Every subsequent `liquidate` and `clean_bad_debt` call reverts, so the position's debt keeps accruing while collateral cannot be seized. Once the account is insolvent, bad debt cannot be cleaned either — the cost is socialized onto suppliers (supply-index write-down) if and when it can run at all. This is permanent freezing of liquidation for that account and protocol insolvency risk; the accepted "temporary freezing of funds" / "protocol insolvency" impact categories apply.

### Likelihood Explanation
Fully permissionless: `supply`, `liquidate`, and `clean_bad_debt` are all unprivileged entrypoints. The attacker needs only dust of a listed collateral whose feed is currently bad — a recurring real-world condition (Reflector round gaps, RedStone staleness, sanity-band violation during a crash). No privileged action, leaked key, or third-party honesty assumption is required; the attacker exploits the protocol's own fail-closed price reading across all collateral legs.

### Recommendation
Allow liquidation to skip collateral legs whose price is unusable: either value unpriceable supply legs at zero inside `calculate_account_risk_totals` for the liquidation path (failing closed *against* the borrower, not for them), or give `liquidate`/`clean_bad_debt` a way to restrict the valuation/seizure set to priceable assets. At minimum, gate `supply` so a new leg cannot be opened while that asset's strict price is unavailable, and let a borrower remove their own unpriceable dust leg so it cannot permanently block liquidation.

### Proof of Concept
See `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_supply_stale_shield.rs` and `audit_liquidate_and_clean_stale_leg.rs`:

```rust
// make WBTC feed stale, then plant a dust supply leg while it is stale
t.mock_reflector_client().set_price_at(&wbtc, &usd(60_000), &(now - 3_600));
t.try_supply(ALICE, "WBTC", 0.001).unwrap();          // admitted despite stale feed

t.set_price("USDC", usd_cents(50));                    // real collateral crashes
assert_contract_error(t.try_liquidate(LIQUIDATOR, ALICE, "ETH", 1.0), PRICE_FEED_STALE);
assert_contract_error(t.try_clean_bad_debt_by_id(alice_id), PRICE_FEED_STALE);
assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw(ALICE, "WBTC", 0.0001), PRICE_FEED_STALE);

t.set_price("WBTC", usd(60_000));                      // only feed recovery unblocks it
t.try_liquidate(LIQUIDATOR, ALICE, "ETH", 1.0).unwrap();
```
