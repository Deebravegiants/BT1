### Title
Bad-debt write-down clamps the supply index to a nonzero floor, leaving wiped-out supply shares as live claims that drain fresh deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
The use-after-free analog: `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` "frees" all supplier backing when socialized bad debt meets or exceeds total supplied value, but instead of zeroing the share base it clamps `supply_index` up to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`). The pre-existing `supplied` shares are never burned, so each wiped-out supplier retains a *positive* claim computed against the floored index. Those resurrected claims can withdraw real tokens from cash deposited by later suppliers — a stale reference to destroyed backing being used to seize new funds.

### Finding Description
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes `remaining = total_supplied_value − capped_bad_debt` and multiplies the supply index by the resulting reduction factor, then applies `.max(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-88`). When `bad_debt ≥ total_supplied_value`, `remaining = 0`, `new_supply_index = 0`, and the clamp raises the index back to `RAY/1000` rather than resetting `supplied` to zero. Every share `s` outstanding then unscales to `s × RAY/1000` of claim — up to 1/1000 of the original claim value — even though the socialization was supposed to have written supplier value to zero.

This is reachable from the permissionless path: `clean_bad_debt(caller, account_id)` admits any account with `total_debt > total_collateral` and collateral `≤ 5 WAD` — there is no cap on the debt itself (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs:196-243`). Pool `seize` for the `Borrow` side socializes `unscale_borrow_ceil(position)` into the debt market's supply index (`contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:23-28`). On a market where a collapsed-oracle or hyper-leveraged account carries debt exceeding the entire supplied book, one `clean_bad_debt` call drives the supply index to the floor while `supplied` shares remain on every supplier's account.

The stranded claim is then a live withdrawal right: `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, scaled)` pays `unscale_supply_floor(scaled)` against `cash`, guarded only by `require_reserves`. The protocol's own unit test demonstrates the end state: after the floor clamp, `unscale_supply_floor(old_scaled) > 0`, a fresh deposit `c` is credited, and the stale position withdraws exactly `c`, leaving `cash < fresh_claim` and `cash == 0` — the fresh supplier's deposit is unrecoverable (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:316-368`, `:372-427`).

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency amplification. Suppliers whose claims were nominally wiped out retain a residual claim worth up to 0.1% of their shares' original face value per unit of index — a nonzero, withdrawable right backed by nothing. Any subsequent liquidity entering the market (fresh supply, repayments building cash, recapitalization proceeds beyond the measured shortfall definition) is paid out first-come-first-served to holders of these dead claims. The pool can be drained to zero again, and honest post-wipeout depositors lose their entire deposit.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a market where socialized bad debt ≥ total supplied value — i.e., the market is already fully insolvent in that leg. That is a tail condition but a plausible one for thin markets or after an oracle-sanctioned price collapse, and the trigger (`clean_bad_debt`) is permissionless with no flash-loan or pause protection beyond `require_not_flash_loaning`. Once clamped, the condition is permanent: nothing ever restores the index or removes the stranded shares, and `recapitalize` explicitly does not repair it (it fills measured backing shortfall without minting shares or restoring the index — `docs/reference/invariants.md` INV-LIQ-04). Each fresh deposit thereafter is extractable. Likelihood is moderate; impact conditional on the wipeout state being reached.

### Recommendation
On total wipeout, do not resurrect claims at the floor. Either:
- In `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, when `capped == total_supplied_value` (reduction factor 0), also zero `supplied`/`revenue` in the `Cache` so no shares survive to unscale, or
- Track the write-down as a separate unbacked-claims ledger and have `resolve_withdrawal`/`require_reserves` treat floor-clamped residual value as unpayable.

At minimum, make the controller's supply entry path refuse new deposits into a market whose supply index sits at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` with nonzero `supplied`, so fresh cash cannot be captured by stranded shares while the market is insolvent.

### Proof of Concept
The scenario is exercised verbatim by the committed unit test `test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` in `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:316-369`:

1. Market seeded with `supplied = 1_000_000 RAY` shares, `supply_index = RAY`, `cash = 0`.
2. `clean_bad_debt` on a fully insolvent account (permissionless; collateral ≤ $5 dust, debt > collateral) routes through `seize` → `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` with `bad_debt = 2_000_000 RAY` value.
3. `supply_index` clamps to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`; the 1M RAY of shares remain and `unscale_supply_floor` returns a positive `stranded` amount.
4. A new supplier deposits `fresh_cash = stranded`, minting shares whose claim equals the deposit.
5. The holder of the pre-wipeout shares calls `withdraw` (full balance): `resolve_withdrawal` → `require_reserves` passes because the fresh deposit is cash, and `gross == fresh_cash` is paid out.
6. Final state: `cash == 0`, fresh supplier's claim `> cash` — funds permanently stolen/unrecoverable.

Uncertain/verified boundary: the pool-level test operates on raw `Cache`, bypassing ops-level entry gates. Whether a post-wipeout market still admits fresh supply through `controller.supply`/`pool` supply ops depends on the backing check (`guards::backing_shortfall`) not rejecting the deposit — the docs note "New supply checks backing," but the measured shortfall may still permit entry because the stranded claim is what creates the shortfall. The drain path through `withdraw` requires only `require_reserves(gross) <= cash`, which any fresh deposit satisfies.
