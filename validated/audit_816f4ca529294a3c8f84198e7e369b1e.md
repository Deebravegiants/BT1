### Title
Permanent market freeze via i128 overflow in accrual (`supplied * index` exceeds i128 before index caps engage) - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
Analog to CVE-2023-46930 (a reachable crash/SEGV that aborts the process): in XOXNO Lending, an unprivileged user can drive a pool market into a state where every accrual panics with `MathOverflow`. Since all state-changing verbs accrue first, the market is permanently frozen — no withdraw, no repay, no liquidation, no `clean_bad_debt`. This is a contract-level "crash" reachable through the public entrypoints.

### Finding Description
Every pool operation calls `global_sync` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs`, which loops `accrue_chunk` → `accrue_step` → `update_supply_index` / `calculate_supplier_rewards` in `common/src/rates/index.rs`. Both compute ray-scaled products:

- `update_supply_index`: `let total_supplied_value = supplied.mul(env, old_index);` (line 34) — `Ray::mul` panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` when the product exceeds `i128` (`common/src/math/fp.rs`).
- `calculate_supplier_rewards`: `borrowed.mul(env, new_borrow_index)` (lines 80–81) — same panic.

The index caps (`MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, `MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY`) clamp only *after* the multiplication, so they do not prevent the overflow: the pre-clamp product `scaled_position * index` can already exceed `i128::MAX`. A whale supplying a large amount of a high-decimal (e.g., 18-decimal) asset, combined with a borrower taking ~98% utilization on a steep rate curve, grows the index until `scaled * index / RAY` leaves the `i128` domain — before the index cap engages. This is demonstrated by the repository's own test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs` (lines 320–361), which asserts `MathOverflow` from `update_indexes` and that subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` calls revert with the same error.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds. Once `scaled * index` overflows, `accrue_step` panics on every invocation; because `global_sync` runs at the top of supply/borrow/withdraw/repay/liquidate/settle paths, no operation on that market can ever complete. Supplier principal and accrued yield are locked forever; the debt can never be repaid or liquidated, freezing the corresponding collateral and leaving bad debt that `clean_bad_debt` also cannot reach. The test explicitly asserts `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` both fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW` after the cliff.

### Likelihood Explanation
The triggering state is reachable by a single unprivileged address sequence: supply a large principal of an 18-decimal listed asset, supply collateral, borrow to ~98% utilization, then let time pass (the repo test reaches the cliff within 40 simulated years on the XLM curve; higher-rate curves reach it sooner). The final trigger is the permissionless `update_indexes` entrypoint, which anyone can call. Likelihood is bounded by the capital requirement (whale-scale position in a high-decimal asset) and the sustained high-utilization duration, consistent with a Medium finding.

### Recommendation
- In `update_supply_index` and `calculate_supplier_rewards`, clamp the index *before* multiplying (e.g., `min(index, RAY * i128::MAX / scaled)`), or use the existing `mul_div_floor_saturating`/`mul_div_ceil_saturating` helpers instead of `Ray::mul` so the product saturates instead of panicking.
- Alternatively, add an early-exit in `accrue_step` that pins both indexes at their caps once either is at the cap, skipping the value products entirely.
- Enforce supply caps (`require_cap_within_asset_domain` / `max_cap_for_decimals`) tightly enough that `scaled * MAX_*_INDEX_RAY` provably fits `i128` for every listed market.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance lists an 18-decimal asset market with a steep rate curve (e.g., the XLM curve) and caps are lifted via `lift_caps` for the whale.
2. Attacker calls `supply` with principal ≈ `BILLION * 10^18` units of the asset.
3. Attacker supplies collateral in a second market and calls `borrow` for ~98% of the supplied liquidity.
4. Time advances; utilization stays near the steep segment, growing `borrow_index` and `supply_index` each accrual.
5. Anyone calls `update_indexes` (or any verb) once `scaled * index / RAY > i128::MAX`: `Ray::mul` inside `update_supply_index` (common/src/rates/index.rs:34) / `calculate_supplier_rewards` (line 80) panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow`. From this point every subsequent call panics identically — `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt` — permanently freezing all funds in the market. Confirmed by `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-361`.
