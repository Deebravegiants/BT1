### Title
Deposit LP-mint accounting trusts requested transfer amount instead of actual vault balance delta, enabling fee-on-transfer/deflationary-token pools to be drained - (File: program/src/processor.rs)

### Summary
`process_deposit` computes `deduct_coin_amount`/`deduct_pc_amount` and the resulting `mint_lp_amount` from the AMM invariant using vault balances read *before* the token transfer, then calls `Invokers::token_transfer` and unconditionally mints LP based on the pre-computed `deduct_*_amount`, without ever re-reading the vault balance to confirm that amount actually arrived.

### Finding Description
In `process_deposit` (`program/src/processor.rs`), the coin/pc vault balances are fetched once via `Self::unpack_token_account` [1](#0-0) , and `deduct_coin_amount`/`deduct_pc_amount`/`mint_lp_amount` are derived purely from that snapshot and the user-specified `max_coin_amount`/`max_pc_amount` [2](#0-1) . The program then performs the SPL transfers and mints LP tokens for the *requested* amount, with no post-transfer balance check: [3](#0-2) . `Invokers::token_transfer` is a thin wrapper around `spl_token::instruction::transfer` with no balance verification of its own [4](#0-3) .

Because a pool creator freely chooses `coin_mint`/`pc_mint` in `Initialize2` (per the instruction docs) [5](#0-4) , an attacker can create a pool using a token whose transfer routine burns/deducts a percentage on every transfer (the exact mechanic described in the Crypto Burger incident, where a manipulated $BURG transfer/burn behavior let the attacker distort the pool's real token balance versus the AMM's assumed balance). When such a token is deposited, the AMM vault receives less than `deduct_coin_amount`, yet `amm.lp_amount` is incremented and LP tokens are minted as if the full amount had landed in the vault. This creates LP shares that are not fully backed by the paired asset (pc token, e.g., BNB/USDT), i.e., insolvent pool accounting.

Withdraw and swap operations re-read live vault balances at execution time, so they are self-correcting for later operations, but the LP minted during the tainted deposit permanently overstates the depositor's claim relative to the real coin held in the vault. The attacker (or any subsequent LP holder) can then withdraw disproportionately more of the honest paired token than they contributed, draining the counter-asset from the pool — exactly the "burn part of the liquidity, then withdraw/liquidate the rest" pattern in the reported incident.

### Impact Explanation
This allows an attacker who controls the deposited token's mint/transfer behavior (deflationary/fee-on-transfer/burn-on-transfer token) to mint LP tokens disproportionate to the value actually deposited, then redeem those LP tokens for a larger share of the pool's other (honest) asset — a direct theft of LP/pool funds and insolvent pool accounting, matching the Medium-severity bug class in the report.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by any unprivileged actor: a user only needs to (1) create a pool via `Initialize2` with a token contract they control that reduces balances on transfer, and (2) call `Deposit` with attacker-chosen accounts/amounts — both are standard, permissionless instructions with no special signer privileges required. No off-chain, RPC, validator, or SPL-token-program-internal assumptions are needed; the flaw is purely in this program's failure to verify the post-transfer vault delta.

### Recommendation
After each `Invokers::token_transfer` (and similarly `token_transfer_with_authority`) call in `process_deposit`/swap paths, re-read the destination vault's token account balance and use the *actual* delta (post-balance minus pre-balance) rather than the requested amount when computing `mint_lp_amount`, `swap_amount_out`, or PnL accounting. Alternatively, restrict pool creation/deposits to tokens verified to transfer the exact requested amount (e.g., reject mints with transfer hooks/fees), consistent with how modern AMMs guard against fee-on-transfer/rebasing tokens.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker deploys `EvilCoin`, a SPL-token-like mint whose `Transfer` instruction burns X% of every transferred amount (or is paired with a custom program invoked instead of a stock `SPL Token` transfer if a wrapped/modified mint is used).
2. Attacker calls `Initialize2` to create an AMM pool with `coin_mint = EvilCoin`, `pc_mint = USDT` [5](#0-4) .
3. Attacker calls `Deposit` with `max_coin_amount = N`, `max_pc_amount` computed from ratio. `process_deposit` computes `deduct_coin_amount = N` and a corresponding `mint_lp_amount` from the pre-transfer vault snapshot [2](#0-1) .
4. `Invokers::token_transfer` moves `N` EvilCoin from attacker to vault, but only `N * (1 - burn_rate)` actually lands in `amm_coin_vault` due to the token's burn-on-transfer logic [4](#0-3) .
5. `amm.lp_amount` is incremented by `mint_lp_amount` and LP tokens are minted to the attacker based on the full `N`, not the actually-received (lower) amount [6](#0-5) .
6. Attacker calls `Withdraw`, redeeming LP tokens for a share of `total_pc_without_take_pnl`/`total_coin_without_take_pnl` computed from the pool's real (live) balances at withdraw time [7](#0-6) ; because their LP share is inflated relative to what they actually deposited, they extract more USDT than their true contribution, draining the pool.
