### Title
Permissionless third-party `supply` can indefinitely block bad-debt socialization by pushing an insolvent account's collateral above the dust cap - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs)

### Summary
The bug class in the external report is "an action is taken on a party's position without that party ever signaling consent." In XOXNO Lending, `supply` lets any authenticated caller add collateral to a foreign account — no owner or delegate authorization and no consent from the account owner — as long as the target hub asset is an already-existing supply position of that account (`require_third_party_existing_supply`, `contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs:78-97`). This permissionless top-up can be abused to keep an insolvent account's remaining collateral above the `clean_bad_debt` dust threshold, indefinitely blocking permissionless bad-debt socialization.

### Finding Description
`process_supply` only authenticates `caller` and then, for a non-owner/non-delegate caller, requires that every supplied `HubAssetKey` already exists in `account.supply_positions` (`supply.rs:86-96`). The victim's consent is never required, and the credited collateral raises the account's `total_collateral`.

`clean_bad_debt` is the only permissionless path to socialize residual bad debt: `socialize_bad_debt` with `BadDebtGate::DustCapped` requires `is_socializable_bad_debt(total_debt, total_collateral)`, i.e. insolvency *and* remaining collateral at or below the dust cap (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs:196-243`). The alternative, `process_force_socialize_bad_debt`, is owner-gated (`liquidation/mod.rs:246-248`), and the automatic `check_bad_debt_after_liquidation` inside `liquidate` uses the same dust cap (`liquidation/mod.rs:135`).

An unprivileged attacker can therefore:

1. Wait for (or cause via `liquidate`) an account to become insolvent with residual collateral just under the dust cap.
2. Call `supply(caller, victim_account_id, spoke_id, [(existing_collateral_key, dust_gap + epsilon)])`, topping up one of the victim's existing collateral positions by slightly more than the gap to the dust threshold.
3. Every subsequent `clean_bad_debt` call reverts with `CannotCleanBadDebt`, because `total_collateral` now exceeds the cap.

Since supply positions accrue via the supply index, the gap only shrinks slowly; the attacker can repeat the top-up whenever the dust cap is crossed again (e.g. after partial liquidation). Because `supply` is `#[when_not_paused]` but cleanup paths and `withdraw` remain open during pause, this griefing is not even mitigated by the pause mechanism.

### Impact Explanation
Bad-debt socialization is the protocol's mechanism for writing down the supply index and removing insolvent accounts. Blocking it means the protocol carries zombie insolvent accounts whose debt continues accruing against suppliers, and the supply-index write-down that distributes the loss fairly is postponed indefinitely. This maps to the "temporary freezing of funds" / delayed-insolvency-resolution impact class: supplier funds remain locked behind unresolved bad debt for as long as the attacker is willing to fund dust-level top-ups, and each repetition costs the attacker only slightly more than the dust gap (the dust cap is intentionally small, and the top-up is credited to an account that will likely be seized anyway). Perpetual repetition approximates permanent freezing if governance does not intervene via the owner-gated `force_socialize_bad_debt`, which additionally requires the governance timelock to be invoked for each blocked account.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any authenticated address can execute the attack; no delegation, NFT ownership, or privileged role is needed. The only requirements are: (a) an insolvent account exists with collateral near the dust cap — a routine post-liquidation state since `check_bad_debt_after_liquidation` is designed to trigger exactly there — and (b) the victim holds at least one still-listed supply position the attacker can top up, which is guaranteed since residual collateral is what the dust cap measures. The cost per blocking action is bounded by the dust threshold (deliberately small) plus the accrual gap, making sustained griefing cheap relative to the delayed-loss exposure of all suppliers in the affected market. Impact is Medium: funds are delayed rather than stolen, and the attacker pays per repetition.

### Recommendation
Restrict third-party `supply` further, or decouple the bad-debt gate from attacker-malleable collateral:

- Require owner-or-delegate authorization for `supply` on accounts that are insolvent (HF < 1 or debt exceeding collateral), so a stranger cannot enlarge a dying account's collateral; or
- Track "donated" third-party collateral separately and exclude it from the dust-cap calculation in `is_socializable_bad_debt`; or
- Make the dust gate evaluate only collateral that predates insolvency, or give `clean_bad_debt` a proportional sweep that seizes remaining collateral to the liquidator pool before socializing, removing the economic incentive to keep it alive.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice supplies collateral and borrows; prices move so her account's HF < 1.
2. A liquidator calls `liquidate`, repaying most debt; residual collateral is left at `dust_cap - x` (the design intent of the dust-gated cleanup).
3. Attacker Bob calls `supply(Bob, alice_account_id, alice_spoke_id, [(key_of_existing_collateral, x + 1)])`. `require_third_party_existing_supply` passes because the hub asset is already in `alice.supply_positions` (`supply.rs:89-95`); measured receipt credits Alice's account (`supply.rs:116-129`).
4. Anyone calling `clean_bad_debt(caller, alice_account_id)` now reverts: `is_socializable_bad_debt` sees `total_collateral > dust_cap` → `CannotCleanBadDebt` (`liquidation/mod.rs:229-235`).
5. Bob repeats step 3 whenever accrual or further liquidation brings collateral back under the cap. Alice's consent is never requested at any point — the exact analog of a listing created without the seller's signature.
