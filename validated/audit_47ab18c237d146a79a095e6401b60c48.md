### Title
Deletion of the protocol treasury account permanently halts the chain at the next epoch boundary reward credit - (File: `runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs`)

### Summary
The externally reported bug describes a Solidity contract where a privileged role (`feeCollector`) can legitimately renounce itself to `address(0)`, after which a later, unconditional fee-distribution step (`claimFees()`) still computes and "sends" a share to that now-permanently-null address, silently destroying value instead of redirecting it. The reachable analog in nearcore is in the validator/epoch reward crediting path: the protocol treasury account is a normal NEAR account (e.g. `"near"`), and its owner can permissionlessly self-delete it via a standard `DeleteAccount` action (allowed because deletion only requires `actor_id == account_id` and zero locked stake, per `check_actor_permissions` at [1](#0-0) ). Unlike the contract-reward path (`apply_action_receipt`), which gracefully treats a missing receiver account as "reward stays burnt," the validator/treasury-reward path in `update_validator_accounts` does not tolerate a missing treasury account: it treats it as fatal `StorageInconsistentState`, which propagates up and panics the node.

### Finding Description
`update_validator_accounts` runs at every epoch boundary to distribute treasury and validator rewards [2](#0-1) . For the protocol treasury account specifically, it does:
```rust
let mut account = get_account(state_update, account_id)?.ok_or_else(|| {
    StorageError::StorageInconsistentState(format!(
        "Protocol treasury account {} is not found",
        account_id
    ))
})?;
``` [3](#0-2) 

This is the exact structural analog of `claimFees()` in the report: a fixed percentage/share is unconditionally computed and credited to a role/account (`protocol_treasury_account`) that can be permanently removed by its own legitimate owner action, and the code path has no fallback for "role renounced/removed."

The `protocol_treasury_account` is just a regular account referenced by `AccountId` in genesis config, with no special protection preventing its own owner from deleting it, as long as it has no locked stake (`account.locked().is_zero()`, checked in `check_actor_permissions` for `DeleteAccount`) [1](#0-0) . `action_delete_account` then removes the account and refunds its balance to a beneficiary [4](#0-3) .

At the next epoch boundary, `RewardCalculator::calculate_reward` unconditionally inserts a treasury share into the rewards map regardless of whether the treasury account still exists [5](#0-4) . That reward then reaches `update_validator_accounts`, which — because the treasury account is not in `stake_info` (it is not a validator) — falls into the branch that calls `get_account(...).ok_or_else(...)`, producing a fatal `StorageInconsistentState` error instead of a normal `ActionError`.

This `RuntimeError`/`StorageError` is not treated as a recoverable, per-transaction failure. Call sites explicitly panic on it: `chain/chain/src/runtime/mod.rs` matches `Error::StorageError` in `apply_chunk` and calls `panic!("{err}")` for anything other than `FlatStorageBlockNotSupported`/`MissingTrieValue` [6](#0-5) , and the `runtime_user` test harness panics directly on `RuntimeError::StorageError` [7](#0-6) . The `StorageError::StorageInconsistentState` variant's own doc comment states "it's also probably ok to panic in every place that produces this error" [8](#0-7) , confirming this is treated protocol-wide as a should-never-happen invariant violation that halts block processing on all nodes applying that chunk deterministically — i.e., a chain stall, not merely one node's local error.

### Impact Explanation
Deleting the (single, protocol-wide) treasury account causes every node applying the epoch-boundary chunk to hit the same fatal `StorageInconsistentState` and panic deterministically (since state transition is deterministic across all validators). This is a chain-halting condition, not a bounded fund-loss issue as in the original Solidity report — it is more severe because normal chain operation stops entirely until the software is patched or the state is otherwise resolved, until the protocol_treasury_account is re-created before the epoch boundary is processed (which is a race that ordinary users/validators do not control from the affected shard's perspective once the delete receipt is already applied).

### Likelihood Explanation
The action that triggers this is a standard, permitted `DeleteAccount` action requiring only a `FullAccess` key on the treasury account itself and zero locked stake — no protocol-level protection prevents the treasury account owner from doing this, whether by mistake, loss of key custody leading to unexpected deletion tooling, or deliberate action. It requires control of one specific account's key ("near" on mainnet), so it is not exploitable by an arbitrary unprivileged attacker against someone else's funds, but it does not require any validator/node-level privilege or network-layer capability — it is reachable purely via a normal signed transaction against the treasury account, matching the "privileged-but-self-inflicted renounce" pattern of the original report.

### Recommendation
In `update_validator_accounts` (`runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs`), handle the case where the protocol treasury account no longer exists without treating it as `StorageInconsistentState`. Options include: silently dropping the treasury reward (documented, non-fatal, analogous to the "receiver reward stays burnt" behavior already implemented for contract rewards at [9](#0-8) ), or redirecting the undistributed share to validators/total-supply burn, and/or disallowing self-deletion of the configured `protocol_treasury_account` at the `DeleteAccount` action level entirely.

### Proof of Concept
1. On a chain where `protocol_treasury_account = "near"` (or any configured treasury account) has a `FullAccess` key and zero locked stake, submit a `DeleteAccount { beneficiary_id: <any> }` transaction from `"near"` to itself. This passes `check_actor_permissions` (`actor_id == account_id`, `locked() == 0`) [1](#0-0)  and `action_delete_account` removes the account and pays out its balance [10](#0-9) .
2. At the next epoch boundary, `RewardCalculator::calculate_reward` still inserts a nonzero `epoch_protocol_treasury` reward keyed by `protocol_treasury_account` [11](#0-10) .
3. `Runtime::update_validator_accounts` processes the `ValidatorAccountsUpdate`; since `"near"` is not in `stake_info`, it goes to the treasury-only branch, calls `get_account(state_update, "near")`, gets `None`, and returns `Err(StorageError::StorageInconsistentState(...))` [12](#0-11) .
4. This propagates through `Runtime::apply` as `RuntimeError::StorageError`, which `apply_chunk` converts to `Error::StorageError` and then unconditionally `panic!`s (not `FlatStorageBlockNotSupported`/`MissingTrieValue`) [6](#0-5) , halting every node that applies that chunk.
