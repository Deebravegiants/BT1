### Title
Bad-debt index floor resurrects wiped supply claims and drains fresh deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
Bad-debt seizure caps the write-down of `supply_index` at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead of reducing or zeroing suppliers’ remaining share claims. If the market is completely wiped out, surviving supply shares retain a positive claim at the floor despite zero cash. A later depositor mints shares at the floored index and adds fresh cash; an old supplier can then withdraw against that cash, leaving the new depositor undercollateralized.

### Finding Description
When a borrow position is seized, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` caps `bad_debt` at total supplied value, computes a reduced supply index, and clamps it upward to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` [1](#0-0) . A borrow-side seize immediately burns the debt after applying that write-down [2](#0-1) . Thus, a total wipeout does not produce a zero index or clear surviving supply shares; it leaves each share with a residual positive value.

The controller then persists the account deletion and NFT burn after asking the pool to seize both supply and debt sides [3](#0-2) . However, unrelated suppliers’ pool-level scaled shares remain stored in the market totals and can later be withdrawn through the ordinary `withdraw` path, which burns shares, enforces only cash/utilization/solvency checks, and transfers tokens out [4](#0-3) .

### Impact Explanation
A wiped market can be restarted by a victim’s fresh supply. Existing pre-wipeout shares regain economically meaningful backing from that new cash even though the previous bad-debt cleanup intended to socialize the loss. An attacker who holds residual supply shares can call `controller.withdraw` and take the fresh deposit, causing theft of user funds and leaving the new supplier’s claim only partially backed.

The repository’s own regression test encodes this sequence: after the supply index clamps to the floor, a wiped survivor keeps a positive claim, a deposit creates an insolvent book with `owed > cash`, and the old claim extracts exactly the new deposit [5](#0-4) .

### Likelihood Explanation
This is reachable by an unprivileged actor through the permitted account lifecycle: create supply and debt positions, make the account eligible for `clean_bad_debt`, then wait for or create a new deposit in the same market. The cleanup path permissionlessly invokes `pool_seize_positions_call` before removing the account [6](#0-5) . The exploit does not depend on reusing a deleted account ID; it relies on surviving scaled supply shares belonging to another supplier in the wiped market.

### Recommendation
When bad debt consumes the entire supplied value, explicitly zero or retire all remaining supply claims, or prevent the market from accepting new supply until recapitalization restores full backing. A robust fix is to make total-loss socialization reduce all remaining supplier shares to zero or introduce a market tombstone/recapitalization gate that rejects `supply`, `withdraw`, and borrow operations while `supply_index` is at the floor and cash backing is absent. Add an end-to-end test covering `clean_bad_debt` on a wiped market, followed by a fresh user deposit and attempted old-share withdrawal.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice supplies `1_000` units of an asset and Bob borrows the full amount through `controller.supply` and `controller.borrow`.
2. The account becomes bad-debt eligible and a caller invokes `controller.clean_bad_debt(account_id)`.
3. `pool_seize_positions_call` processes the borrow side, computes `bad_debt`, calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, and burns Bob’s debt [2](#0-1) .
4. Because the write-down clamps at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, Alice’s scaled supply still unscale to a positive amount instead of zero [7](#0-6) .
5. Carol supplies an amount equal to Alice’s stranded claim through `controller.supply`; the pool mints Carol’s shares at the floored index and credits cash.
6. Alice calls `controller.withdraw` for the market. The pool resolves her residual shares to cash, passes `require_reserves`, debits the fresh deposit, and transfers it to Alice [8](#0-7) .
7. Carol’s claim now exceeds remaining cash, reproducing the insolvency demonstrated by `test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` [9](#0-8) .

pub(crate) fn apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache: &mut Cache, bad_debt: Ray) {
    let total_supplied_value = cache.supplied().mul(cache.env(), cache.supply_index());

    if total_supplied_value == Ray::ZERO {
        return;
    }

    let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
    let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);

    let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
    let new_supply_index = cache
        .supply_index()
        .mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);

    cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
}

/// Enforces reserve, utilization, and solvency guards, then debits cash for
/// the net transfer. Liquidations and footprint-only closes skip utilization.
fn gate_and_debit(env: &Env, cache: &mut Cache, net_transfer: i128, skip_utilization_check: bool) {
    cache.require_reserves(net_transfer);

    if !skip_utilization_check {
        guards::require_utilization_below_max(env, cache);
    }
    guards::require_supply_for_debt(env, cache);
    cache.debit_cash(net_transfer);
}
