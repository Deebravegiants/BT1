### Title
Relayer withdrawal permanently zeroes accrued fees before destination payout succeeds, with no pro‑rata/carry‑forward when destination collateral is insufficient - (File: modules/pallets/relayer/src/withdrawal.rs)

### Summary
`pallet-relayer::withdraw` reads a relayer's full `Fees[dest_chain][address]` balance, dispatches a cross-chain POST instructing the destination `EvmHost`/HostManager to transfer `available_amount` of the fee token to the beneficiary, and then unconditionally zeroes the `Fees` entry — all before any confirmation that the destination actually holds enough collateral to pay the full amount. This mirrors the Sherlock BalancedVault issue: the accounting is zeroed to the full "unclaimed" amount without checking that the payer (destination host) can actually cover it, and there is no pro-rata reduction or carry-forward mechanism to preserve the shortfall for a later claim.

### Finding Description
The withdrawal flow is:
1. `Pallet::withdraw` in [1](#0-0)  reads `available_amount = Fees::<T>::get(dest_chain, address)` and only checks it against a minimum withdrawal threshold — it never checks the destination's actual fee-token balance.
2. It builds a `WithdrawalParams { beneficiary_address, amount: available_amount, token: params.fee_token }` payload and dispatches an ISMP POST to the destination chain's `HostManager`/`EvmHost` [2](#0-1) .
3. Regardless of whether that dispatch will ever be successfully delivered and executed, the pallet immediately zeroes the relayer's `Fees` entry for the *entire* `available_amount`: [3](#0-2) .
4. On the destination side, `EvmHost.withdraw` (called via the HostManager) performs a plain `IERC20.safeTransfer`/native `call` for the full requested amount with no partial/pro-rata logic: [4](#0-3) . If the destination `EvmHost`'s fee-token balance is less than `available_amount` (e.g., protocol fee revenue on that specific destination chain hasn't yet accumulated to that level, or has already been drawn down by other relayer withdrawals/governance withdrawals sharing the same pool), this transfer reverts, as explicitly exercised by the test `test_host_manager_insufficient_balance` [5](#0-4) .

This is structurally the same root cause as the reported BalancedVault issue: the ledger entry that represents "amount owed to the user" (`_unclaimed[account]` / `Fees[dest_chain][address]`) is unconditionally zeroed to the full claimed amount at claim time, without first verifying that the payer actually has sufficient collateral to honor that specific claim, and without any mechanism to pro-rate the payout or preserve the unclaimed remainder for the user to claim later. The `BandwidthManager`/`IntentsBase` withdrawal paths in this codebase show the same unconditional-transfer pattern [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) , but the relayer-fees case is the one that most directly maps to the audit finding because the accounting entry lives on a *different chain* (Hyperbridge) from the payout (the destination EVM chain), so the source-side zeroing is fully decoupled from destination-side settlement success.

### Impact Explanation
If the ISMP POST triggering `EvmHost.withdraw`/HostManager transfer reverts on delivery because the destination host's fee-token balance is momentarily lower than the relayer's full accrued `available_amount` (a very plausible situation, since revenue on any single destination chain accrues independently of how much a given relayer has accumulated across all deliveries), the outcome is:
- The `Fees` entry on Hyperbridge is already zero (funds "spent" from the relayer's perspective).
- The destination-side transfer either reverts (and the request is not marked delivered, `timeout: 0` meaning it never times out either) or partially succeeds depending on downstream handling — in either failure case there is no pro-rata payout and no mechanism inside `pallet-relayer` to restore any unpaid remainder to the relayer's claimable balance.

This can cause relayers to lose legitimately earned fees or be forced to rely on manual/out-of-band remediation, since the protocol accounting offers no automated recovery path once `Fees` has been zeroed against a withdrawal that could not be (fully) honored downstream.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate: it requires the destination `EvmHost`'s fee-token balance for the relevant chain to be below what a specific relayer has legitimately accrued in `Fees` at withdrawal time. This can happen naturally as fee revenue and relayer accrual rates diverge across many relayers/chains, or if governance/host-executive withdraws host revenue concurrently. It does not require an adversarial actor — it is a race/accounting design flaw reachable by any relayer performing a normal, permissionless `withdraw_fees` call.

### Recommendation
Before zeroing the `Fees` entry, either:
1. Verify (or have the destination confirm via a response) sufficient collateral is available before crediting/settling and use a two-phase commit — only zero `Fees` (or zero only the portion) after confirmed successful delivery/execution on the destination; or
2. Compute a pro-rata payout amount similar to the fix recommended for `BalancedVault.claim`, dispatching only the amount currently available and leaving the shortfall in `Fees` for a future withdrawal, rather than zeroing the full `available_amount` unconditionally at dispatch time.

### Proof of Concept
1. Relayer accrues `Fees[Evm(1)][relayer] = 1000` fee-token units via `accumulate_fees` on Hyperbridge.
2. `EvmHost` on `Evm(1)` currently holds only `400` fee-token units of revenue (e.g., other relayers have already withdrawn the rest, or fee accrual on that chain lags overall accrual).
3. Relayer calls `withdraw_fees` with `dest_chain = Evm(1)`; `pallet-relayer::withdraw` reads `available_amount = 1000`, dispatches the POST, and immediately sets `Fees[Evm(1)][relayer] = 0` ( [8](#0-7) ).
4. When the POST is delivered to `Evm(1)`'s `HostManager`/`EvmHost.withdraw`, `IERC20.safeTransfer(beneficiary, 1000)` reverts because the host only holds `400` (mirrors `test_host_manager_insufficient_balance` [5](#0-4) ).
5. The relayer's `Fees` balance on Hyperbridge is already `0`; there is no code path that restores any of the `1000` units or re-queues a reduced, pro-rated withdrawal, so the relayer's accrued fee is unrecoverable through the normal withdrawal flow.
