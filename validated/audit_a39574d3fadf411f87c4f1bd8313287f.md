### Title
Lagging accepted oracle leg inflates collateral valuation through midpoint blending - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs)

### Summary
A dual-source oracle accepts two independent observations whenever each is within its configured staleness window and their prices remain inside the configured tolerance ratio. The aggregator then returns their arithmetic midpoint rather than preferring the newer observation. An attacker can use `borrow` while one leg still serves an old, higher price to obtain credit against an inflated collateral valuation.

### Finding Description
`engine::blend` combines a primary and anchor observation with `midpoint_price_or_zero` and rejects the result only when the legs violate the configured tolerance ratio or a leg is stale under its own configured `max_stale_seconds`.

The age-spread check is incomplete: it applies only when both legs have `FeedNature::Market`. If the lagging leg is configured as another feed nature, a large timestamp difference does not mark the result stale, even though the older observation may no longer represent the market price.

The affected sequence is:

1. Configure the collateral asset with a fresh market leg and a slower accepted anchor leg.
2. The market price falls, while the anchor continues to serve an old higher price that remains inside its configured staleness limit.
3. `borrow(caller, account_id, borrows, to)` fetches the midpoint price through `Context::load_markets`.
4. `calculate_account_risk_totals` values collateral with the inflated midpoint.
5. `require_post_pool_risk_gates` compares debt against the inflated LTV-weighted collateral and permits a borrow that would fail under the current market observation.

The existing harness test `audit_borrow_withdraw_liquidate_stale_anchor_blends_5pct_skew_into_ltv` demonstrates the condition: a 23-hour-old anchor at `$1.00` is combined with a fresh `$0.91` market price under a 10% tolerance, producing approximately `$0.955`. A `70,000 USDC` borrow succeeds with the lagging anchor but fails with `INSUFFICIENT_COLLATERAL` when the anchor is fresh.

### Impact Explanation
Borrowers can extract more debt than their collateral supports under the current market valuation. If the lagging leg subsequently updates to the lower market price, the account's collateral value drops while the minted debt remains. Depending on the asset's configured LTV and market depth, this can leave bad debt for suppliers after liquidation and collateral seizure.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires an oracle source to remain within its configured freshness bound while materially lagging the other source. An unprivileged borrower does not need oracle authority: they only need to call `borrow` during the lag window. Feeds with long accepted update intervals or different operational cadences create this condition naturally.

### Recommendation
Apply a cross-source timestamp-spread bound to every two-source configuration, not only pairs where both sources are labeled `Market`. Alternatively, when the leg timestamps diverge materially, fail closed or select a conservative collateral valuation instead of averaging an old anchor with a fresh market price. The limit should be much tighter than either leg's individual `max_stale_seconds` for market-priced collateral.

### Proof of Concept
Use the scenario encoded in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_borrow_withdraw_liquidate_stale_anchor_blend.rs`:

- Fresh primary XLM price: `$0.91`
- Accepted lagging anchor XLM price: `$1.00`
- Anchor age: `82,800` seconds
- Anchor configured maximum: `86,400` seconds
- Oracle tolerance: `1000` bps
- Resulting accepted midpoint: approximately `$0.955`
- Collateral: `100,000 XLM`
- Liquidity supplied by another account: `500,000 USDC`
- Requested borrow: `70,000 USDC`

With the lagging anchor, the call path is:

```text
supply(attacker_account, XLM, 100_000)
borrow(attacker, attacker_account_id,
       [HubPayment { hub_asset: USDC, amount: 70_000 }],
       Some(attacker))
```

The borrow succeeds because the accepted midpoint overstates current collateral value. In the control case where the anchor reports the fresh `$0.91` price, the same `borrow` request reverts with `INSUFFICIENT_COLLATERAL`.
