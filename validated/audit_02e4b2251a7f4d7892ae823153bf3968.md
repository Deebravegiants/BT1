The M-04 class is: post-trade accounting trusts a **declared** amount instead of measuring the **actual** balance change, so an unfunded or under-funded "payment" is treated as real and the excess is refunded/spendable out of protocol custody. Mapping this onto XOXNO Lending, the strongest match is in the pool's repay path: `ops::repay::apply` computes the overpayment refund from the declared inbound amount and pays it out of real token custody without verifying that the declared amount was actually received.

### Title
Pool `repay` refunds the declared amount above debt out of custody without verifying the payment was received - (contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs)

### Summary
`repay` treats the caller-declared leg amount as delivered cash. When the actual debt retired (`net_repay`) is smaller than the declared amount — in the extreme, a market with zero debt where `current_debt_ceil == 0` — the whole declared amount is classified as "overpayment" and returned to `payer` via `transfer_out` directly from pool custody. Because the refund is derived from the declared figure rather than from a measured balance delta, an unfunded repay mints a refund out of suppliers' cash — the same shape as M-04, where the check accounted for the wrong quantity (declared ETH spend rather than actual WETH balance change).

### Finding Description
In `pool/src/ops/repay.rs`, `resolve_repay` computes `net_repay = min(declared, current_debt_ceil)`; `ops::repay::apply` then refunds `declared - net_repay` to `payer` with `transfer_out`. The pool's own regression test pins the hole explicitly:

- `contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3590` `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody`: "Nothing transferred in, no debt to retire: the whole amount is 'excess' … the whole amount is refunded out of real custody with the book untouched." The test calls `client().repay(&payer, &ract(0, custody_before))` with `borrowed == 0`, receives `actual_amount == 0`, and `assert_unfunded_refund_drained_custody` confirms the pool paid `custody_before` out of its token balance.
- The same test notes `ops::recapitalize::apply` shares the identical gap: excess over the shortfall is refunded from custody against a declared, not measured, inbound amount.

The `RepayRoundsToZeroShares` assertion in `ops::repay::accounting` is satisfied on its `net_repay == 0` disjunct, so a fully-refunded leg passes all internal accounting checks. The controller-side strategy path (`repay_debt_from_controller`, `apply_liquidation_repayments`) prefunds the pool via `transfer_amount_measured` before calling `apply_repay_batch`, so the declared amount happens to be backed there — but the pool op itself performs no measurement and trusts whatever amount the action declares. Any entrypoint reaching `repay`/`recapitalize` with a payer-controlled declared amount that is not prefunded (a direct `repay` on the pool, or `recapitalize`) converts declared-but-undelivered value into a real payout.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds: the refund is a `transfer_out` of pool custody that is never backed by an inbound transfer and never debits `cash` or passes `require_reserves`. On a market with no outstanding debt, a single call declaring `amount = pool_balance` pays the entire reserve balance to the attacker while the books record `actual_amount = 0`. On markets with debt, any declared amount above the realized repayment refunds the difference unfunded. This drains supplier backing and can render the market insolvent (withdrawals under-collateralized), i.e. direct theft plus protocol insolvency.

### Likelihood Explanation
The pool unit test demonstrates the drain works end-to-end in one call on a zero-debt market. Whether it is reachable in production depends on the pool entrypoint's caller gate: the strategy/liquidation paths always prefund via measured transfers, so exploitation requires a path where `repay` or `recapitalize` is invoked with a declared amount without a preceding measured pull — most directly `pool.repay(payer = attacker, …)` or `pool.recapitalize` if those are not restricted to the controller. The test invokes `repay` directly through the pool client, indicating the op itself imposes no prefunding precondition. Any token holder (fully unprivileged) can attempt it; no oracle manipulation, flash capital, or privileged role is needed.

### Recommendation
Measure the inbound payment before computing the refund: snapshot `token.balance(pool)` before the pull/prefunding and compute `received = balance_after - balance_before`, then refund `received - net_repay` rather than `declared - net_repay`. Apply the same change to `ops::recapitalize::apply` (refund `received - applied_shortfall`). Alternatively, require `cash`/`require_reserves` to debit the refunded amount and assert `balance >= cash + borrowed` post-op so an unfunded refund cannot leave custody.

### Proof of Concept
1. A USDC market exists with `cash = B > 0` and `borrowed = 0` (all prior debt repaid, or a market where suppliers provided liquidity that was never borrowed and sits in custody).
2. Attacker calls `pool.repay(payer = attacker, actions = [(hub_asset(USDC), B)])` without transferring any tokens.
3. `resolve_repay` sees `current_debt_ceil = 0`, takes the full-close branch, `net_repay = 0`, and `RepayRoundsToZeroShares` passes on the `net_repay == 0` arm.
4. `ops::repay::apply` computes `overpayment = B - 0 = B` and executes `transfer_out(attacker, B)` — the full declared amount paid from real custody, as pinned by `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody`.
5. Result: attacker receives `B` tokens for zero input; `cash`/`borrowed` books are untouched, so suppliers' shares are now backed by less custody than the index claims — theft and insolvency in one call.
