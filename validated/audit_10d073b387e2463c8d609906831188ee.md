### Title
SimplexPaymaster's Chainlink price oracles are not guarded against Arbitrum sequencer downtime, allowing stale-price gas payment exploitation and paymaster fund drainage - (File: evm/src/utils/SimplexPaymaster.sol)

### Summary
`SimplexPaymaster` prices ERC-20 gas payments using Chainlink `AggregatorV3Interface` feeds for both the native asset and each registered token, with only a `maxOracleAge` staleness bound checked against `updatedAt` [1](#0-0) . The contract is deployed and operated on Arbitrum mainnet as one of the paymaster's supported chains, confirmed by the dedicated `ARBITRUM_SEQUENCER` RPC override used for BEEFY submissions on that chain [2](#0-1) . Nowhere in the oracle price path does the contract consult Chainlink's L2 Sequencer Uptime Feed to detect Arbitrum sequencer downtime.

### Finding Description
`_getOraclePrice` (referenced from `swapAndDeposit` and the gas-pricing path) pulls `latestRoundData()` and enforces only a maximum age via `maxOracleAge`, reverting with `StaleOraclePrice` if the feed hasn't updated recently enough, and `InvalidOraclePrice` for non-positive answers [3](#0-2) . This freshness check is time-based only; it cannot distinguish between "feed is fresh because the market is healthy" and "feed is fresh because the sequencer just resumed after an outage and pushed a burst of updates that happen to satisfy `maxOracleAge`, while the underlying market moved significantly during the downtime window." During an Arbitrum sequencer outage, transactions (including price-affecting UserOps or governance updates) cannot land normally; when the sequencer comes back online, a backlog of price data and pending UserOps get processed against a market that has since diverged, exactly the class of issue Chainlink's own L2 documentation warns about and recommends guarding via the Sequencer Uptime Feed.

The `Params` struct that governs `maxOracleAge`, `nativeOracle`, and per-token `tokenOracle` feeds is set purely through governance (`UpdateParams`/`RegisterToken`) with no sequencer-liveness gate baked into the paymaster's pricing logic itself [4](#0-3) . Because the fee-recycling swap (`swapAndDeposit`) and the gas-cost quoting relative to native gas (`_validatePaymasterUserOp` → `getTokenPrice`) both derive their acceptable price bounds solely from `_getOraclePrice`, any stale-during-outage price is treated as trustworthy the moment it satisfies the timestamp check, with no independent circuit breaker for sequencer health.

### Impact Explanation
An unprivileged UserOp sender (bundler-submitted, reachable by anyone building a UserOp against this paymaster) can exploit sequencer downtime windows to get favorable ERC-20-for-gas pricing, effectively underpaying for gas relative to real market rates, or overpaying tokens can be swept by `swapAndDeposit` at unfavorable slippage-derived minimums that no longer reflect the true market. Because the paymaster holds pooled EntryPoint stake/deposit and accumulated stablecoin surplus, systematic mispricing directly drains treasury value — this is a concrete theft-of-funds vector reachable by any bundler-relayed UserOp, satisfying the "concrete theft" bar for a Medium-severity finding.

### Likelihood Explanation
Arbitrum sequencer outages, while infrequent, are a documented and recurring operational risk (the network has experienced multi-hour outages historically). The paymaster is confirmed to run on Arbitrum mainnet [5](#0-4) , and any Chainlink feed serving this contract on that chain is subject to the same sequencer-dependency issue as the originally reported Perennial oracle. The attack requires no privileged access — just submitting a UserOp (or governance-authorized swap trigger, but the price-consuming path is reachable by ordinary UserOps) during/after a known outage window, making this a low-effort, opportunistic exploit once an outage is observed.

### Recommendation
Integrate Chainlink's L2 Sequencer Uptime Feed (`0xFdB631F5EE196F0ed6FAa767959853A9F217697` on Arbitrum) into `_getOraclePrice`: check `answer == 0` (sequencer up) and enforce a grace period after `startedAt` before trusting any price feed read on Arbitrum. Revert (or fail-safe reject) all Chainlink price reads while the sequencer is reported down or within the grace period following recovery.

### Proof of Concept
1. Arbitrum sequencer goes offline for an extended period; Chainlink feeds referenced by `nativeOracle`/`tokenOracle` stop updating on-chain, but their last `updatedAt` remains within `maxOracleAge` because the paymaster's staleness window (e.g., hours per the config docs) is wider than the outage.
2. During this window, off-chain market prices diverge materially from the last on-chain round.
3. The sequencer resumes; the first batch of UserOps for `SimplexPaymaster` are processed against the stale-but-still-"fresh-enough" round (per `_getOraclePrice`'s only `updatedAt`-based check, as exercised in `testStaleOracleReverts`/`testNonPositiveOraclePriceReverts` at [3](#0-2) ), letting a UserOp sender pay less token value than the true market gas cost, or letting `swapAndDeposit` execute at a stale-derived `amountOutMin` that undervalues the swap, both draining value from the paymaster's treasury.
