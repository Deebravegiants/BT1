### Title
Webhook shop-domain spoofing due to HMAC not covering the shop identity field - (File: lib/shopify_api/webhooks/request.rb)

### Summary
`ShopifyAPI::Webhooks::Request` computes its HMAC-signable string from the raw body only, excluding the `X-Shopify-Shop-Domain` header. `ShopifyAPI::Webhooks::Registry.process` validates that HMAC and then trusts `request.shop` (derived from the header) as the tenant identity passed to the app's webhook handler, without that header ever being bound to the signature.

### Finding Description
`Request#to_signable_string` returns `@raw_body` only [1](#0-0) . `Request#shop` is read from the `shopify-shop-domain`/`x-shopify-shop-domain` header with no cryptographic binding to the body or the HMAC [3](#0-2) . `HmacValidator.validate` computes `OpenSSL::HMAC.hexdigest` over `verifiable_query.to_signable_string` (i.e., the body) and compares it against the `hmac-sha256` header, using the app's single, shop-independent `Context.api_secret_key` [5](#0-4) . `Registry.process` calls this validator and, if it passes, immediately hands `request.shop` to the registered handler as the authoritative tenant identifier [4](#0-3) .

Because the app's webhook HMAC secret (`api_secret_key`/`client_secret`) is the same for every shop that installs the app, and the `shop-domain` header is never part of the signed content, the equality that should hold — "the shop the HMAC authenticates" == "the shop the handler believes it received data from" — is broken. Any party who can obtain one validly-signed webhook body (e.g., by installing the app themselves and receiving webhooks for their own shop, or a body they legitimately captured) can replay that exact raw body to the app's webhook endpoint with an arbitrary `X-Shopify-Shop-Domain` header value. `HmacValidator.validate` still succeeds because it only checks the untouched body bytes, so `Registry.process` proceeds and calls the handler with `WebhookMetadata.new(..., shop: request.shop, ...)` where `shop` is the attacker-chosen value [6](#0-5) .

This is the same bug class as the report: a field (the token/asset identity in the report; here the `shop` tenant identity) that is acted upon by privileged logic without being covered by the cryptographic check that is supposed to establish authenticity (the Aave reward-token check vs. the webhook HMAC).

### Impact Explanation
This is a cross-tenant identity confusion: a host application relying on `WebhookMetadata#shop` from this gem to key per-shop side effects (writing/updating data, revoking access, marking uninstalls, updating billing state, etc., keyed by shop domain) can be made to apply another shop's data/action under an attacker-controlled shop identity, or vice versa. Since any app installer possesses a validly HMAC-signed body for their own shop, they can forge the apparent origin of that webhook to any other shop known to use the app — a cross-tenant boundary violation using only the credential the attacker is legitimately entitled to (their own shop's webhook traffic to the shared app secret).

### Likelihood Explanation
Any user who installs the target app can receive genuine, validly-signed webhooks for their own shop and then replay the exact body with a modified `shop-domain` header to the app's public webhook endpoint. No access to `api_secret_key`, access tokens, or any other privileged credential is required beyond normal app installation, which is the standard unprivileged-internet-user posture for this class of report.

### Recommendation
Bind the `shop` (and ideally `topic`, `api_version`, `webhook_id`) into the HMAC-verified signable content, or otherwise cryptographically bind the shop-domain header to the signed payload before trusting `request.shop`. At minimum, document/require host applications to independently validate that the shop in the webhook matches a shop with an active, known installation/session before acting on `WebhookMetadata#shop`, and consider exposing an API that verifies shop-domain consistency as part of `HmacValidator.validate`/`Registry.process` rather than leaving verification solely to the raw body.

### Proof of Concept
1. Install the target Shopify app on shop `attacker.myshopify.com`; capture a genuine incoming webhook request, e.g. body `{"id":1}}` with headers including `X-Shopify-Hmac-Sha256: <valid-hmac-for-body>` and `X-Shopify-Shop-Domain: attacker.myshopify.com`.
2. Replay the identical raw body and HMAC header to the app's webhook endpoint, but change `X-Shopify-Shop-Domain` to `victim.myshopify.com`.
3. `Utils::HmacValidator.validate(request)` in `lib/shopify_api/utils/hmac_validator.rb#L12-L31` recomputes the HMAC over the (unchanged) raw body only and succeeds, because the shop header was never part of the signed content.
4. `Registry.process` in `lib/shopify_api/webhooks/registry.rb#L188-L200` proceeds and calls the registered handler with `WebhookMetadata.new(..., shop: "victim.myshopify.com", ...)`, even though the payload actually originated from, and was signed for, `attacker.myshopify.com`.
