### Title
Sustained high-utilization whale market overflows `borrowed * borrow_index` inside accrual, permanently freezing all pool operations — (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
Analog of CVE-2017-11555 (crash/illegal-access → remote DoS): the interest-accrual step computes total debt as `borrowed.mul(env, new_borrow_index)` in `calculate_supplier_rewards` (common/src/rates/index.rs:80-81). `Ray::mul` widens the product to `I256` but converts the result back to `i128` and panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` when `borrowed * new_borrow_index / RAY` exceeds `i128::MAX`. `accrue_step` (common/src/rates/simulate.rs:68-69) calls this on every mutating pool verb via `interest::global_sync` (contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33), so once scaled debt value crosses the `i128::MAX` ceiling, every subsequent call — `update_indexes`, `supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `claim_revenue`, `flash_loan`, router strategies — reverts. The index cap `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` is supposed to bound growth but does not engage in time, because the ceiling that is hit first is the *value* ceiling (`scaled_debt × index`), not the index cap.

### Finding Description
`update_borrow_index` caps the borrow index at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY = 1e36` (common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19), which keeps `index × factor` inside `i128` with headroom (verified by `test_borrow_index_at_the_ceiling_multiplies_without_overflow`). But `calculate_supplier_rewards` multiplies the *scaled borrow shares* by the index before the cap can protect anything: for a market with scaled debt near `1e36` RAY-units (≈1 billion whole tokens at 18 decimals), an index of only ~170× makes `borrowed × index / RAY ≈ 1.7e38 = i128::MAX`. `accrue_step` computes `new_borrow_index` and immediately calls `calculate_supplier_rewards`, so `new_total_debt` overflows, `Ray::mul`/`to_i128` returns `None`, and the code panics with `MathOverflow`. Because `global_sync` runs at the top of every entrypoint and never commits partial state, the panic is permanent and unrecoverable — no amount of repayment or liquidation can execute, since they all accrue first.

The in-repo integration test documents exactly this cliff: `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:315-356) supplies `1e27` raw units of an 18-decimal token, borrows 98%, advances time year-by-year, and observes `try_update_indexes_for` fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`; subsequent `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` also fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW`.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of all user funds in the affected market: suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot liquidate, and protocol revenue cannot be claimed. Every permissionless verb on that (hub, token) book reverts because accrual precedes all of them and the overflow site is inside `accrue_step` itself. There is no rescue path — `clean_bad_debt`, `recapitalize`, and even governance `execute` on pool ops all go through `global_sync` or the same math.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable entirely by unprivileged actions: an attacker (or organic whale flow) only needs to `supply` a very large position and `borrow` at sustained high utilization on a market whose listed caps admit such size, then wait for compounding to push the index past ~170× while debt value stays above the `i128` ceiling. No privileged action is required at trigger time — `update_indexes` is a permissionless entrypoint. The requirement is a large listed market (caps permitting ~1e9×10^decimals positions) and years of high utilization on a steep curve segment, so this is a latent, time-dependent liveness failure rather than an instant exploit; severity is bounded accordingly but the failure mode is absolute once reached.

### Recommendation
Make accrual saturate rather than panic at the value ceiling: compute `new_total_debt` via a saturating mul (e.g., `mul_div_floor_saturating` semantics on `borrowed × index`), or clamp `new_borrow_index` to `min(computed, i128::MAX * RAY / borrowed)` before calling `calculate_supplier_rewards`, so debt valuation caps out and indexes can still advance to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. Alternatively, bound scaled debt at borrow time so `borrowed × MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY / RAY` always fits `i128` (a global debt-value cap derived from `i128::MAX / MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`), and enforce it in `calculate_scaled_borrow`/cap validation rather than relying on per-market cap parameters.

### Proof of Concept
Reproduced by the existing harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321):

1. List/enable an 18-decimal market on the XLM curve with caps lifted to admit a `1e9 × 10^18` supply.
2. `supply(BOB, BIG18, 1e27)`; `borrow(ALICE, BIG18, 0.98e27)` against COL collateral — both unprivileged calls.
3. Advance ledger time in 1-year steps, calling permissionless `update_indexes(BIG18)` each year.
4. After a few years at ~98% utilization, `update_indexes` reverts with `GenericError::MathOverflow` while `borrow_index` is still below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (asserted at lines 343-353).
5. `withdraw(BOB, BIG18, 1)` and `repay(ALICE, BIG18, 1.0)` both revert with the same `MATH_OVERFLOW` (lines 355-356) — the market is permanently frozen.
