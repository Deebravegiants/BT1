### Title
Supply cap fails open via saturating scaled-cap conversion once the supply index drops below RAY - ([File: contracts/controller/src/spoke_usage.rs])

### Summary
`enforce_spoke_cap` converts the configured asset-unit `supply_cap` into the scaled share domain with `cap_scaled = floor_saturating(cap × 10^(27−d) × RAY / supply_index)` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs::calculate_scaled_cap`). The conversion **saturates at `i128::MAX` instead of reverting**, so when `supply_index < RAY` the scaled cap can saturate to `i128::MAX` and the check `next_scaled <= cap_scaled` at `contracts/controller/src/spoke_usage.rs:153-155` passes for any supply amount. The supply index is driven below RAY by bad-debt write-downs (`seize_positions` borrow-side entries socialize losses onto the supply index, floored at `RAY / 1000`), which an unprivileged caller triggers through `clean_bad_debt`. This mirrors CVE-2016-0780: an improperly enforced quota fails open and lets an unprivileged user exceed a resource limit meant to bound exposure.

### Finding Description
- `enforce_spoke_cap` (entry check for `supply`, `flash_position` collateral pushes, `multiply`/`swap_collateral` supply credits) computes `cap_scaled` via `calculate_scaled_cap` and reverts with `SpokeSupplyCapReached` only if `usage + new_shares > cap_scaled` (`contracts/controller/src/spoke_usage.rs:144-156`).
- `calculate_scaled_cap` is `floor_saturating`: `cap × 10^(27−d) × RAY / index` clamped at `i128::MAX` (documented in `docs/reference/invariants.md:513-519` and `skills/xoxno-lending/math.md:437-446` — "the scaled cap can fail open").
- At the maximum admitted cap `i128::MAX / 10^(27−d)` (`max_cap_for_decimals`), `cap_scaled` saturates whenever `index < RAY`. For smaller caps it saturates once `index < RAY × cap × 10^(27−d) / i128::MAX`, i.e., proportionally deeper below RAY.
- The supply index falls below RAY only through bad-debt write-downs in `seize_positions` (borrow-side cleanup path), reachable via the permissionless `clean_bad_debt` flow after positions go underwater; the index floor is `RAY / 1000`, where even modest caps saturate (e.g., cap ≥ ~1.7e15 base units at 7 decimals).
- Result: a spoke whose market suffered bad debt loses its supply-cap enforcement entirely. An unprivileged `supply` can then deposit arbitrarily more than `supply_cap` of that asset into the spoke.

### Impact Explanation
The supply cap is the protocol's per-spoke exposure limit; it exists so a spoke cannot concentrate unlimited collateral in one asset/hub book sharing a single physical pool balance (`docs/reference/architecture.md:61-65`). With the cap failed open, an attacker can grow a position far beyond the configured ceiling in a distressed market — precisely when the protocol most needs the limit — and that enlarged position becomes borrowable collateral (subject only to LTV/liquidity checks). A second adverse move on the same asset then creates bad debt far exceeding what governance budgeted via the cap, contributing to protocol insolvency borne by other suppliers through further supply-index write-downs. Indirectly the cheap-share entry at a depressed index also dilutes the recovery available to pre-existing suppliers and recapitalizers.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires `supply_index < RAY`, which only occurs after a bad-debt event — i.e., an already-stressed market. The unprivileged trigger (`clean_bad_debt`) is permissionless, and once the condition holds every subsequent `supply` on that spoke is uncapped with zero cost beyond the supplied tokens. No admin key, upgrade, or oracle manipulation is needed for the bypass itself, but reaching the precondition depends on real market losses, so likelihood is conditional rather than arbitrary.

### Recommendation
Make `calculate_scaled_cap` revert (e.g., `GenericError::MathOverflow` or a dedicated `SpokeError`) when the scaled conversion exceeds `i128::MAX`, rather than saturating. Alternatively, enforce the cap in the unscaled token domain by converting `usage` to tokens at the current index and comparing `usage_tokens + new_tokens <= cap`, which is immune to index-level saturation.

### Proof of Concept
1. Admin configures a spoke asset with the maximum admitted `supply_cap` (`i128::MAX / 10^(27−d)`), or any cap whose `cap × 10^(27−d)` product exceeds `i128::MAX × index / RAY` at the post-write-down index.
2. Market accrues bad debt (e.g., collateral price drop leaves positions undercollateralized past the dust threshold).
3. Any address calls `clean_bad_debt`; `seize_positions` writes the debt down onto the supply index, pushing `supply_index` below `RAY` (floor `RAY/1000` per `docs/reference/invariants.md:515-517`).
4. Attacker calls `supply` with an amount exceeding `supply_cap` in asset units. `apply_entry` → `enforce_spoke_cap` computes `cap_scaled = i128::MAX` (saturated), `next_scaled <= cap_scaled` trivially holds, and the deposit succeeds, minting shares at the depressed index.
5. Repeat arbitrarily: usage grows without bound and the spoke's exposure limit is permanently unenforced until the index recovers above RAY through accrual.

Note: the saturation behavior is disclosed in `docs/reference/invariants.md` and `skills/xoxno-lending/math.md` as a known property of the conversion; if this is considered a documented/accepted design limitation under the scan rules, this finding should be downgraded accordingly.
