### Title
Account spending authority is checked against the stale creation-time `Account.owner`, not the live position-NFT owner, so a transferred account's previous owner keeps full borrow/withdraw control - (File: contracts/controller/src/account.rs)

### Summary
The controller maintains two notions of ownership. `require_account_owner` resolves the *live* owner by reading the position NFT (`storage::account_owner`), but `require_owner_or_delegate` — the gate that protects `borrow`, `withdraw`, `multiply`, `flash_position`, `swap_debt`, `swap_collateral`, `repay_debt_with_collateral`, `migrate_from_blend`, and the `Migrate`/`Multiply` guards in `load_or_create_account` — compares `caller` against `account.owner`, the address persisted in the `Account` struct at NFT mint time (`create_account` in `account.rs`). The position NFT's `transfer`/`transfer_from` are stock OpenZeppelin methods on a separate contract; they cannot write back to controller storage, so `account.owner` is never resynchronized after a transfer. This is the same defect class as the Kirby advisory: an integral ownership-sensitive operation is authorized by a stale/secondary permission source instead of the authoritative one.

### Finding Description
`create_account` stores `owner` in the `Account` struct and writes `AccountMeta` once at mint time; nothing in `account.rs` or the NFT contract updates `account.owner` later. `require_owner_or_delegate` returns success whenever `caller == owner` (the stored field), and the delegate lookup is keyed under that same stored owner (`storage::get_delegates(env, account_id, owner)`). After `position-nft::transfer` moves the NFT to a new holder:

- The **previous** owner still satisfies `caller == account.owner` and can call `process_borrow` (`positions/debt.rs`, `require_owner_or_delegate` at the top) and `process_withdraw` (`positions/supply.rs`) against the account, sending proceeds to an arbitrary `to` address via `require_external_recipient`.
- Any delegates the previous owner granted remain active (they are keyed under the stored owner, which is unchanged).
- The **new** NFT owner is *not* `account.owner` and holds no delegates under that key, so they cannot pass `require_owner_or_delegate` for `borrow`/`withdraw`/strategies; only the `require_account_owner` paths (`renew_account`, `add_delegate`, `remove_delegate`) recognize them, since that helper reads `storage::account_owner` (the NFT) rather than the stored field.

The divergence between the two ownership sources — used for `renew_account`/`add_delegate`/`remove_delegate` in `account.rs` but not for the fund-moving entrypoints — is exactly the missing-authorization shape: avatar-equivalent state (account control) is gated by `files.*`-equivalent (stale stored field) instead of `user.update`-equivalent (live NFT ownership).

### Impact Explanation
An address that sells or transfers its account NFT retains full spending authority: it can `withdraw` all collateral and `borrow` to the account's LTV limit to an arbitrary recipient, draining the position the buyer just paid for. This is theft of user funds. Symmetrically, the legitimate new owner is locked out of every value-moving entrypoint (temporary/permanent freezing of funds until the position is emptied or liquidated). No privileged role, oracle manipulation, or timing assumption is needed — the only requirement is that the NFT changed hands after `account.owner` was written.

### Likelihood Explanation
Position-NFT transfers are an explicitly supported, permissionless surface (`position-nft::transfer`, `transfer_from`, `approve` are stock OZ methods classified `caller-auth` in `scripts/permissionless_entrypoints.txt`, with the justification that moving the NFT "moves the whole account"). Any secondary-market sale or wallet migration of an account NFT triggers the condition. The attacker needs only to have once owned the account — the stored `owner` never expires.

Caveat: I could not fully trace `storage::get_account`/`storage::account_owner`; if the loader silently overwrites `account.owner` from the NFT on every read, this finding collapses. However, the existence of two separate ownership checks (`require_account_owner` reading the NFT vs. `require_owner_or_delegate` trusting the struct field) and delegate keys persisted under the stored owner strongly suggest the field is stale after transfer.

### Recommendation
Make `require_owner_or_delegate` (and the delegate storage key) resolve ownership from the live position NFT (`storage::account_owner`) rather than the persisted `Account.owner` field, or resynchronize `account.owner` from the NFT on every `storage::get_account` load. Remove or treat `Account.owner` as a cache that can never be authoritative, mirroring how `require_account_owner` already works for `renew_account`/`add_delegate`/`remove_delegate`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice calls `supply(caller=Alice, account_id=0, spoke_id=S, assets=[(USDC, 1000e6)])`; `create_account` mints NFT id `N` and stores `Account { owner: Alice, .. }`.
2. Alice calls `position-nft::transfer(from=Alice, to=Bob, token_id=N)` — a stock caller-auth method; the controller is not invoked.
3. Alice calls `withdraw(caller=Alice, account_id=N, withdrawals=[(USDC, 0)], to=Some(Alice))`. `process_withdraw` calls `require_owner_or_delegate(N, Alice, account.owner)`; `account.owner` is still `Alice`, so `caller == owner` passes, the withdraw-all sentinel empties the position, and funds go to Alice.
4. Bob, the NFT owner of record, calls `withdraw(caller=Bob, ...)`; `Bob != account.owner` (Alice) and Bob holds no delegate grant under key `(N, Alice)`, so it panics with `NotAuthorized`. Bob owns the NFT but cannot touch the collateral; Alice owns nothing yet controls it.
