### Title
Supplier withdrawal can raise utilization and capture inflated borrower interest - (File: `contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs`)

### Summary
A depositor that controls all or most of a market’s supply can withdraw idle liquidity, increase `borrowed / supplied`, and thereby increase the interest charged to existing borrowers. A normal withdrawal burns the depositor’s supply shares before enforcing only the post-state `max_utilization` bound, so the operation can be calibrated to raise utilization exactly to the configured cap. If the depositor is the sole remaining supplier, they still receive all supplier rewards despite having removed most of their principal, allowing them to earn more total interest after withdrawing than before.

### Finding Description
`Controller::withdraw` is reachable by an account owner or delegate with caller authorization and arbitrary `(HubAssetKey, amount)` withdrawal legs. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

The pool withdrawal resolves the requested shares, calls `burn_supply`, and only then checks reserves and `require_utilization_below_max`; liquidation withdrawals are the only paths that skip that utilization check. [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

Market utilization is the indexed debt divided by indexed total supply, so reducing `supplied` while debt remains constant directly raises utilization. [5](#0-4)  The borrow rate is a three-segment monotonically increasing piecewise-linear function of utilization. [6](#0-5) 

Every accrual step recomputes utilization from the current state, derives the borrow rate, grows the borrow index, and splits the resulting interest between suppliers and the protocol. [7](#0-6)  Supplier rewards are distributed over the remaining supply through the supply index. [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8) 

In a low-liquidity market where the attacker owns all supply, burning part of their position leaves them as the owner of 100% of the remaining supply. Their share of supplier rewards therefore stays at 100%, while the higher utilization increases total borrower interest; they also hold the withdrawn tokens outside the pool. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10) 

A later dust `supply` is sufficient for the attacker to force an accrual because the pool supply path accrues interest before minting the new shares. [12](#0-11) 

### Impact Explanation
Existing borrowers pay the manipulated higher borrow index for the entire elapsed interval, while the attacker captures the supplier portion through their remaining shares. [13](#0-12)  The excess is economic value transferred from borrowers to the manipulating supplier and protocol revenue rather than compensation for additional capital supplied. [14](#0-13) 

The same action can also drive utilization to `max_utilization`. Because the post-withdrawal check permits the state to reach the cap, subsequent interest-driven utilization growth can leave no withdrawal headroom until borrowers repay or liquidations occur. [15](#0-14) 

The impact is bounded by outstanding debt, elapsed time, the configured curve, reserve factor, and `max_utilization`, but it requires no privileged role or external failure. It therefore constitutes theft of user funds through manipulated variable-rate interest, with a secondary temporary withdrawal-freeze effect at the cap.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack needs only an authorized lending account and enough capital to be the dominant supplier in a newly created or thin market. The attacker can execute it through `supply`, wait for victims to borrow, and then call `withdraw` with an amount calculated to leave utilization just below `max_utilization`. [16](#0-15) [1](#0-0) 

The sole-supplier case is deterministic because the attacker retains all remaining supply shares; with competing suppliers, profitability depends on the attacker’s retained share, curve slopes, reserve factor, and achievable utilization increase. Withdrawn liquidity is limited to available cash, and `require_supply_for_debt` prevents removing the last supply while debt exists. [17](#0-16) [18](#0-17) 

Likelihood is Medium: no oracle manipulation, privileged role, reentrancy, or leaked key is required, but the attack is most profitable when the attacker controls nearly all supply and the market has meaningful outstanding debt.

### Recommendation
- Do not evaluate a withdrawal solely against a static post-state utilization cap. Apply a lower withdrawal-specific utilization threshold or dynamic liquidity reserve so a supplier cannot intentionally leave the market at the cap.
- Introduce a rate-change limiter or time-weighted utilization for interest accrual, preventing a single withdrawal from instantly repricing all existing debt for the full elapsed interval.
- Model supplier profitability explicitly when configuring curves. A concave curve reduces the benefit for partial suppliers, but it does not eliminate the sole-supplier case because a monopoly supplier continues to receive all supplier rewards after reducing supply.
- Consider withdrawal cooldowns, periodic withdrawal limits, or a withdrawal fee accruing to borrowers/suppliers when a withdrawal materially raises utilization.
- Add regression tests for a dominant supplier withdrawing to `max_utilization`, then forcing accrual through a dust supply, and assert that retained-share rewards do not exceed pre-withdrawal rewards by more than an explicitly accepted threshold.

### Proof of Concept
Assume indexes are initially `RAY`, `reserve_factor = 10%`, `max_utilization = 0.9`, `mid_utilization = 0.5`, `optimal_utilization = 0.8`, `base_borrow_rate = 0.005`, `slope1 = 0.025`, `slope2 = 0.05`, `slope3 = 0.10`, and `max_borrow_rate >= 0.13`.

1. The attacker calls `Controller::supply` with `account_id = 0` and supplies `200_000` units of the debt asset, becoming its sole supplier. [19](#0-18) 

2. A victim supplies collateral and calls `Controller::borrow` for `100_000` units, making utilization `100_000 / 200_000 = 0.5`. [20](#0-19) [5](#0-4) 

3. At `u = 0.5`, the annual borrow rate is `0.005 + 0.025 = 0.03`. At `u = 0.9`, it is `0.005 + 0.025 + 0.05 + ((0.9 - 0.8) / 0.2) * 0.10 = 0.13`. [21](#0-20) 

4. The attacker calls `Controller::withdraw` with `withdrawals = [(hub_asset, 88_888.888...)]` and `to = None`. This leaves approximately `111_111.112` supplied, `11_111.112` cash, and `100_000` debt, so utilization is just under the `0.9` cap and the withdrawal passes. [1](#0-0) [22](#0-21) 

5. Before the withdrawal, annual supplier rewards are approximately `100_000 * 0.03 * 90% = 2_700`. Afterward, they are approximately `100_000 * 0.13 * 90% = 11_700`. The attacker still owns all remaining supplier shares, so their annual supplier income rises by about `9_000` despite withdrawing `88_888.889` tokens. [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8) 

6. After time elapses, the attacker supplies one unit through `Controller::supply`; the pool accrues at the manipulated pre-deposit state before minting that dust share, committing the elevated borrow and supply indexes. [12](#0-11)

/// Burns `burned` from market supply and returns the user's remaining scaled position.
fn burn_position(env: &Env, cache: &mut Cache, position: Ray, burned: Ray) -> Ray {
    cache.burn_supply(burned);
    position.checked_sub(env, burned)
}

/// Enforces reserve, utilization, and solvency guards, then debits cash for
/// the net transfer. Liquidations and footprint-only closes skip utilization.
fn gate_and_debit(env: &Env, cache: &mut Cache, net_transfer: i128, skip_utilization_check: bool) {
    cache.require_reserves(net_transfer);

    if !skip_utilization_check {
        guards::require_utilization_below_max(env, cache);
    }
    guards::require_supply_for_debt(env, cache);
    cache.debit_cash(net_transfer);

pub fn update_supply_index(env: &Env, supplied: Ray, old_index: Ray, rewards_increase: Ray) -> Ray {
    if supplied == Ray::ZERO || rewards_increase == Ray::ZERO {
        return old_index;
    }

    let total_supplied_value = supplied.mul(env, old_index);

    if total_supplied_value == Ray::ZERO {
        return old_index;
    }

    let new_value = total_supplied_value.checked_add(env, rewards_increase);
    let grown = fp_core::mul_div_floor_saturating(env, new_value.raw(), RAY, supplied.raw());

    let bounded_old = old_index.raw().min(MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY);
    Ray::from(grown.min(MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY).max(bounded_old))

pub(crate) fn require_utilization_below_max(env: &Env, cache: &Cache) {
    if cache.supplied() == Ray::ZERO || cache.params().max_utilization >= Ray::ONE {
        return;
    }

    let borrowed = cache.borrowed().mul_ceil(env, cache.borrow_index());
    if borrowed == Ray::ZERO {
        return;
    }
    let supplied = cache.supplied().mul_floor(env, cache.supply_index());
    assert_with_error!(
        env,
        supplied > Ray::ZERO && borrowed.div_ceil(env, supplied) <= cache.params().max_utilization,
        CollateralError::UtilizationAboveMax
