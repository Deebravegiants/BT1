Audit Report

## Title
Sender's HRMP deposit is permanently trapped when a pending, unconfirmed `HrmpOpenChannelRequest` is offboarded together with its initiating para - (File: polkadot/runtime/parachains/src/hrmp.rs)

## Summary
When a para calls `hrmp_init_open_channel` and self-deregisters via `paras_registrar::Pallet::do_deregister` before the recipient confirms the channel, `Hrmp::clean_open_channel_requests` skips refunding the sender's deposit because it conditions the refund on the sender not being in the `outgoing` (offboarding) list. Since a deregistered `ParaId` can never again dispatch a `Parachain` origin, the reserved deposit on its sovereign account becomes permanently unrecoverable without privileged intervention.

## Finding Description
`Registrar::do_deregister` calls `schedule_para_cleanup`, which queues the para for offboarding at the next session [5](#0-4) . At the session boundary, `perform_outgoing_para_cleanup` calls `clean_open_channel_requests(config, outgoing)` [6](#0-5) , which purges pending open requests but only refunds the sender's deposit `if !outgoing.contains(&req_id.sender)` [1](#0-0) . If the sender is the offboarding para, the deposit reserved in `init_open_channel` on `origin.into_account_truncating()` [3](#0-2)  is never unreserved, while the storage entries are still correctly removed, keeping storage internally consistent but leaving a genuine accounting orphan. This is asymmetric with the established-channel cleanup path `close_hrmp_channel`, which unconditionally refunds both `sender_deposit` and `recipient_deposit` regardless of offboarding status [7](#0-6) , confirmed by the passing test `refund_deposit_on_offboarding` which restores para_a's balance to 100 [8](#0-7) . The existing test `no_dangling_open_requests`, covering the exact pending-request-plus-deregistration scenario, only asserts para_b's balance recovery and storage consistency, never checking para_a's balance [4](#0-3) , consistent with the sender's deposit being silently lost without any test guarding against it.

## Impact Explanation
The reserved `hrmp_sender_deposit` becomes permanently stuck on the deterministic sovereign account of the deregistered `ParaId`. Since that account cannot originate a `Parachain` XCM origin post-deregistration, and `unreserve` is an internal `Currency` trait operation not exposed as a dispatchable a normal signed account can invoke on itself in this context, recovery requires governance/root-level intervention (e.g., `force_unreserve`). This is a concrete, self-contained loss of the specific deposit amount, not merely theoretical.

## Likelihood Explanation
Fully reachable via unprivileged extrinsics: any para manager can call `hrmp_init_open_channel`, then call the unprivileged `Registrar::deregister` before the recipient's `hrmp_accept_open_channel`, and let the scheduled session elapse. No race condition, validator collusion, or governance action is required, and the behavior is deterministic and repeatable whenever a pending unconfirmed request exists at self-deregistration time.

## Recommendation
In `Hrmp::clean_open_channel_requests`, unconditionally unreserve the sender's own deposit when purging a pending open request, regardless of whether the sender is in the `outgoing` list, matching the unconditional refund behavior of `close_hrmp_channel`. The recipient-side refund check may remain conditioned on the recipient's own offboarding status.

## Proof of Concept
Add a test to `polkadot/runtime/parachains/src/hrmp/tests.rs` extending the existing `no_dangling_open_requests`/`refund_deposit_on_offboarding` patterns: register para_a and para_b with balances, call `Hrmp::init_open_channel(para_a, para_b, ...)`, then `deregister_parachain(para_a)` before para_b confirms, run to the offboarding session, and assert that `free_balance(para_a)` remains at `100 - hrmp_sender_deposit` and `reserved_balance(para_a)` remains at `hrmp_sender_deposit` indefinitely, even though `Hrmp::assert_storage_consistency_exhaustive()` passes, demonstrating the trapped deposit as described in the submission's proof of concept.
