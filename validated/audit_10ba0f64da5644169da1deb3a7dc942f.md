### Title
Equal-weighted "TWAP" mean misprices collateral when Reflector observations are unevenly spaced - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/reflector.rs)

### Summary
`OracleReadMode::Twap` is advertised as a time-weighted average, but `read_twap` computes a plain arithmetic mean over the returned samples (`try_twap_mean_price`). The spacing check only enforces a *minimum* gap of `resolution` between consecutive samples plus total window coverage; it explicitly accepts gapped histories ("A skipped round is normal"). Samples covering long time spans therefore receive the same weight as samples covering a single resolution period, so clustered observations skew the mean — the same defect as the Olympus SMA report.

### Finding Description
In `read_twap` (`contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/reflector.rs:146-203`), the validation loop requires `newer_ts - older_ts >= resolution` per adjacent pair and `newest_ts - oldest_ts >= (records-1) * resolution` overall, but places no upper bound on individual gaps and performs no interval weighting. The result is `sum(prices) / count`. With e.g. `Twap(3)` and a 60 s resolution, a history spaced (60 s, 300 s) and one spaced (300 s, 60 s) are both accepted yet weight the endpoint prices very differently relative to the true time-weighted mean. The doc comment itself concedes this: "Computes an arithmetic mean of the Reflector observations, despite the `Twap` name implying a time-weighted average" (`reflector.rs:142-145`), and the test `test_twap_read_accepts_a_gapped_history_that_still_spans_the_window` codifies acceptance of uneven spacing.

The resolved observation becomes the asset's `price_wad` consumed by controller `Context`-cached strict prices, feeding health factor and `min_borrow_collateral_usd` in `calculate_account_risk_totals`. TWAP mode is the *required* smoothing mechanism for single-source market feeds — `feed_shape`/`smoothing` reject spot-only market legs (`SpotOnlyNotProductionSafe`), so this mean is what stands between a trending market and mispriced collateral.

### Impact Explanation
During a sustained price trend with irregularly spaced Reflector samples (skipped rounds are normal per the code's own comment), the arithmetic mean lags or overshoots the true time-weighted price in the direction of the clustered samples. An upward-skewed mean overstates collateral value: an unprivileged borrower can call controller `borrow` and extract more debt than their collateral's fair value supports, leaving bad debt that `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` later socialize into the supply index — a direct loss to suppliers. A downward-skewed mean enables profitable `liquidate` calls against positions that are actually healthy by time-weighted valuation, transferring the liquidation bonus from users to the liquidator.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation needs (a) an asset priced via a single-source Reflector `Twap` feed, (b) a naturally gapped observation window (missed rounds occur routinely; the code explicitly tolerates them), and (c) a directional trend over the window so that clustering matters. The attacker cannot inject samples, but only needs to *time* an ordinary `borrow`/`liquidate` when the skew favors them — both are unprivileged entrypoints. The deviation magnitude is bounded by the trend strength over the TWAP window and by the sanity band, so impact is moderate rather than unbounded: **Medium**.

### Recommendation
Weight each sample by the time interval it represents — e.g., compute `Σ p_i · (t_i − t_{i+1}) / (t_first − t_last)` over the sorted history — instead of a flat mean in `read_twap`/`try_twap_mean_price`. Alternatively, bound per-gap size (reject histories where any adjacent spacing exceeds `k · resolution`) so equal weighting cannot materially diverge from the time-weighted mean.

### Proof of Concept
Mirror the Olympus test shape against `read_twap` using a mock `ReflectorOracle` (as in `contracts/price-aggregator/tests/oracle/support.rs` `TwapReflector`): configure `Twap(3)` with 60 s resolution and a linearly rising price of +1% per hour. Feed A: timestamps at T, T−60, T−360 (recent cluster). Feed B: T, T−300, T−360 (early cluster). Both pass all spacing and span checks; the arithmetic means differ measurably and both diverge from the true time-weighted mean, demonstrating that the reported price depends on publish cadence rather than elapsed time. A borrower executing `borrow` while feed-A skew holds obtains leverage against an overstated collateral price.
