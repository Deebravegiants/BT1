### Answer

I could not confirm this vulnerability with the evidence available. Tracing the call chain from `collateral-add` (mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar:1020) through `get-assets` (mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar:482-492), the price list and the asset list that `merge-price` (mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar:506-510) zips together via `map` are both derived from the *same* deterministic ordering:

1. `mask-to-list-collateral` produces a fixed-order `iter` list of asset ids from the safe mask. [1](#0-0) 
2. `get-status-multi` → `status-multi` in the assets registry maps over that exact `ids` list (preserving order/length) and embeds each entry's own `id` into the result via `unwrap-status`/`status`. [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 
3. `get-oracle` and `get-asset-id` are subsequently `map`ped over `assets-list`, which by construction preserves the same order as `iter`. [4](#0-3) 
4. `price-multi-resolve` is called with `oracles-list` and `asset-ids`, both index-aligned to `assets-list`; the final `map merge-price assets-list prices-list` therefore pairs each asset with its own oracle-resolved price by position, but that position corresponds one-to-one to the same asset id throughout the pipeline, not an arbitrary/attacker-reorderable position.

I was unable to locate and fully read the implementation of `price-multi-resolve` itself (it is referenced but its defining contract/body was not found within the indexed content), so I cannot rule out with 100% certainty that it internally reorders or drops/duplicates entries relative to its `asset-ids` input. However, every other step in the identified chain (`mask-to-list-collateral` → `status-multi` → `get-oracle`/`get-asset-id` → `merge-price`) preserves strict positional correspondence to asset id, and nothing in `collateral-add`'s reachable path (attacker-controlled `amount`, `ft`, `price-feeds`) allows the attacker to alter that ordering or inject a mismatched-length price list — `write-feeds` only affects which price values are cached for the *same* set of asset ids, not the ordering of `get-assets`'s fold. [5](#0-4) 

Since I cannot demonstrate a concrete divergence in the `assets`/collateral-debt identity through the traced code, and the described "position not by id" concern appears mitigated by consistent index-aligned derivation at every stage that I could verify:

#### No vulnerability found for this question.

Note: due to index coverage limits, the exact body of `price-multi-resolve` was not retrievable; if a background Devin session has full repo access, it would be worth independently confirming that function preserves 1:1 order/length with its `asset-ids` argument to fully close out this question.
