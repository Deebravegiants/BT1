### Title
Bad-debt seizure clamps supply index to floor but leaves stranded supply shares that later drain fresh deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs)

### Summary
The borrow-side seize path in `pool::ops::seize::apply` socializes bad debt by pushing the supply index down to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` and burning the debt shares, but it never burns, freezes, or otherwise reconciles the residual supply shares that survive at the clamped floor. Those shares still unscale to a positive claim, so once any new cash enters the market a wiped-out supplier can withdraw it, consuming a fresh depositor's funds. This is the same bug class as CVE-2022-2097: a routine claims to process a buffer in full but silently leaves a residual tail (unencrypted bytes there; unburned shares here) that later leaks value it should not own.

### Finding Description
`apply` handles `AccountPositionType::Borrow` by computing `bad_debt = unscale_borrow_ceil_ray(position)`, calling `interest::apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(&mut cache, bad_debt)`, then `cache.burn_debt(position)` and committing — with no adjustment to `supplied` and no check on what the floor clamp leaves behind [1](#0-0) .

`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` can only lower the supply index as far as `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (RAY/1000). When the socialized debt exceeds what a full index wipe covers, the index is clamped up at the floor while every scaled supply share remains in `supplied`. The pool's own test `test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` pins exactly this state: after the wipeout, `cache.unscale_supply_floor(alice_scaled)` is still positive ("wiped survivor keeps a stranded claim"), and `cash == 0` only masks it because `require_reserves` fails on an empty market [2](#0-1) .

The same test then shows the leak: a new depositor supplies `alice_stranded`, receiving shares priced at the floor index, and the stranded survivor withdraws `gross == deposit` — "Alice extracts exactly Bob's fresh deposit", leaving the honest depositor's claim unbacked (`cash < bob_claim`) [3](#0-2) .

Nothing in `shares.rs` intervenes: `burn_supply`/`absorb_supply_as_revenue`/`require_revenue_backed` only constrain `revenue <= supplied`; there is no guard that distinguishes residual post-floor shares from live ones [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) .

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds and permanent protocol insolvency in the wiped market. After a large bad-debt cleanup (reachable via the permissionless `liquidate`/`clean_bad_debt` controller paths that emit `PoolSeizeEntry` with `side = Borrow`), any supplier holding residual scaled shares can wait for fresh deposits and withdraw them through the normal `withdraw` → `resolve_withdrawal` → `require_reserves` → `debit_cash` flow. The new depositor's principal is paid to the wiped-out survivor, and the market's books become permanently insolvent (`owed > cash`), as the test asserts.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a bad-debt event large enough that socializing it drives the supply index into the floor clamp — i.e., seized debt exceeding the market's remaining supply value by more than a 1000x factor, which arises in near-empty markets or during the dust-threshold `clean_bad_debt` path where small balances make floor-clamping realistic. Once clamped, exploitation needs only (a) a residual share holder and (b) any subsequent depositor in that market — both unprivileged actions. No timing, oracle, or privilege assumptions are needed.

Caveat: the test name ("...without_supply_guard") suggests a guard may have been contemplated in a higher layer (e.g., a deposit-time check on post-floor markets in `ops` or the controller). I verified `seize.rs` contains no such guard and the residual claim survives `commit`; I was not able to fully audit every deposit entrypoint (`ops::supply`, controller `supply`) for a "market was floor-clamped" check before finalizing. If such a check exists, impact reduces to permanent freezing of the wiped market rather than theft.

### Recommendation
After `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` in `ops::seize::apply` (Borrow side), detect the floor-clamped condition (`supply_index == SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR` while `supplied > 0` with the debt fully burned) and either (a) burn the residual `supplied` shares so the market restarts clean, or (b) set a tombstone flag that rejects subsequent `supply`/`mint_supply` until governance `recapitalize` restores the market. Symmetrically, `resolve_withdrawal`/`require_reserves` should not allow a stranded floor-index claim to satisfy itself out of fresh cash — the residual must be written off at seize time, not honored later.

### Proof of Concept
The pool's own regression test demonstrates the exploit against production primitives:

1. Seed a market: Alice holds `alice_scaled = 1000 * RAY` supply shares, equal scaled debt outstanding, indexes at RAY, `cash = 0` (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:436-444`).
2. Bad-debt seize runs `apply` → `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(bad_debt)` + `burn_debt`: `supply_index` clamps to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` while Alice's `1000 * RAY` shares remain in `supplied`; `unscale_supply_floor(alice_scaled) > 0` (`interest.rs:452-459`, `seize.rs:24-28`).
3. Bob deposits `deposit = alice_stranded` via `mint_supply` + `credit_cash` (`interest.rs:466-469`).
4. Alice calls `withdraw` → `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, alice_scaled)` → `require_reserves(gross)` passes → `debit_cash(gross)`: `gross == deposit` — Alice is paid Bob's entire principal (`interest.rs:479-485`).
5. Bob's claim `bob_claim` now exceeds `cash`: the market is insolvent and Bob cannot withdraw (`interest.rs:487-493`).
