### Title
Third-party dust supply force-restamps a liquidator-favoring risk tuple onto a foreign account's collateral leg, bypassing the HF ≥ 1.05 gate - (contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs, contracts/controller/src/risk/params.rs)

### Summary
`update_account_threshold` is the intended permissionless path for restamping cached per-position risk parameters, and it deliberately refuses to apply a tuple that favors the liquidator (lower `liquidation_threshold`, higher `liquidation_bonus`, lower `liquidation_fees`) when the resulting health factor would fall below 1.05 (`clears_min_hf` in `risk/params.rs` and the final HF assertion in `sync_account_thresholds`). However, the same `refresh_supply_risk_params` restamping runs inside `merge_supply_leg` on the ordinary `supply` path, which is permissionless for an account's already-existing legs (INV-AUTH-03). The project's own risk documentation concedes the asymmetry: a supply to the leg can apply the liquidator-favoring tuple at any health factor, while `update_account_threshold` cannot. This gives any unprivileged address a write primitive over a foreign account's stored liquidation terms — the analog of the CVE's unauthorized-modification-of-critical-data class — at the cost of a dust deposit.

### Finding Description
- `supply(caller, account_id, spoke_id, assets)` allows a third party to top up a hub asset the target account already holds; it cannot open new slots but can mutate existing ones.
- Merging that deposit calls `refresh_supply_risk_params`, which copies the *currently listed* `(ltv, liquidation_threshold, liquidation_bonus, liquidation_fees)` tuple into the stored `AccountPosition`. `favors_liquidator` detects tuples that worsen the borrower's liquidation terms (lower threshold or higher bonus, or lower fee).
- On the `update_account_threshold(has_risks = true)` path, `clears_min_hf` recomputes HF with the new threshold and `sync_account_thresholds` asserts `hf >= 1.05`, so a keeper cannot push an account toward liquidation. On the supply-merge path no equivalent post-refresh HF gate protects the existing account from the adverse tuple (the documented behavior in `docs/explanation/decisions-r23.md`: "it cannot apply the lower bonus to an account near liquidation, but a supply to the leg can").
- Consequently, after a governance listing change that lowers `liquidation_threshold` or raises `liquidation_bonus`, any address can supply one base unit of the victim's existing collateral asset and stamp the worse terms onto the victim's position immediately — even at HF below 1.05, including pushing the account below HF = 1 where liquidation seizes collateral with the newly raised bonus.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / unauthorized modification of a user's stored risk parameters. The victim's collateral is re-priced under a lower liquidation threshold and a higher bonus than the terms under which the position was opened, converting a solvency buffer into an immediately liquidatable position; a liquidator (which can be the attacker) then seizes collateral at the inflated bonus. The attacker's cost is a dust deposit (recoverable, since the supply is credited to the victim — which is exactly why the attack is asymmetric: the attacker donates dust to gain a profitable liquidation).

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by any unprivileged address whenever governance tightens a spoke listing (lower LT or higher bonus) while accounts hold the affected leg — a routine administrative action. No flash loan, oracle manipulation, or privileged role is required: a single `supply` call on the victim's existing leg triggers the restamp. The main constraint is that the position must already carry the collateral leg, and the pre-change tuple must have been more borrower-favorable.

Caveat: `docs/explanation/decisions-r23.md` documents this asymmetry in the context of Liqvid RWA collateral listing, so reviewers may classify it as an accepted design tradeoff; it is not labeled an ADR decision to permit third-party adverse restamping, only an observed consequence. If the intent was that the 1.05 gate protects near-liquidation accounts universally, the supply path is a bypass of that protection.

### Recommendation
Apply the same `clears_min_hf` / post-refresh HF ≥ 1.05 guard to `merge_supply_leg` when the refreshed tuple is liquidator-favoring (`favors_liquidator` returns true) and the account carries debt — i.e., either keep the stored tuple when the new HF would drop below 1.05, or revert the top-up. Third-party top-ups into legs whose stored tuple is already current remain unaffected.

### Proof of Concept
1. Victim supplies asset C (LT 8000, threshold T0, bonus B0) and borrows D so that HF ≈ 1.04 under T0.
2. Governance executes `edit_asset_in_spoke` lowering C's `liquidation_threshold` to T1 < T0 and raising `liquidation_bonus` to B1 > B0 (a normal listing tightening).
3. Permissionless `update_account_threshold(caller=attacker, has_risks=true, [victim])` correctly reverts with `HealthFactorTooLow` because the new threshold would give HF < 1.05 — the tuple is held.
4. Attacker instead calls `supply(caller=attacker, account_id=victim, spoke_id, [(hub, C, 1)])` (one base unit). `merge_supply_leg` runs `refresh_supply_risk_params` with `FullTuple`, `favors_liquidator` is true, and the stored position is overwritten with `(T1, B1)` — with no HF guard on this path.
5. Victim's HF is now computed under T1 and falls below 1. Attacker calls `liquidate(attacker, victim, debt_payments, SeizeMode::Transfer)`, repaying the cheap debt leg and seizing C collateral at the elevated bonus B1.
6. Net effect: attacker spent dust plus the liquidation repayment and captured the widened bonus spread; the victim's terms were rewritten by a non-owner, something the protocol's own `update_account_threshold` path is explicitly built to prevent.
