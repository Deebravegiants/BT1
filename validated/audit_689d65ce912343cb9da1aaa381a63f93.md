### Title
Unhandled i128 overflow in interest accrual permanently freezes a whale-scale market - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
`calculate_supplier_rewards` multiplies the scaled debt book by the new borrow index with a checked `Ray::mul` that panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` once `borrowed * borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX`. Every pool verb accrues interest before acting, so once the ceiling is crossed, the market can never accrue again: `supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `update_indexes`, liquidation repayments, `clean_bad_debt`, and `recapitalize` all revert permanently, freezing every supplier's and borrower's funds in that market.

### Finding Description
Accrual computes `old_total_debt = borrowed.mul(env, old_borrow_index)` and `new_total_debt = borrowed.mul(env, new_borrow_index)` at common/src/rates/index.rs:80-81. `Ray::mul` overflows at the i128 ceiling (~1.7e38). The supply side has the same exposure at `supplied.mul(env, old_index)` (index.rs:34) and `supply_index_reward_shortfall` (index.rs:60-62).

The codebase's own caps only bound the *index*: `update_borrow_index` clamps at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (index.rs:13-19) and `update_supply_index` clamps at `MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` (index.rs:43-44). Neither bounds the *value* `scaled_amount * index`. A raw scaled book of `1e36` (one billion 18-decimal tokens times RAY) only needs the borrow index to reach ~170x to overflow — well below the index cap — and the borrow index keeps compounding every accrual.

This is demonstrated by the protocol's own harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` in tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-361: after enough accrual, `update_indexes` fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` fail identically because they accrue first.

This is the analog of CVE-2020-19717 (unhandled failure → denial of service): a reachable arithmetic fault with no recovery path that permanently halts the market.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds for all users of the affected market. Suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay or be liquidated, and even governance-triggered flows (`recapitalize`, `clean_bad_debt`) accrue first and revert. There is no admin escape hatch because the panic precedes all state mutation paths.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium/low: an unprivileged attacker cannot warp ledger time, so the overflow cannot be triggered instantly. However, the attacker fully controls the preconditions — they can supply a massive position and self-borrow to pin utilization at ~98% on the steep segment of the interest curve — after which ordinary time-based accrual deterministically drives the market into the cliff. It requires whale-scale capital (on the order of 10^11 token units at 18 decimals) and sustained high utilization, and per-market caps (`lift_caps` in the test suggests governance-set caps would normally constrain size) may limit how large the book can grow. If caps permit a large market, the freeze is deterministic, not probabilistic.

### Recommendation
- Make `Ray::mul` used in accrual saturate or return `Result` and clamp `new_total_debt`/`total_supplied_value` to `i128::MAX` rather than panicking, matching the saturating pattern already used in `mul_div_floor_saturating` and `protocol_fee_shares` (index.rs:94-99).
- Alternatively, cap the scaled book value: enforce `supplied * supply_index <= i128::MAX` (and the same for `borrowed * borrow_index`) inside `update_indexes`/`calculate_supplier_rewards`, or cap `supplied`/`borrowed` at market level so the product can never reach the ceiling — effectively implementing the index cap as a value cap.
- At minimum, provide a recovery path that does not accrue (e.g., a guarded index-reset or forced withdraw path) so a tripped market is not bricked forever.

### Proof of Concept
Existing test, tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-361:

```rust
let mut t = LendingTest::new()
    .with_market(big("BIG18", 18, xlm_curve()))
    .with_market(col())
    .with_max_utilization_disabled_all_markets()
    .build();
lift_caps(&t, "BIG18", 18);
lift_caps(&t, "COL", 7);
let principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18);
t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);
let debt = principal / 100 * 98;
t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);
// advance ledger time; accrual eventually panics
// assert_contract_error(.., errors::MATH_OVERFLOW) on
//   update_indexes, withdraw, repay — market frozen
//   while last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY
```

The attacker-facing flow: `controller.supply` of a whale position, `controller.borrow` against own collateral to ~98% utilization, then wait for accrual via `update_indexes`/any verb until `borrowed.mul(borrow_index)` overflows at index.rs:80-81. Thereafter every entrypoint on that market reverts with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, permanently locking all user funds in it.
