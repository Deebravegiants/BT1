### Title
Accrual-time i128 overflow on grown debt permanently bricks a market's pool operations - (File: common/src/rates/scaling.rs)

### Summary
`global_sync` runs `accrue_step` before every state-changing pool operation, and `accrue_step` recomputes debt value via `scaled_to_original` (`scaled.mul(index)`), which widens to `I256` and then panics with `MathOverflow` when the result does not fit `i128`. Once a market's `borrowed_shares × borrow_index` product crosses `i128::MAX`, accrual reverts unconditionally, so `supply`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `borrow`, `seize`, and `claim_revenue` on that market all trap before any state change — permanently. This is the availability analog of CVE-2024-21204 (attacker-driven conditions cause a repeatable crash): an unprivileged borrower creates the state, and time/accrual finishes it, after which no transaction can unwind it.

### Finding Description
- `common/src/rates/scaling.rs::scaled_to_original` (lines 14–16) computes `scaled * index / RAY` through `mul_div` and panics via `to_i128` (`fp_core.rs:300-303`) when the RAY-scaled debt value exceeds `i128::MAX ≈ 1.7e38`.
- `common/src/rates/simulate.rs::accrue_step` (line 60) calls `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` on every compounding chunk, and `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs::global_sync` (lines 20-33) invokes it before every pool mutation. The panic happens before `mark_accrued`, so `last_timestamp` never advances and every subsequent call re-runs the same overflowing step — an unrecoverable wedge.
- The trigger is reachable without any privileged input: `borrowed` shares grow through ordinary `borrow` (gated only by collateral, caps, and the liquidation buffer), and `borrow_index` grows monotonically to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY = 1e36` through permissionless `update_indexes` and ordinary interest accrual (`common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19`).
- Magnitude: at `borrow_index ≈ 1e36`, a scaled debt of only ~`1.7e29` raw RAY (≈170 token-equivalents of scaled shares) overflows; at `borrow_index = 1e27` it needs ~`1.7e11` tokens of debt. Any combination of large debt and index growth between those endpoints trips it. `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` caps the index but does not cap the product — INV-IDX-01 itself concedes "Debt-value overflow can still revert accrual before that ceiling is reached."

### Impact Explanation
Once the product overflows, every mutating entrypoint on that (hub, token) market reverts in `global_sync` before touching state: suppliers cannot withdraw (`withdraw` → accrue → panic), borrowers cannot repay (`repay` → accrue → panic), liquidations and bad-debt cleanup that touch the pool's accrual path cannot run, and `claim_revenue` fails. Because no path reduces `borrowed` shares or resets `last_timestamp` without first completing accrual, the market's cash and all supplier claims are frozen permanently — a "permanent freezing of funds" outcome, not a fail-closed guard that self-heals.

### Likelihood Explanation
The condition requires sustained high utilization (to keep the borrow rate near `max_borrow_rate`) for enough elapsed time that `borrowed × borrow_index` crosses `i128::MAX`, or an outright huge borrow on a high-decimal market. An unprivileged attacker can hold utilization pinned by borrowing close to `max_utilization`, and the index compounds even when the market is idle — nothing bounds the product. The cost is real (interest on the position, collateral at risk of liquidation), and it takes substantial accrual, so likelihood is moderate-to-low; the outcome, however, is total and irreversible for that market, matching the Medium-severity availability-only profile of the source CVE.

### Recommendation
Saturate rather than panic in the accrual path: in `accrue_step`, compute `borrowed_original` with a saturating mul (or clamp `borrowed` value to `i128::MAX`) and treat an overflowed debt value as "utilization = RAY, interest = capped" so accrual still commits `last_timestamp`. Alternatively, cap `borrowed` shares at borrow time such that `borrowed × MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY ≤ i128::MAX`, which bounds the product by construction at `mint_debt`.

### Proof of Concept
1. On a market with `max_borrow_rate` at the `2 × RAY` cap, an unprivileged account supplies collateral and calls controller `borrow` to hold high utilization; over successive `update_indexes`/accrual calls the borrow index compounds toward `1e36`.
2. When `borrowed_scaled.raw() * borrow_index.raw() / RAY > i128::MAX` — e.g., `borrowed ≈ 1.7e29` raw RAY at the index ceiling — the next call to any pool mutator enters `global_sync` → `accrue_step` → `scaled_to_original` → `mul_div` widened path → `to_i128` → `panic_with_error!(GenericError::MathOverflow)`.
3. `last_timestamp` is never advanced (`mark_accrued` is reached only after the loop), so every later `supply`/`withdraw`/`repay`/`seize`/`claim_revenue` on that market repeats the same panic. Supplier cash is permanently unrecoverable; `recapitalize` cannot rescue it because it also accrues first.

Note: the exact threshold depends on live index and supply scale; I did not execute a live reproduction, but the panic path (`scaled_to_original` → `to_i128`) and the unconditional `global_sync` gate on every mutator are confirmed in `common/src/rates/simulate.rs:51-94`, `common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`, `common/src/math/fp_core.rs:300-303`, and `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33`.
