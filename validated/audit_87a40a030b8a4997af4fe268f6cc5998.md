### Title
Reserve-donation inflation of Aquarius LP oracle price enables over-borrowing and protocol insolvency — (File: common/src/oracle/lp_stable.rs)

### Summary
Markets whose oracle is a single `AquariusLp`/`AquariusStableLp` source price the LP collateral directly from the Aquarius pool's *live* reserves (`aquarius_pool_reserves_call`) divided by total shares. An unprivileged attacker who holds a majority of the pool's LP shares can inflate the reported reserves by a direct token transfer (or a one-sided liquidity deposit) to the pool contract, raising the oracle's LP share price, then supply the LP tokens as collateral and borrow far beyond the real value — leaving bad debt in the lending pool.

### Finding Description
`aquarius::read` in `contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs:90-114` reads `reserve_a`, `reserve_b`, and `total_shares` straight from the Aquarius pool and feeds them into `fair_lp_price_wad` (`common/src/oracle/lp.rs:57-87`) or `fair_stable_lp_price_wad` (`common/src/oracle/lp_stable.rs:80-110`). These "fair value" formulas neutralize *swap-based* imbalance (constant-product uses `2·sqrt(v_a·v_b)`; stable uses `D·min(price_a, price_b)`), but they assume reserves are backed by issued shares. A donation is not a swap: it raises a reserve without minting shares.

- Constant-product: LP price ∝ `sqrt(v_a·v_b)`; donating `3×` a leg's value doubles the reported price.
- Stable: `D` grows roughly linearly with a one-sided reserve increase, so LP price rises near-linearly in the donation.

The attacker supplies their LP shares to the controller via `supply`, the position is valued with the inflated `price_wad` through `Context::cached_price`/`position_value_floor` (`contracts/controller/src/context.rs:154-160`), and `borrow` clears the LTV/health-factor gate on that inflated collateral. They then walk away from the debt; `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` socialize the loss into the supply index. The donated tokens remain ~pro-rata recoverable through the attacker's own majority shareholding, so the net cost is only the `(1 − f)` fraction donated to minority LPs, while the gain is `LTV · f · Δ` of borrowed assets. Configs confirm single-source LP oracles are deployed (e.g., `configs/ops/mainnet/1bce94…json`: single `AquariusLp` source, `tolerance` 0/0, `min_pool_value_wad` = 1e24), and `min_pool_value_wad` only bounds total pool value, not the donation vector.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency: borrows executed against artificially inflated LP collateral exceed the collateral's true value; the resulting bad debt is written down against all suppliers via the supply-index write-down.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires the attacker to own a majority (>~55–60% for typical LTVs) of an Aquarius pool whose LP share is a listed collateral market, plus capital on the order of the pool's reserves for the donation. No privileged access, no oracle compromise, and no timing dependence — all steps (token transfer to the pool, `supply`, `borrow`) are permissionless and atomic within one transaction or a short sequence.

### Recommendation
Measure reserves excluding unbacked donations — e.g., read pool-tracked internal reserves rather than raw token balances if Aquarius exposes both, or cap the LP price against a time-delayed/`virtual_price` observation reported by the pool itself. Alternatively require `min_pool_value_wad` to be enforced on a TWAP-style reserve snapshot, and document a minimum acceptable decentralization of LP share ownership for any market collateralized by a single-source LP oracle.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker acquires >60% of shares of the Aquarius stable pool `P` whose share token is a listed collateral market (`supply` accepts it).
2. Attacker transfers `Δ` of token A directly to `P` (`token.transfer(attacker → P)`), inflating `reserve_a` without minting shares.
3. `supply(lp_token, shares)` — `aquarius::read` computes `fair_stable_lp_price_wad` on the inflated reserves, raising `price_wad`.
4. `borrow(asset, amount)` with `amount` up to `LTV · inflated_value`, exceeding the true collateral value.
5. Attacker keeps the borrowed assets; the lending market's bad debt is absorbed by suppliers (`clean_bad_debt` / supply-index write-down), while the donation remains largely inside shares the attacker still owns.
