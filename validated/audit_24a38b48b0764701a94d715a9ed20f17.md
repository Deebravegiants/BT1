### No vulnerability found for this question.

**Rationale:** The claimed broken equality (`userInfo.userChainType` matching the signer implied by `signature`) is not actually security-relevant for the `TRON` branch, because unlike `SOLANA` and `STELLAR_SEP53`, the `TRON` (and `ERC191`) branches never embed `userInfo.userAddress` into the outgoing payload at all.

For `SOLANA`/`STELLAR_SEP53`, the code explicitly constructs `public_key` from the caller-supplied `userInfo.userAddress` and forwards it as the trusted signer key ( [1](#0-0) , [2](#0-1) ). Because ed25519 signatures don't support public-key recovery, the contract must be told which key to check against, so the local `assert` exists purely to stop the SDK itself from mis-encoding an address under the wrong curve/format before it's trusted downstream.

For `TRON`, the returned `signed_data` only contains `standard: "tip191"`, `payload`, and `signature` — no `public_key` or address field derived from `userInfo` at all ( [3](#0-2) ). This mirrors the `ERC191` branch, which also has no such assert ( [4](#0-3) ). The signer identity for `tip191`/`erc191` standards is derived by the intents contract/relay via secp256k1 signature recovery (ecrecover) over the keccak256 hash of the exact `payload` bytes, as reflected in the hashing helpers `computeSignedTip191Hash`/`computeTip191Hash` and `computeSignedErc191Hash`/`computeErc191Hash` ( [5](#0-4) , [6](#0-5) ). Since `userInfo.userChainType`/`userInfo.userAddress` play no role in constructing the TRON payload, there is nothing for a local assert to protect: the recovered signer is cryptographically bound to whoever actually produced the signature, regardless of what the caller claims their chain type to be. An attacker can never cause the system to attribute an intent to a `signer_id` other than the one whose key actually produced the signature — they can only sign/act as themselves. Therefore the equality `signer_id == recovered public key from signature` is preserved by construction on the verification side, and the absence of the local assert in the `TRON` branch does not introduce the described High-impact vulnerability.
