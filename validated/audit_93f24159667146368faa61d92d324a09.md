### Title
Early withdrawers escape bad-debt loss: supply-index socialization penalizes suppliers who exit after `clean_bad_debt` - ([File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs])

### Summary
The Napier issue is a redemption whose payout floats with a live conversion rate, so earlier redeemers are paid at a better rate than later ones even though both hold identical claims. XOXNO Lending has the same shape in its bad-debt path: `clean_bad_debt` (and `force_socialize_bad_debt`) writes the insolvent account's debt off by **reducing the market's supply index** (`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`), while withdrawals before that call pay the full, un-written-down floor value of the supplier's shares out of pool cash. A supplier who front-runs the permissionless cleanup exits at the pre-loss index; suppliers who wait absorb the entire debt write-down pro-rata on a smaller, already-drained cash balance. The number of tokens a supplier receives for identical shares depends purely on redemption timing relative to the cleanup transaction — the exact bug class of the report.

### Finding Description
- Bad debt is socialized by lowering the affected market's `supply_index`: `new_supply_index = max(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR, floor(old_index * (supply_value - bad_debt) / supply_value))` — `docs/reference/formulas.md:386-395`, exercised by `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/bad_debt_index.rs:196-218` (`clean_bad_debt` demonstrably decreases the supply index).
- `clean_bad_debt` is permissionless once collateral ≤ $5 and ceil debt > half-up collateral — `docs/reference/invariants.md:463-469`. Any unprivileged address can trigger the write-down, and any supplier can withdraw before it.
- Withdrawals convert shares to tokens at the **current** supply index and pay cash (`resolve_withdrawal`, `common/src/rates/scaling.rs:105-121`; `Cache::resolve_withdrawal`, `contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:97-105`). The backing shortfall gate applies only to supply entry and recapitalization — "These checks apply at their respective boundaries; they do not establish full backing after every mutation" (`docs/reference/formulas.md:101-103`), and withdrawal is not blocked by an existing shortfall.
- Concretely: market has 1000 ETH supplied value and a pending $5-dust insolvent account with 200 ETH unrecoverable debt. Before cleanup, supplier A withdraws their 500 ETH claim in full at index ~1.0. Cleanup then writes the index down by 200/(remaining supply), and the remaining supplier B bears the whole 200 ETH loss on a smaller cash pool — A got 500 ETH, B's identical-per-share claim is worth proportionally less plus reduced liquidity. Just like Alice (12.5 ETH) vs Bob (10.833 ETH) in the report, the faster redeemer is paid at a rate the later redeemer can no longer obtain.

### Impact Explanation
Loss of user funds concentrated on slower suppliers: whoever withdraws after `clean_bad_debt`/`force_socialize_bad_debt` absorbs the entire socialized loss, while earlier exiters of identical standing withdrew at the un-impaired index. It also creates a bank-run incentive — rational suppliers race to exit whenever an account nears the cleanup threshold, draining pool cash and worsening the write-down for everyone left.

### Likelihood Explanation
The trigger requires only a realizable bad-debt account (permissionless at ≤$5 collateral, reachable by a single crash), an unprivileged `clean_bad_debt` call, and ordinary `withdraw` calls. Any supplier monitoring the market can submit `withdraw` before anyone calls `clean_bad_debt`. The timing window is guaranteed to exist because eligibility is publicly checkable while cleanup is a discrete, unprivileged transaction.

### Recommendation
The write-down is a documented design choice (ADR-0012), but the timing asymmetry can be mitigated by freezing or throttling withdrawals from the affected market once an account becomes cleanup-eligible, or by charging an exit write-down at withdrawal time whenever pending socializable debt exists (compute the would-be index reduction inside `withdraw`/`resolve_withdrawal` before paying cash). Alternatively, batch cleanup eligibility checks into `update_indexes` so the write-down is applied atomically with the accrual that reveals it, removing the race window.

### Proof of Concept
1. Bob supplies 100 ETH to market (hub, ETH). Alice supplies $8 of collateral and borrows 0.002 ETH; collateral price crashes so debt > collateral and collateral ≤ $5 (`clean_bad_debt` eligible — `invariants.md:463-469`).
2. Before anyone calls `clean_bad_debt`, Bob calls `withdraw` with amount 0 (full position). `resolve_withdrawal` pays floor(scaled × pre-write-down supply_index) in tokens — Bob exits whole.
3. Any keeper calls `clean_bad_debt(account)`; `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` lowers the ETH `supply_index` by the debt/total-supply ratio (`bad_debt_index.rs:196-218`).
4. A second supplier Carol with identical shares now withdraws; her identical scaled shares unscale to materially fewer tokens at the written-down index — and the pool has less cash after Bob's exit.

If the intent was a different bug class (e.g., liquidation or revenue claims), the revenue-claim path has a related but weaker timing asymmetry (`burn_claimable_revenue` pays `min(cash, floor(revenue×index))` — `contracts/pool/src/cache/shares.rs:54-75`), but it is owner-gated and not unprivileged.
