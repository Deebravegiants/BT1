### Title
Unpriced dust collateral can shield an unhealthy account from liquidation - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs)

### Summary
`supply` accepts a listed collateral leg without resolving its oracle price, while liquidation prices every supply leg before checking whether the account is unhealthy. An indebted user can therefore plant a dust position whose feed has become stale or unusable, causing subsequent liquidations and bad-debt cleanup to revert instead of realizing collateral.

### Finding Description
`supply` calls `process_deposit`, which only applies entry gates, transfers the offered assets to the pool, creates supply positions, and submits the resulting pool actions; none of that path requires an aggregate valuation of the account's other assets. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

Liquidation calls `build_liquidation_plan`, which calculates account risk over all supply and debt positions before asserting `health_factor < 1`. [3](#0-2) 

The risk calculation loads markets for the complete union of supply and debt keys, then calls `cached_price` for every supply leg. [4](#0-3) 

`load_markets` fetches every unique asset's strict price before loading indexes, and `cached_price` has no fallback that skips a zero-valued or unavailable leg. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

Consequently, any oracle error for one dust collateral aborts the whole plan before its economic value can be considered.

### Impact Explanation
This is temporary freezing of recovery actions and can allow bad debt to accumulate while the poisoned feed remains unavailable. The harness reproduces the sequence: supplying `0.001` WBTC while its feed is stale succeeds, the account later becomes liquidatable from the USDC price crash, `liquidate` reverts with `PRICE_FEED_STALE`, and `clean_bad_debt` also reverts with `PRICE_FEED_STALE`. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7) 

A listed Aquarius LP collateral can make the outage self-inflicted: an LP holder can keep a dust share, withdraw enough liquidity from the backing pool to push pool value below `min_pool_value_wad`, and then use that share as the poisoned leg. [9](#0-8) 

### Likelihood Explanation
The attacker only needs an already indebted account, a listed collateral market whose price is unavailable, and enough of that asset for one nonzero supply share. Supply is permissionless for the account owner, and the poisoned leg can be economically negligible because pricing iterates over position keys rather than ignoring dust. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10) 

The path remains blocked only while the oracle fault persists; restoring the feed makes the same liquidation succeed. [12](#0-11) 

### Recommendation
During liquidation and bad-debt cleanup, do not require a strict price for collateral legs below a governance-defined valuation dust bound, or bound their value to zero while still seizing/removing the leg according to an explicit policy. Alternatively, reject new supply of an asset whose price cannot currently be resolved, so borrowers cannot add an already unpriceable leg; this alone does not cover positions that become unpriceable after deposit.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker owns account `A` with USDC collateral and ETH debt.
2. WBTC's configured feed becomes stale.
3. Attacker calls:

   `supply(caller=attacker, account_id=A, spoke_id=spoke, assets=[(HubAssetKey { hub_id, asset=WBTC }, 0.001)])`

   The call succeeds because supply does not fetch a strict WBTC price.
4. USDC's price falls enough that `A` is otherwise unhealthy.
5. A liquidator calls:

   `liquidate(liquidator=L, account_id=A, debt_payments=[(HubAssetKey { hub_id, asset=ETH }, amount)], seize_mode=SeizeMode::Transfer)`

   `build_liquidation_plan` prices the WBTC dust leg, receives `PriceFeedStale`, and aborts before repayment or seizure.
6. `clean_bad_debt(caller=L, account_id=A)` follows the same all-position valuation path and aborts while the stale leg remains.
7. Restoring the WBTC feed makes the identical liquidation succeed, demonstrating that the dust leg's price availability—not insufficient debt repayment—is the blocker. [13](#0-12)

    pub(crate) fn fetch_prices(&mut self, assets: &Vec<Address>) {
        let missing = collect_uncached_keys(&self.env, assets, &self.token_prices);
